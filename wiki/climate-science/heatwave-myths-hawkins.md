# Heatwave myths and how to bust them

## What is it

This page collects the most common sceptical questions about heatwaves and offers short, usable answers. The structure follows a July 2026 **Guardian article by Jonathan Watts**, who put each myth to **Prof Ed Hawkins at the University of Reading** — the climate scientist who invented the famous "climate stripes" graphic. **The core message that runs through every rebuttal: the warming is caused by burning fossil fuels, the physics has been known since the 1850s, and every heatwave today is hotter than it would have been without human emissions.**

The Guardian piece listed 13 myths; the clipping captured 11-12 clearly. The ones below are the ones we have — and they cover the main shape of the denial, from "it's natural" through to "humans are puny, it's too late".

## Why it matters

Heatwaves are the climate impact most people have direct experience of in Europe, so they are also where denial and distraction land hardest. A reader who can respond calmly to the main lines — "it's just the sun", "it's El Niño", "we had 1976 and we were fine", "the warnings are overreach" — can hold the line in a real conversation without needing to be a climate scientist. That is the point of a myth-busting kit, and this one has a strong single-expert anchor behind it.

## The myths and the rebuttals

### 1. Isn't climate change caused by something other than humans?

**Say this:** No. Global warming is caused by human activities, primarily burning fossil fuels. Every heatwave and every summer is hotter than it would have been without those greenhouse-gas emissions. That is the simple message from climate scientists around the world.

### 2. "This is a complex subject with real uncertainty. Isn't there room for doubt?"

**Say this:** The physics is simple and has been known since the **1850s**. The first quantification of CO2's warming effect was in the **1890s**. In **1938**, Guy Callendar, an English steam engineer, was the first to show the world had already warmed by about 0.3°C and link it to coal burning. That was more than 80 years ago. Today the evidence is overwhelming. There is no doubt among scientists, even if other people refuse to believe it.

### 3. "Aren't this year's heatwaves caused by El Niño or natural cycles?"

**Say this:** We've always had extreme weather patterns, and we always will. The difference is that the consequences are now worse because of our warmer world. The current El Niño has only just started and won't peak until Christmas 2026 — it is not the main driver of the summer heatwaves we are seeing now.

### 4. "What about the theory that the Earth is moving closer to the sun?"

**Say this:** Earth's orbit does affect climate, but over **tens of thousands of years**. At the time steam engines were invented in the 18th century, the orbit was actually pushing the Earth onto a slightly cooler path. What we are seeing instead is rapid warming — and that is from Industrial Revolution fossil-fuel burning, not orbital change.

### 5. "I remember the 1976 UK heatwave. How is this summer any different?"

**Say this:** 1976 was an isolated northern-European hotspot — nearby regions had a cool summer. The peak of **June 2026 was about 2°C hotter than June 1976** in the UK, and it shattered records across a much wider area of Europe. Southern Europe and other regions have been breaking records too. In 1976, two weeks above 32°C killed about **250 extra people per day** in the UK. A similar pattern in today's hotter world would be even more dangerous.

### 6. "Isn't it impossible to attribute any single extreme event to climate change?"

**Say this:** Climate change doesn't cause the weather patterns that lead to extreme events — those have always happened. But when they do occur, **climate change makes the consequences more severe** (hotter heatwaves, heavier rainfall). That amplification can be measured with **attribution studies**. There are many methods now, and they all show the same big picture: heatwaves are becoming hotter because of human activities.

### 7. "Aren't the records too short to show clear trends?"

**Say this:** Temperature records go back to the **early 1800s**, and ice-core records go back hundreds of thousands of years. The last time the world was as hot as it is now was about **125,000 years ago**. Today's CO2 levels are higher than anything in those ice-core records. The increase comes from burning fossil fuels and deforestation.

### 8. "Can't we just enjoy heatwaves — beaches and ice-cream?"

**Say this:** Lots of people do. But many others cannot — people with heart disease or diabetes, elderly people, children at school unable to keep cool, patients in hospital, workers in overheated workplaces. For them, a heatwave is miserable or dangerous. At very high temperatures, even fit people are at risk. The media should use more balanced images.

### 9. "Other countries manage with mid-30s°C. Why should the UK get so bothered?"

**Say this:** **UK homes and offices were built for the cooler climate of the past**, not the present and certainly not the future. They were designed to keep warm in winter, not cool in summer. The UK has little air conditioning and no shutters on windows, unlike Mediterranean countries. British society has to adapt or suffer the consequences.

### 10. "Aren't heat warnings just scaring people? Government overreach?"

**Say this:** **No. They save lives.** The 2003 European heatwave killed an estimated **40,000-70,000 people** because the continent was not prepared. Since then, we have much better warning systems: drink water, keep curtains shut during the day, don't go out at peak heat. Temperatures have risen, but excess deaths have fallen.

### 11. "Isn't there an upside — CO2 is plant food, so better harvests?"

**Say this:** No. We can only grow what the weather lets us grow. In idealised conditions, some plant species do grow better in high CO2, but in the real world **harvests are suffering** from hot dry periods and changed rainfall patterns.

### 12. "Humans are puny compared with nature. Isn't it too late?"

**Say this:** It is never too late. Because human actions are causing this, human actions can also solve it. We can keep burning fossil fuels and watch heatwaves get hotter — or we can reach **global net zero**, which stabilises the climate and stops things getting worse. That is the choice we have to make, for our children and grandchildren.

## Three cross-cutting lessons from the whole set

1. **The physics is older than you think.** 1850s theory, 1890s quantification, 1938 observation. When someone frames climate science as "new and uncertain", the historical dates alone are the rebuttal.
2. **Weather is what has always happened; climate change changes the odds.** Attribution studies now measure the amplification. The rebuttal to "but we've had hot summers before" is not "no we haven't" — it's "the stakes are now higher".
3. **Adaptation is a real policy problem, not an excuse to do nothing.** The UK homes-built-for-the-cooler-past point and the 2003 heatwave warning-systems point are both adaptation stories. Mitigation (net zero) and adaptation (warning systems, building retrofits, shutters) go together.
