# Myth: solar panels and wind turbines create huge amounts of waste

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Solar and wind create far less waste than coal, per unit of electricity — about 50 times less than coal for solar, 500 times less for wind. The photos of dumped turbine blades that go around social media leave out how much coal ash we've been quietly producing for decades. Renewables aren't waste-free, but they're vastly less wasteful than what they're replacing.

## Why it sounds right

Because there are real photos, and they get shared a lot. A pile of old wind blades or dumped solar panels in a field is a striking image. What the photo doesn't show you is the coal ash lagoons that have been sitting outside every coal plant for a century, or the volume of oil sludge from decades of drilling. The visible dumping site is small compared to the invisible fossil fuel one.

## What is actually true

**Waste per megawatt-hour of electricity:**

| Source | kg per MWh |
|---|---|
| Coal | 89 |
| Solar PV | 2 |
| Wind | 0.2 |

- Coal creates about **50 times more waste than solar** and **500 times more than wind**, per unit of electricity.

**Waste over 25 years, for one average UK person** (if all their electricity came from one source):

| Source | Total waste (kg) |
|---|---|
| Municipal household waste | 12,100 |
| Coal | 10,700 |
| Plastic | 1,900 |
| Electronic waste | 600 |
| Solar PV | 200 |
| Wind | 20 |

A person's coal waste over 25 years would fill about 10 tonnes. Their solar waste: 200 kg. Wind: 20 kg.

**Toxicity matters too:**
- **Wind turbine blades**: 80-85% of turbine parts are recyclable. Blades are the hard bit — hard to recycle, often go to landfill — but they're classified as **non-hazardous** in the US and Europe. Low health risk.
- **Solar panels**: contain small amounts of **lead** in silicon panels, and traces of **cadmium** in cadmium telluride panels (~0.1% of panel weight). Manageable at end-of-life if collected properly.
- **Coal ash**: contains **mercury, arsenic, lead, cadmium and chromium** — far more dangerous, and produced continuously for the plant's life.

Switching from coal to renewables cuts both **volume** and **toxicity** of waste.

## If they push back

**"But wind blades are ending up in landfill."**

Some are, and this is a real problem the industry is working on. **Siemens** and others are producing recyclable blades. Several European governments are proposing landfill bans for turbine blades by the end of the decade. Compared to coal ash landfills, the volume is tiny — but "small compared to worse" isn't a reason to stop working on the specific problem.

**"Solar panels are full of toxic materials."**

Silicon panels contain small amounts of lead (soldering); cadmium-telluride panels contain trace cadmium. Both can be extracted and reused, and manufacturers are already doing this where end-of-life panels are properly collected. The material-toxicity issue is real but well-understood and manageable.

**"We haven't figured out recycling yet."**

Aluminium and glass in solar panels can already be recycled — the main materials are the same as in drinks cans and bottles. Recycling rates are still low in many countries, but most panels installed today have another 10-20 years of life. The industry has time to build the recycling chain, and countries like Germany and Japan are already ahead.

## Go deeper

**Coal was quietly the worst.** For most of the past century, coal ash was dumped in vast open lagoons next to power plants, in volumes that dwarf anything modern renewables produce. Coal ash spills — Kingston (Tennessee, 2008) and Dan River (North Carolina, 2014) among the worst — have contaminated large watersheds. The photos of dumped wind blades that dominate social media are marketing waste from a much cleaner industry, not evidence of a scale problem.

**Wind blades — the specific challenge.** Blades are made of fibreglass and epoxy resins that are hard to separate. Currently, most old blades either go to landfill (classified as non-hazardous), are shredded for cement kiln fuel, or repurposed structurally (bridges, playgrounds, benches). Genuinely recyclable blades (Siemens Gamesa RecyclableBlade, others) are entering service. This is one problem the industry knows about and is solving.

**Solar recycling is more advanced than most people think.** Aluminium frames and glass are already collected and recycled in mature markets. Silicon wafers can be reused in new panels. Rare earths in modules can be recovered. Getting recycling rates up in every country is a policy priority (EU WEEE Directive, updated 2024), not an unsolved technical problem.

**Look ahead**: even if solar and wind use doubles, the waste they produce will still be tiny next to coal, plastic, or household waste. This is a manageable industrial challenge, not a runaway problem.
