# Myth: Renewables can't keep the lights on when the wind stops

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Grid operators handle this with links between countries, storage and shifting demand. The last stretch is hard, but the first three quarters is being built right now.

## Why it sounds right

Wind and solar really are variable, and a grid has to balance supply and demand every second, so the worry makes sense. Grid operators take it seriously, which is why they plan for it rather than dismiss it.

## What is actually true

There's no single fix. Variability is managed with a mix of tools: spreading wind and solar farms over a wide area, interconnection between countries, storage, and shifting flexible demand to windy hours. Grid-scale batteries now respond faster than gas plants can, and their costs have fallen steeply. Ireland already runs large shares of its electricity on wind for long periods.

The last stretch to a fully reliable zero-carbon grid is hard and still being worked out. The **first 70 or 80%** is a solved engineering problem, and it's being built today.

## If they push back

**"So you admit you need gas backup"**

For now, some. The question is how fast that shrinks, rather than whether renewables work. Nobody serious claims we finish this tomorrow.

## Also heard as

- "you need baseload power"
- "what happens on a still winter night"
- "we'll always need gas as backup"
