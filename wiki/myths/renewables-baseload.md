# Myth: Renewables can't keep the lights on when the wind stops

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Grid operators solve this with interconnection, storage and demand shifting. The last stretch is genuinely hard. The first three quarters is being built right now.

## Why it sounds right

Wind and solar genuinely are variable, and a grid that has to balance supply and demand every second cannot ignore that. Grid operators take this seriously, which is why it gets solved rather than dismissed.

## What is actually true

Variability is managed with a portfolio rather than a single fix: geographic spread, interconnection between countries, storage, and shifting flexible demand to windy hours. Grid scale batteries now respond faster than gas plants can, and their costs have fallen steeply. Ireland already runs large shares of its electricity on wind for extended periods. The honest position is that the last stretch to a fully firm zero carbon grid is genuinely hard and still being worked out, while the first 70 or 80% is a solved engineering problem being deployed today.

## If they push back

**"So you admit you need gas backup"**

For now, some. The argument is about how fast that shrinks, not whether renewables work. Nobody serious claims we finish this tomorrow.

## Also heard as

- "you need baseload power"
- "what happens on a still winter night"
- "we'll always need gas as backup"
