# Myth: clean energy uses too much water to be sustainable

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Overall, clean energy uses far less water than fossil fuels, because coal, gas and nuclear plants need huge amounts for cooling and solar and wind don't. The real problem is local: lithium and copper mining in a few dry places like northern Chile, and that needs fixing there.

## Why it sounds right

The local water impacts are real and severe. In parts of northern Chile, mining uses more than half of all the fresh water in the region, and Indigenous communities have been badly affected. Investigative reporting on lithium and copper mining focuses on exactly these places. If you've read it, "mining is killing water supplies" is a documented fact in those areas. Where it goes wrong is in stretching that into "clean energy uses too much water" worldwide, when the fossil fuel system uses far more.

## What is actually true

Where the local problem is real:
- **16% of critical mineral mines** are in areas with high water stress, meaning demand comes close to or exceeds the water available.
- For copper and lithium, about half of world supply comes from regions with high or extreme water stress.
- In parts of northern Chile, mining uses over half of all fresh water, causing serious conflict with local communities.

But mining is a small share of overall water use. Even in Chile, mining uses just 4% of water, while farming uses 72%.

Today, EVs use more water than petrol cars:
- In China, an EV uses 262 m³ of water over its life, against 137 m³ for a petrol car, almost double.
- In the US, an EV uses about ⅓ more water than a petrol car.
- The reason is that 82% of that water goes into making the electricity, not building the car or battery, and most electricity is still made from fossil fuels.

Once the electricity is clean, EVs win on water too. Charged with solar or wind, they use about two-thirds less water than petrol cars.

Compared with fossil power:
- Solar and wind use 5-10× less water than natural gas per unit of electricity.
- Solar and wind use 10-100× less water than coal.
- Coal, gas and nuclear plants all need huge volumes of water to cool their equipment. Solar panels and wind turbines need almost none.

Water use for power in the US is already falling as the country moves from coal to gas and then to solar and wind.

## If they push back

**"But EVs use more water than petrol cars."**

Today, yes, because the electricity is mostly from fossil fuels, and those power plants need enormous amounts of cooling water. As the grid gets cleaner, EVs cross over to using less water than petrol cars, and on a solar and wind grid they use two-thirds less.

**"Chile is running out of water because of lithium."**

Northern Chile has a real crisis, and lithium brine extraction is part of it. The fix is **direct lithium extraction** (DLE), which pulls lithium from brine without evaporating the water, and it is already being used. Still, lithium mining uses 4% of water in Chile and farming uses 72%, so both need tackling.

**"So clean energy is fine on water then?"**

Locally, no: some mining regions are being pushed to their water limits. Globally, yes: clean energy uses far less water than the fossil system it replaces. Both are true.

## Go deeper

**Two ways to get lithium.** Hard rock mining, as in Australia, digs it out of the ground, and uses about as much water as other mining. Brine extraction, used in Chile, Argentina and Bolivia (the "lithium triangle"), pumps salty underground water to the surface and leaves it in open pools to evaporate. That loses a lot of water in a region that has very little, and the mining also pollutes water used by local Indigenous communities. The fix is **Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE)**, a chemical process that pulls lithium straight out of brine without evaporating the water. Several companies, including Vulcan Energy, Lilac Solutions and Standard Lithium, are already bringing it to market.

**How fossil fuel plants use water.** Coal and gas plants use huge volumes of water for **cooling**. They usually take water from rivers, run it through the plant to absorb heat, then release it warmer than it was. Some plants use closed-loop cooling towers, which use less water but are less efficient. Nuclear plants have similar needs. Solar panels and wind turbines have no cooling loop and use almost no water once built.

**Recycling closes the loop.** Recycling batteries, solar panels and wind turbines sharply cuts the need for new mining, which reduces water demand in exactly the places where water stress is worst. Recycling is growing but isn't mature yet, so this is a real solution 10-20 years away.

**What policy needs to do.** Global water use for energy should keep falling as fossil fuels retire. Local water conflicts need direct work with mining communities, water-saving extraction such as DLE, stricter mining permits and, where necessary, siting new mines in less water-stressed regions. None of this is unsolvable, but it needs specific attention rather than being lumped into "clean energy is unsustainable."
