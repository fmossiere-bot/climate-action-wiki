# Myth: clean energy uses too much water to be sustainable

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Overall, clean energy uses much less water than fossil fuels — coal, gas and nuclear plants all need huge volumes for cooling, and solar and wind don't. The real problem is local: lithium and copper mining happens in a few specific places that are already very short on water, especially northern Chile. EVs also use more water than petrol cars today, but only because they're mostly charged with fossil electricity — with solar and wind, they use two-thirds less.

## Why it sounds right

Because the local water impacts are real and severe. In parts of northern Chile, mining uses more than half of all the fresh water in the region. Indigenous communities have been badly affected. Investigative journalism on lithium and copper mining focuses on exactly these places. If you're reading that reporting, "mining is killing water supplies" isn't a myth — it's a documented fact in specific places. The mistake is generalising it into "clean energy uses too much water" globally, when the fossil fuel system uses far more.

## What is actually true

**Where the local problem is real:**
- **16% of critical mineral mines** are in areas with **high water stress**
- For **copper and lithium**, about **half of world supply** comes from high or extreme water stress regions
- In parts of northern **Chile**, **mining uses over half** of all fresh water — creating serious conflict with local communities

**But mining is a small share of overall water use:**
- Even in Chile, **mining uses just 4% of water**; **farming uses 72%**

**EV vs petrol — today's picture:**
- **China**: an EV uses **262 m³** of water over its life vs **137 m³** for petrol — almost double
- **US**: an EV uses about **⅓ more water** than a petrol car
- Reason: **82%** of the water use comes from **making the electricity**, not building the car or battery — and most electricity today is still fossil fuels

**Once electricity goes clean, EVs win on water too:**
- EVs charged with **solar or wind** use about **two-thirds less water** than petrol cars

**Solar and wind vs fossil energy — the big picture:**
- Solar and wind use **5-10× less water than natural gas** per unit of electricity
- Solar and wind use **10-100× less water than coal**
- Coal, gas and nuclear plants all need huge volumes of water to cool their equipment
- Solar panels and wind turbines need essentially none

**Water use in the US is already falling** as the country moves from coal → gas → solar and wind.

## If they push back

**"But EVs use more water than petrol cars."**

Today, yes — because the electricity is mostly fossil fuels. Fossil power plants need enormous amounts of water for cooling, and that shows up in the EV lifecycle. As the grid decarbonises, EVs cross over to using less water than petrol cars. In a solar/wind grid, they use two-thirds less.

**"Chile is running out of water because of lithium."**

Northern Chile has a real crisis, and lithium brine extraction is part of it. The specific fix is **direct lithium extraction** (DLE) — pulling lithium from brine without evaporating the water. This works and is being deployed. The broader point still stands: lithium mining uses 4% of water in Chile; farming uses 72%. Both need addressing.

**"So clean energy is fine on water then?"**

Locally, no — some mining regions are being pushed to their water limits. Globally, yes — clean energy uses far less water than the fossil system it's replacing. Both things are true.

## Go deeper

**Two ways to get lithium.** **Hard rock mining** (Australia) digs it out of the ground; water use is comparable to other mining. **Brine extraction** (Chile, Argentina, Bolivia — the "lithium triangle") pumps salty underground water to the surface and lets it sit in open pools to evaporate. That evaporates lots of water in a region that has very little of it, and the mining also **pollutes** water for local Indigenous communities. The fix is **Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE)** — a chemical process that pulls lithium straight out of brine without evaporating water. Several companies (Vulcan Energy, Lilac Solutions, Standard Lithium) are already commercialising it.

**Where fossil fuel plants use their water.** Coal and gas plants use water for **cooling** — huge volumes of it. They typically withdraw water from rivers, run it through the plant to absorb heat, then discharge it warmer than they took it. Some plants use closed-loop cooling towers that consume less but are less efficient. Nuclear plants have similar demands. Solar panels and wind turbines have no cooling loop and use essentially no water once built.

**Recycling closes the loop.** Recycling batteries, solar panels and wind turbines dramatically cuts the need for fresh mining, which reduces water demand at exactly the places where local water stress is worst. The recycling infrastructure is scaling up but isn't fully mature yet — this is a real solution 10-20 years out.

**The pattern for policy.** Both problems need addressing — global water use should keep falling as fossil fuels retire, but local water conflicts need direct engagement with mining communities, water-saving extraction technology (DLE), stricter mining permits, and where necessary, moving new mines to less water-stressed regions. This isn't unsolvable, but it needs specific attention rather than being lumped into "clean energy is unsustainable."
