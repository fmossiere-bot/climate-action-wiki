# Myth: nuclear power is too dangerous

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Nuclear power isn't risk-free, but it is one of the safest energy sources we have. Per unit of electricity, it kills fewer people than coal or gas by a factor of hundreds, and it's roughly on par with solar and wind. The fear is much bigger than the actual risk. What is dangerous is the fossil fuel system nuclear could displace — that quietly kills 4 to 8 million people a year through air pollution.

## Why it sounds right

Because Chernobyl and Fukushima are burned into public memory, and any nuclear accident is dramatic, one-off, image-heavy. Chernobyl became a Netflix series. Fossil fuel deaths happen quietly, spread out, 11,000 to 22,000 people every single day from air pollution — and there's no headline for that. Anyone whose reference point is the drama of the accidents will overweight nuclear's real risk.

## What is actually true

Deaths per terawatt-hour of electricity generated (Markandya & Wilkinson, Sovacool, UNSCEAR):

| Energy source | Deaths per TWh |
|---|---|
| Brown coal | 33 |
| Coal | 25 |
| Gas | 2.8 |
| Hydro | 1.3 |
| Wind, Solar, Nuclear | <0.1 each |

- Nuclear causes **>99% fewer deaths than coal** and **~98% fewer than gas**.
- Wind, nuclear and solar are all so close in the data that we can't confidently rank them — but all three are dramatically safer than fossil fuels. That's the point.

The two big disasters, honestly counted:

- **Three Mile Island (1979, level 5)**: zero deaths.
- **Fukushima (2011, level 7)**: no one died directly in the disaster. About 40-50 injuries. One later cancer death (disputed). Around **2,313 premature deaths from the evacuation** (elderly displacement, healthcare disruption). Total ~2,314. Wider-population cancer studies find no measurable increase.
- **Chernobyl (1986, level 7)**: confirmed direct toll under 100. A reasonable broader estimate is **300 to 500 deaths**. Higher WHO figures (up to 4,000 long-term) are now considered too high; UNSCEAR follow-ups found no solid evidence of higher cancer rates in the wider population.

Even taking the highest figure (4,000 across all nuclear history), coal air pollution kills more people every single week.

## If they push back

**"You can't compare a rare huge accident with steady day-to-day deaths."**

You can and you have to. Both are real. Fossil fuel deaths are not less real because they happen quietly. If your decision framework only counts dramatic events, you end up preferring the system that kills more people overall.

**"What about the next Chernobyl?"**

A future accident is not impossible, especially as more plants get built. The right response is safer reactor designs and sensible siting, not sticking with fossil fuels — which are quietly killing millions today.

**"Nuclear plants are terrorism targets."**

Modern containment structures are extremely hard to breach. Water reservoirs behind hydro dams are also potentially catastrophic if attacked. The response is security, not abandonment.

## Go deeper

**Why the perception gap is so wide.** Nuclear accidents are rare, dramatic and photogenic. Fossil fuel deaths are quiet, distributed and hard to attribute individually. Media coverage tracks drama, not scale. So the ratio of coverage to actual harm is upside-down: nuclear gets the coverage, fossil fuels do the damage.

**Fukushima's real death toll was the evacuation.** Almost none of the ~2,314 excess deaths at Fukushima came from radiation. They came from the trauma and healthcare disruption of a mass evacuation of over 100,000 people. That is a public-health tragedy, but it is a tragedy about **how emergencies are managed**, not about nuclear physics. Future evacuations should learn from this.

**Chernobyl was an outlier, not the norm.** The RBMK reactor design at Chernobyl had specific flaws (a positive void coefficient, no proper containment building) that were known to Soviet designers and would never be permitted today. Modern reactors are fundamentally different. Comparing all future nuclear risk to Chernobyl is like judging modern aviation by 1970s crash statistics.

**The three points that matter.** (1) Nuclear is dramatically safer than fossil fuels. (2) Nuclear is roughly as safe as solar and wind. (3) Zooming into the tiny difference between nuclear/solar/wind is not useful — the data isn't precise enough, and the gap with fossil fuels dwarfs it.
