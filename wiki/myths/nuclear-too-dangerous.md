# Myth: nuclear power is too dangerous

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Nuclear isn't risk-free, but for the electricity it makes it kills hundreds of times fewer people than coal or gas, about the same as solar and wind. The quiet danger is the fossil fuel system it could replace, whose air pollution kills 4 to 8 million people a year.

## Why it sounds right

Chernobyl and Fukushima are fixed in public memory, and any nuclear accident is sudden, dramatic and filmed. Chernobyl even became a Netflix series. Fossil fuel deaths happen quietly and spread out: air pollution kills 11,000 to 22,000 people every single day, and none of them make the news. If your picture of nuclear comes from those accidents, it's natural to overestimate the risk.

## What is actually true

Deaths per terawatt-hour of electricity generated (Markandya & Wilkinson, Sovacool, UNSCEAR):

| Energy source | Deaths per TWh |
|---|---|
| Brown coal | 33 |
| Coal | 25 |
| Gas | 2.8 |
| Hydro | 1.3 |
| Wind, Solar, Nuclear | <0.1 each |

Nuclear causes **more than 99% fewer deaths than coal** and about 98% fewer than gas. Wind, nuclear and solar are so close in the data that they can't be ranked with confidence, but all three are far safer than fossil fuels.

The major accidents, counted in full:

- Three Mile Island (1979, level 5): zero deaths.
- Fukushima (2011, level 7): no one died directly in the disaster, and there were about 40-50 injuries. One later cancer death is disputed. Around 2,313 premature deaths came from the evacuation, mostly elderly people displaced and cut off from healthcare, for a total of about 2,314. Studies of the wider population find no measurable increase in cancer.
- Chernobyl (1986, level 7): the confirmed direct toll is under 100. A reasonable broader estimate is 300 to 500 deaths. Higher WHO figures, up to 4,000 long-term deaths, are now considered too high, and UNSCEAR follow-ups found no solid evidence of higher cancer rates in the wider population.

Even taking the highest figure of 4,000 across all of nuclear history, coal air pollution kills more people every single week.

## If they push back

**"You can't compare a rare huge accident with steady day-to-day deaths."**

You can, and you have to, because both are real. Fossil fuel deaths aren't less real for being quiet. If you only count dramatic events, you end up preferring the system that kills more people overall.

**"What about the next Chernobyl?"**

A future accident isn't impossible, especially as more plants are built. The answer is safer reactor designs and sensible siting, rather than sticking with fossil fuels that are killing millions today.

**"Nuclear plants are terrorism targets."**

Modern containment structures are extremely hard to breach. Hydro dams would also be catastrophic if attacked. The answer is good security, not giving up the technology.

## Go deeper

### Why the perception gap is so wide

Nuclear accidents are rare, dramatic and easy to photograph. Fossil fuel deaths are quiet, spread out and hard to pin on any one cause. News coverage follows drama rather than scale, so nuclear gets the coverage while fossil fuels do most of the damage.

### Fukushima's death toll came from the evacuation

Almost none of the roughly 2,314 excess deaths at Fukushima were caused by radiation. They came from the trauma and broken healthcare of evacuating over 100,000 people. That is a real public health tragedy, but it's about **how emergencies are managed**, not about nuclear physics. Future evacuations should learn from it.

### Chernobyl was an outlier

The RBMK reactor at Chernobyl had specific design flaws, including a positive void coefficient and no proper containment building. Soviet designers knew about them, and they would never be allowed today. Modern reactors are very different. Judging all future nuclear risk by Chernobyl is like judging modern flying by 1970s crash statistics.

### What the data says overall

Nuclear is far safer than fossil fuels, and roughly as safe as solar and wind. Arguing over the tiny differences between nuclear, solar and wind isn't useful: the data isn't precise enough, and the gap with fossil fuels is far bigger.
