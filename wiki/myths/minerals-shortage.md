# Myth: There aren't enough minerals in the world to build all this

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

We dig up about 15 billion tonnes of fossil fuel every year and set fire to it. The transition needs around 30 million tonnes of minerals a year, and you can use those again.

## Why it sounds right

Clean technology does use more minerals. The IEA notes that a typical electric car needs about six times the mineral inputs of a conventional one, and an onshore wind plant about nine times more than a gas plant.

## What is actually true

Using more minerals isn't the same as running out. The Energy Transitions Commission found no fundamental shortage of key materials: known geological resources exceed projected demand out to 2050. Demand for critical minerals roughly triples by 2030, to around **30 million tonnes a year**. Compare that with the roughly 15 billion tonnes of fossil fuels extracted every year now. A 2023 study in Joule put total demand to 2050 at up to 1.96 billion tonnes of steel, 241 million tonnes of aluminium and 82 million tonnes of copper, small fractions of accessible reserves. It found annual production would not need to grow by more than 20% for supply to meet demand. And unlike coal, these materials can be recovered and used again.

## If they push back

**"But the mines don't exist yet"**

That's the real constraint, and it's about permits and investment timelines, not geology. It's worth arguing about, but it's a different argument.

## Also heard as

- "we'll run out of lithium"
- "there isn't enough copper for the grid"
- "peak minerals"

## Go deeper

Mining has social and environmental consequences. But compare the environmental impact of burning fossil fuels with that of mining materials for renewable energy. Yes, mining will be needed, and renewable energy isn't completely clean, but it is far better than the alternative and its impact will shrink over time.
