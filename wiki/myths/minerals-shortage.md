# Myth: There aren't enough minerals in the world to build all this

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

We dig up about 15 billion tonnes of fossil fuel every year and set fire to it. The transition needs around 30 million tonnes of minerals a year, and you can use those again.

## Why it sounds right

Clean technology genuinely is more mineral intensive. The IEA notes a typical electric car needs about six times the mineral inputs of a conventional one, and an onshore wind plant about nine times more than a gas plant.

## What is actually true

More intensive is not the same as impossible. The Energy Transitions Commission found no fundamental shortage of key materials, with known geological resources exceeding projected demand out to 2050. Critical mineral demand roughly triples by 2030, to around 30 million tonnes a year, set against roughly 15 billion tonnes of fossil fuels extracted every year now. A 2023 study in Joule put total demand to 2050 at up to 1.96 billion tonnes of steel, 241 million tonnes of aluminium and 82 million tonnes of copper, small fractions of accessible reserves, and found annual production need not grow by more than 20% for supply to meet demand. Unlike coal, these materials can be recovered and used again.

## If they push back

**"But the mines don't exist yet"**

That is the real constraint, and it is about permitting and investment timelines, not geology. Worth arguing about. Different argument though.

## Also heard as

- "we'll run out of lithium"
- "there isn't enough copper for the grid"
- "peak minerals"
