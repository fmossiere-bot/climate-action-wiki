# Myth: electric cars are only good for short trips

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

That was true in 2010, when the average EV had 80 miles of range. It isn't now. Most new EVs comfortably do 200+ miles on a charge, plenty do 400+, and the top of the range is over 500. The average UK driver does 20 miles a day, the average American 40. For daily driving, range is a solved problem. The only real issue left is charging reliability on the occasional long trip.

## Why it sounds right

Because the myth is a decade behind the technology. Early EVs really were only good for short trips — the first Nissan Leaf, the BMW ActiveE, all managed less than 100 miles per charge. That was the honest state of the art around 2010-2012. People whose reference point is that era, or who haven't looked closely since, are working from an accurate memory of a very different market.

## What is actually true

Range has grown massively in a decade:

- **2010 average EV range: ~80 miles** on a single charge
- **2021 average: ~220 miles** — nearly triple
- **US average (bigger cars are popular): ~450 miles**
- Plenty of mainstream models now offer **280+ miles**
- Top of the market: in 2011 the longest-range EV (BMW ActiveE) managed **94 miles**. By 2022, the record was **520 miles** (Lucid Air Dream R). The record will keep moving.

Daily driving reality:

- **Average UK driver: 20 miles/day** (even less if two-car household)
- **Average US driver: ~40 miles/day**

Both fit comfortably within even the cheapest, lowest-range new EVs.

Long trips: on a motorway at the speed limit, you'd cover about **280 miles in 4 hours**. Drivers are advised to take a **15-minute break every few hours anyway**. That lines up with when the EV needs a top-up.

Battery degradation is often exaggerated: studies across tens of thousands of vehicles find batteries lose about **2% capacity per year**, much slower than most people assume.

## If they push back

**"But I do a 500-mile road trip once a year."**

Two options. Either buy a longer-range EV (they exist), or take one 20-minute charging stop mid-way (which you would take as a break anyway). If it's truly a one-off, some drivers rent a petrol car for that specific trip. Over 15 years of ownership, one long trip a year is not enough to justify choosing a worse daily car.

**"What if I can't find a charger?"**

That's a real concern — but it's a **charging infrastructure** problem, not an EV problem. If your petrol car ran out of fuel because there were no petrol stations, you'd blame the fuel network, not the car. The fix is more reliable public chargers on the routes people drive, not a different car.

**"Batteries degrade over time so range gets worse."**

By about 2% a year, on average. Over 10 years that's 20% off the starting range. A 300-mile EV becomes a 240-mile EV. Still way beyond the daily need for almost everyone.

## Also heard as

- "You can't take an EV on a road trip"
- "Range anxiety is a real thing"
- "EVs are city cars, not proper cars"

- "EVs are only good for the school run"

## Go deeper

**Range anxiety is largely charger anxiety.** Surveys of EV drivers keep finding that what people call "range anxiety" is really "charger anxiety" — worry about whether the charger will be there, working, and available when needed. The car itself is rarely the problem. Fixing charging reliability solves most of range anxiety.

**Not everyone actually needs 500 miles of range.** There's a tendency to design EVs for the very edge case (long road trip) rather than the daily reality (short commute). This makes cars heavier, more expensive and more mineral-intensive than they need to be. The [Smaller EVs Are Better](../sectors/transport/smaller-evs-better.md) argument applies here: 250 miles of range is enough for almost everyone, and building beyond that has costs.

**The record will keep moving.** Battery energy density has been improving by roughly 5-8% per year for a decade. Ranges will keep growing.
