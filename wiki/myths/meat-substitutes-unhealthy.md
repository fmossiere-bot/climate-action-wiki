# Myth: meat substitutes are ultra-processed junk food

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Technically they're ultra-processed, but the ultra-processed foods linked to disease are mainly processed meats and sugary drinks, not veggie burgers. Plant-based meats are usually lower in calories, saturated fat and salt, and higher in fibre, than the meat they replace.

## Why it sounds right

"Ultra-processed" has a bad reputation, and for good reason: eating a lot of it is linked to obesity, diabetes, cancer and heart disease. Plant-based meats do meet the technical definition. They have many ingredients and they're made in factories, so it's easy to lump them in with everything else. The catch is that "ultra-processed" covers very different foods, and the evidence points to the harm coming from particular groups, processed meats and sugary drinks, rather than from all processed food equally.

## What is actually true

Compared with processed meat, meat substitutes usually have fewer calories, less total fat and less saturated fat, and more fibre. Some match a beef burger's 20-25 g of protein per serving, while others come in lower, at around 15 g. Protein quality is worth knowing about: animal foods contain the full range of amino acids, while plant foods often need combining, which a varied plant diet does easily. Substitutes can fall short on calcium, zinc and vitamin B12 unless they're fortified, and many products now are.

A typical "ultra-processed" checklist has 8 markers:

| Criteria | Processed meat | Plant-based meat |
|---|---|---|
| Convenient | ✅ | ✅ |
| High in calories | ✅ | ❌ |
| High in sugar | ❌ | ❌ |
| High in saturated fat | ✅ | ❌ |
| High in salt | ✅ | ❌ |
| Many ingredients | ✅ | ✅ |
| Low in fibre | ✅ | ❌ |
| Couldn't be made in a normal kitchen | ✅ | ✅ |

Plant-based meat ticks **3 of 8** markers, and processed meat ticks 6 of 8. On the markers that matter for health, they aren't in the same group.

High overall consumption of ultra-processed food is linked to obesity, diabetes, cancer and heart disease. But when researchers look inside the category, the foods doing the damage are processed meats and sugary drinks. Other ultra-processed foods, including meat substitutes, show no link to higher disease risk, and some studies even find a lower risk. Eating more plant-based protein is linked to better health and a lower risk of dying early.

## If they push back

**"But they're still ultra-processed."**

Yes, technically. But the category is very mixed. A veggie burger and a can of Coke both meet the definition, yet they behave very differently in the body. The health harms come from calorie density, saturated fat, salt, low fibre and specific ingredients such as the nitrates in processed meat, and meat substitutes don't score badly on those.

**"Cows are natural, factories aren't."**

Being natural isn't a measure of nutrition. Much of what goes into a beef burger also passes through a factory: mincing, stabilisers, packaging. What matters is what's inside the food, not where it was put together.

**"What about processing damaging the food?"**

That's a fair concern. Some processing does affect nutrition, for example very high heat, very long processing or chemical solvents. Modern extrusion and mycoprotein processes keep most of the nutritional value, though not all. If it worries you, tofu, tempeh and plain beans and lentils are the reference point: cheaper and healthier than most substitutes, though less convenient.

## Go deeper

**Why the NOVA classification is debated.** Most claims about ultra-processed food rest on the NOVA food classification system, developed by the Brazilian researcher Carlos Monteiro's team. It sorts foods into four groups by how processed they are. Public health researchers find it useful but blunt, because it can put very different foods together: a homemade wholegrain bread and a Twinkie can end up in the same group depending on how it's applied. More recent research looks at the specific ways foods cause harm rather than treating "ultra-processed" as a single risk.

**The "convenience" tick isn't a health warning.** One standard marker of an ultra-processed food is that it's designed to be quick to prepare. That says nothing about health. Bagged salad is convenient and healthy. Bacon is convenient and isn't.

**Fortification matters more than most people think.** Many plant-based products have added B12, calcium, iron and zinc, nutrients that animal foods often contain and plants often lack. Fortification isn't cheating. Public health has relied on it for decades, with iodised salt and folic acid in bread. A fortified plant meat can match or beat the nutrition of the meat it replaces.

**The best diet is still the one you'll actually eat.** Even if substitutes are fine nutritionally, lentils, beans, tofu and tempeh are healthier and cheaper basics than any meat substitute. Substitutes work best as a bridge for people moving away from meat, rather than the end point. Both come well ahead of processed meat on nutrition and on climate.
