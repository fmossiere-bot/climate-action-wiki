# Myth: we'll just swap fossil fuel dependency for mineral dependency

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

There's a real concentration in mineral supply — China refines 80% of the world's rare earths, the DRC mines 70% of cobalt. But this is a different kind of risk than fossil fuel dependency. Fossil fuels are fuels: cut off the supply and the lights go out. Minerals are used to build things: cut off supply and you can't build **new** clean tech, but the panels and turbines you already have keep working. Nobody can turn off the sun.

## Why it sounds right

Because the concentration numbers really are stark. China dominates rare earth refining, cobalt production is heavily DRC-centred, lithium is concentrated in Australia and South America. If you frame this as "we'd depend on China for energy instead of Saudi Arabia," it sounds like trading one dependency for another. What that framing misses is that fossil dependency is on the running of the system, whereas mineral dependency is on the building of it.

## What is actually true

**Mining concentration:**
- **>70% of cobalt** is mined in the DRC
- **>60% of rare earths and graphite** are produced in China
- **>70% of platinum** comes from South Africa

**Refining concentration (even more concentrated):**
- China refines **>80% of world rare earths**
- China also dominates refining of cobalt, lithium and copper
- China dominates finished-product production too: **batteries and EVs**

**Fossil fuel dependency for comparison:**
- **86% of the world's population** live in countries that **import fossil fuels** (energy insecure)
- Only **14%** live in fossil fuel exporters
- When Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022, **up to 50,000 people across Europe died** from cold + energy poverty; high energy prices also pushed food prices up (via fertiliser costs)

**Renewables shift the picture dramatically:**
- If the world ran on renewables, **92% of the world's population** would live in countries with enough sun or wind to cover **at least 10× their own energy needs**
- Only **8%** would have marginal renewable resources

## The key distinction

**Fossil fuels are fuels** — cut supply, existing plants stop working.

**Minerals are for building** — cut supply, existing solar panels/turbines/EVs keep working. You just can't build **new** ones for a while.

So mineral dependency threatens the **speed of the energy transition**. Fossil fuel dependency threatens **whether the lights stay on right now**. These are very different kinds of risk. A country cut off from Chinese rare earths for 6 months keeps warm, keeps driving, and its EVs keep running. A country cut off from Russian gas for 6 months has people freezing to death.

## If they push back

**"So we'd just be dependent on China instead of Saudi Arabia."**

For the near-term build-out, partially yes. But once you have solar panels and turbines installed, nobody can cut off the sun or the wind. Fossil fuels require an unbroken supply chain forever. Minerals require a supply chain for the build-out phase only.

**"China could weaponise rare earths."**

They already tried, briefly, in the 2010 dispute with Japan. Prices spiked and the world responded by finding non-Chinese sources. Reserves and refining capacity outside China grew fast. Rare earth cartelisation is much harder than oil cartelisation because rare earths are geologically widespread — they just aren't refined widely.

**"But we still need to build a lot for decades."**

Yes. That's why the diversification push matters. **US, Australia, Norway, Sweden, Portugal** are all developing new mining and refining capacity. Norway recently discovered Europe's largest rare-earth deposit. The **US Inflation Reduction Act** is putting significant money into domestic mineral processing. AI-driven mineral discovery (KoBold Metals, HyperSpectral) is making new deposits easier to find. This is a solvable diversification problem, not a dependency trap.

## Go deeper

**Why "fuels vs building blocks" matters.** Consider the difference between running out of petrol (car doesn't run) and running out of steel (can't build new cars). If someone cuts off your steel supply, your existing cars keep working. If someone cuts off your petrol, they stop today. That is the same asymmetry that applies to fossil fuels vs. minerals. The strategic vulnerability of running an oil-based economy is genuinely different from that of running a mineral-based one.

**Diversification is genuinely underway.**
- **US**: keeps finding new lithium deposits; the Inflation Reduction Act channels large sums into domestic mineral processing
- **Australia**: keeps finding new rare-earth deposits
- **Norway**: recently discovered Europe's largest rare-earth source
- **Portugal**: developing lithium mining
- **Indonesia**: nickel processing capacity growing fast
- Ritchie's summary: "more of this, please"

**AI-driven mineral discovery.** Startups like **KoBold Metals** and **HyperSpectral** use satellite imaging, geochemistry and machine learning to identify deposits humans might have missed. This has already led to significant new mineral finds and is expected to accelerate. Caveat: AI itself uses a lot of energy, so its own footprint has to be part of the calculation.

**Refining, not mining, is the real chokehold.** Even when countries diversify mining, refining is often still done in China (~80% of rare earths, most cobalt). Refining capacity outside China is what's actually short. Building non-Chinese refining is politically and environmentally complicated (it's dirty, energy-intensive work) but it's happening — new plants are being built in Malaysia, Australia, France and the US.
