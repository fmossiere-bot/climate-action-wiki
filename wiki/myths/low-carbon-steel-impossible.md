# Myth: we'll never be able to make low-carbon steel

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

We already know how to make green steel — use hydrogen instead of coal, or electrify the process directly. Both are proven at pilot scale and the first commercial plants are being built now in Sweden, Spain and the US. The problem is scale and cost, not technology. Green steel adds about 25% to the price of steel, but that's about $150 on a car and $400 on a house.

## Why it sounds right

Because two-thirds of the world's steel is made using coal (technically "coke," made from coal). That's been true for over a century — anyone who works in steel knows the coal is central. If you frame the problem as "steel needs coal," decarbonising sounds impossible. What that framing misses is that coal isn't there because steel physically requires it. Coal is there because it strips oxygen out of iron ore. Hydrogen does the same thing without producing CO2.

## What is actually true

**Where steel emissions come from:**
- **7% of global greenhouse gas emissions**
- **~⅔ of the world's steel** is made using coke (from coal); the remaining **⅓** is recycled scrap in electric arc furnaces
- 1 tonne of new steel = ~**0.8 tonnes of coal** burned = most of the emissions

**Two proven routes to remove coal:**

**1. Hydrogen instead of coal.** Hydrogen strips oxygen from iron ore, just like coal does. The by-product is water (H2O), not CO2. Requires clean hydrogen.

**2. Direct electrification.** Use electricity instead of burning coal.

A third option — **carbon capture** at the current plant — works but is more expensive.

**The scale numbers:**
- 1 tonne of "green steel" needs **~4 MWh of electricity**
- Making the world's 1.3 billion tonnes of steel this way would need **5,200 TWh** — about **⅕ of world electricity generation today**
- Total investment needed globally: about **€1.3 trillion**, or **€50 billion/year over 25 years** (per H2 Green Steel figures)

**The cost premium is genuinely small on finished products:**
- Green steel: **~25% more** per tonne
- A $25,000 car: about **$150 more** (0.5-1%)
- A $250,000 house: about **$400 more** (0.2%)
- An offshore wind turbine: **0.5-2%** cost increase

**Who's building it:**
- **H2 Green Steel** — plant under construction in Sweden with government support
- **ArcelorMittal** — converting a Spanish plant to hydrogen with government support
- **Boston Metal** — direct electrification, backed by Microsoft; first plant expected 2026

**Most European steel companies** have committed to cutting emissions **30-50% or more by 2030**.

## If they push back

**"Green steel is a lab curiosity."**

Not any more. H2 Green Steel's Boden plant is under construction. Boston Metal's first plant is expected in 2026. ArcelorMittal has committed billions to hydrogen conversion. These are commercial-scale projects, not lab demonstrations.

**"It needs too much electricity."**

5,200 TWh globally is about a fifth of current world generation. That's not nothing — but a **25% rise in global electricity demand over 25 years** matches the trajectory of the grid buildout for other electrification (EVs, heat pumps). It's part of the transition, not blocking it.

**"25% more expensive means nobody buys it."**

The finished-product impact is tiny. A car maker paying 25% more for its steel raises car prices by less than 1%. Buyers won't notice. What matters is car makers demanding green steel — because their own Scope 3 emissions targets require it.

## Go deeper

**Where the coal actually goes.** In a traditional blast furnace, iron ore is mixed with coke (baked coal). At high temperature, the carbon in the coke strips oxygen out of the iron ore, producing iron plus CO2. Coal is not fuel here — it's a chemical reducing agent. Replacing it means finding another agent that removes oxygen. Hydrogen does exactly this: **iron ore + hydrogen → iron + water vapour**. No CO2 emitted from the chemistry itself.

**Why scrap recycling isn't enough.** About ⅓ of world steel is already made from scrap in electric arc furnaces — low emissions if the electricity is clean. Scrap recycling should be maximised, but there isn't enough scrap in the world to make all steel this way. New steel from iron ore is still needed for growth, especially in the developing world. Green steel is how you decarbonise that new-steel share.

**Location will matter.** Green steel plants need cheap clean electricity. Existing steel plants are often located near coal mines or in high-electricity-cost countries. The next generation of green steel plants is being sited in places with cheap renewables (Sweden's hydro/wind, Spain's solar, later maybe Morocco or Australia). The **industrial geography of steel is about to change** — and it changes as clean electricity availability changes, not as steel demand changes.

**Buyer-side pull is emerging fast.** Car makers, wind turbine manufacturers, and construction firms all have Scope 3 emissions targets. Their supply chains include a lot of steel. **Volvo, Mercedes and BMW** have all committed to source significant volumes of green steel. That buyer pull is what makes the industry economics work — plants like H2 Green Steel have signed 5-year offtake contracts before they've even opened.
