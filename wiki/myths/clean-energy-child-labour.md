# Myth: clean energy relies on child labour and exploitation

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

The abuse in cobalt mines in Congo is real and urgent, but the same cobalt goes into your phone and laptop, and clean energy is one of the few industries actually moving away from it. It's a problem to fix in the supply chain, not a reason to keep burning coal.

## Why it sounds right

The reporting on cobalt mining in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) is horrifying, and it is accurate. Children do work in small informal mines. People do die in mine collapses, and toxic exposure damages workers' health. Anyone who reads about it is right to be angry, and right to wonder whether the EV on their driveway is part of it. Where the argument slips is in applying that concern only to clean tech, while ignoring the same cobalt in the phone in your pocket and the cobalt once used in petrol car catalytic converters.

## What is actually true

Cobalt has been called the "blood diamond of batteries".
- **74% of world cobalt** is mined in the DRC.
- Informal mining there is often brutally exploitative: hundreds of thousands of workers, including children, are paid a few dollars a day.
- Mine collapses, falling rocks, chemical exposure and eye damage are common.
- EVs are now the biggest single use of cobalt, at 45%. Electronics such as phones and laptops use 26%.

The DRC itself is very poor:
- More than 60% of people live on less than $2 a day.
- 80% don't have electricity or safe drinking water.
- Mining is 90% of the country's exports and a vital income for millions.

Other minerals carry similar risks:
- About 50% of world mineral reserves are in countries with "weak" or "poor" governance scores.
- That includes 94% of rare earths, 70% of cobalt, 59% of nickel, 41% of copper and 34% of lithium.

There are signs of change:
- BloombergNEF's cobalt forecasts keep falling. In 2019 it predicted over 300,000 tonnes of cobalt would be needed by 2030. Three years later, that forecast was under 150,000 tonnes.
- Tesla now puts LFP batteries, which contain no cobalt, in more than half its new cars.
- BYD is investing in sodium-ion batteries, which use neither cobalt nor lithium.
- The EU Critical Raw Materials Act (2023) requires regular supply chain audits and caps how much of a mineral can come from a single country.

## If they push back

**"So EVs are built on child labour."**

Some of them, partly, yes. So is the phone in your pocket, and so is fossil fuel refining (cobalt is used as a catalyst in petrol engines). This is a problem across global supply chains, and clean energy is one of the sectors moving away from cobalt fastest.

**"We should boycott these countries."**

It sounds simple, but it would backfire. Mining is a huge share of DRC income and one of the main ways people make a living there, so cutting the country out would push millions further into poverty without ending the exploitation. What helps is reforming supply chains: better wages, safer conditions and getting children into school.

**"Switching to LFP batteries fixes everything."**

It removes cobalt from that battery, which is a real win. LFP still uses lithium, though, which has its own mining and water concerns. Sodium-ion batteries use neither cobalt nor lithium, and each generation of the technology has fewer contested materials, so things are improving.

**"Rich countries just import the problem."**

That's also true. The rich countries buying phones and EVs know what is happening in the DRC and don't do enough about it. Mining countries and importing countries share the responsibility, and conditions will only improve with pressure from both ends of the chain.

## Go deeper

**Cobalt is not unique to clean energy.** The same DRC mines supply cobalt for smartphones, laptops, digital cameras and, until recently, petrol car catalytic converters. Nobody campaigned about "your iPhone runs on child labour" the way people now campaign about "your Tesla runs on child labour." That inconsistency is worth naming. It suggests the concern is sometimes used as an argument against one particular technology rather than against the exploitation itself.

**Battery chemistry is moving away from cobalt fast.** LFP (lithium iron phosphate) batteries use no cobalt, and BYD, CATL and Tesla have all shifted large shares of production to them. Sodium-ion batteries, with no cobalt and no lithium, are being made at scale in China. Solid-state batteries are being developed with different chemistries again. The cobalt problem is being engineered out of the technology.

**Tracing supply chains is now a legal duty in the EU.** The **EU Critical Raw Materials Act (2023)** requires companies importing critical minerals to audit their supply chains regularly. Companies that can't show ethical sourcing face restrictions. The US and Japan are adopting similar rules. It is the first time major economies have put real force behind ethical mineral requirements.

**Boycotting the DRC would make things worse for the workers.** Mining is 90% of DRC exports and directly supports a large share of the population. Cutting the country out of global supply chains would collapse incomes and push more workers into worse, often illegal, informal mining. The pressure belongs on mine operators and mineral buyers to improve pay and safety and end child labour, rather than on the country to stop mining.
