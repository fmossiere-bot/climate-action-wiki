# Myth: clean energy relies on child labour and exploitation

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Exploitation in mineral supply chains is real — cobalt from the Democratic Republic of Congo especially, where children work in unsafe informal mines. It's an urgent problem. But it isn't unique to clean energy. Your phone, your laptop and even parts of the fossil fuel supply chain use the same cobalt from the same mines. And clean energy is one of the few industries actually moving away from cobalt — Tesla, BYD, Chinese battery makers. This is a problem to fix, not a reason to keep burning coal.

## Why it sounds right

Because the reporting on cobalt mining in the DRC is horrifying and accurate. Children do work in artisanal mines. People do die in mine collapses. Toxic exposure damages workers' health. Anyone who reads about it is right to be angry — and right to worry about whether an EV in their driveway is complicit. The mistake is applying that concern only to clean tech while ignoring the same cobalt in the phone in your pocket and the historical cobalt used in petrol car catalytic converters.

## What is actually true

**Cobalt — the "blood diamond of batteries":**
- **74% of world cobalt is mined in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)**
- Informal DRC mining is often brutally exploitative — hundreds of thousands of workers, including children, paid a few dollars a day
- Mine collapses, falling rocks, chemical exposure and eye damage are common
- **EVs are now the biggest single use of cobalt: 45%.** **Electronics (phones, laptops) use 26%.**

**Context on the DRC itself:**
- **>60%** of people live on less than **$2 a day**
- **80%** don't have electricity or safe drinking water
- **Mining is 90%** of the country's exports and a vital income source for millions

**Other minerals with governance risks:**
- **~50% of world mineral reserves** are in countries with "weak" or "poor" governance scores
- **94% of rare earths, 70% of cobalt, 59% of nickel, 41% of copper, 34% of lithium** are in these countries

**Signs of change:**
- **BloombergNEF cobalt forecasts keep falling.** In 2019 it predicted over **300,000 tonnes** of cobalt needed by 2030. Three years later, that forecast was **under 150,000 tonnes**.
- **Tesla now puts LFP (no cobalt) batteries in more than half its new cars**
- **BYD** is investing in **sodium-ion batteries** — no cobalt, no lithium
- **EU Critical Raw Materials Act (2023)** forces regular supply chain audits and caps how much of a mineral can come from a single country

## If they push back

**"So EVs are built on child labour."**

Some of them, partly, yes. So is the phone in your pocket. So is fossil fuel refining (cobalt is used as a catalyst in petrol engines). This isn't an EV problem — it's a global supply chain problem, and clean energy is one of the sectors moving away from cobalt fastest.

**"We should boycott these countries."**

Simple, wrong answer. Mining is a huge share of DRC income and one of the main livelihoods there. Cutting the country out entirely would push millions further into poverty without fixing the exploitation. The right answer is **reforming supply chains** — better wages, safer conditions, getting children into school. That's harder than boycotting, but actually helps people.

**"Switching to LFP batteries fixes everything."**

It removes cobalt from that specific battery — a real win. But LFP still uses lithium, which comes with its own mining and water concerns. Sodium-ion batteries use neither cobalt nor lithium. Each generation of the technology has fewer contested inputs. The trajectory is genuinely improving.

**"Rich countries just import the problem."**

Also true. The rich countries buying phones and EVs know what's happening in DRC and don't do enough to change it. Both mining countries and importing countries share responsibility. Improving conditions requires pressure from both ends of the chain.

## Go deeper

**Cobalt is not unique to clean energy.** The exact same DRC mines supply cobalt for smartphones, laptops, digital cameras and until recently even for petrol car catalytic converters. Nobody was campaigning about "your iPhone runs on child labour" the way people now campaign about "your Tesla runs on child labour." That inconsistency is worth naming — it suggests the concern isn't really about exploitation, it's sometimes used as an argument against a specific technology.

**Battery chemistry is moving away from cobalt fast.** LFP (lithium iron phosphate) batteries use no cobalt. BYD, CATL and Tesla have all shifted large shares of production to LFP. Sodium-ion batteries are being commercialised at scale in China — no cobalt, no lithium. Solid-state batteries are being developed with different chemistries again. The cobalt problem is being engineered out of the technology, not left to fester.

**Supply chain traceability is now legally required in the EU.** The **EU Critical Raw Materials Act (2023)** forces companies importing critical minerals to audit their supply chains regularly. Companies that can't demonstrate ethical sourcing face restrictions. Similar rules are being adopted in the US and Japan. This is the first time major economies have put real teeth behind ethical mineral requirements.

**Boycotting the DRC would make things worse for the workers.** Mining is 90% of DRC exports and directly supports a large share of the population. Cutting the country out of global supply chains would collapse incomes and push more workers into worse (often illegal) informal mining. The right lever is pressure on the mine operators and mineral buyers to improve conditions — pay, safety, no child labour — not on the country to stop mining altogether.
