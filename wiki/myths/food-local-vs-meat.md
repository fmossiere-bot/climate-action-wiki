# Myth: Buying local matters more than cutting down on meat

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

What you eat matters far more than how far it travelled. For beef, transport is under 1% of the footprint.

## Why it sounds right

Buying local is good for plenty of reasons: it supports local businesses, the food is fresher, you eat with the seasons, and you know how it was produced. Food miles are also easy to picture. The carbon just isn't mostly in the journey.

## What is actually true

Transport is a small share of food emissions overall, and almost nothing for the foods with the biggest footprint. For beef, transport is **well under 1%** of the total. Nearly all of it comes from land use and from the methane the animals produce. WRI's analysis finds that, per gram of protein, beef uses around 20 times the land of beans and produces around 20 times the emissions. The 2025 EAT-Lancet report found that a "planetary health" diet would cut agricultural emissions other than CO2 by 15 to 34%, free up an area of land the size of India, and prevent around 15 million deaths a year. Modelling by LSHTM and Cornell, published in Nature, found a healthier global diet could cut land use emissions by 85% by 2050.

## If they push back

**"Irish grass fed beef is different"**

It does better than feedlot beef on some measures, but its footprint is still many times that of plants. Eating less beef, and better beef, is the fair answer.

## Also heard as

- "food miles are the problem"
- "local beef is fine"
- "it's the transport that does the damage"
