# Myth: heat pumps are too expensive

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Over their lifetime heat pumps usually cost less than a gas boiler, but the upfront price is a real barrier: in the UK it's still about £2,000 more even after the £7,500 grant. That's a problem we can fix with bigger grants and fairer electricity prices, not a problem with the technology.

## Why it sounds right

The upfront bill really is bigger. In the UK an air-source heat pump costs around £12,000 to install, against £2,750 for a gas boiler. Households don't think in "lifetime cost", they think in "what does the installer's quote say." And even with the grant applied, that quote comes to about £4,500, well above the boiler. That is a real barrier, especially for households on lower incomes, and pretending otherwise doesn't help anyone.

## What is actually true

The upfront gap is real, running costs depend on the country, and over a lifetime heat pumps usually come out ahead.

Upfront cost in the UK:

- Heat pump installation: about **£12,000** (air-source)
- Gas boiler: about £2,750 (£2,500-£3,000)
- Gap before grants: ~£9,000
- UK government grant: £7,500
- Gap after the grant: ~£2,000 more than a gas boiler

That gap falls hardest on people with the least. A Nesta survey found heat pumps are rare in the poorest parts of the UK and common among wealthier households. Even at £2,000 above a boiler, they are out of reach for many.

Running cost:

- UK electricity costs **~4× more than gas** per unit
- A heat pump delivers ~3 units of heat per unit of electricity, so it breaks even at roughly a COP of 3 (the COP, or coefficient of performance, is the units of heat you get per unit of electricity)
- The average UK heat pump COP is 2.9, right on the break-even line

So in the UK today some households save and some pay more, especially in cold snaps when the COP drops. Elsewhere the picture is better:

- Average households in the US, Europe, South Korea and Japan saved money on heating by switching in 2021-22
- Savings in continental Europe grew after gas prices spiked following Russia's invasion of Ukraine

Lifetime cost:

- Counting installation and running costs over the whole lifespan, heat pumps usually work out **cheaper than a gas boiler**, even in countries where governments pay large subsidies
- They typically save a few hundred dollars a year on running costs, which pays back the extra upfront cost over time
- They are the cheapest way to take the carbon out of heating, cheaper than direct electric heaters or hydrogen boilers

## If they push back

**"So they're cheaper only if you can afford the upfront."**

Yes, and that is the real problem hidden inside the wider claim. The direct fix is **bigger grants for lower-income households**. The current UK grant helps middle-income households but isn't enough for those on low incomes.

**"Electricity is 4× the cost of gas in the UK. There's no way it's cheaper to run."**

The heat pump's efficiency closes most of that gap. At COP 3.0 it uses ⅓ of the energy a boiler does, so 4× the price × ⅓ of the energy ≈ 1.3× the cost, which is close to break-even. Heat pump tariffs from Octopus and OVO can bring running costs below gas: an efficient heat pump on one of these tariffs can halve a household's heating bill.

**"Energy taxes make electricity more expensive than gas."**

Yes, that's another real part of the problem. In many countries energy taxes sit on electricity rather than gas, mostly because gas was assumed to be what homes used. Moving those taxes off electricity, or onto gas, would tip the sums further towards heat pumps.

## Go deeper

**Why running costs differ between the UK and Europe.** UK electricity is expensive partly because the costs of renewables policy are paid through electricity bills rather than general taxes. In continental Europe, especially France (nuclear) and the Nordic countries (hydro), electricity is priced closer to gas. That's why the sums look much better today for a household in Munich or Stockholm than for one in Manchester.

**Heat pump tariffs are an overlooked lever.** Octopus's Cosy tariff, OVO's Heat Pump Plus and similar products offer cheaper electricity in the hours when a heat pump is most likely to be running. Households on these tariffs often pay less than they did with gas, even with a modest COP. Rolling them out more widely would make a big difference.

**Cheaper over a lifetime doesn't help if you can't pay upfront.** Someone who can't cover the £2,000 gap will never see the lifetime savings. That is why the answer is bigger grants aimed at those who need them, rather than waiting for prices to fall.

**Why heat pumps are still the cheapest route.** Hydrogen boilers would need hydrogen at fossil fuel prices, which isn't going to happen. Direct electric heating is 100% efficient, while heat pumps are 300%+, so they use a third of the electricity. Subsidising heat pumps does cost governments money, but the alternatives cost more.
