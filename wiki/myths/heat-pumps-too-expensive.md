# Myth: heat pumps are too expensive

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Running costs are usually lower than a gas boiler, and lifetime cost usually wins too. But the sticker price is genuinely a barrier. In the UK an air-source heat pump is around £12,000 to install versus £2,750 for a gas boiler. Even with the £7,500 government grant, that's still £2,000 more. The problem isn't the technology, it's affordability upfront — which is fixable with bigger grants and fairer electricity pricing.

## Why it sounds right

Because the upfront cheque really is bigger. Households don't think in "lifetime cost" — they think in "what does the installer's quote say." And in the UK, the quote genuinely is £4,500 higher than the boiler alternative even with the grant applied. That's a real barrier, especially for lower-income households. Pretending otherwise doesn't help anyone.

## What is actually true

**Upfront cost (UK):**
- Heat pump install: about **£12,000** (air-source)
- Gas boiler: about **£2,750** (£2,500-£3,000)
- Gap: **~£9,000** before grants
- **UK government grant**: **£7,500**
- **Net gap after grant**: ~**£2,000** more than a gas boiler

**The distributional problem is real:** Nesta survey — heat pumps are rare in the poorest UK areas, common among wealthier households. Even at £2,000 above a boiler, they are out of reach for many.

**Running cost:**
- **UK electricity costs ~4× more than gas** per unit
- But heat pumps deliver ~3 units of heat per unit of electricity, so break-even is roughly a COP of 3
- **Average UK heat pump COP: 2.9** — right at the break-even edge

Result in the UK today: some households save, some pay more (especially in cold snaps when COP drops). This is not the case globally:

- **Average US, European, South Korean and Japanese households saved money on heating by switching in 2021-22**
- Continental European savings widened after the Russia-Ukraine gas price spike

**Lifetime cost:**
- Including install + running over the whole lifespan, heat pumps usually work out **cheaper than a gas boiler** — even in countries where governments pay large subsidies
- Heat pumps typically save a few hundred dollars a year on running costs, which recoups the extra upfront over time
- Heat pumps are the **cheapest route to decarbonise heating**, cheaper than direct electric heaters or hydrogen boilers

## If they push back

**"So they're cheaper only if you can afford the upfront."**

Correct, and this is the specific problem inside the wider claim. **Bigger grants for lower-income households** is the direct policy fix. The current UK grant helps middle-income households but is not enough for low-income ones.

**"Electricity is 4× the cost of gas in the UK. There's no way it's cheaper to run."**

The efficiency ratio makes up most of that gap. A heat pump at COP 3.0 uses ⅓ the energy of a boiler. 4× cost × ⅓ energy ≈ 1.3× cost — close to break-even. Modern heat pump tariffs from Octopus and OVO can bring the running cost below gas — combining an efficient heat pump with one of these tariffs can **halve** a household's heating bill.

**"Energy taxes make electricity more expensive than gas."**

Yes, and this is another honest bit of the problem. In many countries, energy taxes are loaded onto electricity rather than gas — historically because gas was assumed to be the household default. Shifting those taxes off electricity, or onto gas instead, would tilt the economics further in favour of heat pumps.

## Go deeper

**Why the running-cost picture is different in the UK vs Europe.** UK electricity is priced high partly because renewables policy costs are paid through the electricity bill rather than through general taxation. Continental European electricity, especially in France (nuclear) and Nordics (hydro), is priced closer to gas parity. That's why heat pump economics look much stronger for a household in Munich or Stockholm than one in Manchester today.

**Heat pump-specific tariffs are the underrated lever.** Octopus's Cosy tariff, OVO's Heat Pump Plus and similar products offer cheaper electricity in specific hours designed around when a heat pump is likely to be running. Households on one of these tariffs frequently pay less than they did with gas, even with a modest COP. Wider rollout of such tariffs is one of the highest-impact interventions available.

**Lifetime affordability is genuinely better, but that doesn't help low-income households right now.** Somebody who cannot cover the upfront £2,000 gap will never see the lifetime savings. That is exactly why the policy solution is bigger targeted grants — not "wait for prices to fall."

**Why heat pumps are still the cheapest decarbonisation path.** Hydrogen boilers would need hydrogen at fossil-fuel prices, which isn't going to happen. Direct electric heating is 100% efficient but heat pumps are 300%+, so they use a third of the electricity. The government cost of subsidising heat pump adoption is real, but the alternatives are more expensive still.
