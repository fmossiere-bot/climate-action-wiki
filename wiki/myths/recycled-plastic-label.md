# Myth: It says recycled on the packaging, so plastic recycling is working

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Recycled on the label often means the company bought some recycled material somewhere in the system, not that it is in the packet. The rules allow it.

## Why it sounds right

The label is legally accurate, which is exactly what makes this one hard. Nobody is lying outright.

## What is actually true

The trick is an accounting method called mass balance. A manufacturer averages a small amount of recycled feedstock across an entire production system and then labels individual products as recycled, even when the item in your hand is around 95% virgin naphtha. A 2026 Guardian and IJ4EU investigation found products from Kraft Heinz and Mondelez carrying recycled claims on this basis. The EU's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation legalises the approach in 2026 rather than closing it.

## If they push back

**"So should I stop recycling?"**

No, keep recycling. Just stop treating recycled labels as proof, and push for reuse and less packaging, which is where the real gain is.

## Also heard as

- "this bottle is made from recycled plastic"
- "the brands have sorted this out"
- "chemical recycling solved it"
