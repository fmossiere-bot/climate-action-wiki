# Myth: It says recycled on the packaging, so plastic recycling is working

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

"Recycled" on the label often means the company bought some recycled material somewhere in its system, not that it's in the packet in your hand. And the rules allow it.

## Why it sounds right

The label is legally accurate, which is what makes this one hard to spot. Nobody is lying outright, and it's reasonable to trust what the packet says.

## What is actually true

It works through an accounting method called **mass balance**. A manufacturer spreads a small amount of recycled feedstock across a whole production system on paper, then labels individual products as recycled, even when the item in your hand is around 95% virgin naphtha (new plastic made from oil). A 2026 Guardian and IJ4EU investigation found products from Kraft Heinz and Mondelez carrying recycled claims on this basis. The EU's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation makes the approach legal in 2026 rather than closing the loophole.

## If they push back

**"So should I stop recycling?"**

No, keep recycling. Just don't treat a recycled label as proof, and push for reuse and less packaging, which is where the bigger gains are.

## Also heard as

- "this bottle is made from recycled plastic"
- "the brands have sorted this out"
- "chemical recycling solved it"
