# Myth: wind farms kill huge numbers of birds

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Wind turbines do kill some birds and bats. But per year in the US, cats kill 2.4 billion birds, buildings 600 million, cars 200 million, and wind turbines about 1.2 million. Wind is one of the smallest killers, not one of the biggest. And there are proven fixes — painting one blade black, or turning turbines off in very low wind — that cut deaths dramatically at almost no cost.

## Why it sounds right

Because it's easy to imagine, and there's a real, specific problem hiding inside it. Turbines really do kill birds. Videos of eagles hitting blades are horrifying. Certain species (raptors, migratory birds) really are at higher risk. And the RSPB — a charity dedicated to protecting birds — has publicly worried about specific wind farms. So the concern isn't invented. The question is how big it is compared to everything else killing birds.

## What is actually true

**Birds killed per year in the US, by cause:**

| Cause | Birds killed per year |
|---|---|
| Cats | 2.4 billion |
| Buildings (windows) | 600 million |
| Cars | 200 million |
| Pesticides | 67 million |
| Power lines | 28 million |
| Phone/comms towers | 6.6 million |
| Wind turbines | 1.2 million |

Wind turbines kill **~2,000× fewer birds than cats**, and far fewer than cars, buildings or pesticides.

Per-turbine figures:
- Each turbine kills **4-18 birds a year** on average
- **6-20 bats a year** — bats are actually more at risk than birds, as a share of population

Some birds are much more affected than others:
- **Birds of prey** (eagles, hawks) use hilltops to catch wind — exactly where wind farms want to be built
- **Migratory birds** on flyways are at higher risk if turbines sit on their route
- Killing rare or endangered birds is worse than killing common ones — a numbers-only comparison misses that.

## If they push back

**"Trump was right when he said windmills kill all the birds."**

He said "all the birds," which is comically wrong. Turbines kill about 0.05% of what cats kill each year in the US. The RSPB, a charity dedicated to protecting birds, has built its own turbine to power its headquarters. That is not the behaviour of an organisation that thinks turbines are catastrophic for birds.

**"But it's the wrong birds — eagles and raptors."**

Right, and that specific risk is real. Which is why targeted mitigation matters. Fixes that reduce raptor and bat deaths without cutting energy output much:
- **Turn turbines off in low wind** (bats can't fly well in higher wind, so most bat strikes happen below 5-6 m/s). Pennsylvania study: 44% bat-death reduction at 5 m/s cutoff, **93% reduction** at 6.5 m/s. Cost to energy production: tiny.
- **Paint one blade black.** A Norwegian test cut bird deaths by **>70%**, and no white-tailed eagles were killed after painting.
- **Play high-pitched sounds** to warn off bats. Texas trial cut deaths of two bat species by 54% and 78%.
- **GPS tracking of eagles** near turbines, with automatic shutdown when they approach.
- **Australian wind farm cut bird deaths 54%** while losing only **0.1%** of income.
- **Cadiz, Spain**: bird deaths halved while losing **0.07%** of energy output.

**"Climate change harms birds too."**

Massively. Fossil fuels harm birds through climate change, air pollution, oil spills, coal mining runoff, acid rain. If you care about bird populations, the fossil fuel system is doing far more harm than the wind farms replacing it.

## Go deeper

**The big absolute number matters — but so does the relative one.** 1.2 million bird deaths per year in the US from turbines is a lot in absolute terms. It matters. Individual bird deaths — especially of rare species — matter. But it is a **fraction of a percent** of the toll from cats, buildings and cars, most of which we accept without protest. Applying "we must eliminate all bird deaths" only to wind farms is inconsistent.

**Bats are the underrated concern.** Bats are hit disproportionately hard by wind turbines relative to their population sizes. Some bat species are at genuine risk from unmitigated wind farms. The good news: low-wind cutoffs and acoustic deterrents dramatically reduce bat deaths at very low cost to energy production. This should be standard practice, not optional.

**Painting blades black.** Almost every serious mitigation proposal for wind-farm bird deaths starts here. Spinning white blades blur into a moving grey that birds can't see. Painting one blade in high contrast breaks the visual pattern. A Norwegian test on Smøla wind farm cut bird deaths by more than 70%. The paint costs very little. This should be a standard requirement in high-risk locations.

**Where fossil fuels really do worse.** Coal mining destroys bird habitat directly. Oil spills kill millions of birds directly, quickly, and photogenically — but they get quickly forgotten. Climate change is projected to push large numbers of bird species toward extinction. The comparison "wind turbines vs no energy" is silly. The real comparison is "wind turbines vs fossil fuels," and fossil fuels lose it by a wide margin.
