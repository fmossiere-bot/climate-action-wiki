# Myth: wind farms kill huge numbers of birds

> [!WARNING] Verdict: Mostly false

## Say this

Wind turbines do kill some birds, about 1.2 million a year in the US, but cats kill 2.4 billion, buildings 600 million and cars 200 million. And simple fixes, like painting one blade black or stopping turbines in very low wind, cut those deaths sharply for almost no cost.

## Why it sounds right

It's easy to picture, and there's a real problem inside it. Turbines do kill birds, and videos of eagles hitting blades are horrible to watch. Some species, such as birds of prey and migrating birds, really are at higher risk. The RSPB, a charity set up to protect birds, has raised concerns about specific wind farms. So the worry isn't made up. The question is how big it is compared with everything else that kills birds.

## What is actually true

Birds killed each year in the US, by cause:

| Cause | Birds killed per year |
|---|---|
| Cats | 2.4 billion |
| Buildings (windows) | 600 million |
| Cars | 200 million |
| Pesticides | 67 million |
| Power lines | 28 million |
| Phone/comms towers | 6.6 million |
| Wind turbines | 1.2 million |

Wind turbines kill about **2,000 times fewer birds than cats**, and far fewer than cars, buildings or pesticides.

Per turbine, on average:

- Each turbine kills 4-18 birds a year.
- Each turbine kills 6-20 bats a year. As a share of their population, bats are more at risk than birds.

Some birds are much more affected than others:

- Birds of prey (eagles, hawks) use hilltops to catch the wind, which is exactly where wind farms tend to be built.
- Migrating birds are at higher risk when turbines sit on their route.
- Losing rare or endangered birds matters more than losing common ones, and a comparison based only on numbers misses that.

## If they push back

**"Trump was right when he said windmills kill all the birds."**

"All the birds" is plainly wrong. Each year in the US, turbines kill about 0.05% of the number of birds cats kill. The RSPB, a charity set up to protect birds, has built its own turbine to power its headquarters. That's not what an organisation does if it thinks turbines are a disaster for birds.

**"But it's the wrong birds, eagles and raptors."**

That's right, and that risk is real, which is why targeted fixes matter. These cut deaths of birds of prey and bats without losing much energy:

- **Stop turbines in low wind.** Bats struggle to fly in stronger wind, so most bat strikes happen below 5-6 m/s. A Pennsylvania study found bat deaths fell 44% with a cut-off at 5 m/s, and 93% with a cut-off at 6.5 m/s. The loss of energy was tiny.
- **Paint one blade black.** A Norwegian test cut bird deaths by more than 70%, and no white-tailed eagles were killed after the blades were painted.
- **Play high-pitched sounds** to warn bats away. A trial in Texas cut deaths of two bat species by 54% and 78%.
- **Track eagles by GPS** near turbines, with turbines shutting down automatically when an eagle comes close.
- **An Australian wind farm** cut bird deaths by 54% while losing only 0.1% of its income.
- **In Cadiz, Spain,** bird deaths halved for a loss of only 0.07% of energy output.

**"Climate change harms birds too."**

Very much so. Fossil fuels harm birds through climate change, air pollution, oil spills, run-off from coal mines and acid rain. If you care about bird numbers, the fossil fuel system does far more harm than the wind farms replacing it.

## Go deeper

**The total matters, and so does the comparison.** 1.2 million birds killed by turbines each year in the US is a lot, and every death matters, especially of rare species. But it is a fraction of a percent of the toll from cats, buildings and cars, most of which we accept without complaint. Demanding zero bird deaths from wind farms alone, and from nothing else, is inconsistent.

**Bats are the overlooked concern.** For the size of their populations, bats are hit much harder by wind turbines than birds are, and some bat species are at real risk from wind farms without safeguards. The good news is that low-wind cut-offs and sound deterrents greatly reduce bat deaths at very little cost to energy output. They should be standard practice, not optional.

**Painting blades black.** Almost every serious proposal to reduce bird deaths at wind farms starts here. Spinning white blades blur into a moving grey that birds can't see, and painting one blade in a contrasting colour breaks up that pattern. A Norwegian test at the Smøla wind farm cut bird deaths by more than 70%, and the paint costs very little. It should be required in high-risk locations.

**Where fossil fuels do worse.** Coal mining destroys bird habitat directly. Oil spills kill millions of birds quickly and very visibly, yet they are soon forgotten. Climate change is expected to push large numbers of bird species towards extinction. Comparing wind turbines with having no energy at all makes little sense. The real comparison is wind turbines against fossil fuels, and fossil fuels lose it by a wide margin.
