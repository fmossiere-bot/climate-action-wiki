# Myth: heat pumps don't work in cold climates

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

They lose a little efficiency, but they still work. The best proof is that Norway, Sweden and Finland — three of the coldest countries in Europe — have the highest heat pump adoption per household. Alaska is full of them too. If they didn't work in the cold, that wouldn't happen. Real efficiency in a UK winter is around 2.7, still much better than the 0.7 to 0.8 a gas boiler manages.

## Why it sounds right

Because it's technically true that heat pumps do lose some efficiency below zero. Every heat pump manufacturer publishes performance curves that show this. Anyone who reads the spec sheet and stops there concludes "yeah, they must struggle." The mistake is treating a small efficiency drop as "doesn't work" rather than "still much better than the alternative."

## What is actually true

Heat pump efficiency is measured as **COP (Coefficient of Performance)** — how many units of heat you get out per unit of electricity in. A COP of 3 means 1 kWh of electricity produces 3 kWh of heat.

- **Between 5°C and –10°C**, heat pumps average a COP of about **2.7** — solidly efficient
- Field study of **550 heat pumps** across Switzerland, Germany, UK, Canada and US: COP ranged from **3.3 in Canada** to **2.5 in China**
- Compare to alternatives:
  - **Gas boiler**: 0.7–0.8 (70–80% efficient — you lose 20–30% up the flue)
  - **Electric radiator**: ~1.0
  - **Heat pump in a UK winter**: ~2.7 — beats both

**Real-world users agree.** Nesta surveyed 2,500 UK heat pump users:
- **3 out of 4 people** said they were happier, or just as happy, with their heat pump compared to their old heating system
- Satisfaction was similar across old Victorian houses and modern homes — so the "doesn't work in old leaky homes" version of the myth also doesn't hold up

The countries that have adopted them most:
- **Norway, Sweden, Finland**: highest heat pump penetration per household in Europe
- **Alaska**: common even with winters below –20°C

## If they push back

**"But they use loads more electricity when it's cold."**

Yes, and that's the honest bit. As the outside temperature drops, COP drops. A UK cold-snap winter day might have a heat pump running closer to COP 2.0 or slightly below. It still beats a gas boiler on energy, though not necessarily on cost (see the [cost myth](heat-pumps-too-expensive.md)).

**"My old Victorian house won't work with one."**

The Nesta survey found similar satisfaction rates in old and new homes. Good insulation helps everything, but heat pumps do not require perfect insulation. If your radiators are appropriately sized and the installer is competent, an old house works fine.

**"But badly installed ones don't work well."**

This is the real bottleneck. A poorly installed heat pump underperforms badly. Installing one is a skilled job combining engineering, plumbing, pipe-fitting and refrigeration. The UK has roughly **3,000 trained heat pump engineers** and needs closer to **30,000 by 2030**. Training is the choke point, not the technology.

## Go deeper

**Why the cold-weather myth persists.** A lot of the anecdotal complaints trace back to **badly installed heat pumps** — undersized radiators, poor commissioning, wrong controls settings. In the Nesta survey, the small minority of unhappy users typically had installation problems, not technology problems. So "they don't work in the cold" often means "the one my neighbour got installed wasn't set up right." That is a training problem, not a physics problem.

**Insulation isn't a prerequisite.** There's a common view that you can't get a heat pump until you've done a full retrofit. That's wrong. Insulation helps every heating system and is worth doing eventually, but people should not have to wait years to install a heat pump. The Nesta data supports this.

**What actually matters:**
1. **Insulate homes over time** — it helps regardless of heating source
2. **Train enough installers** — the UK needs a 5–10× increase in the next five years
3. **Standard, reliable installation practices** — quality inconsistency is the main real complaint
