# Myth: The battery dies after a few years and costs a fortune to replace

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Out of 15,000 EVs studied, 1.5% had needed a new battery. Most cars past 100,000 miles still had 90% of their range.

## Why it sounds right

Everyone has had a phone whose battery died within two years, so it is a completely reasonable thing to assume. EV packs are managed and cooled quite differently.

## What is actually true

An analysis of 15,000 EVs by Recurrent Motors found only 1.5% had needed a battery replacement, and 90% of cars past 100,000 miles still held at least 90% of their original range. Most manufacturers warrant the pack for eight years as a minimum. Nissan UK reported that almost all of the batteries it has ever made are still in cars, after twelve years of selling them.

## If they push back

**"And then it goes to landfill"**

It does not. Packs with half their capacity left are being repurposed into grid storage, and recyclers recover over 90% of the lithium.

## Also heard as

- "battery lifetime is horribly uncertain"
- "you'll need a new battery at 8 years"
- "the replacement costs more than the car is worth"
