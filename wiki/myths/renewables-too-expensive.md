# Myth: renewable energy is too expensive

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

That was true 15 years ago. It stopped being true around 2015. Today solar and wind are cheaper than new coal or gas in almost every country, and in most cases cheaper than fossil fuel plants that are already built and running. Fossil fuels look cheap on paper because they hide huge costs — air pollution alone kills millions and costs almost $6 trillion a year globally.

## Why it sounds right

Because for decades it was true. Solar panels used to be a hobbyist product. Wind turbines were subsidised. Fossil fuels were unambiguously cheaper. Anyone whose reference point is a 2000s energy debate is working from accurate memory. What changed is that between about 2010 and 2020, solar dropped ~85% and wind roughly halved, and the ratio flipped.

## What is actually true

- **Solar costs fell tenfold** since 2010. In the last decade alone, ~85% drop. Analysts expect another **50% drop by 2030**.
- In **2023, 96% of new solar and onshore wind projects** were cheaper than building a new coal or gas plant
- **Three-quarters of new solar and wind projects were even cheaper than fossil fuel plants that were already built and running**
- **Australia**: solar plus batteries about **30% cheaper** than gas peaking plants
- **India**: solar plus batteries already cheaper than many existing fossil fuel plants
- More than **a quarter of global emissions** are now covered by some kind of carbon tax or trading scheme

Renewables get cheaper because of the **learning curve** — every doubling of installed capacity has historically taken 20-30% off the cost. Fossil fuels don't follow a learning curve. Their prices swing with world events but have no long-term downward trend.

**The hidden cost of fossil fuels:**
- **Air pollution**: burning coal, oil and gas causes an estimated **$6 trillion a year in damages globally** — from heart disease, cancer, stroke and lung illness (millions of premature deaths per year)
- **Climate damage**: not priced into fossil fuel bills, but landing on future generations and poorer countries

## If they push back

**"Solar and wind are only cheap because of subsidies."**

That was true 15 years ago. Not any more. Subsidies helped renewables cross the initial cost gap when they were expensive. Today they're cheap because of **manufacturing scale**, not subsidies. Compare the sticker price of new solar in a competitive auction against new gas — the solar wins without subsidy in most markets.

**"When you add batteries, it's not cheap any more."**

Batteries add cost, and are needed to cover the intermittency problem. But solar + batteries are already **cheaper than gas at peak times** in Australia. Battery costs are following the same learning curve solar did — down about 90% over the past decade. The all-in system cost still comes out ahead in most places.

**"Fossil fuels look cheap in my energy bill."**

Because your bill doesn't include the air-pollution deaths, the healthcare costs, or the climate damage. Those costs are real — they're just paid by other people, mostly poorer people and future generations. That's what "hidden cost" means.

## Go deeper

**The learning curve is the whole story.** Solar's cost collapse wasn't magic. It was manufacturing scale and iteration. Every doubling of installed solar cut the cost 20-30%. That is now the biggest single force in the transition. Fossil fuels don't work this way. They're extracted, not manufactured; their cost tracks commodity prices, not learning curves.

**LCOE is a useful but imperfect measure.** The number most often quoted is **Levelised Cost of Electricity (LCOE)** — total lifetime cost divided by lifetime power output. It's useful for comparing sources, but it misses two things: (1) local variation — a solar panel in Andalusia beats one in Reykjavík; (2) system integration — the last 20-30% of a grid needs firm backup, and integrating renewables costs money too. Fair comparisons account for both.

**The finance gap.** Solar and wind have most of their cost upfront (capital) and very low ongoing cost (fuel = zero). That makes them very sensitive to interest rates. In rich countries with cheap capital, they're a bargain. In poorer countries paying 12% interest instead of 5%, the upfront cost problem is much harder. This is why [climate finance for poorer countries](poor-countries-need-fossil-fuels.md) matters so much.

**Fossil fuels won't get cheaper.** After decades of declining, coal, oil and gas prices have not fallen structurally. They swing on wars, cartels and speculation. Renewables have declined every year of the last decade and are expected to keep declining. The lines have already crossed.
