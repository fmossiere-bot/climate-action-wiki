# Myth: renewable energy is too expensive

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

That was true 15 years ago, but it stopped being true around 2015: today solar and wind are cheaper than new coal or gas almost everywhere, and often cheaper than fossil fuel plants that are already running. Fossil fuels only look cheap because their air pollution alone costs almost $6 trillion a year worldwide and kills millions of people.

## Why it sounds right

For decades it was true. Solar panels used to be a niche product for enthusiasts, wind turbines needed subsidies, and fossil fuels were clearly cheaper. Anyone whose picture of energy comes from the debates of the 2000s is remembering correctly. What changed is that between about 2010 and 2020, the cost of solar fell by about 85% and wind roughly halved, and the order flipped.

## What is actually true

- Solar costs have fallen **tenfold** since 2010, with a drop of about 85% in the last decade alone. Analysts expect a further 50% fall by 2030.
- In 2023, 96% of new solar and onshore wind projects were cheaper than building a new coal or gas plant.
- Three-quarters of new solar and wind projects were cheaper even than fossil fuel plants that were already built and running.
- In Australia, solar plus batteries is about 30% cheaper than gas plants used at times of peak demand.
- In India, solar plus batteries is already cheaper than many existing fossil fuel plants.
- More than a quarter of global emissions are now covered by some kind of carbon tax or emissions trading scheme.

Renewables get cheaper because of the **learning curve**: each time installed capacity has doubled, costs have historically fallen by 20-30%. Fossil fuels don't follow a learning curve. Their prices swing with world events, but they show no long-term fall.

Fossil fuels also carry hidden costs that don't appear on the bill:

- Air pollution: burning coal, oil and gas causes an estimated $6 trillion a year in damage worldwide, through heart disease, cancer, stroke and lung illness, and millions of early deaths each year.
- Climate damage: not included in the price of fossil fuels, but paid by future generations and poorer countries.

## If they push back

**"Solar and wind are only cheap because of subsidies."**

That was true 15 years ago, but not any more. Subsidies helped renewables close the gap when they were expensive. Today they're cheap because they are made at **huge scale**. Compare the price of new solar in a competitive auction with new gas, and solar wins without subsidy in most markets.

**"When you add batteries, it's not cheap any more."**

Batteries do add cost, and they're needed to cover the times when the sun isn't shining or the wind isn't blowing. But in Australia, solar plus batteries is already cheaper than gas at peak times. Battery costs are following the same learning curve solar did, falling about 90% over the past decade. In most places, the full system still comes out cheaper.

**"Fossil fuels look cheap in my energy bill."**

That's because your bill doesn't include the deaths from air pollution, the healthcare costs or the climate damage. Those costs are real. They're simply paid by other people, mostly poorer people and future generations. That is what "hidden cost" means.

## Go deeper

**The learning curve explains most of it.** Solar's fall in cost wasn't magic. It came from making panels at ever larger scale and improving them each time. Every doubling of installed solar cut the cost by 20-30%, and that is now the biggest single force behind the transition. Fossil fuels don't work this way: they are extracted rather than manufactured, and their cost follows commodity prices, not a learning curve.

**LCOE is useful but imperfect.** The figure most often quoted is the Levelised Cost of Electricity (LCOE): the total lifetime cost of a power plant divided by all the electricity it produces. It is handy for comparing sources, but it misses (1) local variation, since a solar panel in Andalusia beats one in Reykjavík, and (2) the cost of fitting renewables into the grid, since the last 20-30% of a grid needs reliable backup, and that costs money too. A fair comparison takes both into account.

**The finance gap.** Most of the cost of solar and wind is paid upfront, and running costs are very low because the fuel is free. That makes them very sensitive to interest rates. In rich countries where borrowing is cheap, they're a bargain. In poorer countries paying 12% interest instead of 5%, the upfront cost is much harder to meet. This is why [climate finance for poorer countries](poor-countries-need-fossil-fuels.md) matters so much.

**Fossil fuels won't get cheaper.** After decades of falling, coal, oil and gas prices have stopped going down over the long run. They swing with wars, cartels and speculation. Renewables have got cheaper every year for the last decade and are expected to keep doing so. The lines have already crossed.
