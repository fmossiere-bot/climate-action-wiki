# Myth: Heat pumps don't work in cold weather and gas is cheaper anyway

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

A heat pump gives you three to five units of heat per unit of electricity, because it moves heat instead of burning something. Norway and Finland have the highest adoption in Europe.

## Why it sounds right

A badly specified heat pump in a poorly insulated house genuinely does disappoint, and plenty of people have had that experience. Installation quality matters more here than with a boiler.

## What is actually true

A heat pump moves heat rather than making it, so it delivers roughly three to five units of heat for every unit of electricity. A gas boiler can never exceed one, because it is limited by the energy in the gas. On cold weather, the countries with the highest heat pump adoption in Europe are the Nordic ones, which settles the physics argument fairly directly. Running cost depends on the local ratio of electricity to gas prices, which is a tax and market design question rather than a technology one.

## If they push back

**"My neighbour got one and hated it"**

Probably a sizing or insulation problem rather than the technology. Bad installs are the single biggest cause of unhappy owners.

## Also heard as

- "they can't heat a house in winter"
- "you need to rip out all your radiators"
- "gas boilers are more efficient"
