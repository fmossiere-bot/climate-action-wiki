# Myth: Heat pumps don't work in cold weather and gas is cheaper anyway

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

A heat pump gives you three to five units of heat for every unit of electricity, because it moves heat instead of burning something. And Norway and Finland, hardly warm places, have the highest adoption in Europe.

## Why it sounds right

A heat pump that's the wrong size for a poorly insulated house really can disappoint, and plenty of people have had that experience. How well it's installed matters more than it does with a boiler.

## What is actually true

A heat pump moves heat rather than making it, so it delivers **roughly three to five units of heat** for every unit of electricity. A gas boiler can never get more than one unit of heat from one unit of gas, because it is limited by the energy in the fuel. As for the cold, the Nordic countries have the highest heat pump adoption in Europe, which answers the physics question fairly directly. Whether it's cheaper to run depends on the local price of electricity compared with gas, and that comes down to taxes and how energy markets are set up rather than the technology.

## If they push back

**"My neighbour got one and hated it"**

That's most likely a sizing or insulation problem rather than the technology. Bad installation is the single biggest reason owners are unhappy.

## Also heard as

- "they can't heat a house in winter"
- "you need to rip out all your radiators"
- "gas boilers are more efficient"
