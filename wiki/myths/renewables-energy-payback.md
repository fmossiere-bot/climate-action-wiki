# Myth: It takes more energy to build a wind turbine than it ever produces

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

A wind turbine pays back the carbon it took to build in about seven months, then keeps running for twenty-odd years.

## Why it sounds right

Making solar panels and turbines does produce carbon, and most of a turbine's lifetime emissions happen before it ever turns. That part is true.

## What is actually true

The payback is quick. A solar panel makes up for its manufacturing emissions in four to eight months, then runs for 25 to 30 years. A wind turbine takes about seven months and runs for 20 to 25 years, with close to zero pollution once it's spinning. So a turbine spends **well over 95%** of its working life producing energy that is pure gain.

## If they push back

**"What about when the wind isn't blowing"**

That's a different question, and a fair one. It's about storage and grid design, rather than whether the turbine is worth building.

## Also heard as

- "solar panels never pay back their manufacturing"
- "they only work because of subsidies"
- "the carbon cost of building them cancels it out"
