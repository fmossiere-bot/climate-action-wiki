# Myth: meat substitutes are worse for the climate because they're processed

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

They're not. Meat substitutes use energy in factories, but they still cause much less carbon than real meat, especially beef and lamb — often more than ten times less than pork. Even compared to the most climate-friendly beef farms, substitutes come out ahead almost every time. Peas and beans still beat processed substitutes, but the substitutes still comfortably beat any meat.

## Why it sounds right

Because factory-made food does use energy — running fermenters, heating, packaging, refrigerated logistics — and that shows up as emissions. It sounds intuitive that "grown naturally on a farm" would be lower carbon than "manufactured in a factory." What that intuition misses is how much land use and methane emissions come from actual livestock production, which is much bigger than any factory energy input.

## What is actually true

Ritchie compares carbon emissions **per 100 g of protein**, whole supply chain (farming, processing, packaging):

- **Beef and lamb** are by far the highest-emitting protein sources on the planet
- **Pork** is lower than beef/lamb but still very high
- **Meat substitutes** (Impossible Burger, Beyond Meat, tofu, Quorn/mycoprotein, peas) all cause **much lower emissions than beef and lamb** — often **more than 10× lower than pork**
- Meat substitutes also beat **most chicken and egg production** — only the most efficient chicken farms come close
- **Lab-grown (cultivated) meat**: lower than beef, roughly similar to chicken
- Even when comparing substitutes to the **most climate-friendly beef and pork farmers** (not the average), substitutes still win almost every time
- Straight plant foods (**peas, beans, lentils**) still beat processed substitutes, but the substitutes still beat any meat

## If they push back

**"But they use so much electricity to make."**

They do — and it's still nothing next to methane from ruminants. Livestock emissions are dominated by methane (from cow digestion) and land-use change (from clearing forest for pasture). Factory energy for a plant burger is a small addition compared to what a cow burps out over its life.

**"But I read lab-grown meat could be 25× worse than beef."**

That headline came from a **specific study using a biopharmaceutical-style production method** — an unusual, energy-intensive way of making lab meat, not how the industry actually plans to do it. Standard cultivated-meat processes are much lower carbon than beef, even when run on today's fossil-heavy grid. If they run on solar or wind, they're dramatically better.

**"Are substitutes really better than the best beef?"**

Yes, in almost every case. Even the most climate-efficient beef producers still emit more per 100 g of protein than a plant burger does. The gap between "best beef" and "average beef" is real, but both sit above substitutes.

## Go deeper

**Why methane dominates.** Ruminants (cows, sheep, goats) produce **methane** as a by-product of digesting grass — up to 500 litres of methane per cow per day. Methane is a much stronger warming gas than CO2 over the 20-year timeframe. This is why beef and lamb have such an enormous per-calorie footprint compared to poultry or plant sources, and why no amount of "efficient farming" can bring beef down to plant levels.

**The grid effect improves substitutes over time.** Meat substitutes use electricity for processing. As grids decarbonise, the carbon footprint of substitutes falls automatically. Livestock don't get this benefit — a cow will still emit the same methane in 2040 as today, regardless of what the electricity mix looks like. So the substitute vs meat gap widens over time.

**Lab-grown meat is a young technology.** Currently produced at very small scale. Standard cultivated meat processes have emissions closer to chicken than to beef. The "25× worse than beef" claim referenced a biopharmaceutical production style that no serious commercial producer uses. Studies of realistic industrial-scale processes find lower emissions than beef even on today's fossil-heavy grid — and much lower on clean electricity.

**Where the real hierarchy sits.** Best to worst on emissions per 100 g protein: peas/beans/lentils → tofu, tempeh → most meat substitutes → efficient chicken → average chicken/pork → cultivated meat → efficient beef → average beef → high-emission beef. Substitutes sit comfortably in the low-emission zone.
