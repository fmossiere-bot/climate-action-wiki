# Myth: meat substitutes are worse for the climate because they're processed

> [!WARNING] Verdict: False

## Say this

Factories do use energy, but meat substitutes still cause far less carbon than meat, often more than ten times less than pork and much less than beef or lamb. Beans and peas are better still, but substitutes beat even the most climate-friendly beef farms.

## Why it sounds right

Factory-made food does use energy: running fermenters, heating, packaging, refrigerated transport. That shows up as emissions. It feels natural to think that food grown on a farm must be lower carbon than food made in a factory. What that leaves out is how much land and methane goes into raising livestock, which is far bigger than any factory's energy use.

## What is actually true

Hannah Ritchie compares emissions **per 100 g of protein** across the whole supply chain, from farming to processing and packaging:

- Beef and lamb are by far the highest-emitting protein sources on the planet
- Pork is lower than beef and lamb, but still very high
- Meat substitutes (Impossible Burger, Beyond Meat, tofu, Quorn/mycoprotein, peas) all cause much lower emissions than beef and lamb, often more than 10× lower than pork
- Substitutes also beat most chicken and egg production, and only the most efficient chicken farms come close
- Lab-grown (cultivated) meat is lower than beef and roughly similar to chicken
- Even against the most climate-friendly beef and pork farms (not the average ones), substitutes win almost every time
- Plain plant foods (peas, beans, lentils) beat processed substitutes, but the substitutes still beat any meat

## If they push back

**"But they use so much electricity to make."**

They do, but it's small next to the methane from cattle and sheep. Livestock emissions are dominated by methane from digestion and by land-use change from clearing forest for pasture. The factory energy behind a plant burger is a small addition compared with what a cow burps out over its life.

**"But I read lab-grown meat could be 25× worse than beef."**

That headline came from a study that assumed a **biopharmaceutical-style production method**, an unusual and energy-hungry way of making lab meat that the industry doesn't plan to use. Standard cultivated-meat processes are much lower carbon than beef, even on today's fossil-heavy grid, and far better again on solar or wind.

**"Are substitutes really better than the best beef?"**

Yes, in almost every case. Even the most climate-efficient beef farms emit more per 100 g of protein than a plant burger. The gap between the best beef and average beef is real, but both sit above substitutes.

## Go deeper

**Why methane dominates.** Cows, sheep and goats (ruminants) produce methane as they digest grass, up to 500 litres of methane per cow per day. Over a 20-year timeframe, methane warms the planet much more strongly than CO2. That's why beef and lamb have such a large footprint per calorie compared with poultry or plants, and why no amount of efficient farming can bring beef down to plant levels.

**Cleaner grids make substitutes better over time.** Meat substitutes use electricity for processing, so as grids get cleaner, their footprint falls on its own. Livestock don't get this benefit: a cow in 2040 will emit the same methane as today, whatever the electricity mix. So the gap between substitutes and meat grows wider over time.

**Lab-grown meat is a young technology.** It's still made at very small scale. Standard cultivated-meat processes have emissions closer to chicken than to beef. The "25× worse than beef" claim was based on a biopharmaceutical-style production method that no serious commercial producer uses. Studies of realistic industrial-scale processes find lower emissions than beef even on today's fossil-heavy grid, and much lower on clean electricity.

**The full ranking.** From lowest to highest emissions per 100 g of protein: peas, beans and lentils → tofu and tempeh → most meat substitutes → efficient chicken → average chicken and pork → cultivated meat → efficient beef → average beef → high-emission beef. Substitutes sit comfortably in the low-emission part of the list.
