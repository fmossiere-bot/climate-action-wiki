# A healthier global diet could cut farm emissions by 85%

**Source:** ScienceDaily, London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine press release, 23 July 2026 — https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260716023557.htm
**Peer-reviewed study:** Gibson M et al. (2026), *Food systems transformation would reshape global agriculture*, *Nature*, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10775-2

## What it is

A summary of a new **Nature paper by an LSHTM–Cornell team with 10 modelling groups**, which puts specific economic and land-use numbers on what would happen if the world actually adopted the **EAT-Lancet 2025 Commission's planetary health diet**, combined with improved farming efficiency and halved food loss and waste. It is essentially the **economic-modelling follow-up to the [EAT-Lancet 2025 planetary health diet](../sectors/agriculture-food/eat-lancet-planetary-diet-2025.md)** already in the wiki.

## Key facts cited

- **Net CO₂ emissions from agricultural land-use change** could be **85% lower in 2050 than in 2020** if the world adopted the recommended diet plus efficiency and food-waste reductions
- **Global farmland use** would fall by up to **6% versus current trends**
- **Livestock production value** would fall by **~42% (~$630 billion)** by 2050 vs 2020; ruminant sector alone down **~70% (~$274bn)** — **around 400 million fewer ruminant animals worldwide**
- **Vegetable, fruit, nut and legume production value** would rise by **~57% (~$890 billion)**
- **EAT-Lancet 2025 headline maintained**: shift could prevent **15 million premature deaths per year**
- Earlier research: current global food system creates **$10–20 trillion in hidden costs annually** (health, environmental damage, lost productivity)

## Regional impacts (2050 vs 2020)

- **United States**: total agricultural production value **–21% (–$76bn)**; crop value **+20% (+$40bn)**; livestock value **–73% (–$116bn)**
- **India**: total value **+46% (+$198bn)**; crop **+65% (+$208bn)**; livestock **–8% (–$10bn)** — the country would gain economically as well as environmentally
- **Europe**: total **–35% (–$190bn)**; crop **–8% (–$22bn)**; livestock **–66% (–$168bn)**

Regional differences reflect farming systems, current diets, production costs, land availability, trade and food types produced.

## Caveats the authors flag

- Model assumes a **"costless consumer preference shift"** — that people willingly change what they eat without financial, cultural or practical barriers. In reality, healthy foods are not equally affordable or accessible everywhere.
- Scenarios are **foresight modelling** ("useful early guide," not a forecast).
- Transformation would create **severe economic disruption for livestock-dependent communities** without careful policy planning.

## Notable quotes

- **Dr Matt Gibson** (lead author, LSHTM/Cornell): *"Transforming food systems would deliver enormous potential benefits to our health and the environment but, as our results make clear, they would also lead to fundamental changes to global agriculture and affect the lives of millions of farmers and food producers. Rather than using these results as an excuse for inaction, it's critical that governments rise to the challenge and make difficult decisions for the good of our health and the planet. This means confronting powerful groups that profit from the status quo."*
- **Daniel Mason-D'Croz** (Cornell): *"A transformation of this magnitude cannot begin in 2050. Foresight modeling like that highlighted in this study is a valuable tool to inform actions today."*

## Why it matters

The [EAT-Lancet 2025 Commission](../sectors/agriculture-food/eat-lancet-planetary-diet-2025.md) already established the health case and the outline climate case. This paper adds the **specific economic numbers**: which sectors shrink, which grow, by how much, and where. The **85% cut in land-use-change emissions** is larger than the 15% non-CO2 agri-emissions cut cited in the 2025 EAT-Lancet report because it focuses on the **land-use side** of the food-system footprint, not on-farm emissions alone. It is the missing piece for policymakers who need to plan **just transitions** for farming communities alongside dietary shifts.
