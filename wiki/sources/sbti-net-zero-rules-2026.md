# SBTi sets new rules for corporate net zero

**Source:** Wall Street Journal (WSJ Pro Sustainable Business), Yusuf Khan, 11 June 2026. https://www.wsj.com/pro/sustainable-business/climate-standard-setter-sbti-sets-new-rules-for-companies-seeking-net-zero-43a38733

## What it is

The Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) sets the rules many large companies follow when they promise to reach net zero. It has published a long-awaited update to its corporate net-zero standard, and the Wall Street Journal reports on what has changed.

Until now, companies were expected to cut emissions mainly within their own operations and supply chains. The new standard loosens that. Companies can now count some market-based tools, such as environmental credits and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) credits, and carbon removals as part of their plan. The SBTi describes the overall shift as moving from a rigid rulebook to a **"best-efforts, continuous improvement" framework**.

## Why it matters

The SBTi's stamp of approval is one of the main ways the public can tell a serious corporate climate pledge from a vague one. Allowing credits and removals gives companies more flexibility, which may help those in sectors where cutting emissions directly is hard. It also gives them more room to lean on purchases rather than real reductions.

The standard still says that physically cutting emissions comes first and that credits are no substitute for action. How strictly that is applied will decide whether the change helps or weakens corporate climate targets. The SBTi's chief executive, David Kennedy, called the change "a continuous improvement journey".

## Key facts

- Companies may now count purchased environmental credits, such as SAF credits and renewable energy certificates, in their target calculations.
- Carbon removal, such as direct air capture (DAC) and reforestation credits, is allowed **from 2035**, but only for leftover emissions that are hard to eliminate.
- Under the "best-efforts" approach, companies must use every lever they control and be open about what stands in their way.
- Power purchase agreements (PPAs, long-term contracts to buy electricity) now count if they supply low-carbon electricity in the company's own region. Contracts in other regions are allowed where there are structural barriers.
- Matching electricity use with clean supply hour by hour is described as "preferable" but is not required for setting targets. Tech companies that use a lot of electricity must report their hourly matching share.
- Physical decarbonisation remains the main expectation. Credits are not a substitute for action.
