# Why is the Tropical Forests Forever fund short of money? DW

**Source:** DW, Jeannette Cwienk, 13 November 2026 (originally written in German). https://www.dw.com/en/could-lack-of-money-sink-tfff-rainforest-fund-before-it-even-launches/a-79878673

## What is it

The **Tropical Forest Forever Facility (TFFF)** was launched at the COP30 climate summit in Belém, Brazil, in 2025. Its idea is simple to state: make keeping a tropical forest standing pay better than cutting it down. Instead of grants, the fund borrows money cheaply from governments and private investors, invests it on financial markets, and passes part of the returns to countries that keep their forests intact.

A year on, DW reports that the fund may struggle to get off the ground. It needs $10 billion in government seed money by the end of 2026, but confirmed pledges from countries, NGOs and foundations total only **$7.9 billion**, leaving it about $2 billion short.

## Why it matters

More than 140 countries promised at the 2021 Glasgow climate summit to halt deforestation by 2030, yet logging continues. Over 8 million hectares of forest were lost in 2024, and nearly 490 million hectares have gone since 1990. Brazil shows things can change: deforestation in its Amazon fell by 41% between August 2025 and 2026, to the lowest rate since records began. The TFFF is meant to make that kind of progress last, whoever is in government.

Supporters such as Felix Finkbeiner, founder of the German charity Plant-for-the-Planet, like that it gives forest countries more control than traditional aid. Today, he says, if Ghana wants help to protect its rainforest, the World Bank in Washington decides. Contributions stay in the fund for 40 years, so a change of government in a donor country cannot pull the money out.

## How the money would work

- Governments would eventually put in $25 billion and private investors $100 billion. As a first step, the fund would issue about $40 billion in bonds to banks and investment firms, paying around 5% interest a year. Government money would absorb any losses.
- About 2.7% of the fund's profits would go to forest countries, at **$4 per hectare per year** of forest kept standing. At least 20% must go to Indigenous peoples and local communities.
- Under this system, the Democratic Republic of the Congo would receive about $333 million for its 2024 forest area, according to the independent TFFF Watch tracker.
- Payments are only for intact forest, not timber plantations, and are checked by satellite. Money is deducted for cleared or damaged areas, and payments can be frozen if countries do not spend them on forest protection.
- The fund aims to invest in bonds from emerging and developing countries and to avoid oil, gas and coal.

## Key facts

- Eight countries have said they will contribute, including Brazil, Indonesia, France, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and Luxembourg.
- Norway is the largest would-be donor at $3 billion, but only if the full $10 billion is in place by the end of the year.
- Germany has pledged about €1 billion from 2027, paid over 10 years, which its parliament still has to approve.
- Canada, China, South Korea and the United Arab Emirates are considering joining.
- Philanthropic organisations have pledged $88 million, including $73 million announced during the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey.
