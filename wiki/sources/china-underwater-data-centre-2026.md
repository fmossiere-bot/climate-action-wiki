# World's first underwater data centre powered by wind is now online

**Source:** New Atlas, Bronwyn Thompson, 1 June 2026. https://newatlas.com/energy/china-underwater-data-center-opens/

## What it is

China has switched on the world's first commercial **underwater data centre**, sitting on the seabed off Shanghai in the Lin-gang Special Area. It was built by a subsidiary of China Communications Construction.

Most of its power comes from offshore wind. Instead of using fresh water to carry away heat, it uses the surrounding seawater. Because it sits under the sea, it takes up more than 90% less land than a conventional data centre.

## Why it matters

Data centres use a lot of electricity, and a large share of that goes on keeping the servers cool. Many also draw heavily on fresh water, which is already scarce in some regions. Putting the servers in the sea tackles both problems at once.

There is an open question, though. Nobody yet knows what happens to local sea life when heat is released into the water on a commercial scale. That needs watching before the idea spreads.

## Key facts

- The centre holds 192 server racks over four levels. It runs at 2.3 MW today, with a planned capacity of 24 MW.
- A circulating copper-pipe heat exchange system cuts electricity use by **22.8%**.
- Offshore wind farms are expected to supply 95% of the electricity it needs.
- Seawater acts as the coolant, so no fresh water is needed.
- It uses more than 90% less land than an above-ground facility.
- Prof Li Zhen of Tsinghua University says that in an undersea data centre of the same scale, "the electricity used for cooling would only account for about one-tenth of total power consumption".
- If the approach were used worldwide, it could save up to 50 billion kWh of electricity a year.
