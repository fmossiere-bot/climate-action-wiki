# ReFarm Wicklow — farmer-led wildlife ponds and blended finance for nature

**Source:** The Journal / The Rewildlife, Jack Morley, 2 September 2026 — https://www.thejournal.ie/readme/rewilding-life-7148661-Sep2026/

## What it is

Feature by Jack Morley (The Rewildlife) on **ReFarm Wicklow**, the first pilot project of the not-for-profit **ReFarm**, co-founded by sustainability investor **Anke Heydenreich** and ecologist **Dr Brendan Dunford** (of the Burrenbeo Trust, Farming for Nature and Hare's Corner fame). **The idea is simple and clever: rather than buying land to rewild it, ReFarm raises blended finance (public + private) and pays farmers to do biodiversity actions on the land they already farm.** The Wicklow pilot focuses on one habitat: **wildlife ponds** (minimum 150 m², average 761 m², up to €3,000 per farm), financed by Wicklow County Council and construction firm John Paul Construction, with independent research from Trinity College Dublin (Profs Jane Stout and Martha O'Hagan).

## Key facts

- **~80 farms** across Ireland now work with ReFarm; **due to double** in 2026
- **Ponds must be ≥150 m²**, budget up to **€3,000 per farm** — Wicklow average has been **761 m²**
- **Ponds support 60% of Ireland's native freshwater species** (ecologist Aoife O'Rourke) and can host more biodiversity than rivers or lakes for their size
- **Farmers manage ~70% of Ireland's land** — the numbers-matter argument for a farmer-partnership model
- Application process described as "reasonably simple"; ponds under 1 m deep avoid planning hurdles
- **Four county councils (Leitrim, Longford, Laois, Meath)** launched their own ReFarm pond projects in 2026 following Wicklow's success
- Wicklow's 2026 round was **oversubscribed and already closed**
- Backers: **John Paul Construction** (Mike McLoughlin, head of health, safety & sustainability), plus philanthropic and public funding via **Wicklow County Council** (biodiversity officer Hannah O'Kelly)
- Research partner: **Trinity College Dublin** (Prof Jane Stout, Prof Martha O'Hagan; also ecologist Kate Harrington)
- Fits into Wicklow's **Biodiversity Action Plan 2026-2031** (50 actions over 5 years)

## Notable quotes

- **Brendan Dunford:** *"When it comes to the crisis that we have in Ireland at the moment around biodiversity, we view farmers as the solution, and we're prepared to invest in that."*
- **Paul Carberry** (West Wicklow farmer, 50 acres, had dug 2 ponds pre-ReFarm): *"With ponds, they form a life of their own. All the amphibians and different plants and birds, and particularly wetland birds… it's fantastic."*
- **Hilda Crampton** (Castleruddery Organic Farm): *"Communication was practically instantaneous. Everything to do with payments and repayments, it happened so quickly. We were absolutely in shock at how fast it all happened."* — two days after diggers arrived, a fully formed pond replaced scrubby wet ground: *"It looks like it was always there."*
- **Anke Heydenreich:** *"ReFarm Wicklow is a perfect example of how blended finance came together to work with farmers, to invest in nature. The idea is to prove that it makes environmental, social and economic sense so we can replicate it nationally across different counties and possibly further afield."*
- **Aoife O'Rourke** (ecologist): *"If there was one action that I would ask people to take, if they wanted to bring nature back to the land, it would be by creating a clean, fresh water wildlife pond."*
- **Kate Harrington** (Trinity ecologist): *"There's been some studies done which have shown that with ponds across a landscape, there can be more biodiversity there than there can be in river or lake systems. Even though they're small, they're greater than the sum of their parts."*
- **Mike McLoughlin** (John Paul Construction): *"You have the research side from Trinity, you have Brendan, that level of expertise… It's a recipe that can really work… This is a project with 100-plus farmers, but this is something that every farmer in Ireland could be involved in."*
- **Hannah O'Kelly** (Wicklow biodiversity officer): *"We very quickly got a dozen farmers or landowners who were interested. There was very little paperwork involved… it was all very clear what they had to do and what they were going to get in return."*

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **Blended finance for nature that actually works.** Most nature-finance conversations stall on the "who pays and who benefits" question. ReFarm sidesteps that by keeping farmers in ownership of the land and paying them for defined actions with fast, low-paperwork disbursement. The Trinity College Dublin research partnership provides independent evidence of ecological outcomes, which is what makes the funding credible for corporates like John Paul Construction.
2. **Ponds are an under-appreciated biodiversity lever.** Ireland has lost many ponds to agricultural drainage over decades. Restoring them supports 60% of Ireland's native freshwater species, buffers water flow, and — as farmers reported — is delivered fast (days, not months) and cheaply (max €3,000).
3. **A replicable model.** The Wicklow pilot has already spread to Leitrim, Longford, Laois and Meath. If it scales further, this becomes a template for farmer-led biodiversity investment across Ireland and beyond — genuinely "positive tipping-point" adjacent, in the sense that the ambition doubles each year and the funder network compounds.
