# ReFarm Wicklow: farmer-led wildlife ponds and blended finance for nature

**Source:** The Journal / The Rewildlife, Jack Morley, 2 September 2026. https://www.thejournal.ie/readme/rewilding-life-7148661-Sep2026/

## What it is

Jack Morley of The Rewildlife writes about **ReFarm Wicklow**, the first pilot project run by ReFarm. ReFarm is a not-for-profit set up by sustainability investor Anke Heydenreich and ecologist Dr Brendan Dunford, who is known for the Burrenbeo Trust, Farming for Nature and Hare's Corner.

The idea is simple. Instead of buying land to rewild it, ReFarm brings together public and private money (known as blended finance) and pays farmers to carry out work for nature on the land they already farm.

The Wicklow pilot focuses on one habitat: wildlife ponds. Each pond must be at least 150 m², the Wicklow average has been 761 m², and each farm can receive up to €3,000. Wicklow County Council and the building firm John Paul Construction pay for it, and Trinity College Dublin, led by Professors Jane Stout and Martha O'Hagan, studies the results independently.

## Why it matters

Paying for nature often gets stuck on who pays and who benefits. ReFarm avoids that problem. Farmers keep their land, are paid for clearly defined work, and the money arrives quickly with little paperwork. The independent research from Trinity College Dublin shows whether the ponds actually help wildlife, which is what gives a company like John Paul Construction the confidence to fund it.

Ponds are easy to overlook. Ireland has lost many of them over the decades as farmland was drained. Bringing them back supports **60% of Ireland's native freshwater species** and helps slow the flow of water across the land. Farmers in the piece say a pond can be dug in days, and the cost is capped at €3,000.

The model is also spreading. After Wicklow, four more county councils (Leitrim, Longford, Laois and Meath) started their own ReFarm pond projects in 2026. If it keeps growing, it could become a template for farmer-led nature work across Ireland and beyond.

## Key facts

- About 80 farms across Ireland now work with ReFarm, a number due to double in 2026.
- Ponds must be at least 150 m², with a budget of up to €3,000 per farm. The Wicklow average has been 761 m².
- According to ecologist Aoife O'Rourke, ponds support 60% of Ireland's native freshwater species and, for their size, can hold more wildlife than rivers or lakes.
- Farmers manage about **70% of Ireland's land**, which is why working with them matters so much.
- Applying is described as "reasonably simple". Ponds less than 1 m deep avoid planning hurdles.
- Four county councils (Leitrim, Longford, Laois and Meath) launched their own ReFarm pond projects in 2026 after Wicklow's success.
- Wicklow's 2026 round was oversubscribed and has already closed.
- Backers include John Paul Construction (through Mike McLoughlin, head of health, safety and sustainability), plus philanthropic and public money through Wicklow County Council (biodiversity officer Hannah O'Kelly).
- The research partner is Trinity College Dublin (Professor Jane Stout, Professor Martha O'Hagan and ecologist Kate Harrington).
- The project is part of Wicklow's Biodiversity Action Plan 2026-2031, which sets out 50 actions over 5 years.

## Voices in the piece

Brendan Dunford, ReFarm's co-founder, puts farmers at the centre: *"When it comes to the crisis that we have in Ireland at the moment around biodiversity, we view farmers as the solution, and we're prepared to invest in that."* His co-founder Anke Heydenreich sees the pilot as proof of a model: *"ReFarm Wicklow is a perfect example of how blended finance came together to work with farmers, to invest in nature. The idea is to prove that it makes environmental, social and economic sense so we can replicate it nationally across different counties and possibly further afield."*

The farmers speak from experience. Paul Carberry farms 50 acres in West Wicklow and had already dug 2 ponds before ReFarm: *"With ponds, they form a life of their own. All the amphibians and different plants and birds, and particularly wetland birds… it's fantastic."* Hilda Crampton of Castleruddery Organic Farm was surprised by the speed: *"Communication was practically instantaneous. Everything to do with payments and repayments, it happened so quickly. We were absolutely in shock at how fast it all happened."* Two days after the diggers arrived, a full pond had replaced a patch of scrubby wet ground. *"It looks like it was always there."*

The ecologists explain why ponds punch above their weight. Aoife O'Rourke says: *"If there was one action that I would ask people to take, if they wanted to bring nature back to the land, it would be by creating a clean, fresh water wildlife pond."* Kate Harrington of Trinity adds: *"There's been some studies done which have shown that with ponds across a landscape, there can be more biodiversity there than there can be in river or lake systems. Even though they're small, they're greater than the sum of their parts."*

The funders talk about how easy it was to take part. Mike McLoughlin of John Paul Construction says: *"You have the research side from Trinity, you have Brendan, that level of expertise… It's a recipe that can really work… This is a project with 100-plus farmers, but this is something that every farmer in Ireland could be involved in."* Hannah O'Kelly, Wicklow's biodiversity officer, recalls: *"We very quickly got a dozen farmers or landowners who were interested. There was very little paperwork involved… it was all very clear what they had to do and what they were going to get in return."*
