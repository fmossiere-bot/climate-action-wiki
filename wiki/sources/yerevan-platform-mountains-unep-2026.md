# The Yerevan Platform on mountains and freshwater: UNEP

**Source:** UN Environment Programme (UNEP), press release, Yerevan, 28 October 2026. https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/press-release/armenia-partnership-unep-launches-global-initiative-drive-action

## What is it

During the UN biodiversity conference (COP17), Armenia, which held the COP presidency, launched the **Yerevan Platform on Mountains and Related Freshwater Ecosystems** with UNEP. It is one of the Armenian presidency's four flagship initiatives. The other three cover subsidy reform, conservation areas and biodiversity monitoring.

The platform will bring mountain countries together with the downstream countries that depend on their water, in a regular dialogue. The aim is to spot where money, expertise and support are most needed: protecting water supplies, restoring damaged habitats, and backing the communities who look after mountain landscapes. Technical help will be shaped by what each country asks for, rather than one plan for all.

## Why it matters

Mountains act as the world's **water towers**. Together with their rivers and lakes, they supply 55-60% of the planet's yearly freshwater flows. Yet they are among the least protected and least funded ecosystems. When they degrade, the effects travel downhill: water shortages, landslides and failing ecosystems for people far from the slopes. UNEP's executive director Inger Andersen summed it up: "what happens in mountains does not stay in the mountains."

For Armenia, a mountainous country, the issue is personal. COP17 president Hambardzoum Matevosyan said success means mountains and freshwater appearing "not as a footnote, but as a priority" in national plans and budgets.

## Key facts

- Mountains support the livelihoods of around 1.2 billion people, including Indigenous peoples and local communities.
- 25 of the world's 36 biodiversity hotspots include mountain ecosystems, which hold 85% of the world's species of amphibians, birds and mammals.
- Only 19% of mountain areas are protected.
- The launch followed the first-ever Mountain Ecosystems Day at a CBD COP, with sessions on science, funding, Indigenous peoples and pastoralists, and regional efforts from the Alps and Carpathians to the Hindu Kush Himalaya, the Andes and the Caucasus.
- A Kenyan pastoralist leader stressed that moving herds between lowlands and mountains is how pastoralists cope with drought, and that restrictions on movement and land access are breaking these systems down.
- Next steps: the Bishkek +25 Second Global Mountain Summit in October 2027, and a progress update at COP18.
