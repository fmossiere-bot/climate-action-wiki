# Ireland has completed just 10 of 74 climate commitments

**Source:** TheJournal.ie, Jane Matthews, 29 July 2026. https://www.thejournal.ie/climate-targets-progress-report-7116989-Jul2026/

## What it is

A progress report from Ireland's Department of Climate and the Environment finds that the government has completed only **10 of the 74 measures** it committed to in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 Climate Action Plans.

The 2026 plan itself had still not been published when the article was written. It has been delayed to Q3 2026, which opposition politicians called "inexcusable."

## Why it matters

Climate plans are full of targets and pledges. This report is the government's own count of how many of those promises have actually been delivered, and the answer is roughly one in seven. It gives a clear, dated check on progress.

The department itself warns that the delays put Ireland's binding emissions targets at risk, add to climate change and will make climate spending more expensive later on.

## Key facts

- 10 of 74 measures were completed over three years (the 2023-2025 plans combined).
- Completed items include the second Circular Economy Strategy, 200km of new cycling infrastructure and a new national strategy for managing transport demand (reducing the need for car journeys).
- The department blames delays on the other 64 measures on limited staff and capacity, consultation requirements, funding and procurement problems, and the need to line up with other national or EU policy.
- Agriculture was Ireland's highest-emitting sector in 2025, responsible for 36% of all national emissions. Methane made up 70% of farming emissions, mostly from cattle, sheep and manure.
- Neither of the two farming commitments from 2023-2025 has been delivered: a framework to reduce feed costs for organic livestock, and a draft carbon farming framework. The second is now over two years late and is due later in 2026.

## What is still waiting

Several other promised measures have not arrived yet:

- shared mobility hubs in four cities and towns
- about 225 EV charging points at sports clubs
- a roadmap to phase out fossil fuel heating in all buildings
- a retrofit programme from the Office of Public Works (OPW) for traditional and historic buildings

## Voices in the piece

Taoiseach Micheál Martin called the report "a clear reminder that we must move faster". Climate Minister Darragh O'Brien said there is "no room for delay". From the opposition, Labour's Ciarán Ahern said it shows "just how far away from achieving our climate action targets this government is".
