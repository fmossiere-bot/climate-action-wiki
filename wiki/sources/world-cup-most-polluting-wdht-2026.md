# Welcome to the most polluting Games ever

**Source:** We Don't Have Time, Markus Lutteman, June 2026. https://app.wedonthavetime.org/posts/44991735-7f37-43f6-bca2-c677af540e95

## What it is

An analysis of the environmental contradictions of the 2026 FIFA World Cup. FIFA's website talks about sustainability. Yet it expanded the tournament to **48 teams and 104 matches** spread across 16 cities on one continent. It signed a four-year sponsorship deal with Saudi Aramco, the state oil company. And it is holding the event in a country, the United States, that has withdrawn from the Paris Agreement.

## Why it matters

Football is the world's most popular sport, and millions of fans will follow their teams across North America. The bigger format means much more flying, and so more emissions, before a ball is even kicked in the knockout rounds.

Fans and players also feel the effects of a warming climate directly. Most of the venues face extreme heat, flooding or heavy rain. Closer to home, in the UK, local matches are already being called off more often because of flooded pitches.

The piece also looks at **sportswashing**: when a country or company uses sport to polish its reputation. FIFA's deal with Aramco, and its choice of Saudi Arabia to host the 2034 World Cup, put a fossil fuel producer at the centre of the game.

## Key facts

- Research from the University of Manchester suggests the 2026 World Cup could be the most polluting in the tournament's history.
- The expanded format forces fans into journeys of thousands of kilometres. Bosnia's group games, for example, are in Toronto, LA and Seattle, more than 5,000 km of travel before the knockout rounds.
- Queen's University Belfast found that 14 of the 16 venues exceed safety thresholds for extreme heat, flooding or heavy rain.
- FIFA signed a 4-year deal with Saudi Aramco in 2024. Weeks before signing, Aramco's chief executive called phasing out oil "a fantasy".
- Research by SGR (Scientists for Global Responsibility) estimates the Aramco sponsorship could lead to an extra **30 million tonnes of CO₂e** in 2026.
- The 2034 World Cup has been awarded to Saudi Arabia, a country that has repeatedly blocked progress at UN climate talks.
- In the UK, more and more local football matches are being cancelled because of extreme weather, mainly flooding. The Met Office projects winters 30% wetter by 2070 if emissions stay high.
- FIFA's revenue for the 2023–2026 cycle is about $13 billion, almost twice the previous cycle.

## Voices in the piece

Dr Oscar Berglund of the University of Bristol, puts it bluntly: "FIFA has made elite men's football the primary target of petrostate sportswashing."
