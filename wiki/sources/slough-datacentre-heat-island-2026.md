# 'Slough is like an experiment': Europe's largest datacentre hub leaves town sweltering

**Source:** The Guardian, Aisha Down, 26 June 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jun/26/slough-is-like-an-experiment-europes-largest-datacentre-hub-leaves-town-sweltering

## What it is

A feature on Slough, a town in Berkshire 16 km west of Heathrow that has become **Europe's largest datacentre hub**. An estimated 30 to 40 huge facilities run computing for Amazon, Google, Oracle and Microsoft, on a campus of roughly 1 gigawatt.

The article asks whether this cluster added to the local heat during the UK's June 2026 heatwave. It draws on what residents say and on new research from Cambridge suggesting that datacentres create a measurable urban heat island, a patch of town that stays hotter than its surroundings.

## Why it matters

Datacentres are usually discussed in terms of electricity and emissions. If the Cambridge finding holds at this scale, the UK's rush to build very large datacentres also brings a local problem: heat. Communities next to these clusters, often less well-off ones, get extra heat on top of heatwaves made worse by climate change. The benefits, such as jobs and tax, are spread much more widely.

The piece follows earlier reporting on how much power UK datacentres need and their reliance on gas. It strengthens the case for rules on where datacentres can be built and for putting their waste heat to use.

## Key facts

- Slough hosts an estimated 30-40 large datacentres, with roughly 1 GW of capacity in total.
- A preprint from Cambridge (Andrea Marinoni and colleagues, arXiv 2603.20897) finds that datacentres raise local temperatures by **2°C on average and up to 9°C** right around them. A preprint has not yet been reviewed by other scientists.
- The study uses decades of satellite data and controls for the effects of urban growth and climate change.
- Marinoni's data covers an older generation of datacentres, of about 100 MW at most. Slough is roughly 10× larger and, in his words, "almost like an experiment by itself".
- The main operators in Slough are Equinix and Digital Realty, which rent space to big tech companies.
- On 24 June 2026, the weather station nearest the tech park recorded 36.7°C, several degrees hotter than other stations in Slough.
- One resident recalls 45°C in a Slough car park in 2022, when it was 39°C in nearby Windsor.
- The UK government has proposed using waste heat from datacentres to warm thousands of homes.

## Voices and limits

The article mixes residents' experiences (a shop manager, a worker on an industrial park, people who have lived in Slough all their lives) with careful caveats from the lead researcher. It does not claim that datacentres alone explain Slough's heat. The new evidence sits alongside climate change and the heat trapped by concrete and tarmac in any built-up area.
