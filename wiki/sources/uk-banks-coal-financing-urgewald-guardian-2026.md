# UK banks are Europe's biggest coal financiers, Urgewald finds

**Source:** The Guardian, Matthew Taylor, 30 September 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/sep/30/uk-banks-are-europe-biggest-providers-coal-financing-report-finds

## What is it

The German environmental and human rights group **Urgewald** tracked the loans and underwriting that 744 commercial banks provided to coal companies between 2022 and 2025. It counted every company in the "coal value chain": mines, power stations, logistics, exploration and trading. The Guardian reported its findings in September 2026.

<!-- tag: uk -->
British banks came out as Europe's biggest backers of coal. They provided $8.3bn (£6.2bn) since the COP26 summit in Glasgow in 2021, where world leaders promised to "phase down" coal. German banks provided $4.9bn and French banks $3.4bn over the same period.

## Why it matters

Coal is the most polluting fossil fuel, and COP26 was meant to start its decline. Globally, though, bank money for coal has barely moved. It averaged about **$117bn a year** between 2022 and 2025. Cuts in some places have been cancelled out by increases elsewhere.

<!-- tag: uk, company-evaluations -->
The UK figure is driven by two banks that both have public net zero commitments. Barclays' coal financing rose by 34%, from about $1.2bn in 2022 to $1.6bn in 2025. HSBC's more than doubled, from $200m to $414m. Overall, money from UK banks to the coal value chain grew by 17% over the period, while EU banks cut theirs by 46%. Urgewald's director, Heffa Schücking, asked why the two banks were "moving in the opposite direction to the rest of Europe."

## Key facts

- Chinese banks provided 62% of global coal financing, $289bn, up 8% over the four years.
- US banks provided $67bn, up 23%. Indonesian banks raised their coal financing by 64%, from $1.4bn in 2022 to $2.3bn.
- Banks in the EU, Taiwan, Malaysia and Thailand cut their coal financing significantly.
- HSBC says it will phase out finance for thermal coal power and mining by 2030 in EU and OECD markets and by 2040 elsewhere. It reports that financed emissions from thermal coal mining fell by 94% between 2020 and 2024, and its thermal coal exposure fell from about $1bn to about $0.5bn.
- Barclays says many of the companies counted are diversified energy or mining firms, and that it does not finance companies earning more than 30% of revenue from thermal coal. It says it has facilitated more than $300bn of sustainable and transition finance in three years.

## Two ways of counting

The banks and Urgewald are measuring different things. HSBC and Barclays count their exposure to coal activities under their own definitions. Urgewald counts all financing to any company active in the coal value chain, including large diversified groups. Urgewald says its definition is widely accepted by financial institutions. Both sets of numbers can be accurate at the same time, which is why the definition behind a bank's coal claim matters as much as the figure.

Schücking welcomed the cuts in the EU as proof that a clear coal policy can "disrupt the flow of money to the industry", but warned that this progress was "being swallowed up" by rising coal finance elsewhere.
