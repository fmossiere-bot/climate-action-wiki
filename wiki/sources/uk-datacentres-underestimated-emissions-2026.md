# Officials hugely underestimated the impact of AI datacentres on UK carbon emissions

**Source:** The Guardian, Damien Gayle, 24 April 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/apr/24/officials-hugely-underestimated-impact-of-ai-datacentres-on-uk-carbon-emissions

## What it is

The UK government has raised its estimate of the carbon emissions from AI data centres by a factor of **more than 100**. Its earlier figure turned out to be a serious undercount. The change followed an investigation by the campaign group Foxglove and the climate website Carbon Brief, and was first reported by Politico.

## Why it matters

Data centres for AI are being built quickly, and they use a great deal of electricity. If the government's own numbers miss the scale of that demand, its climate plans will miss it too. The new estimate puts AI data centres at up to 3.4% of all UK emissions over the next decade, a share that is hard to ignore when every other sector is being asked to cut back. How much they end up emitting depends on choices still to be made about efficiency and how fast the grid moves to clean power.

## Key facts

- The previous estimate was a maximum of 0.142 million tonnes of CO₂ a year. It has since been quietly deleted.
- The revised estimate is **34–123 million tonnes of CO₂** over 2025–2035, which is 0.9–3.4% of the UK's projected total emissions.
- The lower end of that range depends on AI models becoming more efficient and the electricity grid cutting its emissions faster.
- The revision was first reported by Politico, and followed an investigation by Foxglove and Carbon Brief.
- Foxglove warns that, if left unchecked, the build-out of AI data centres "could double the electricity consumption of the entire country".
- The government declined to comment on the record.
