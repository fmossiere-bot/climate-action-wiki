# Paris Agreement progress reports 2026: more countries acting, still far off track

**Source:** UN Climate Change News, 27 October 2026. https://unfccc.int/news/un-climate-change-reports-show-paris-agreement-supporting-whole-of-economy-approaches-but-progress

## What is it

Every year before the UN climate summit, the UN climate secretariat (UNFCCC) has to publish a set of reports that check how countries are doing under the Paris Agreement. In late October 2026, two weeks before COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye (9 to 20 November), it released three of them, plus two reports on climate finance.

The three main reports look at different things. The **NDC Synthesis Report** adds up countries' national climate plans (Nationally Determined Contributions, or NDCs) to see where they would take global emissions. The Biennial Transparency Report (BTR) synthesis looks at what countries say they have actually done. The National Adaptation Plan (NAP) progress report looks at how countries are preparing for climate impacts.

The overall message is mixed. Climate policy is now built into whole economies in a way it was not ten years ago. But the emission cuts that countries have pledged are still nowhere near what science says is needed.

## Why it matters

These reports are the official scorecard of the Paris Agreement. The most important number is small: if countries do what their current plans say, global emissions in 2035 would be only about **6% lower than in 2019**. Even with every pledge fully delivered, the cut would be around 9%. Last year's synthesis put the figure at 10 to 12%, so the picture has not improved.

For anyone following COP31, these figures explain why the summit was framed around "implementation": turning plans that already exist into real projects, money and results. UN climate chief Simon Stiell put it this way: "collective progress remains clearly insufficient, that global transitions are very uneven, and that many countries face substantial barriers to delivering their targets and pledges."

## Key facts

- Between 1 January 2024 and 31 August 2026, 127 new NDCs were recorded, covering 154 Parties (the EU files one plan for its member states) and around 70% of global greenhouse gas emissions.
- Current pledges would cut global emissions by around 6% by 2035 compared with 2019. Full implementation of all pledges would bring that to about 9.0%.
- Plans are broader than before: 98% of Parties now involve cities, regions, businesses or civil society in delivering their NDCs, up from 70% in earlier plans. 90% say the first global stocktake shaped their plan, and 65% highlight clean energy as a source of energy security.
- As of 20 October 2026, 146 countries had started a national adaptation planning process, and 81 developing countries had submitted a plan, including 29 of the least developed countries (LDCs) and 17 small island developing states (SIDS). Getting the money to carry those plans out remains the main barrier.
- 137 Parties have now filed transparency reports, covering about 85% of global emissions. Between 2020 and 2022, 61 Parties (about 66% of 2020 emissions) made progress towards their NDC targets.
- One data source found that rich countries met their goal of USD 100 billion a year in climate finance for developing countries in 2022, 2023 and 2024.
- Global climate finance from all sources rose by 32% to an average of USD 1.9 trillion a year in 2023-2024, but adaptation finance and money reaching developing countries still fall short.

## The adaptation gap: plans are not projects

The adaptation report moves from counting plans to asking whether they are being carried out. Its answer is that many are stuck. Countries struggle to write project proposals, then to get them approved, then to receive the money once approved. There are few accredited bodies that can channel funds, and long processing times slow everything down. In the report's words, approval "does not equal access to finance." For a farming community waiting for an irrigation scheme or a coastal town waiting for flood defences, that delay is where adaptation fails.

## What comes next

The secretariat also announced the Yearbook of Global Climate Action 2026, to be launched at the start of COP31. It tracks action by businesses, cities and regions, drawing on more than 80 cooperative initiatives that reported over 200 actions. Alongside the negotiations, COP31's Global Climate Action Agenda focuses on electrification, finance, waste and cutting methane, a greenhouse gas that traps a lot of heat but breaks down within about a decade or so.

## Limits of the figures

The NDC figures depend on which plans were filed by the 31 August 2026 cut-off, so later submissions are not included. The finance figures come from several sources that measure progress differently, which is why the report only says that "one source" found the USD 100 billion goal was met. The transparency data cover 2020 to 2022, so they describe the recent past rather than today.
