# Robot survey finds 121 forms of life on Hywind Scotland's floating turbines

**Source:** EcoPortal, Kelly Lippke, 13 August 2026 — https://www.ecoportal.net/en/robot-inspected-underwater-components/32017/
**Underlying peer-reviewed study:** Karlsson, R. et al., *Artificial hard-substrate colonization in the offshore Hywind Scotland Pilot Park*, Wind Energ. Sci., 7, 801–814, 2022 — https://doi.org/10.5194/wes-7-801-2022

## What it is

A remote-controlled submersible robot inspected 41 underwater components across the five floating turbines of Equinor's **Hywind Scotland Pilot Park**, 12 miles off the Scottish coast. Unlike fixed-bottom turbines, these float on platforms anchored by mooring cables and suction anchors, reaching hundreds of feet down. Biological researchers logged every organism spotted on the mooring lines, steel frames and anchors during the routine maintenance inspection.

## Key facts cited

- Five floating turbines, 41 underwater components surveyed, 12 miles off the Scottish coast
- **121 distinct forms of sea life** identified across the installations
- Near the surface: dense clusters of orange starfish, spiny sea urchins, thick mats of plumose sea anemones
- Deeper, near the suction anchors: squat lobsters, brown crabs, large fish sheltering in steel crevices
- Researchers documented **soft corals and possible deep-sea stony corals** growing on the frame — slow-growing, sensitive organisms
- Combined structure forms an **artificial reef reaching roughly 400 feet down**
- Turbine sites block bottom-trawling, leaving an accidental sanctuary for marine life to establish undisturbed
- Growth varies by depth: fast-growing, light-dependent species near the surface; cold-water, dark-adapted species deeper down
- Caveat: single five-turbine site; multi-year studies still needed to confirm whether platforms generate new marine life or simply concentrate existing populations

## Why it matters

This is a **floating** offshore wind case, distinct from the fixed-bottom Formosa Wind Farm case already in the wiki (see [86 new fish species on Taiwan offshore wind](taiwan-offshore-wind-reef-fish-2026.md)). Floating platforms are how offshore wind expands into deeper water, so evidence that they too host reef-like biodiversity — including slow-growing corals — extends the "offshore wind as accidental reef" pattern to a second turbine technology and a colder, North Atlantic ecosystem.
