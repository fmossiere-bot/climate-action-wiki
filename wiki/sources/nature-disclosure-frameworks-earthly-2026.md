# Core sustainability disclosure frameworks and nature investments — Earthly overview

**Source:** Earthly blog, Giacomo Bartoleschi and Faith Sayo, 23 July 2026 — https://earthly.org/blog/what-core-sustainability-disclosure-frameworks-require-from-your-nature-investments

## What it is

A **vendor-authored overview** (Earthly, a nature-based credit provider) mapping **eight major sustainability disclosure frameworks** to what each requires from corporate **nature investments** — carbon credits, biodiversity credits, and nature-based projects. The framework map itself is genuinely useful; the vendor's Keystone 3.0 marketing pitch that runs through the piece is not the reason to read it.

## The eight frameworks and what they demand

| Framework | Focus | Nature-investment requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2** | Corporate net-zero targets | Disclose investments against **OER recognition tiers**; project-level impact/monitoring data; verified mitigation outcomes; annual progress reporting |
| **TNFD** | Nature-related financial disclosure | Report investment-level metrics (A21.0, A23.2, A24.0, A24.3, A24.4); core + additional impact metrics; structured evidence-based project data |
| **IFRS S2** — Climate-related disclosures | Adopted in 30+ jurisdictions, >40% of global market cap | Evidence credits are **additional, permanent, managed for leakage**; certification scheme; project-level information |
| **ESRS E1-7** (EU CSRD) | Mandatory for large EU companies + relevant to non-EU with EU ops | Volume, standard, biogenic sink classification, location — all **traceable to point of purchase**; multi-year contracts as planned cancellations |
| **SBTN** — Science Based Targets for Nature | Freshwater / land / ocean / climate / biodiversity | Biodiversity credits by ecosystem and activity type; metrics attributable to SBTN categories; not aggregated across projects |
| **ISO 14068-1** — Carbon neutrality | International standard for CN claims | Type, vintage, certifying body, retirement record; alignment with ISO Core Principles |
| **GHG Protocol Corporate Standard** | Underpins most others | Baseline, additionality, leakage, permanence, double counting — all five tests |
| **CDP** — Carbon Disclosure Project | 23,000+ companies globally | Relevant to questions 3.1.1 (environmental risks), 3.5.4 (compliance strategy), 4.5.1 (monetary incentives), 5.10.1 (internal carbon price) |

## Notable data points

- **[EY 2025 Nature Action Barometer](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/09/How-Major-Corporations-Are-Changing-Their-Sustainability-Reporting-Practices):** 93% of companies mention nature in reporting, but **only 26% can evidence that impact**
- **Eight in ten companies** reporting on sustainability are now doing it across **more than one framework simultaneously**
- **KEY ESG 2026 ESG statistics guide**: 76% of executives cite **data quality** as their top ESG reporting challenge; **fewer than 30%** feel confident in the accuracy of their ESG data
- **MSCI Carbon Project Ratings**: just under **30% of nature-based projects** achieve a high-quality rating (BBB+ or higher)
- The **EU CSDDD** is cited as a major additional disclosure burden for EU-facing businesses

## Why it matters

Two useful takeaways for the wiki:

1. The **frameworks converge on similar data requirements** — additionality, permanence, leakage, verification, retirement, project-level metrics — which is why a single well-designed procurement process can serve most disclosure needs
2. The **93% mention / 26% evidence** gap from the EY Barometer is a specific number worth remembering — it captures how much corporate nature reporting is currently framing without substance, and why the SBTi V2 + TNFD + ISSB architecture matters

## Caveats

This is a **vendor marketing piece** — Earthly sells carbon and biodiversity credits and its "Keystone 3.0" pitch is embedded throughout. The framework mappings themselves are accurate and useful; the "how Earthly helps" sections should be read as commercial guidance, not neutral policy analysis. There are equivalent independent framework mappings from BSR, WBCSD, GRI and others that would be more appropriate for policy work.
