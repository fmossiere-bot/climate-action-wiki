# Core sustainability disclosure frameworks and nature investments: an Earthly overview

**Source:** Earthly blog, Giacomo Bartoleschi and Faith Sayo, 23 July 2026. https://earthly.org/blog/what-core-sustainability-disclosure-frameworks-require-from-your-nature-investments

## What it is

Companies increasingly put money into nature: carbon credits, biodiversity credits and nature-based projects such as restoring forests or wetlands. They then have to report on those investments under a growing number of rulebooks, known as **disclosure frameworks**. This overview maps eight of the main frameworks and sets out what each one asks companies to show.

It was written by Earthly, a company that sells nature-based credits, and it promotes its own Keystone 3.0 product throughout. The framework map is the useful part. The sales pitch is not why you would read it.

## Why it matters

The frameworks largely ask for the same things: proof that a project is additional (it would not have happened anyway), permanent, does not shift the damage somewhere else (leakage), is independently checked, and that credits are properly retired so they cannot be sold twice. They also want data at the level of each project. That overlap means one well-designed buying process can meet most reporting needs.

One figure shows the gap between talk and proof. According to the EY 2025 Nature Action Barometer, **93% of companies** mention nature in their reports, but only 26% can back up their impact with evidence. That gap is why stronger standards from SBTi V2, TNFD and the ISSB matter.

## Key facts

- [EY 2025 Nature Action Barometer](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/09/How-Major-Corporations-Are-Changing-Their-Sustainability-Reporting-Practices): 93% of companies mention nature in their reporting, but only 26% can show evidence of that impact.
- Eight in ten companies that report on sustainability now do so under more than one framework at the same time.
- KEY ESG's 2026 ESG statistics guide: 76% of executives say data quality is their biggest ESG reporting challenge, and fewer than 30% feel confident their ESG data is accurate.
- MSCI Carbon Project Ratings: just under 30% of nature-based projects achieve a high-quality rating (BBB+ or higher).
- The EU's CSDDD (Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive) is cited as a major extra reporting burden for businesses that operate in the EU.

## The eight frameworks and what they ask for

| Framework | Focus | What it requires for nature investments |
|---|---|---|
| **SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2** | Corporate net-zero targets | Disclose investments against **OER recognition tiers**; project-level impact and monitoring data; verified mitigation outcomes; annual progress reporting |
| **TNFD** | Nature-related financial disclosure | Report investment-level metrics (A21.0, A23.2, A24.0, A24.3, A24.4); core and additional impact metrics; structured, evidence-based project data |
| **IFRS S2** (climate-related disclosures) | Adopted in 30+ jurisdictions, >40% of global market cap | Evidence that credits are **additional, permanent and managed for leakage**; certification scheme; project-level information |
| **ESRS E1-7** (EU CSRD) | Mandatory for large EU companies, and relevant to non-EU companies with EU operations | Volume, standard, biogenic sink classification and location, all **traceable to point of purchase**; multi-year contracts reported as planned cancellations |
| **SBTN** (Science Based Targets for Nature) | Freshwater, land, ocean, climate, biodiversity | Biodiversity credits by ecosystem and activity type; metrics attributable to SBTN categories; not aggregated across projects |
| **ISO 14068-1** (carbon neutrality) | International standard for carbon neutrality claims | Type, vintage, certifying body, retirement record; alignment with ISO Core Principles |
| **GHG Protocol Corporate Standard** | Underpins most of the others | Baseline, additionality, leakage, permanence, double counting: all five tests |
| **CDP** (Carbon Disclosure Project) | 23,000+ companies worldwide | Relevant to questions 3.1.1 (environmental risks), 3.5.4 (compliance strategy), 4.5.1 (monetary incentives), 5.10.1 (internal carbon price) |

## Limits of the source

This is marketing material. Earthly sells carbon and biodiversity credits, and its Keystone 3.0 pitch runs through the whole piece. The framework mappings are accurate and useful, but the sections on how Earthly can help are commercial advice, not neutral policy analysis. Independent mappings from BSR, WBCSD, GRI and others are better suited to policy work.

The piece is also a useful starting point for future wiki pages on TNFD, IFRS S2, ESRS, SBTN and ISO 14068-1.
