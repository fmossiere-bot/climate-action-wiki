# Coffeeangel: Brewing a Better Future with Biodiversity

**Source:** Coffeeangel website. https://coffeeangel.com/pages/coffeeangel-brewing-a-better-future-with-biodiversity

## What it is

Coffeeangel is an Irish speciality coffee company. This page from its website explains a partnership with Dunsany Nature Reserve in Co. Meath and the cleantech firm Bionua. Together they run **Nature Capital Credits**, a way of paying for carbon storage and nature restoration on the reserve.

Coffeeangel has committed 5% of its profits over five years to the programme. The page also sets out the state of nature in Ireland, explains how the credits work, and describes how Bionua checks the results using satellite images, sensors on the ground and AI.

## Why it matters

Ireland's wildlife is in poor shape, and much of the decline has happened within living memory. Farmland birds have halved since the 1970s, and most protected habitats are struggling. Small businesses often want to help but do not know where to start.

This page shows one local route: a coffee company paying a nearby reserve to store carbon and restore habitat, with the results measured against international standards. Keep in mind that it is the company describing its own scheme, so the figures have not been checked independently here.

## Key facts

- Coffeeangel commits 5% of its profits for five years to Nature Capital Credits at Dunsany Nature Reserve.
- Dunsany has captured and stored **7,153,104.64 kg of CO₂** since 2024, about 7,153 tonnes.
- The minimum target for the first year is 80,000 kg (80 tonnes) of carbon removed and stored.
- Each Nature Capital Unit stands for 1 tonne of carbon removed plus a matching share of the biodiversity gain.
- The results are verified under three ISO standards: 14064-1, 14064-2 and 14064-3.
- Bionua monitors the site with multispectral satellite imagery, ground sensors and AI analysis.
- Dunsany Nature Reserve is 35 km from Dublin.

## Ireland's biodiversity, in numbers

The page sets the scheme against the wider picture in Ireland:

- 85% of protected habitats are in unfavourable condition.
- Farmland bird populations have fallen by 50% since the 1970s.
- 30% of semi-natural grasslands have been lost in the last decade.
- 1 in 5 assessed species is threatened.
- 1 in 3 bee species is threatened.
