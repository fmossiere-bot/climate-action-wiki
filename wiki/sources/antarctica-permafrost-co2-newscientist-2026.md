# Antarctica's permafrost is emitting worrying amounts of CO2

**Source:** New Scientist, Syris Valentine, 4 November 2026 (updated 5 November 2026)
**Study:** Ruggiero and colleagues, Global Biogeochemical Cycles, DOI: 10.1029/2026GB009134

## What is it

Scientists have worried for years about greenhouse gases escaping from thawing permafrost in the Arctic. A new study suggests something similar may be happening in Antarctica. Livio Ruggiero of Italy's National Research Council and his team measured carbon dioxide seeping out of frozen ground in the Taylor Valley, a freezing desert on the edge of the continent, in the southern summers of 2020, 2023 and 2025.

In 2020 the soil gave off on average about 1.7 grams of CO2 per square metre per day. By 2025 that had risen five-and-a-half times, to **9.5 grams per square metre per day**, which is about what Arctic soils release in summer. The team double-checked the readings in the lab. "The increase was amazing," Ruggiero says.

The source seems to differ from the Arctic. There, most of the gas comes from long-frozen plants rotting as they thaw. In Antarctica the CO2 appears to come from ancient underground stores, set moving as warming changes how water flows through the continent. One idea is that salty water from lakes under the ice, which has built up salt and CO2 over very long periods, is draining faster as glaciers melt. That brine may thaw the permafrost from below and let the gas escape. The team plans to test this on a future expedition.

## Why it matters

Scaled up to all of Antarctica's ice-free ground, the measurements suggest emissions of up to **76.8 million tonnes of CO2 a year**. That is close to Chile's total greenhouse gas emissions in 2024, 78.7 million tonnes. The authors stress this is a rough estimate, not a proper inventory. Even so, it points to a source of carbon that nobody has yet counted, and they argue it should be built into carbon budgets for the southern hemisphere.

Other scientists are interested but cautious. Peter Morse of the Geological Survey of Canada notes the leak does not look like it is driven by permafrost thaw, and says "what is driving the rise is unclear." Ida Kubiszewski of University College London, who led a 2024 paper on Antarctic tipping points that included permafrost, calls the study "an important piece of the puzzle" because the processes are still poorly understood.

## Key facts

- Three field campaigns in Taylor Valley, Antarctica: southern summers of 2020, 2023 and 2025, with hundreds of measurements.
- Average soil CO2 release rose from about 1.7 g per square metre per day in 2020 to 9.5 g in 2025, a five-and-a-half-fold rise in five years.
- 9.5 g per square metre per day is roughly what Arctic soils emit in summer.
- Scaled to all ice-free ground in Antarctica: up to 76.8 million tonnes of CO2 a year, comparable to Chile's 78.7 million tonnes in 2024.
- Unlike in the Arctic, the gas seems to come from ancient underground CO2 stores rather than rotting plant matter.
- The cause is still uncertain. The leading hypothesis is salty water from subglacial lakes thawing permafrost from below.

## Limits of the figures

The measurements come from one valley, and the continent-wide figure is an extrapolation the authors themselves call far from a rigorous inventory. Three campaigns over five years show a sharp rise but cannot yet tell a lasting trend from natural variation.
