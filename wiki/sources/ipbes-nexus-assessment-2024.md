# IPBES Nexus Assessment — Interlinkages among Biodiversity, Water, Food and Health (Summary for Policymakers, 2024)

## What it is

The **IPBES Nexus Assessment** is the first comprehensive UN scientific assessment to look explicitly at how five global crises interact: **biodiversity loss, water availability and quality, food insecurity, health risks, and climate change**. It was produced by 165 selected experts (plus 70 contributing authors) from every region of the world, drawing on **6,500 publications and Indigenous and local knowledge**, and approved at IPBES Plenary 11 in Windhoek, Namibia (10-16 December 2024). Co-chaired by **Pamela McElwee (USA)** and **Paula Harrison (UK)**, with David Obura (Kenya) as third co-chair. **Its central argument: treating biodiversity, water, food, health and climate in isolation is failing on all fronts, and only integrated ("nexus") governance can deliver the Sustainable Development Goals, the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and the Paris Agreement together.**

## Why it matters

The Nexus Assessment is the most authoritative statement to date that climate action, biodiversity protection, food security and public health are one problem, not five. It is the IPBES equivalent of an IPCC assessment for the biodiversity-nature-food-water world, and its findings shape how governments, funders and civil society think about "transformative change" over the coming decade.

## Past and current nexus interactions (Section A)

For 30-50 years, all assessed biodiversity indicators have declined between **2 and 6 per cent per decade**. Global economic, demographic and trade trends (10 of 12 indirect-driver indicators up since 2001) have intensified all direct drivers of biodiversity loss: **land- and sea-use change, unsustainable exploitation, climate change, pollution, and invasive alien species**. Freshwater biodiversity is being lost fastest, driven by unsustainable water withdrawal (agriculture alone is **~80% of humanity's water demand**), wetland degradation and forest loss. Coral reefs affect nearly 1 billion people within 100 km of them, and roughly one third of reef-building species are already at high extinction risk. Food production has risen but at the cost of biodiversity, water and climate: **42% of the global population could not afford a healthy diet in 2021**, and **~3 million deaths in 2017 were linked to low-whole-grain diets**.

## Future nexus interactions (Section B)

Scenarios that continue current trends produce large negative outcomes across all nexus elements. **Costs of addressing biodiversity loss would double if delayed by 10 years (from 2021 to 2030)**, and **delayed climate action adds at least $500 billion per year**. Single-sector scenarios (biodiversity-only, or food-only, or climate-only) fail because trade-offs between them are ignored. Positive futures require integrated action: sustainable healthy diets, integrated landscape and seascape management, coordinated biodiversity conservation across terrestrial, freshwater and marine realms, and pollution reduction across all three.

## Response options that address nexus interactions (Section C)

**Seventy-one response options** were assessed across 10 categories (conserve, restore, manage, consume sustainably, reduce pollution and waste, integrate planning, manage risk, ensure rights and equity, align financing, and other). Many are **low-cost**: agroecology, ecological intensification, health impact assessments, integrated multi-trophic aquaculture. Many response options **not primarily designed for biodiversity** (e.g. sustainable healthy diets, rights-based approaches, Indigenous food systems) turn out to deliver more biodiversity benefit than biodiversity-specific options alone. Implementing all 71 options would collectively support **all 17 SDGs, all 23 Kunming-Montreal GBF targets, and the Paris Agreement's long-term mitigation and adaptation goals** simultaneously.

## Governing the nexus, and the finance gap (Section D)

Existing governance is fragmented, siloed and short-term; it undermines the very frameworks it exists to serve. Current economic and financial systems **invest $7 trillion per year in activities that damage biodiversity and other nexus elements**. Meanwhile, **the biodiversity finance gap alone is $0.3-1 trillion per year**, and the additional investment needed to meet SDG water, food, health and climate targets is **at least $4 trillion per year**. Transformative change of governance requires: more integrative, inclusive, equitable, accountable and adaptive approaches; adoption of metrics beyond GDP; recognition of Indigenous and local rights-holders in decision-making; and access to finance for developing countries, addressing debt as a barrier to just transitions.

## Summary findings

- **$7 trillion/year** currently invested in activities that damage biodiversity and other nexus elements
- **$0.3-1 trillion/year** biodiversity finance gap; **$4 trillion/year** additional investment needed for related SDGs
- **42% of global population** could not afford a healthy diet in 2021 (86% in low-income countries, 70% in lower-middle-income)
- **80% of humanity's water demand** goes to food production
- Biodiversity indicators show **2-6% decline per decade** for 30-50 years across all assessed
- Costs of biodiversity action **double if delayed 10 years**; delayed climate action adds **≥$500bn/year**
- **71 response options** assessed; **all 17 SDGs, 23 GBF targets and Paris Agreement** achievable if implemented as a bundle
