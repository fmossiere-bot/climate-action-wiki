## What it is

Two reports on the same technology, written for different readers. "Beyond Lithium" is an explainer covering the chemistry, the trade-offs and an Irish angle. The "Executive Briefing" focuses on business and policy: CATL's projects, cost forecasts and where supply could get stuck.

Both reach the same conclusion. **Sodium-ion batteries** moved from the lab into commercial use in 2024-2026. They are expected to work alongside lithium-ion rather than replace it. Sodium would take over most large batteries that store power for the grid, plus cheaper city cars, while lithium keeps the market for long-range electric cars.

## Why it matters

If the reports are right, the energy transition now has a second battery chemistry made at commercial scale. Until 2023-2024, almost all battery coverage was about lithium. A second option means less dependence on one set of mines and one supply chain.

Sodium also avoids some of lithium's hardest problems. It is cheap and found almost everywhere, including in seawater. The best formulations use no cobalt or nickel, which takes much of the human cost of cobalt mining out of the picture. And these batteries cope well with cold, which suits places like Ireland and northern Europe.

## Key facts

All figures below come from the reports and have not been independently checked.

**Chemistry and trade-offs**

- Sodium ions are larger and heavier than lithium ions. The batteries use hard carbon for the negative side (instead of graphite) and iron-based Prussian blue or layered oxide materials for the positive side (instead of NMC, the nickel-manganese-cobalt mix in many car batteries).
- Both sides can use aluminium foil to carry current. Lithium batteries need more expensive copper on one side, so this is one of sodium's cost advantages.
- Each cell stores about **160-175 Wh/kg** (lab targets reach 200). That compares with 200-205 Wh/kg for LFP (lithium iron phosphate) and 255+ Wh/kg for NMC.
- An SUV running only on sodium-ion gets 250-350 km of range, against 400-600 km with lithium. This is the clearest limit for cars.

**Supply chain, ethics and cost**

- Sodium is more than 1,000× more common than lithium in the Earth's crust, and 60,000× more common in seawater.
- Raw material costs about $500 a tonne, compared with about $20,000 a tonne for battery-grade lithium carbonate, which has spiked as high as $70,000.
- The cleanest formulations use no cobalt or nickel. The reports note that about 75% of the world's cobalt comes from the Democratic Republic of Congo, where small-scale mining is linked to serious human rights abuses and child labour.
- Sodium avoids the brine evaporation used to extract lithium in South America's "Lithium Triangle", which uses a lot of water.
- 95%+ of the sodium-ion factory capacity planned for 2030 is in China, which leaves Europe and North America dependent on one country.

**Heat, cold and transport**

- The batteries keep about 90% of their rated capacity at -40°C and work safely up to 70°C.
- They can be fully drained to 0 Volts for shipping, which lithium batteries cannot. That removes the risk of fire at sea, a real advantage for island nations.
- Grid batteries need little or no active cooling or heating.

**Commercial projects**

- CATL unveiled its TENER Sodium BESS (battery energy storage system) in Munich in June 2026. Each block holds more than 30 MWh and runs at 65 dB. Energy used for cooling drops from 2% to 1%, and a voltage control system adds 2% to round-trip efficiency. It fits the same footprint as LFP systems. First deliveries in China are due in September 2026, and international ones from June 2027.
- CATL has invested about €1.2 billion over a decade in sodium-ion research and holds more than 1,600 patent families. It has new 40 GWh capacity at Fuding and a planned 160 GWh base in Jining.
- HyperStrong signed a 3-year, 60 GWh contract with CATL in April 2026, which the reports call "the largest sodium-ion order in history".
- BYD broke ground on a 30 GWh sodium battery plant in Xuzhou, China.
- Qingna Technology has a 20 GWh plant project in Sichuan.
- General Motors and Peak Energy (backed by GM Ventures) are building prototype sodium-ion cells for grid storage at the Wallace Battery Cell Innovation Center in Warren, Michigan, aimed at data centres and utilities.
- Global capacity for grid storage is projected to grow from 10 GWh in 2025 to 150 GWh by 2030, a 15× increase.
- On the Technology Readiness Level scale, which runs from early research to proven commercial use, sodium-ion moved from 3-4 in 2020 to 8 in 2024.
- The long-term target is a battery pack cost of about $40/kWh once supply chains mature.

**The US pullback**

- US-based Natron Energy has closed, and LG Energy Solution set up its sodium-ion pilot line in Nanjing, China, rather than at home. The reports see this as more evidence that production is centred on China.

## The Irish angle

The reports say that Eleven Energy's "Galvani" home battery, a sodium-ion system for houses, is sold through Midsummer Ireland. It is marketed for Irish garages and sheds because it still discharges well down to -20°C without needing to heat itself.

They also describe Ireland's TRIDENT project, backed by the Disruptive Technologies Innovation Fund and the International Energy Research Centre, which has worked on linking sodium-ion storage to inverters for small home-scale grids. Ireland's large grid batteries (Lumcloon, Shannonbridge, Cushaling) currently use LFP and NMC lithium chemistries.

## Voices in the reports

The executive briefing quotes mostly industry figures. William Wu, director of CATL's Energy Storage Technology Center, says: "CATL is committed to promoting energy independence around the world... We believe that sodium and lithium together will form the twin foundations of the future energy storage system." Amanda Xu, CATL's chief technology officer for energy storage, says: "The energy storage industry has moved beyond a race for scale. Today, success is increasingly defined by the ability to create long-term value... Readiness creates certainty."

Kurt Kelty, vice-president for battery, propulsion and sustainability at General Motors, puts it simply: "The future of batteries will be defined by matching the right chemistry to the right job." The International Energy Agency is quoted as saying: "Sodium-ion expertise and production capacity can act as a strategic hedge against the risk of lithium price spikes."

## Limits of the figures

These are AI-generated summaries, and the documents behind them are not named. No individual claim comes with a source or date, so numbers such as the 30 MWh blocks, the 60 GWh contract and the 15× growth forecast cannot be traced. The reports read like well-informed trade press, but they could not be checked step by step without more research.

The Irish material is thinner than it looks. The Galvani battery and the TRIDENT project are plausible but were not verified for this page. Anything used on Ireland pages should first be checked against Midsummer Ireland's website, the Disruptive Technologies Innovation Fund project list and International Energy Research Centre publications.

The framing also leans towards industry. CATL executives are quoted at length, the "twin foundations" and "right chemistry to the right job" lines come from company leaders, and no independent critics are quoted. Finally, there is no peer-reviewed data on energy density or lifespan. The figure of 90% capacity at -40°C is a widely repeated manufacturer claim that still needs independent testing.
