## What the reports cover

The two reports are complementary. "Beyond Lithium" is pitched as an educational overview (chemistry, trade-offs, Ireland angle); the "Executive Briefing" leads with commercial and policy framing (CATL deployments, cost projections, geopolitical bottlenecks). The core argument in both: **sodium-ion batteries have moved from lab to commercial deployment in 2024-2026, and will complement — not replace — lithium-ion by taking over most grid-scale stationary storage and entry-level urban EVs, while lithium-ion keeps the long-range EV market.**

## Key claims from the reports (verify before citing)

### Chemistry and physical trade-offs

- Sodium ions are **physically larger and heavier** than lithium ions, so they use **hard carbon anodes** (not graphite) and **iron-based Prussian blue or layered oxide cathodes** (not NMC)
- **Aluminium foil as current collector on both electrodes** (lithium needs more expensive copper on the anode) — one of the cost advantages
- **Cell-level energy density: ~160-175 Wh/kg** (lab targets 200), vs **LFP 200-205 Wh/kg** and **NMC 255+ Wh/kg**
- An SUV on pure sodium-ion gets **250-350 km range**, vs 400-600 km for lithium — the clearest limit for mobility use

### Supply chain / ethics / cost

- **Sodium is over 1,000× more abundant in Earth's crust than lithium, 60,000× more abundant in seawater**
- **Raw material cost ~$500/tonne vs ~$20,000/tonne** (and spike peaks of $70,000) for battery-grade lithium carbonate
- **Eliminates cobalt and nickel** in the most sustainable formulations — the reports highlight that ~75% of world cobalt comes from the DRC, where artisanal mining is linked to severe human rights abuses and child labour
- **Water advantage**: sidesteps lithium brine evaporation in South America's "Lithium Triangle"
- **95%+ of planned 2030 sodium-ion manufacturing capacity is in China** — a geographic concentration bottleneck for Europe and North America

### Thermal + transport

- **Retains ~90% of nominal capacity at -40°C**; operates safely to **70°C**
- Can be **fully discharged to 0 Volts for shipping** (lithium cannot) — eliminates thermal runaway risk during ocean transit, especially useful for island nations
- Reduced or eliminated active thermal management for stationary storage

### Commercial deployment (CATL and others)

- **CATL TENER Sodium BESS**, unveiled in Munich June 2026: >30 MWh per block modular architecture; 65 dB operation; auxiliary cooling cut from 2% to 1%; +2% round-trip efficiency from bi-DC voltage regulation; same enclosure footprint as LFP systems. First deliveries in China September 2026, international from June 2027
- **CATL investment:** ~€1.2 billion over a decade in Na-ion R&D, >1,600 patent families; new 40 GWh capacity at Fuding, planned 160 GWh base in Jining
- **HyperStrong signed a 3-year 60 GWh contract with CATL in April 2026** — the reports call this "the largest sodium-ion order in history"
- **BYD** broke ground on a **30 GWh sodium plant in Xuzhou, China**
- **Qingna Technology** 20 GWh plant project in Sichuan
- **General Motors + Peak Energy** (GM Ventures-backed) prototyping sodium-ion stationary cells at the Wallace Battery Cell Innovation Center in Warren, Michigan, for data centres and utilities
- **Global capacity growth projection: 10 GWh in 2025 → 150 GWh by 2030 (15×)** for stationary storage
- **TRL progression: 3-4 in 2020 → 8 in 2024**
- **Pack-cost target: ~$40/kWh** as supply chains mature

### Natron Energy and the US pullback

- **US-based Natron Energy has closed**; **LG Energy Solution set up its sodium-ion pilot in Nanjing (China)** rather than at home — flagged as evidence of the China-centric capacity picture

### Ireland-specific claims

- Ireland's **TRIDENT project**, supported by the **Disruptive Technologies Innovation Fund and the International Energy Research Centre**, has worked on integrating sodium-ion storage with inverters for domestic microgrids
- Reports note that Ireland's large grid-scale storage (Lumcloon, Shannonbridge, Cushaling) is currently LFP/NMC dominated

## Why the two together matter

The two reports complement each other without changing the picture. "Beyond Lithium" is the chemistry and user-angle explainer; the "Executive Briefing" is the commercial and policy case. Taken together they describe a technology that in 2024-2026 crossed from pilot into commercial mass production, with the specific role of **stationary grid storage and entry-level urban mobility**, not as a lithium replacement for premium long-range EVs.

The clearest claim to internalise, if the reports are right: **sodium-ion gives the energy transition a second battery chemistry at commercial scale** — which matters for supply-chain diversification, cold-climate deployment, and the ethical case against cobalt-heavy NMC lithium batteries. That is a genuine shift from the single-chemistry story that dominated battery coverage until 2023-2024.

## Credibility caveats

- **NotebookLM provenance.** The AI synthesises from source documents the user supplied, but those sources are not listed. Named company figures and dates are presented as fact but have not been verified against primary filings for this ingest.
- **No bibliography or dates on individual claims.** Deployment numbers (30 MWh blocks, 60 GWh contracts, 15× capacity growth) cite no primary source. The reports read like well-informed trade-press syntheses but could not be reproduced step-by-step without further research.
- **The Ireland-specific content is thinner than it reads.** "Eleven Energy's Galvani home battery" and the "TRIDENT project" are plausible but not verified in this ingest. **If anything from this source is used on Ireland-hub pages, cross-check with primary sources (Midsummer Ireland's website, the Disruptive Technologies Innovation Fund project list, International Energy Research Centre publications) before publishing a hard claim.**
