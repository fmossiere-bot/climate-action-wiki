# It's good for the planet to scrap a new petrol car and buy electric

**Source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 6 August 2026. https://www.newscientist.com/article/2583523-its-good-for-the-planet-to-scrap-a-new-petrol-car-and-buy-electric/

**Underlying research:** Elliott Campbell (UC Santa Cruz) and Roland Geyer (UC Santa Barbara).

## What it is

New modelling from the United States finds that, for the climate, it is better to scrap a working petrol car and replace it with an electric one than to keep driving the petrol car until it wears out. That holds even when the petrol car is **brand new**.

Most studies simply compare an electric car with a petrol one over their whole lives. This one asks a narrower question that many drivers face: when is the right time to switch?

## Why it matters

Many people feel that keeping an old car running is the greener choice, because building a new car has a carbon cost. The wiki's myth page [Keeping my old car running is greener than buying a new EV](../myths/ev-keep-old-car.md) already uses Carbon Brief figures for a 14-year-old petrol car to show that is usually not true. This study goes further. The same conclusion holds even for nearly new petrol cars, with two clearly named exceptions: a grid that runs heavily on coal, and very low mileage.

That does not mean anyone should rush to scrap a car they bought last year. It does mean there is no climate reason to hang on to a petrol car to the bitter end once an electric one is within reach.

## Key facts

- Over their whole lives, electric cars produce **almost 90% less** emissions than combustion cars (study cited: sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032122000867).
- Campbell and Geyer modelled scrapping a petrol car at different ages, from 1-2 years old to the end of its life. They accounted for the emissions from making the cars, the electricity mix on the grid, how efficient each vehicle is and how far it is driven.
- Their finding: "in the vast majority of cases, and even for a brand new gas vehicle, you would be far better off scrapping the gas vehicle and switching to electric". The savings from cleaner driving outweigh the extra emissions from building the new electric car.
- The exceptions are a grid that relies heavily on coal power, or a petrol car driven less than about 7,000 km a year.
- The authors do not argue that people should scrap brand-new cars. Their point is that there is no environmental benefit in keeping a fossil-fuel car running until the end of its life.
- They say the findings support subsidised scrappage schemes to speed up the switch to electric and cut transport emissions, the largest source of carbon emissions in the US.

## Voices in the piece

Two independent academics responded. Kenneth Gillingham of Yale called the study a "clever angle". Gregory Keoleian of the University of Michigan was more cautious. He pointed out that scrapping a car only 1-2 years old is an "extreme case". In real life, he noted, such cars would probably be sold on rather than crushed. That could have knock-on effects, such as cheaper second-hand petrol cars tempting people away from public transport, and he says this needs more research.
