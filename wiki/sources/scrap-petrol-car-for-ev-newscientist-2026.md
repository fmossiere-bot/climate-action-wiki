# It's good for the planet to scrap a new petrol car and buy electric

**Source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 6 August 2026 — https://www.newscientist.com/article/2583523-its-good-for-the-planet-to-scrap-a-new-petrol-car-and-buy-electric/
**Underlying research:** Elliott Campbell (UC Santa Cruz) and Roland Geyer (UC Santa Barbara)

## What it is

New US modelling finds that, on climate grounds, it is better to scrap a working petrol car — even a brand new one — and replace it with an EV, rather than waiting until the petrol car reaches the end of its life. This goes further than the standard "EVs are cleaner overall" finding: it specifically tests the scrappage-timing question.

## Key facts cited

- Whole-life emissions for an electric car are **almost 90% lower** than for a combustion vehicle (cited study: sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032122000867)
- Campbell and Geyer modelled scrapping a petrol car at different points in its life, from 1-2 years old to end-of-life, accounting for manufacturing emissions, grid electricity mix, vehicle efficiency and mileage
- Finding: **"in the vast majority of cases, and even for a brand new gas vehicle, you would be far better off scrapping the gas vehicle and switching to electric"** — the driving-emissions savings outweigh the extra manufacturing footprint of the new EV
- **Exceptions**: a grid very reliant on coal power, or a petrol car driven less than about **7,000 km/year**
- The paper is not an argument to immediately scrap brand-new cars personally — it is intended to show there is **no environmental benefit to keeping a fossil-fuel car running to the end of its life**
- Authors say the findings support **subsidised scrappage schemes** to accelerate EV uptake and cut transport emissions (the largest source of US carbon emissions)
- Independent academic reactions: Kenneth Gillingham (Yale) calls it a "clever angle"; Gregory Keoleian (Michigan) cautions it studies an "extreme case" (scrapping a 1-2 year old car), noting scrapped petrol cars will likely re-enter the second-hand market, with possible knock-on effects (e.g. cheaper second-hand petrol cars pulling people away from public transport) that need further research

## Why it matters

Directly strengthens and extends the existing wiki myth [Keeping my old car running is greener than buying a new EV](../myths/ev-keep-old-car.md), which already cites Carbon Brief numbers for a 14-year-old petrol car. This new study shows the conclusion holds even for near-new petrol cars, with named, sourced exceptions (coal grid, very low mileage).
