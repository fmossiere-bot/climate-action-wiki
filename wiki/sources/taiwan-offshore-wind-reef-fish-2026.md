# 86 new fish species found on Taiwan offshore wind turbines

**Source:** EcoPortal, Hugo Rojas, 17 July 2026. https://www.ecoportal.net/en/divers-went-check-offshore-wind-turbines/29373/

The article draws on a peer-reviewed study in *Frontiers in Marine Science* (2026): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1733177/full

## What it is

A summary of new research on the **Formosa Wind Farm** off Miaoli County, Taiwan. It has 69 turbines spread over about 90 km² of what used to be flat, sandy seabed.

Marine scientists dived on the same turbine foundations in 2017, 2018 and 2025, and found that the foundations had turned into living artificial reefs. In 2025 they recorded 86 species of reef fish within 50 metres of the foundations. None of these species had ever appeared in earlier fishery records for that spot.

## Why it matters

Offshore wind is growing fast, and the [FACTS about Wind Energy](../solutions/renewables/FACTS%20about%20Wind%20Energy.md) page covers the electricity side. This study adds a benefit for nature. On the right kind of seabed, turbine foundations act as free artificial reefs, with no separate marine reserve needed.

It sits alongside the [solar-on-peatland](../biodiversity-land/solar-on-peatland.md) finding: some renewable energy, built in the right place, can cut emissions and help wildlife at the same time.

The result is not universal, though. It applies to tall, hard structures placed on previously bare, soft seabed. Fixed-bottom offshore wind fits that pattern. Floating offshore wind, coastal solar and other near-shore energy projects would each need their own evidence.

## Key facts

- Formosa Wind Farm has 69 turbines over about 90 km² off Miaoli County, on what was flat, sandy seabed.
- In 2025, divers found 86 reef fish species within 50 metres of the foundations, none previously recorded in the area. The surveys were carried out in 2017, 2018 and 2025.
- The foundations work like a "cliff face": a vertical column of steel from seabed to surface, with welded joints and protective boulders at the base.
- Within months of construction, barnacles, sponges and corals attached to the steel. Shrimp, crabs, bristle worms and sea snails followed, and then larger predators such as groupers, snappers and lobsters moved in to feed and spawn.
- By 2025, the number and variety of species and the shape of the food chain at the foundations were not significantly different from established, purpose-built artificial reefs.
- A wider review in *Fish and Fisheries* found that hard vertical structures attract reef species and raise their numbers and variety locally, while the soft-seabed life further away stays largely unchanged.
- Because the foundations run from surface to seabed, they draw in fish from the surface, the middle of the water and the bottom at the same time.

## Limits of the evidence

Scientists still debate whether structures like these create new fish populations or simply gather existing fish in one place, known as the "attraction versus production" debate. In this case, the 86 species did not appear in local fishery data before the turbines were built, which suggests something more than fish just moving around. The article flags this debate openly rather than glossing over it.
