# 86 new fish species found on Taiwan offshore wind turbines

**Source:** EcoPortal, Hugo Rojas, 17 July 2026 — https://www.ecoportal.net/en/divers-went-check-offshore-wind-turbines/29373/
**Peer-reviewed study:** *Frontiers in Marine Science* (2026) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1733177/full

## What it is

A summary of new peer-reviewed research on the **Formosa Wind Farm off Miaoli County, Taiwan** — 69 turbines spread across roughly 90 km² of previously flat, sandy seabed. A team of marine scientists returned to the same turbine foundations year after year with SCUBA surveys (2017, 2018, 2025) and found that the foundations had become **living artificial reefs**. Their 2025 survey documented **86 species of reef-associated fish exclusively within 50 metres of the turbine foundations** — species that had **never been recorded in any previous fishery records for that location**.

## Key facts cited

- **Formosa Wind Farm**: 69 turbines, ~90 km² off Miaoli County, previously flat sandy seabed
- **86 reef-associated fish species** found within 50 metres of the foundations in 2025 — none previously recorded in the area
- **SCUBA surveys** conducted in **2017, 2018 and 2025**
- Foundations described as **"cliff face" habitat** — a vertical column of steel from seabed to surface, plus welded joints and protective boulders at the base
- **First arrivals** (within months of construction): barnacles, sponges, corals attached to steel
- Following invertebrates: shrimp, crabs, bristle worms, gastropods
- **Higher trophic levels** (groupers, snappers, lobsters) moved in later to feed and spawn
- By 2025, **species richness, diversity and trophic structure** at wind farm foundations were **not significantly different from established, purpose-built artificial reefs**
- **Broader research** (Fish and Fisheries review): hard vertical structures act as attractants for reef-associated species with **locally increased abundance and diversity**, while soft-bottom assemblages beyond the foundations remain largely unchanged
- **Full water column effect**: foundations extend from surface to seabed, drawing surface, middle-column and bottom-dwelling fish simultaneously

## Notable framing

- **Honest caveat**: scientists debate whether these structures create new fish populations or simply concentrate existing ones ("attraction vs production" debate)
- In this case, however, the 86 species were not recorded in local fishery data before construction, which suggests **more than pure redistribution**

## Why it matters

Offshore wind is scaling up fast (the [FACTS about Wind Energy](../solutions/renewables/DRAFT - FACTS about Wind Energy.md) page covers the electricity side). This paper adds a **biodiversity co-benefit** to the case: on the right seabed, wind turbine foundations behave as free artificial reefs, without needing a separate marine reserve programme. It also **complements the [solar-on-peatland](../biodiversity-land/solar-on-peatland.md) finding**: some renewable infrastructure, sited correctly, can deliver climate mitigation **and** biodiversity uplift at the same time.

The result is not universal. It applies to hard-vertical-structure sites in previously featureless soft-bottom habitat. Fixed-bottom offshore wind fits this pattern; floating offshore wind, coastal solar, and near-shore energy infrastructure would each need their own evidence.

## Tone and angle

Popular-science / EcoPortal narrative style with peer-reviewed data. The framing is optimistic but not naive — it flags the attraction-vs-production debate rather than glossing over it.
