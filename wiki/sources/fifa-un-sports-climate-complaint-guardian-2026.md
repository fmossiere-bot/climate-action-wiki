# Fifa accused of a climate "record of failure" over Aramco deal and tournament expansion

**Source:** The Guardian, Paul MacInnes, 17 November 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/football/2026/nov/17/fifa-accused-climate-record-failure-aramco-deal-tournament-expansion

## What is it

Five campaign and research groups have asked the United Nations to review FIFA's place in the **UN Sports for Climate Action Framework**. Members of this voluntary coalition promise to halve their greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 and to work with others on climate action. The framework allows a signatory to be removed if its "sincerity in participation or ability to participate is a cause for concern."

The complaint, sent to the UN climate body (UNFCCC) during COP31 in Turkey, argues that FIFA has "a record of failure" on both promises. The five groups are the New Weather Institute, New Weather Institute Sweden, Fossil Free Football, Scientists for Global Responsibility and FairSquare.

## Why it matters

FIFA runs the most watched sporting event in the world, and its choices send a signal far beyond football. The complaint tests whether voluntary climate pledges in sport mean anything when an organisation's decisions push its emissions up. The groups say they "fully support" the framework, but warn that its goals could be undermined if a member can claim signatory status while contradicting its commitments.

## Key facts

- FIFA's 2021 climate strategy commits it to halving emissions by 2030 and reaching net zero by 2040. The complaint says the strategy does not explain how these targets will be met.
- The men's World Cup grew from 64 matches in 2022 to 104 matches in 16 cities across three countries in 2026, which the complainants call "the most polluting World Cup ever".
- The 2030 World Cup is due to be held in six countries across three continents, possibly with 64 teams and even more matches, in the year FIFA's emissions are meant to have halved.
- FIFA has a multi-year deal with Saudi Aramco as a "major worldwide partner". The letter calls Aramco the world's largest oil company and its "largest historic corporate polluter".
- The complainants say Aramco's advertising will be "inescapable" for fans at the 2027 women's World Cup, and that Aramco is using FIFA to obstruct rather than further climate action.
- FIFA and the UNFCCC were approached for comment. Aramco declined to comment.
