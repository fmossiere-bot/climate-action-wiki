# A quantum computer mines cryptocurrency on a fraction of the power

**Source:** New Scientist, Karmela Padavic-Callaghan, 12 June 2026 (updated 24 June 2026)

## What it is

New Scientist reports on the first experiment in mining cryptocurrency with a quantum computer. A D-Wave Advantage2 machine was used to mine coins on **Quip**, an experimental blockchain set up by Colton Dillion at Postquant Labs.

"Mining" means solving a hard puzzle to add the next block of transactions to the chain. On Quip the puzzle is an optimisation problem, the kind of task found in scheduling or choosing an investment portfolio. These problems are hard for ordinary computers but well suited to quantum machines.

## Why it matters

Cryptocurrency mining burns a great deal of electricity, because ordinary computers race each other to solve the puzzles. In this experiment the quantum machine used about 100 times less power than a conventional computer doing the same job. If that held up at scale, it would change the energy cost of running a blockchain.

Not everyone is convinced yet. Olivier Ezratty of the Quantum Energy Initiative points out that quantum hardware is expensive to build and manufacture, which makes a simple cost comparison hard at large scale. Carlos Perez-Delgado of the University of Kent is more hopeful: "quantum technologies have a real opportunity to have a huge impact".

## Key facts

- Quip gets about 5 minutes a day on the D-Wave Advantage2. In that window it wins **92% of the blocks** it competes for.
- The Advantage2 uses about 12.5 watts, against about 1,334 watts for a conventional machine on the same task. That is roughly 100 times less power.
- A conventional computer able to beat the Advantage2 at this task would need about 300 times the power of the quantum machine.
- Quip is built to be quantum-safe. Many existing blockchains may need software updates to resist attack from quantum computers.
- The longer-term aim is to turn Quip into a worldwide network of quantum computers, opening up access to hardware that is still rare.
- Other firms working on quantum mining puzzles include BTQ Technologies and Quandela, which builds photonic quantum computers.
