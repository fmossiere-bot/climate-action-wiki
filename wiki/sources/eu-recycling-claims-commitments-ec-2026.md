# EU Commission: Coca-Cola, Nestlé, and Danone commit to change recycling claims

**Source:** European Commission press release IP/26/2238, 25 October 2026 — https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_2238

## What it is

The European Commission announces that **Coca-Cola, Nestlé Waters, and Danone** have committed, after a dialogue with the Commission and the **Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) network**, to change the recycling claims on their plastic bottle labels. **The core change: claims like *"I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic"* must be replaced with *"This bottle is 100% recycled. Excluding cap and label"* to make clear what the claim actually covers.** The dialogue was led by the Swedish Consumer Agency and the Hungarian Competition Authority. The original complaint came from the European Consumer Organisation (BEUC) in November 2023.

## Key commitments

The three companies have agreed to:

1. **Replace claims like "I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic"** with **"This bottle is 100% recycled. Excluding cap and label"** — to ensure factually clear and correct information on recyclability and composition
2. **Replace claims like "Recycle me again"** with **"Recycle me"** — so companies do not give the false impression that the bottle was already recycled before purchase and can be recycled into another bottle again
3. **Ensure the overall label and marketing impression is not misleading** regarding environmental benefits — specifically on combining recycling claims with other green claims or symbols
4. **Ensure digital marketing campaigns** on websites and social media **reflect these commitments** too

## Timeline and governance

- **November 2023**: BEUC alert against bottling companies in the EU
- **June 2025**: Dialogue with Coca-Cola concluded
- **February 2026**: Dialogue with Nestlé concluded
- **25 October 2026**: Commission press release confirms all three (plus Danone) have committed
- **Coca-Cola and Nestlé have already implemented some commitments**, particularly on digital campaigns
- **Enforcement next step**: national consumer protection authorities will monitor; may proceed with enforcement if companies fail to comply. Commission may engage with bottling industry associations to extend commitments across the Single Market

## Legal framework

- **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive** (2005/29): traders' commercial practices must not mislead consumers, including on environmental claims
- **Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition** (2024/825): strengthens consumer protection against greenwashing
- The CPC is a network of national authorities that enforce EU consumer protection laws

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **The "excluding cap and label" wording matters.** The current "I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic" claim is technically false — the cap and label are not usually from recycled plastic. The new wording lets consumers see that. This is a narrow but genuine step against greenwashing in a product category almost every EU shopper interacts with daily.
2. **"Recycle me again" vs "Recycle me" is a precision point about mass balance.** The "again" implied closed-loop recycling that doesn't exist: a bottle labelled "100% recycled" is almost always only partially recycled plastic (via mass-balance accounting), and even when fully recycled, the new bottle may not be recyclable back into another bottle. See the [plastic-mass-balance-greenwashing](../circularity-waste/plastic-mass-balance-greenwashing.md) wiki page.
3. **Voluntary dialogue delivered where the ESPR might take years.** The companies agreed to change labelling without being forced through court or new regulation. That is a specific win for the "voluntary commitment plus enforcement threat" governance model — though as the Cali Fund case shows, voluntary mechanisms also have a weak track record.

## Caveats

- **Enforcement depends on national authorities**. The press release is explicit that the Commission and CPC "may proceed" with enforcement if companies fail to comply. The track record of "may" in EU consumer-protection enforcement is uneven.
- **The commitments do not address the underlying mass-balance greenwashing problem** flagged by Guardian and IJ4EU investigation in January 2026 — that "100% recycled" plastic bottles are often only 5% actually recycled, with the rest being virgin naphtha allocated under mass-balance accounting. The new wording "excluding cap and label" is more honest about what the "100%" covers, but still relies on the underlying mass-balance methodology.
- **This covers three companies.** Coca-Cola, Nestlé Waters, and Danone are the biggest players, but the EU bottled water and soft drink market has many more. Industry-level extension is noted as a "next step" but not yet in place.
