# EU Commission: Coca-Cola, Nestlé and Danone commit to change recycling claims

**Source:** European Commission press release IP/26/2238, 25 October 2026. https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_2238

## What it is

The European Commission says that Coca-Cola, Nestlé Waters and Danone have agreed to change the recycling claims printed on their plastic bottles. The agreement came out of talks with the Commission and the **Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) network**, a group of national authorities that enforce EU consumer law. The Swedish Consumer Agency and the Hungarian Competition Authority led the talks.

The main change is in the wording. A label such as "I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic" will become "This bottle is 100% recycled. Excluding cap and label", so shoppers can see what the claim covers. The case started with a complaint from the European Consumer Organisation (BEUC) in November 2023.

## Why it matters

The old wording overstated things. The cap and label are not usually made from recycled plastic, so a bottle described as "100% recycled" was not fully recycled. The new label says so. It is a small step, but it concerns a product that almost every shopper in the EU picks up every day.

Dropping "again" from "Recycle me again" matters too. The word suggested the bottle had already been recycled once and would simply become another bottle. In practice, a bottle sold as "100% recycled" often contains only part recycled plastic, counted through an accounting method called mass balance. Even a fully recycled bottle may not be turned back into a bottle. The [plastic mass balance greenwashing](../circularity-waste/plastic-mass-balance-greenwashing.md) page explains how this works.

The companies changed their labels without a court case or a new law. That shows talks backed by the threat of enforcement can deliver results faster than new rules such as the ESPR (the EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation) might. Voluntary approaches do not always work, though. The Cali Fund, which asked drug companies to pay for using nature's genetic data, has raised almost nothing.

## Key facts

- The three companies covered are Coca-Cola, Nestlé Waters and Danone.
- "I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic" becomes **"This bottle is 100% recycled. Excluding cap and label"**.
- "Recycle me again" becomes "Recycle me".
- The talks were led by the Swedish Consumer Agency and the Hungarian Competition Authority, after a BEUC complaint in November 2023.
- Coca-Cola and Nestlé have already put some of the changes in place, especially in their digital campaigns.

## What the companies agreed

- Replace claims like "I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic" with "This bottle is 100% recycled. Excluding cap and label", so information on recyclability and what the bottle is made of is clear and correct.
- Replace claims like "Recycle me again" with "Recycle me", so they no longer suggest the bottle was already recycled before purchase and can be made into another bottle again.
- Make sure the label and marketing as a whole do not mislead people about environmental benefits, especially when recycling claims are combined with other green claims or symbols.
- Make sure their digital marketing on websites and social media follows the same rules.

## Timeline and next steps

- **November 2023**: BEUC raises the alarm about bottling companies in the EU.
- **June 2025**: talks with Coca-Cola are concluded.
- **February 2026**: talks with Nestlé are concluded.
- **25 October 2026**: the Commission confirms that all three, including Danone, have committed.

National consumer protection authorities will now check that the companies stick to their commitments, and may take enforcement action if they do not. The Commission may also talk to bottling industry associations about extending the same commitments across the Single Market.

## The legal background

The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29) says businesses must not mislead consumers, including about the environment. The Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (2024/825) adds stronger protection against greenwashing.

## Limits of the commitments

Enforcement depends on national authorities. The press release says the Commission and CPC "may proceed" with enforcement if companies fail to comply, and that kind of "may" has a mixed record in EU consumer protection.

The deal also leaves the deeper mass-balance problem in place. A Guardian and IJ4EU investigation in January 2026 found that "100% recycled" plastic bottles are often only 5% recycled in reality, with the rest made from virgin naphtha (a fossil fuel product) that is counted as recycled under mass-balance accounting. The new wording is clearer about what the "100%" covers, but it still rests on that method.

Finally, this covers three companies. They are the biggest players, but the EU market for bottled water and soft drinks has many more. Extending the commitments to the whole industry is described as a "next step" and is not yet in place.
