# Marginal oil and gas wells have an outsized methane impact

**Source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 5 November 2026 (updated 6 November 2026)
**Study:** Omara and colleagues, EarthArXiv preprint, DOI: 10.31223/X59J60

## What is it

Most of the methane leaking from US oil and gas comes from hundreds of thousands of small, low-producing wells scattered across the country, rather than from a handful of big sites. That is the finding of a study led by Mark Omara at the Environmental Defense Fund, a US non-profit, reported here by New Scientist.

The team used data from the methane-spotting satellite **MethaneSat** covering more than 80% of US onshore oil and gas production, from May 2024 to June 2025, and extrapolated it to the rest of the contiguous US. "Marginal" wells are those producing less than 15 barrels of oil equivalent a day. Together they supply about 5% of US oil and gas, yet they cause about 58% of the methane from well sites and 40% of the methane across the whole oil and gas supply chain.

The study is the largest of its kind. It fills a gap between broad global emission maps and satellites that spot single big leaks, which miss small, constant leaks like these.

## Why it matters

Methane is the second most important greenhouse gas to tackle after carbon dioxide, and it acts fast. If a small slice of production causes such a large share of the leaks, tightening rules on these wells, or closing some of them, could cut methane quickly without much effect on how much oil and gas the US produces. The study arrives as the Trump administration moves the other way: last month it relaxed methane rules for oil and gas wells, including lower standards for marginal wells.

Fixing it will not be simple. Daniel Jacob at Harvard University points out that the leaks come from countless pieces of equipment "littered around the landscape", rather than a few super-emitters. Robin Lamboll at Imperial College London calls global progress on methane "very disappointing", given that cutting leaks is "an extremely cheap, sometimes revenue-generating form of climate action".

## Key facts

- Marginal wells (under 15 barrels of oil equivalent a day) make up about **5% of US oil and gas production** but about 58% of well-site methane and 40% of methane across the supply chain.
- Per unit of fuel extracted, marginal wells are 10 times more polluting than high-producing wells.
- The industry target set at COP28 in Dubai in 2023 is a methane intensity (methane emitted per unit of gas sold) of 0.2% by 2030. The world is far off track, at about 2.9% today.
- In some regions, such as Turkmenistan and Venezuela, methane intensity can exceed 100%, according to new research cited in the article.
- The intensity of US marginal wells is estimated at 60%, which is 300 times the industry target.
- The satellite data covers more than 80% of US onshore oil and gas production, May 2024 to June 2025.

## Limits of the figures

The study is a preprint on EarthArXiv and had not been peer reviewed when the article ran. Figures for the parts of the US the satellite did not cover are extrapolated. Lamboll also notes that regulatory rollback and staff cuts in the US make trends harder to track.
