# Ireland's water quality showed no improvement in 2025

**Source:** RTÉ News, Pat McGrath, 17 June 2026. https://www.rte.ie/news/environment/2026/0617/1578808-water-quality-report/

## What it is

Each year Ireland's Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) publishes indicators on the health of the country's rivers, lakes, estuaries and coastal waters. RTÉ reports on the figures for 2025. The short version is that nothing got much better, and water quality is still not good enough in many places.

The main problem is too many **nutrients**, mostly phosphorus and nitrogen washing off farmland and coming out of wastewater. In water, these feed algae and weeds that use up oxygen and choke other life.

## Why it matters

Clean rivers and lakes matter for drinking water, for fish and wildlife, and for anyone who swims, fishes or simply walks by the water. Only just over half of Ireland's rivers and lakes are in good health, and more rivers got worse in 2025 than got better.

The EPA's answer is to look at each river or lake on its own and act on what is going wrong there. A catchment in Co Wexford shows this can work.

## Key facts

- There were no major improvements in water quality in 2025, and standards remain unsatisfactory in many areas.
- Excess nutrients (phosphorus and nitrogen from agriculture and wastewater) are the biggest challenge.
- **43% of rivers** have nitrate levels that are too high. The problem is worst in the east and south-east.
- Over 30% of lakes have too much phosphorus, especially in the border region.
- Just over half of Ireland's rivers and lakes are rated good or better for biological quality, a measure of the plants and animals living in them.
- More rivers dropped a quality class than moved up in 2025.
- The EPA is calling for targeted actions tailored to each body of water to speed up improvement.
- In the Ballyteigue Bannow catchment in Co Wexford, six of 16 rivers improved in 2025.
