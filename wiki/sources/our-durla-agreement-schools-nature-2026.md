# Our Dúlra Agreement: a "COP for schools"

**Sources:** Dublin Gazette, Aishling Conway, 13 November 2026, and South East Radio, Siobhan Murphy, 16 November 2026. The two articles cover the same launch; this page draws on both.

## What is it

**Our Dúlra Agreement** ("dúlra" is Irish for nature) is a pledge for the leaders of Ireland's education system. It is modelled on the commitments countries make at UN climate and biodiversity conferences (COPs). Signatories, such as Education and Training Boards, patron bodies, school trusts and Education Support Centres, commit to helping their schools turn tarmac and mown grass into places where wildlife can thrive.

Christopher O'Sullivan, Minister of State for Nature, Heritage and Biodiversity, launched it at Firhouse Educate Together Secondary School in Dublin 24 in November 2026, during the COP31 climate summit in Antalya and shortly after the COP17 biodiversity conference in Yerevan. The idea grew out of the Irish Schools Sustainability Network, a group of teachers volunteering their time, and is run in practice by the Biodiversity Working Group for Schools, which includes Green-Schools and the National Biodiversity Data Centre.

## Why it matters

A lot of nature work in Irish schools rests on one or two committed teachers. The agreement aims to make it the responsibility of the whole system, so that biodiversity becomes part of school life rather than a side project. It also links schools to Ireland's first national Nature Restoration Plan, required under the EU Nature Restoration Law, with the final plan due in September 2027.

There is a wellbeing case too. The articles cite a study of more than 2,500 schoolchildren in Spain, which found that those in greener schools made bigger gains in working memory and attention over a year, and a Danish study of almost one million people, which found that children who grew up with the least green space had up to a 55% higher risk of developing a mental health disorder later in life.

## Key facts

- The first signatories included 18 Education Support Centres from Donegal to West Cork, Global Action Plan Ireland, the Irish Forest Schools Association and the National Association of Principals and Deputy Principals.
- Newer signatories include Edmund Rice Schools Trust, Educate Together, Kilkenny and Carlow ETB, Galway Roscommon ETB, Le Chéile Schools Trust and Dublin Dún Laoghaire ETB.
- In autumn 2026, six Education Support Centres (Limerick, Dublin West, Cork, Donegal, Laois and Waterford) are running whole-school biodiversity training days. Each school sends a principal or deputy principal with a staff member, and leaves with the start of its own Dúlra plan mapped on its grounds.
- The agreement was drafted in June 2026 at Wildacres, a 17.5-acre nature reserve on the Redcross River in Co. Wicklow.
- The Citizens' Assembly on Biodiversity Loss recommended developing school grounds for nature education, and the Independent Advisory Committee on Nature Restoration has called for ecological literacy across primary and post-primary schools.
- Local coverage in Wexford presented it as a chance for schools there to get more support for outdoor learning and nature-friendly grounds.
