# Fed up with COP, a growing alliance of countries takes a fossil fuel phaseout into its own hands

**Source:** DW, Natalie Muller, 16 November 2026. https://www.dw.com/en/can-a-coalition-of-the-willing-create-momentum-for-a-fossil-fuel-phaseout/a-79926748

## What is it

A DW explainer, written during the second week of COP31 in Antalya, on the "coalition of the willing" that is trying to plan an exit from coal, oil and gas outside the UN talks. The group took shape after COP30 in Belém in 2025. More than 80 countries backed a fossil fuel roadmap there, but opposition from oil producers such as Russia and Saudi Arabia led to a final text that did not mention fossil fuels at all.

In response, 57 countries met in Santa Marta, Colombia, in April 2026, co-hosted by Colombia and the Netherlands. It was the first conference focused specifically on putting the **transition away from fossil fuels (TAFF)** into practice. The article asks what influence this coalition has at COP31.

## Why it matters

Countries agreed for the first time in 2023 to "transition away from fossil fuels", the main driver of global warming. Every summit since has failed to say how. Because UN decisions need consensus, a few producers can block progress. The coalition is a way for willing countries to move anyway, by sharing practical know-how on the hard questions: how to cut fossil fuel subsidies, how to support workers whose jobs depend on the industry, and how to pay for new infrastructure.

The pressure to act keeps growing. COP31 followed a series of deadly heatwaves in Europe and a glacier collapse in Nepal that killed more than 1,400 people. Yet most oil and gas companies, including QatarEnergy, Saudi Aramco and Russia's Gazprom, are still exploring or developing new reserves, according to the German group Urgewald.

## Key facts

- 57 countries attended the Santa Marta conference in April 2026, including Canada, Norway, Brazil, Nigeria, Mexico, Australia, Germany and climate-vulnerable states such as Vanuatu. A second conference will follow in Tuvalu in 2027.
- Germany, France and the Netherlands have published national plans to phase out fossil fuels since Santa Marta. The plans contain no new commitments but are seen as a positive first step.
- Finland presented its TAFF roadmap in the first week of COP31.
- Brazil published its promised global roadmap to transition away from fossil fuels: a nearly 200-page toolkit that envisages countries making a phased transition between 2028 and 2033.
- Fossil fuel subsidies amount to around $920 billion (about €830 billion) a year, according to OECD data, though the figure is falling.
- Renewables made up a third of the world's power generation for the first time in 2025.
- Türkiye is asking countries to back a goal of 35% of final energy use coming from electricity by 2035, up from around 20% today.

## What the coalition does

Analysts do not expect the group to change the COP31 negotiations directly. Chetna Hareesh Kumar, a climate policy analyst at the Berlin-based NewClimate Institute, calls it "more of a long-term game". It could gain members at future summits if it proves inclusive and shows a transition is feasible for very different kinds of countries.

Alexandra Scott, a senior climate diplomacy adviser at the Italian think tank ECCO, sees two roles. It keeps up political momentum by showing "that there is this huge group of countries who really want to accelerate this transition away from fossil fuels." It also builds a "cadre of knowledge" on drawing up national plans, answering the question "how on earth do we even start to transition our whole economy off fossil fuels?"

In Europe, Germany, France and the Netherlands have each published a national fossil fuel phaseout plan since the Santa Marta meeting, and Finland presented its roadmap at COP31. Kumar expects more countries to follow just before the Tuvalu summit.

## Brazil's roadmap and the electrification debate

Brazil's roadmap breaks the transition into what Ana Toni, CEO of COP30, called "smaller actionable solutions". It proposes "levers" tailored to each country's dependence on fossil fuels and its finances, such as cutting subsidies, expanding renewables and reducing methane emissions. Kumar describes it as "customizable", so every country can act whatever its situation.

The electrification goal promoted by the Turkish presidency has drawn criticism for not saying whether the extra electricity has to be clean. Kumar would like electrification tied to renewables and to a fossil fuel phaseout in the final COP31 text: "If something like that could make it to the final text, that would be a really good sign for momentum."

## Limits of the figures

The article was written before COP31 ended, so it does not report the final outcome. The national plans from Germany, France and the Netherlands add no new commitments, and the Brazilian roadmap is voluntary.
