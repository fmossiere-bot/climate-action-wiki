# Solar panels on rewetted peatland boost bird life in Germany (New Scientist, June 2026)

**Source:** New Scientist, Alec Luhn, 8 June 2026 (updated 23 June 2026)

**Peer-reviewed study:** Ecological Solutions and Evidence, Hanna Rae Martens et al. DOI: 10.1002/2688-8319.70259

**Institution:** University of Greifswald, Germany; Wattmanufactur solar company

## What it is

This New Scientist article reports on the first study of what happens to wildlife when solar panels are placed on **rewetted peatland**, meaning bogs that were drained and have since been flooded again. The site covers 30 hectares in northern Germany and is run by the solar company Wattmanufactur.

The solar park had a wider range of bird species than two nearby hay fields. The panels seem to work like shrubs and small trees, giving birds somewhere to perch.

## Why it matters

Peatlands are the largest carbon store on land, holding twice as much carbon as all the world's forests. When peat is drained, microbes break down the ancient carbon inside it and release CO2 for decades or even centuries.

Where the panels go makes all the difference. Some of the 165 solar farms on drained peatland in Germany release more greenhouse gases than their clean electricity saves. The climate benefit only comes when the peat is rewetted first. For farmers and landowners with worn-out peat soils, rewetted peatland with solar panels could offer a new use for the land, but at present the only income is from selling electricity, so more government support would be needed for the idea to grow.

## Key findings

- The solar park on rewetted peatland had more bird species than two nearby hay fields. These included wetland birds (white wagtail, reed bunting, grey heron), woodland birds (tree pipit, Eurasian tree sparrow, buzzard, kestrel) and the threatened meadow pipit.
- The hay fields had only grassland species.
- Peatlands are the largest carbon store on land, holding **twice as much carbon** as all forests.
- 95% of peatlands in Germany and 80% in the UK have been damaged by draining them for farming or for peat digging.
- Some of the 165 solar farms on drained peatland in Germany release more greenhouse gases than their clean electricity displaces.
- The UK has restored 2,500 km² of peatland, only one-tenth of what has been damaged.
- Since 2023 Germany has excluded solar farms on drained peat from guaranteed minimum electricity prices, but developers do not always have to say whether their site is on drained peat. Only five solar farms in Germany are on rewetted peatland.

## Voices in the piece

Catherine Waite, a researcher at Cambridge, sees promise but urges care about where the panels go: "Peatland PV could be a really good way to help regenerate heavily degraded agricultural peat, but that doesn't mean it would also be a good thing to put it on really healthy peatlands elsewhere".
