# US hyperscale data centres use as much electricity as a whole state

**Source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 16 November 2026
**Study:** Guidi and colleagues, *Nature Sustainability*, 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41893-026-01985-7

## What is it

The biggest data centres in the US now use about as much electricity in a year as one of the country's larger states. A team led by **Francesca Dominici** at Harvard University spent two years gathering information on 403 "hyperscale" data centres, the very large sites that run AI tools, cloud computing and large language models. Because most operators don't publish their energy use, the researchers had to estimate it from commercial data and assumptions about each site.

Their central estimate is that these sites used about 82 terawatt-hours (TWh) of electricity between May 2024 and April 2025. A terawatt-hour is a billion kilowatt-hours, so this is about 1.8% of all the electricity used in the US, more than states such as Missouri, Kentucky or New Jersey use in a year. Their upper estimate, 99 TWh, would put the sites level with Indiana, Michigan or Washington, and only five times below Texas, the state that used the most electricity in 2024.

## Why it matters

Where that electricity comes from decides the climate cost. The researchers estimate that about 54% of the power these sites drew came from fossil fuels, giving emissions of about **25.7 million tonnes of CO2 a year**, roughly what 67 gas power plants emit. New sites increasingly run on their own gas generators instead of the grid, which pushes that figure up.

For people living nearby, the costs are local as well: water for cooling, large areas of land, and pressure on power prices. Public opinion has turned. A recent US poll suggests 76% of Americans would oppose a new data centre in their community. In Utah, a plan for a site covering twice the area of Manhattan, which would almost double the state's peak electricity demand, has met fierce local opposition.

## Key facts

- 403 US hyperscale data centres were studied, covering May 2024 to April 2025.
- Central estimate: about 82 TWh a year, around 1.8% of US electricity use, more than 33 individual US states.
- Upper estimate: 99 TWh a year, on a par with Indiana and Michigan.
- About 54% of the electricity came from fossil fuels.
- Emissions: about 25.7 million tonnes of CO2 a year, equivalent to 67 gas power plants.
- 76% of Americans would oppose a new data centre near them, according to a recent poll.
- The findings match the International Energy Agency's estimates of data centre power use.

## Limits of the figures

Outside experts say the results are believable but hard to check. Alex de Vries-Gao, a data scientist at the Free University of Amsterdam, says they are "not particularly shocking" because they match the International Energy Agency's figures, but that the study relies on commercial data and assumptions that can't be verified: "You can't really validate the results." Yury Dvorkin at Johns Hopkins University calls the analysis "coarse", because it leaves out factors such as how grid constraints change the emissions of the power a site uses. Dominici agrees there are "a lot of assumptions baked in", and says that is exactly why operators should publish their own energy and environmental data.
