# Ariana copper and zinc mine: BankTrack's Dodgy Deal dossier

**Source:** BankTrack, undated project dossier (accessed July 2026). https://www.banktrack.org/project/ariana_copper_and_zinc_mine

## What it is

BankTrack, a group that tracks what banks finance, keeps a list of "Dodgy Deals". This dossier covers the **Ariana mine**, a planned underground copper and zinc mine in Marcapomacocha district, in the Junín region of the central Peruvian Andes.

The dossier pulls together the project's history, the risks to water and from earthquakes raised by Lima's water utility SEDAPAL, the court cases, and the banks whose money has kept the project moving. The mine now belongs to the Peruvian company Alpayana, which bought it from Southern Peaks in 2025.

## Why it matters

Ariana is a clear case of bank finance running into people's right to water. The project passed an environmental impact assessment, yet a constitutional court later found it threatened a fundamental right. That gap between "approved" and "safe" is worth understanding.

It also shows that European banks, here Santander, still lend to controversial mining projects far from home. And it shows how the label "critical minerals for the energy transition" can be used to justify a project with serious local costs. Copper and zinc are needed for clean energy, but that does not make every mine acceptable.

## Key facts

- The mine sits inside the **Marcapomacocha water system**, which supplies around 60% of Lima and Callao's water in the dry season, for over 10 million people.
- That system includes lagoons, canals, reservoirs, high-altitude wetlands (bofedales, or peat bogs) and the Cuevas–Milloc Trans-Andean Tunnel, built in the 1960s to carry water west across the Andes.
- SEDAPAL identified four ways the mine could harm the water supply: interrupted groundwater flows reducing how much water is collected, heavy metal pollution, vibration damage to the tunnel, and a possible collapse of the tailings dam (the store of mining waste), which sits just 100 metres from the tunnel.
- The environmental impact assessment was approved in 2016 and the mine was due to open in 2019. A constitutional appeal by Lima residents has blocked it ever since.
- In 2025 the Constitutional Chamber of Lima ruled that the project poses a certain and imminent threat to the right to drinking water. Instead of cancelling the assessment, it ordered a complementary one.
- Alpayana bought the project in 2025 for US$200 million, advised by LXG Capital, a sign that it plans to go ahead.
- Banco Santander Perú S.A. and Banco Santander S.A. gave Alpayana a **US$100 million** refinancing facility in August 2025.
- Peru has recorded 18 tailings dam failures since 1952, mostly set off by earthquakes, and the Ariana site is in an earthquake zone.
