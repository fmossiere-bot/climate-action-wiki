# Dublin's water supply and the Shannon pipeline (RTÉ, June 2026)

**Source:** RTÉ News, Juliette Gash, 24 June 2026. https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0624/1580174-shannon-dublin-water/

## What it is

An RTÉ News report on evidence that Uisce Éireann, Ireland's national water utility, gave to the Oireachtas Infrastructure Committee. The subject was the **Water Supply Project**, a plan to take water from the River Shannon and pipe it east to the Greater Dublin area.

## Why it matters

Uisce Éireann told the committee that water demand in Greater Dublin, along with parts of Wicklow, Meath and Kildare, is on a "knife edge". Without new supply, it says, the region cannot meet demand sustainably. For households and businesses, that means a higher risk of restrictions during dry spells.

The plan is not without critics. Some ask whether fixing leaks in Dublin's pipes should come first, before building a large new pipeline. Uisce Éireann's answer is that both can happen at the same time.

## Key facts

- The project would take 2% of the Shannon's annual flow from an existing reservoir at the Parteen Basin and pipe it east.
- It is described as able to supply up to **50% of Ireland's population**.
- The planning application went to An Coimisiún Pleanála in December 2025.
- 80% of landowners along the pipeline route have signed voluntary agreements with Uisce Éireann. Compulsory purchase order (CPO) powers are available for the rest.
- Leakage on the Dublin network has fallen from 37% to 29%, and the work continues.
- Uisce Éireann says cutting leaks and adding new supply can run in parallel, not one after the other.
- Water levels and leisure uses such as fishing and recreation on Lough Derg and the Parteen Basin are expected to be unaffected.
