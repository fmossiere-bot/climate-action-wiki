# Why restoring Ireland's temperate rainforests matters: Southern Star

**Source:** The Southern Star, Tony O'Shaughnessy, 16 November 2026. https://www.southernstar.ie/news/why-restoring-irelands-temperate-rainforests-matters-4381238

## What is it

A short piece in the West Cork weekly built around a video. A local grief and loss practitioner sits beside a tree covered in mosses, ferns, ivy and navelwort, and shows what Ireland's native **temperate rainforest** looks like. The article then points readers to a fundraising campaign by the charity Hometree.

Her main point is about scale. Rewilding a private garden or field is a good start, she says, but real recovery needs a wide initiative that works directly with farmers and landowners to restore whole landscapes.

## Why it matters

The piece links nature restoration with people's wellbeing. She describes thriving native woodland as a source of grounding and calm for people living with grief and loss. That makes the case for these forests in human terms, alongside the usual arguments about carbon and wildlife.

It also shows a local paper rallying support for a national project at a decisive moment, days before Hometree's fundraising deadline.

## Key facts

- Hometree's Wild Atlantic Rainforest Project works with landowners in West Cork and along the west coast to create several thousand acres of native woodland.
- The project's ten-year goal, as reported here, is 57,000 acres across the Atlantic seaboard. Hometree's own page gives 57,500 acres.
- Hometree had already secured core funding but had to match it with donations, aiming for €1 million from the public by 19 November 2026.
