# AFP launches a climate litigation desk

**Source:** Covering Climate Now, 12 November 2026, republishing an AFP press release. https://coveringclimatenow.org/from-us-story/afps-new-climate-litigation-desk-leads-the-way/

## What is it

Agence France-Presse (AFP), one of the world's three big international news agencies, announced at the COP31 climate summit a new team of journalists who will report only on environmental and climate law. It is called the **Future of the Planet Legal Desk**. It will cover court cases, new laws, corporate accountability and disinformation, with dedicated reporters in regions including Latin America, Asia and Africa. AFP says it is the first global media desk giving sustained, specialist coverage of environmental and climate law.

The desk is led by Ivan Couronne, who previously ran AFP's Future of the Planet department. Its first funders are Australia's Minderoo Foundation and the Climate Change Collaboration in the UK, a group of three charitable trusts (the Aurora Trust, JJ Charitable Trust and Mark Leonard Trust). It is the first big project of AFP's Philanthropic Partnerships Initiative, launched last year.

## Why it matters

More and more climate action is happening in courtrooms: cases against governments for weak climate plans, against companies for misleading claims, and against new fossil fuel projects. These cases are often slow, technical and poorly covered. AFP sends content in six languages to more than 4,000 newsrooms and governments, so its reporting can reach audiences that never read specialist legal news. Couronne said: "This is where a lot of the climate action is moving."

## Key facts

- AFP has bureaus in 210 locations and distributes news in French, English, Spanish, German, Portuguese and Arabic.
- AFP made climate and the environment core editorial priorities in 2019.
- Covering Climate Now cites AFP's earlier investigation into McKinsey's climate activities and its climate fact-checking work.
- The desk will also train journalists inside and outside AFP to report on environmental law.
- Minderoo says its support aims to bring out under-reported voices, especially from the Global South.

## A note on funding

The desk is paid for by philanthropic foundations. AFP describes its coverage as impartial and verified, and the arrangement is openly declared. Readers may still want to know who funds specialist journalism, as with any reporting.
