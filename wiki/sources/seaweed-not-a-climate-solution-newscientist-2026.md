# Why growing seaweed isn't a climate solution (Filbee-Dexter et al., PLOS Biology 2026)

**Source:** New Scientist, Alec Luhn, 8 September 2026.

## What it is

New Scientist's Alec Luhn reports on a study in *PLOS Biology* by Karen Filbee-Dexter of the University of Western Australia and colleagues. They argue that restoring and farming kelp will not be a major climate solution, and that the priority should move from growing more seaweed to protecting what is left.

The numbers explain why. About 60% of the world's kelp forests are already in decline, and they could eventually release more CO2 than they absorb. Global kelp loss emits about **14 million tonnes of CO2 a year**, while an ambitious restoration project draws down only about 50,000 tonnes a year.

## Why it matters

Seaweed farming has been sold as a way to remove hundreds of millions of tonnes of CO2, and it has attracted serious investment. Running Tide raised $70m and then shut down. This study finds that farms often emit more from their boats and plastic lines than they lock away, and that only a small share of the seaweed ever reaches the deep sea, where its carbon would stay out of the air.

It also flips the usual priority. Protecting existing kelp is far cheaper than restoring lost kelp, yet only 2% of kelp forests are protected. The same pattern holds for mangroves and peatlands: keeping what you have beats rebuilding what you lost.

None of this makes kelp worthless. Restoration still helps marine life and coastal fisheries, as the [Formosa](taiwan-offshore-wind-reef-fish-2026.md) and [Hywind](hywind-scotland-robot-reef-survey-2026.md) wind farms and [French sea-urchin control](../climate-adaptation/artificial-reefs-coastal-protection.md) show. The message is for governments and companies: stop treating seaweed as a serious way to offset CO2.

## Key facts

- Seaweeds absorb an estimated 3.7 billion tonnes of CO2 a year through photosynthesis, drawing carbon from seawater, which then pulls more from the air. Most of it is released again when the seaweed is eaten or rots near the coast. Only a small fraction sinks to the deep sea as "blue carbon", stored for thousands of years.
- Kelp forests cover about 640,000 km² worldwide, slightly more than the area of France.
- About 60% of kelp forests are in decline because of ocean heat. Filbee-Dexter's group concludes they could eventually emit more CO2 than they absorb. Kelp loss already emits an estimated 14 million tonnes of CO2 a year.
- In Norway, a project restored 70 hectares of kelp by poisoning the sea urchins that eat it, but this drew down only about 50,000 tonnes of CO2 a year.
- Preserving kelp is at least 8 times cheaper per hectare than restoring it, yet only 2% of kelp forests are in protected areas.
- US start-up Running Tide raised $70 million to grow kelp on wooden buoys designed to sink into the deep sea. It struggled to grow kelp in the open ocean and shut down in 2025.
- Seaweed farming now covers about 4,000 km² of ocean, about a third of the annual kelp loss. A study earlier in 2026 found that 9 out of 11 seaweed farms emitted more CO2 than they stored once boat fuel and plastic lines were counted. Kelp Blue and other aquaculture firms have promised to store and offset hundreds of millions of tonnes of CO2, which this study says is not credible.

## Voices in the piece

Karen Filbee-Dexter wants to change the question people ask: "Instead of asking how much carbon we can remove with seaweeds, we should be asking how much carbon we can stop from being released as these ecosystems are damaged by climate change." She is careful not to write seaweed off altogether: "Seaweed restoration and farming is unlikely to be a major climate change solution. But that doesn't mean it isn't worthwhile." Restoration still supports wildlife, and farming can make low-carbon alternatives to fossil-based products such as fertiliser.

Pamela Walsh of Queen's University Belfast disagrees. She argues that scientists do not yet know well enough how much carbon seaweed stores to dismiss it: "Blue carbon is part of the puzzle. It can make a contribution. The question is how that is determined and validated."
