# "The obsession with endless growth can only end in tears": your questions on extreme weather answered

**Source:** The Guardian, Ajit Niranjan and Jonathan Watts, 3 August 2026 — https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/aug/03/the-obsession-with-endless-growth-can-only-end-in-tears-your-questions-on-extreme-weather-this-summer-answered

## What it is

A Reddit-style reader Q&A with two Guardian environment writers, Ajit Niranjan (Europe heat/wildfires) and Jonathan Watts (Asia floods), covering wildfire and heatwave death tolls, climate attribution, degrowth economics, climate anxiety, and reasons for hope.

## Key facts cited

- Wildfires kill relatively few people directly (hundreds/year globally, vs thousands from floods, hundreds of thousands from heatwaves) — but **wildfire smoke is estimated to cause 1.5 million deaths a year globally**; the 2023 Canadian wildfire season alone was linked to roughly **80,000 smoke-related deaths, 20,000 of them in Europe**
- Rapid-attribution studies have found **40-70% of the death toll** from major recent European heatwaves attributable to human-caused warming
- Attribution is harder for floods/wildfires specifically, though rainfall behind some European floods has been shown to be up to 20% stronger/more likely due to climate change
- Diets: meat and dairy responsible for **12-20% of planet-heating emissions**
- Degrowth/economics: Jonathan Watts calls the "obsession with endless GDP growth on a finite planet" unsustainable, citing **Kate Raworth's Doughnut Economics**, **Bill McKibben's** *Economics as if the World Mattered*, and **Jason Hickel's** degrowth work; argues the world needs "not just economic degrowth but ecological regrowth" — a system that rewards restoring damaged ecosystems
- Critique of mainstream economics: Nobel laureate **William Nordhaus** is criticised (via commentator Genevieve Guenther) for assuming a low GDP hit from climate change while omitting full costs to labour productivity, infrastructure, insurance and non-coastal real estate; most economic models assume smooth linear warming despite scientists warning of tipping points
- Four reasons for hope cited by Watts: the energy transition (especially in China); the rise of the rights-for-nature movement; alternative ways of thinking from the Global South and Indigenous communities; the prospect of global population peaking before 2050
- On individual action: Niranjan argues people have more agency than they realise (diet, transport, consumption), while Watts stresses political/systemic change (fossil fuel taxes, wealth taxes, renewables investment) must be the priority and that oil companies have historically pushed the "individual carbon footprint" framing to deflect blame
