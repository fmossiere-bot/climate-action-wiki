# AI trial to reroute planes around climate-warming contrails

**Source:** BBC News, Shiona McCallum, 18 August 2026 — https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c62em5lpvnjo
**Companion source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 18 August 2026 — "Planes flying over the Atlantic will be re-routed to avoid contrails"

## What it is

A £5m UK trial, **Operation Blue Skies**, will test whether AI-generated forecasts can predict where warming contrails are likely to form over the North Atlantic, so air traffic control can steer planes around those areas. It is the world's first contrail-avoidance trial run across an entire airspace rather than airline-by-airline. Partners: Google, the UK government, the Met Office, NATS (UK air traffic control), and researchers at the University of Cambridge and Imperial College London.

## Key facts cited

- Focus area: **Shanwick Oceanic airspace**, eastern North Atlantic corridor — responsible for about **5% of global contrail warming**, concentrated in the November–February winter season
- Contrails form when hot jet exhaust meets cold high-altitude air, creating ice crystals; persistent contrails trap outgoing heat
- **Contrails are estimated to cause around a third of aviation's total climate warming impact**
- Trial runs across **two winter seasons, 2026-27 and 2027-28**, roughly 20-40 test days each, mostly at night when air traffic is lower
- ~10,000 flights expected to pass through the airspace during trial hours each year; hundreds will be re-routed for a statistically significant dataset
- Aircraft will typically be told to shift altitude by up to **2,000 feet (600 metres)** — the same kind of routine adjustment already used to dodge turbulence
- Estimated extra fuel burn: **~100kg of fuel per re-routed aircraft**, a CO2 penalty "of less than 1%" compared with the contrail warming avoided, which can run to "tonnes or even tens of tonnes" per flight
- Funding: £2.65m from the UK Department for Transport, £1.4m from Google (AI research, engineering, compute)
- Previous airline-level trials of Google's AI contrail forecasts achieved **50-70% reductions in contrail formation or warming**
- This is the first airspace-wide trial: instructions come from air traffic control and apply to all aircraft in the region, with no opt-out
- Results are intended for publication in academic literature

## Why it matters

Contrails are a **non-CO2** aviation warming mechanism that gets far less attention than fuel-burn emissions, despite being roughly as large a contributor to aviation's total climate impact. The existing wiki [FACTS about Air Travel](../sectors/transport/Facts%20about%20Air%20Travel.md) page already flagged AI-based contrail avoidance as a "solution" in general terms; this trial gives it a concrete, funded, dated real-world test with specific numbers.
