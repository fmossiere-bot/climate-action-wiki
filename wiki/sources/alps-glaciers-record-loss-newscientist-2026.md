# Glaciers in the Alps on track for record-breaking ice loss — Alec Luhn, New Scientist, August 2026

**Source:** New Scientist, Alec Luhn, 27 August 2026 (updated 3 September 2026) — "Glaciers in the Alps are on track for record-breaking ice loss"

## What it is

New Scientist field report by Alec Luhn, following **Andreas Bauder's ETH Zurich glaciology team on Switzerland's Rhône glacier** during the summer 2026 melt season. **The headline: 2026 is on track to beat 2022 as the worst year ever recorded for ice loss in the Alps, which would mean another 6% of all Alps glacier ice disappearing in a single year**. The Rhône glacier alone has lost **30 million tonnes of ice this year — enough to fill 12,000 Olympic swimming pools**, with the summer melt line reaching above 3,100 metres (previously rare at that altitude), aluminium measurement stakes nearly tipping over, and a subglacial river collapsing a hole dozens of metres across in the glacier's lower flank. The piece connects the field science to the Rhône river's downstream dependencies: hydropower, nuclear cooling, barge transport and the Côtes du Rhône wine region.

## Key facts

- **Rhône glacier: 30 million tonnes of ice lost in 2026** = 12,000 Olympic swimming pools
- **>9 metres of ice** have melted off the glacier's surface this year
- **Summer melt line reached above 3,100 metres** (Rhône glacier top is 3,600 m); historically rarely passed 2,600 m
- **Glacier loss day for the Swiss Alps came on 2 July 2026** — the point when summer melt exceeds winter snowfall. "It should be in late September," says Rainer Prinz (University of Innsbruck)
- **2026 could beat the 2022 record** (when dark Saharan dust accelerated melt). That would mean **another 6% of all Alps glacier ice gone in one year**
- **Under current policies, >97% of Alps glaciers will be gone by 2100**; only **5% of the Rhône glacier** will remain
- **At 2°C warming, surviving glaciers would almost double — including 269 in the Alps**
- **~1,000 Swiss glaciers (40% of the total)** have already vanished in the past 50 years; another ~30 could vanish this summer alone (Swiss media calls it a "massacre")
- **The Rhône glacier has retreated almost 2 km back into its valley** since featuring in Goldfinger (1964)
- Rhône river supports: **19 major hydroelectric power plants**, cooling for **4 nuclear plants**, up to **5 million tonnes of goods on barges per year**, and irrigation for Côtes du Rhône wine (Grenache, Viognier, Syrah) and much of France's fruit production
- **Birch glacier / Blatten collapse (May 2025):** 20 million tonnes of rock and ice buried the village; 300 residents evacuated 9 days before the collapse; one shepherd killed
- **Fibre-optic cable crevasse detection** (ETH study, July 2026): found that **8% of the volume of a glacier in Valais was empty crevasses** — scientists may be underestimating glacier instability
- **Geotextile cover cost estimate**: covering Switzerland's large glaciers would cost **£1.3 billion per year** and only slow the loss

## Scientists quoted

- **Andreas Bauder** (ETH Zurich): leading long-term monitoring of 100+ glaciers; drilled stakes nearly tipping over this year
- **Rainer Prinz** (University of Innsbruck, Austria): monitoring Austrian glaciers
- **Ilaria Santin** (ETH Zurich): subglacial river collapsing the Rhône's lower flank
- **Mauro Werder** (ETH Zurich): "it's just a matter of time... they will disappear"; "all other solutions are pretty desperate. We really should just reduce CO2 fast"
- **Matthias Huss** (ETH Zurich): "the situation is really treacherous because, with every day of the heatwave, we're using up the reserves of ice, the stocks that have been built up many decades or centuries ago. Ten or 20 years from now… drought will be even more problematic"
- **Thomas Hudson** (ETH Zurich): fibre-optic crevasse study author

## Specific tourist/economic consequences in 2026

- **The world's last year-round ski area has temporarily closed** (unsafe glacier conditions under the Matterhorn)
- **The famous ice tunnel passing through a Chamonix glacier has had to be closed**
- **Belvédère Hotel at Rhône glacier** (featured in Goldfinger, 1964) is abandoned
- **Rhône, Po, Rhine and Danube** rivers all shrunk to record lows this summer; **"peak water"** from Alps glaciers has already passed

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **A single year of record loss is now baked in on top of a long trajectory.** The 2°C-vs-current-policies divergence (269 Alps glaciers survive vs ~3% remaining) is the most consequential number in the piece. **Every year like 2026 moves the irreversibility clock faster.**
2. **The downstream dependencies on glacier melt are much broader than drinking water.** The Rhône river sustains nuclear cooling, hydroelectric generation, river freight and a major wine region. France's energy and food security depend partly on a glacier that will lose 95% of its mass this century on current policies.
3. **Science is catching up to the instability itself.** The fibre-optic crevasse study (8% of a Valais glacier is empty crevasses, hidden up to 25m down) suggests that mass-loss figures are the visible part of a wider instability problem — relevant for both drought planning (less water downstream) and disaster planning (more Blatten-type collapses).

## Caveats

- Alec Luhn's piece is field reporting, not peer-reviewed research. The specific numbers (30 Mt from Rhône, 6% of Alps ice, 97% gone by 2100) are attributed to Bauder, Prinz, Werder and Huss — all senior glaciologists at well-established institutions (ETH Zurich, Innsbruck). The 2°C → 269 Alps glaciers figure and the 5% Rhône remnant figure are cited from previous modelling studies linked by New Scientist.
- The "40% / 1,000 Swiss glaciers vanished in 50 years" figure comes from GLAMOS and matches the number already in the wiki's existing Glaciers in Retreat page.

## Tone and angle

Walking-on-the-glacier reportage — the piece opens with Alec Luhn climbing the Rhône glacier with Bauder's team, feeling the ice shift and crack under his feet, watching rocks tumble off the valley wall. Numbers are placed in the scene, not front-loaded. Scientists quoted are blunt ("they will disappear"; "all other solutions are pretty desperate"); the "we really should just reduce CO2 fast" line closes the piece. The Rhône-as-economic-artery section is where the piece makes the policy case without arguing it.
