# Glaciers in the Alps on track for record-breaking ice loss (Alec Luhn, New Scientist, August 2026)

**Source:** New Scientist, Alec Luhn, 27 August 2026 (updated 3 September 2026). "Glaciers in the Alps are on track for record-breaking ice loss"

## What it is

Alec Luhn spent part of the summer 2026 melt season on Switzerland's Rhône glacier with Andreas Bauder's glaciology team from ETH Zurich. His report finds that 2026 is on track to beat 2022 as the worst year on record for ice loss in the Alps. If it does, another **6% of all the ice in Alpine glaciers** will have gone in a single year.

The Rhône glacier alone has lost 30 million tonnes of ice this year, enough to fill 12,000 Olympic swimming pools. The summer melt line climbed above 3,100 metres, a height it rarely reached before. The aluminium stakes the team uses to measure melt were close to tipping over, and a river running under the ice had collapsed a hole dozens of metres across in the glacier's lower flank.

Luhn then follows the water downstream. The Rhône river feeds hydropower stations, cools nuclear plants, carries goods on barges and irrigates the Côtes du Rhône wine region.

## Why it matters

A record year like 2026 sits on top of a long decline that is already under way. The most important comparison in the piece is between two futures. Under current policies, only about 3% of Alpine glaciers would be left by 2100. If warming is held to 2°C, almost twice as many glaciers would survive worldwide, including 269 in the Alps. Each year like this one makes the loss harder to undo. Mountain glaciers are one of the 16 tipping elements in Tim Lenton's work on climate tipping points.

Glacier melt matters for far more than drinking water. The Rhône supports nuclear cooling, hydroelectric power, river freight and a major wine region, so part of France's energy and food supply depends on a glacier that, on current policies, will lose 95% of its mass this century. For the farmers, boat crews and plant operators along the river, that is a practical problem, not a distant one.

Scientists are also learning that glaciers may be less stable than they look. A fibre-optic study found that 8% of one glacier in Valais was empty crevasses, hidden up to 25m below the surface. That suggests the mass-loss figures show only part of the problem. It matters for drought planning, because less water will flow downstream, and for disaster planning, because collapses like the one at Blatten may become more common.

## Key facts

- The Rhône glacier lost **30 million tonnes of ice** in 2026, equal to 12,000 Olympic swimming pools.
- More than 9 metres of ice melted off the glacier's surface this year.
- The summer melt line rose above 3,100 metres. The top of the Rhône glacier is at 3,600 m, and in the past the melt line rarely went above 2,600 m.
- Glacier loss day for the Swiss Alps came on 2 July 2026. This is the point when summer melt overtakes the snow that fell in winter. "It should be in late September," says Rainer Prinz of the University of Innsbruck.
- 2026 could beat the 2022 record, a year when dark Saharan dust sped up melting. That would mean another 6% of all Alpine glacier ice gone in one year.
- Under current policies, more than 97% of Alpine glaciers will be gone by 2100, and only 5% of the Rhône glacier will remain. At 2°C of warming, the number of surviving glaciers would almost double, including 269 in the Alps.
- About 1,000 Swiss glaciers, 40% of the total, have vanished in the past 50 years. Around 30 more could disappear this summer alone, which Swiss media have called a "massacre".

## More from the report

- The Rhône glacier has retreated almost 2 km back up its valley since it appeared in the James Bond film Goldfinger (1964).
- The Rhône river supports 19 major hydroelectric power plants, cooling for 4 nuclear plants and up to 5 million tonnes of goods carried on barges each year. It also irrigates the Côtes du Rhône vineyards (Grenache, Viognier, Syrah) and much of France's fruit production.
- In May 2025 the Birch glacier above the village of Blatten collapsed. 20 million tonnes of rock and ice buried the village. The 300 residents had been evacuated 9 days earlier, but one shepherd was killed.
- An ETH study from July 2026 used fibre-optic cables to detect crevasses. It found that 8% of the volume of a glacier in Valais was empty space, so scientists may be underestimating how unstable glaciers are.
- Covering Switzerland's large glaciers with geotextile sheets would cost an estimated £1.3 billion a year, and would only slow the loss.

## Effects on tourism and the economy in 2026

- The world's last year-round ski area, under the Matterhorn, has closed for now because the glacier is unsafe.
- The well-known ice tunnel through a glacier at Chamonix has had to close.
- The Belvédère Hotel at the Rhône glacier, seen in Goldfinger (1964), has been abandoned.
- The Rhône, Po, Rhine and Danube all fell to record lows this summer. "Peak water", the point when meltwater from Alpine glaciers was at its highest, has already passed.

## Voices in the piece

Andreas Bauder of ETH Zurich leads long-term monitoring of more than 100 glaciers, and this year saw the stakes his team had drilled into the ice nearly toppling over. Rainer Prinz monitors Austrian glaciers at the University of Innsbruck. Ilaria Santin, also at ETH, described the river under the ice that is collapsing the Rhône glacier's lower flank, and Thomas Hudson of ETH wrote the fibre-optic crevasse study.

Mauro Werder of ETH Zurich is blunt about where this ends: "it's just a matter of time... they will disappear". On ideas such as covering glaciers, he says: "all other solutions are pretty desperate. We really should just reduce CO2 fast".

Matthias Huss, who also works at ETH, worries about the water that will be missing later: "the situation is really treacherous because, with every day of the heatwave, we're using up the reserves of ice, the stocks that have been built up many decades or centuries ago. Ten or 20 years from now… drought will be even more problematic".

## Limits of the figures

This is field reporting, not peer-reviewed research. The main numbers (30 Mt lost from the Rhône glacier, 6% of Alpine ice, 97% gone by 2100) come from Bauder, Prinz, Werder and Huss, all senior glaciologists at ETH Zurich and Innsbruck. The figures for 269 Alpine glaciers surviving at 2°C and 5% of the Rhône glacier remaining come from earlier modelling studies that New Scientist links to. The figure of 1,000 Swiss glaciers (40%) lost in 50 years comes from GLAMOS, the Swiss glacier monitoring network, and matches the [Glaciers in Retreat](../biodiversity-land/Glaciers%20in%20Retreat%20-%20%20Facts,%20Risks,%20and%20What%20Can%20Be%20Done.md) page.
