# Switching rail from diesel to electric

**Source:** The Climate Drive action library, explained by the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD). Undated, figures mostly from 2020 to 2022. https://www.theclimatedrive.org/es/action-library/switch-from-diesel-to-electric-in-rail-transportation

## What is it

A short guide for transport companies on replacing **diesel trains with electric ones**, powered through overhead lines, a third rail, or batteries and supercapacitors on board. Rail is already one of the cleanest ways to move people and goods, but diesel still makes up 54% of the energy the world's railways use, against 45% for electricity, according to the International Energy Agency.

The guide estimates that going electric cuts emissions by 50 to 60% compared with diesel, depending on how clean the local electricity is.

## Why it matters

Transport produces about 16.2% of global CO2 emissions, around 7.3 billion tonnes a year, and rail is about 2% of that. Rail's share is small. But the guide expects the rail market to keep growing fast, so how trains are powered will matter more over time. Electric trains also bring quieter, cleaner air to towns and cities along the line.

The catch is the power source. On a coal-heavy grid the gains shrink, so rail electrification works best alongside a cleaner grid.

## Key facts

- Building electric infrastructure (overhead lines or batteries) produces 10 to 20% more CO2 up front than diesel. In use, electric trains emit up to 65% less, and close to zero on a clean grid.
- Electric trains cost more than 40% less to run than diesel ones (2022 figures), while buying them costs 10 to 15% more.
- Because running costs are so much lower, the guide puts the abatement cost at below minus $200 per tonne of CO2: the switch saves money for every tonne avoided.
- Other benefits: faster acceleration, shorter journeys, less noise and more room for passengers. Trains last around 25 to 35 years.
- Battery trains are not suited to long distances, and adding overhead lines to old diesel routes can be costly, especially in tunnels and remote areas.
- The rail market was worth $281 billion in 2022 and is expected to reach $436 billion by 2030. China built 80% of the more than 5,000 km of new rail laid worldwide in the five years before the guide was written.
