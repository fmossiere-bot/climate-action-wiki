# COP17 adopts rules for a permanent Indigenous Peoples body

**Source:** COP17 Armenia (the conference's official account), LinkedIn post, November 2026. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/a-new-chapter-in-the-history-of-global-ugcPost-7523056869700059136-SdlW/

## What is it

A short post from the COP17 presidency announcing the close of the UN biodiversity conference in Yerevan. After negotiations ran over several times, countries adopted the **rules of procedure for the Subsidiary Body on Article 8(j)** of the Convention on Biological Diversity.

Article 8(j) is the part of the Convention that deals with Indigenous Peoples and local communities, and with respecting their traditional knowledge of nature. COP16 in Cali agreed to set up a permanent body for this; Yerevan agreed how it will actually work.

## Why it matters

Until now, Indigenous Peoples and local communities took part in the Convention mainly through temporary working groups. A permanent body with its own rules gives them a lasting seat in decision-making. That matters because much of the world's remaining nature is on land that these communities look after, and their conservation methods often work.

## Key facts

- The new body makes the participation of Indigenous Peoples and local communities permanent rather than ad hoc.
- According to the presidency, it gives traditional knowledge and community-led conservation a formal place in global biodiversity policy.
- It is also meant to make biodiversity funding easier for communities to reach directly.
- This is the conference organiser's own summary, so it presents the outcome in the best light. The post does not cover the open questions on money and harmful subsidies.
