# 'Severe' stress on oceans: UN World Ocean Assessment III (2026)

**Source:** The Guardian, Karen McVeigh, 8 June 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jun/08/un-world-ocean-assessment-severe-stress-sea-level-rise-doubles-pollution-fishing-climate

## What it is

A Guardian report on the UN's third **World Ocean Assessment**, a health check of the world's seas. Nearly 600 scientists from 86 countries contributed. They found the oceans are under "severe and accelerating" pressure. The report was published on World Ocean Day 2026 and covers the period 2021–2025.

## Why it matters

The speed of change stands out. Sea levels are now rising at twice the rate they were before 2015, from 2mm a year to 4.3mm a year by 2023. Sixteen per cent of all the extra heat the ocean has taken in since 1955 arrived after 2018 alone.

People feel this most on the coast. More than a third of the world's population lives within 100km of the sea, and 11% live on land less than 10m above sea level. For them, faster sea level rise means more flooding, more erosion and harder choices about where to live.

The report also finds that the way the ocean is managed is "fragmented", split between different sectors and regions. That makes it harder to tackle problems such as plastic, overfishing and warming together.

## Key facts

- The rate of sea level rise has **doubled**: from 2mm a year before 2015 to 4.3mm a year in 2023.
- 16% of all the heat the ocean has gained since 1955 was added after 2018.
- The oceans have absorbed 90% of the excess heat trapped in the atmosphere and 30% of the CO₂ from fossil fuels.
- 52.1 million tonnes of plastic enter the ocean every year, adding to an estimated 24.4 trillion microplastic particles.
- Microplastics affect more than 4,000 marine species.
- Only 27% of the ocean floor had been mapped by 2025.
- The High Seas Treaty came into force in 2026. It brings protections to the two-thirds of the ocean that lies outside any country's control.
- More than a third of the world's people live within 100km of a coast, and 11% live on land less than 10m above sea level.
