# What to know ahead of COP31: Kite Insights preview

**Source:** Kite Insights, *What to know ahead of UNFCCC COP31, 2026: a preview for sustainability practitioners*, November 2026 (6 pages). https://kiteinsights.com

## What it is

A short briefing by Kite Insights, a London-based consultancy that advises organisations on climate events, written for business and sustainability professionals heading to COP31. It covers the setting, the main themes, the daily programme and why companies might attend. Its view is that **no sweeping negotiation breakthroughs are expected, so the summit should be judged on delivery**: electrification, adaptation, ocean action and the coalitions forming around them.

## Why it matters

The briefing captures the mood going into Antalya. Energy prices are high after the Iran war disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, an exceptionally strong El Niño is building, and the US is outside the process. It also points out a tension worth keeping in mind: both host countries, Türkiye and Australia, are still pursuing new coal power projects while leading a summit about the energy transition.

## The setting: a split presidency and a tough backdrop

COP31 runs from 9 to 20 November 2026 at the Antalya EXPO Center, with around 100,000 participants expected. It is the first COP where the presidency and the negotiations are split between two countries. Türkiye's environment minister Murat Kurum is president and leads the voluntary Action Agenda. Australia's climate minister Chris Bowen presides over the negotiations, preparing draft texts and choosing co-facilitators. Samed Ağırbaş, president of Türkiye's Zero Waste Foundation, is the Climate High-Level Champion, the role that links governments with businesses and communities.

<!-- tag: us -->
The US announced its second withdrawal from the Paris Agreement in January and sent no official delegation to COP30. Its formal exit is due in early 2027. US contributions have typically made up about 22% of the UN climate secretariat's core budget, so funding is squeezed. Climate action by US states, cities and companies continues, coordinated by groups such as America Is All In.

## Energy, heat and money

Before the Iran war, about a fifth of the world's hydrocarbons passed through the Strait of Hormuz. The disruption has pushed up fuel and food prices, and COP31's leaders present electrification and renewables as protection against volatile prices. The El Niño is bringing drier, hotter weather to the western Pacific, and the World Food Programme warns it could push **49 million more people into acute hunger**.

<!-- tag: eu -->
Squeezed public budgets make finance and adaptation the hardest talks. The EU is the world's largest provider of climate finance, spending €33 billion in 2025, and feels other countries have not matched it. Private finance has not arrived at the scale promised.

## The Action Agenda and electrification

Three headline targets were announced in Bonn on 9 June: raise electricity's share of final energy to 35% by 2035 (known as "35 by 35"), halve the growth in global waste by 2035, and cut energy intensity in buildings by at least 25% by 2035. The Presidency's third letter, dated 24 August, sets out ten priority themes in total. The briefing notes that far fewer people are talking about the transition away from fossil fuels than about electrification, yet whether the extra electricity comes from renewables decides what it does for emissions. Today renewables supply about a third of the world's electricity.

## Oceans, coalitions and adaptation

Some 96% of the planet's major warm-water coral reefs are critically endangered, and most are on track to collapse within 50 years. A World Resources Institute analysis warns that the ocean risks remaining "a theme rather than a concrete outcome". Small groups of countries are already working outside the formal talks, such as the 19-country High-Level Panel for a Sustainable Ocean Economy and the 57 countries that met in Santa Marta in April on leaving fossil fuels. On adaptation, more than 80 developing countries have submitted national adaptation plans, but few have turned them into fundable projects. COP30 agreed to triple adaptation finance by 2035, and COP31 needs to set the path to get there.

## Summary findings

- Around 100,000 participants were expected at the Antalya EXPO Center, 9 to 20 November 2026.
- A UN report in October indicated current policies put the world on track for up to 2.8°C of warming.
- In 2025, a quarter of all new cars sold worldwide were electric (IEA), and the $3.5 trillion invested in renewables was double the amount invested in fossil fuels.
- Deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon fell 36% year-on-year between August 2025 and March 2026. President Lula's re-election gives his government four more years to pursue its pledge to end deforestation by 2030.
- The super El Niño could push 49 million more people into acute hunger, according to the World Food Programme.
- US contributions have made up about 22% of the UN climate secretariat's core budget.
- 96% of the world's major warm-water coral reefs are critically endangered.
