# An all-algae diet for farmed fish

**Source:** Anthropocene Magazine, Douglas Fox, 20 November 2026. https://www.anthropocenemagazine.org/2026/11/if-humans-eat-eco-friendly-diets-why-cant-our-seafood-do-the-same/

## What is it

Farmed salmon and trout look like a way to take pressure off the sea, but they are usually fed on wild-caught sardines, herring, anchovies or mackerel, ground into **fishmeal and fish oil**. A team at the University of California Santa Cruz has spent years developing feeds made from two kinds of marine microalgae, *Nannochloropsis* and *Schizochytrium*, instead.

In 2020 the team showed that tilapia grew normally on these algae. In a new study published on 16 October 2026 in the journal *Antioxidants*, they fed rainbow trout, a carnivorous fish, a diet made entirely of algae. After 84 days, the trout grew about as well as trout on standard fishmeal and fish oil feed.

## Why it matters

Roughly 100 million tonnes of farmed fish and crustaceans are harvested every year, and at least 20 million tonnes of wild-caught fish are turned into fishmeal and fish oil to feed them, mostly for salmon and trout. Even plant-eating farmed fish like tilapia have a hidden cost, because their corn and soy feed needs farmland that could grow food for people. A feed that does not rely on wild fish or extra cropland would loosen one of fish farming's biggest knots.

The study is a step, not a fix. It is one trial on one species, and fish farming also causes greenhouse gas emissions and nitrogen and phosphorus pollution that can create oxygen-starved "dead zones" in nearby waters.

## Key facts

- Trout on the 100% algae diet had similar levels of omega-3 fatty acids, the fats that make oily fish good for human health.
- Protein, essential amino acids and minerals such as iron, calcium, magnesium and zinc were also similar.
- The algae feed was as efficient as standard feed, both in kilograms of feed per kilogram of fish and in dollars of feed per kilogram of fish.
- Early cost analysis suggests the algae feed may be slightly cheaper to buy.
- The same algae are being used or studied for biofuels, farm animal feed and human food supplements.
