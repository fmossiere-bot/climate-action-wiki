# 6 Pressing Questions About Beef and Climate Change, Answered — WRI

**Source:** World Resources Institute (WRI), Richard Waite, Tim Searchinger, Janet Ranganathan and Jessica Zionts. Originally published 2019, updated March 2022.
**URL:** https://www.wri.org/insights/6-pressing-questions-about-beef-and-climate-change-answered

## What it is

A **World Resources Institute explainer** framed as six questions, drawing on WRI's *World Resources Report: Creating a Sustainable Food Future*. It walks a non-expert reader through the climate case for reducing beef consumption in high-consuming countries without demanding vegetarianism or vegan diets. It is dated, but many of the underlying numbers are still the standard references used by climate-and-food commentary.

## Key facts cited

- Total annual emissions from beef production (production + land-use change) were about **3 billion tonnes CO2-eq in 2010** — roughly India's total emissions and about **7% of global emissions** that year (FAO 2017 estimate; conservative because land-use change is only partly counted)
- **Global ruminant meat demand rose 25% between 2000 and 2019**; pastureland expansion was the leading direct driver of deforestation in the first two decades of the century
- Without action, **pastureland could expand by 400 million hectares (larger than India) between 2010 and 2050**
- Beef requires **~20× more land and emits ~20× more GHG per gram of edible protein than beans**
- If ruminant meat consumption in high-consuming countries fell to **~50 calories per day (about 1.5 burgers per person per week)**, it would nearly eliminate the need for further agricultural land expansion (even with 10 billion people by 2050) — that is roughly half the US average and 25% below the European average
- US per capita beef consumption has already **fallen by one-third since the 1970s**, but the decline needs to be **1.5× faster** across high-consuming regions to hit the 50-calorie target by 2050
- Efficiency wedge: Colombia silvopasture (integrating trees and grasses) can **quadruple the number of cows per acre** while cutting methane per pound; Kenya dairy trials cut methane per litre of milk by 8–60% via napier grass and high-protein Calliandra
- Feed additives such as **3-nitrooxypropanol (3-NOP)** and seaweed can reduce cattle burping
- At COP26 (2021), leaders pledged to cut methane 30% and end deforestation by 2030 — beef sits at the intersection of both

## Why it matters

This is the piece most climate journalists reach for when they need a clear, sourced framing of "how much does beef matter?" for a lay audience. Its 50 calories per day figure is one of the few concrete diet targets that reconciles feeding 10 billion people with keeping deforestation under control. It also **defuses the polarised argument** ("do we all have to go vegan?") by showing that even a moderate cut in high-consuming countries makes the maths work.

## Tone and angle

Explanatory and even-handed. Explicitly avoids the "you must stop eating beef" framing while making the resource-intensity case unambiguous. Aimed at readers who might otherwise dismiss the topic as a lifestyle debate.

## Caveats / date

Article was last updated in **March 2022** and reflects data up to about 2019–2021. Newer references (e.g. the FAO 2026 report already in the wiki) show that meat consumption trends have continued to rise, and Terra.do / other more recent framings put diet-change closer to the top of the personal-action ranking. Still valuable as the reference explainer for the beef-specific case.
