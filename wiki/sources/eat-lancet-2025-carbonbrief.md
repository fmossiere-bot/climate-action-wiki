# EAT-Lancet 2025 — three key takeaways on climate and diet change

**Source:** Carbon Brief Staff, 2 October 2025
**URL:** https://www.carbonbrief.org/eat-lancet-report-three-key-takeaways-on-climate-and-diet-change/
**Underlying report:** *The EAT-Lancet Commission on healthy, sustainable and just food systems* — Rockström et al., The Lancet Commissions, DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01201-2 — https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(25)01201-2/abstract

## What it is

A **Carbon Brief summary of the 2025 EAT-Lancet Commission report** on "healthy, sustainable and just food systems." The EAT-Lancet Commission is a group of experts in nutrition, climate, economics, health, social sciences and agriculture, first convened in 2019. The 2025 report **updates and extends the 2019 "planetary health diet"** with improved food-system modelling and, for the first time, an explicit social-justice dimension. Carbon Brief distils it into three takeaways: (1) a plant-rich diet has the best health and climate outcomes; (2) transforming food systems could substantially cut emissions and land use; (3) social justice must be a central goal.

## Key facts cited

- Food systems account for **~1/3 of human-driven greenhouse gas emissions**
- Even if fossil fuels were fully phased out, "food can on its own push us beyond the 1.5C limit" (Prof Johan Rockström)
- Shifting to the planetary health diet could **cut non-CO2 agricultural emissions (methane, N2O) by 15% by 2050**
- Widespread adoption would require a **two-thirds increase in fruit, vegetable and nut production** and permit a **one-third reduction in livestock meat production** vs 2020
- The diet supplies about **2,400 kcal/day** built around whole grains, plant oils, nuts and legumes; still allows **~1 glass of milk/day, 2 servings of meat and 2 eggs per week**
- Adoption could prevent **~15 million "avoidable" deaths per year**
- Food systems are the largest contributor to **five of the nine planetary boundaries** being breached
- Under a "business-as-usual" scenario (SSP2-7.0, 9.6bn people, 2C warming), agricultural production rises 37% and non-CO2 agricultural emissions rise 33% by 2050; land expands by an area the size of Mexico
- Under total dietary adherence, halved food loss and waste and +7–10% productivity: non-CO2 agri-emissions **fall 20%** vs 2020, and agricultural land use falls by **3.4m km² — an area the size of India**
- Combining the dietary shift with ambitious mitigation policies (carbon pricing, land-use regulation, bioenergy, afforestation, renewables): **non-CO2 agri-emissions fall 34%** vs 2020, and land savings roughly double
- The **richest 30% of the world's population contribute more than 70% of environmental pressures from food systems**
- More than half the world's population currently struggles to access healthy diets

## Why it matters

The EAT-Lancet 2019 planetary health diet became the reference framing for "what a climate-compatible healthy diet actually looks like." The 2025 update matters because it: (1) hardens the modelling with recent food-system data, (2) puts a **specific 15%/20%/34% number** on the mitigation potential of dietary change in high-consuming countries, (3) frames the problem as **distributional** — the wealthiest 30% cause 70%+ of food-system environmental pressure — rather than as a lifestyle question everyone must answer equally. Carbon Brief also flags the political dimension: the 2019 report faced a coordinated meat- and dairy-industry backlash orchestrated in part by PR firms, and Rockström signalled the commission is "ready to meet that assault" this time.

## Tone and angle

Explainer journalism. Carbon Brief takes a technical report and picks out the three most policy-relevant takeaways, quotes the commissioners directly, and includes the modelling caveats (e.g. that the diet is designed for health first, not sustainability). It does not shy from naming the disinformation campaign around the 2019 report.
