# Why A Record-Hot Ocean Is Supercharging The El Niño Effect

**Source:** Forbes contributor column, **Ingmar Rentzhog** (CEO and founder of We Don't Have Time), 9 July 2026
**URL:** https://www.forbes.com/sites/we-dont-have-time/2026/07/09/why-a-record-hot-ocean-is-supercharging-the-el-nio-effect/

## What it is

A long-form Forbes column making the case that **the ocean is the climate story most people are missing**. Rentzhog stitches together several parallel developments in summer 2026: a strengthening El Niño building over the hottest oceans ever recorded, marine heatwaves covering ~82% of the global ocean earlier in the year, the fourth global coral bleaching event, the near-shutdown then rescue of the US Ocean Observatories Initiative, the entry into force of the High Seas Treaty, and the trade-off between cleaning up ship sulphate aerosols (which cooled the ocean) and cutting methane fast enough to compensate.

## Key facts cited

- The ocean covers ~71% of the planet and has **absorbed more than 90% of the excess heat** from global warming
- **Ocean heat content rose for the ninth record year running in 2025** (Inside Climate News); Kevin Trenberth (NCAR): every second the sea takes in roughly **12 Hiroshima bombs of heat**
- **21 June 2026**: global sea surface temperature hit **21.0°C** — a record for the time of year — beating 2023 and 2024, in Copernicus Marine data
- **First half of 2026: marine heatwaves touched ~82% of the global ocean at some point**; the Mediterranean was worst hit, with strong-to-extreme heat over ~80%
- **June 2026 NOAA declaration:** El Niño is on; 63% probability of a very strong event (>2.0°C in the tropical Pacific) at the November–January peak — one of the biggest since records began in 1950
- **WMO July outlook:** wetter southern Europe and southwestern US; drier Australia, India, Horn of Africa, Central America, Caribbean; stronger central and eastern Pacific hurricanes; quieter Atlantic season
- **Nature Communications (2024):** deep-ocean warming alone could make extreme El Niños **40–80% more common**, and the effect lasts after we stop emitting
- Carlo Buontempo (Copernicus Climate Change Service) calls current conditions **"uncharted territory"** — no El Niño has ever formed on an ocean this hot
- **Coral:** NOAA's Coral Reef Watch declared the fourth global mass bleaching event, hitting ~84% of the world's reefs across all three oceans, 2023 to mid-2025; a Coral Reefs study found heat stress ran unbroken 2018–2025; IPCC: **70–90% of warm-water corals lost at 1.5°C, >99% at 2°C**; warming reached **1.37°C in 2025**
- Global warming currently on track for **~2.8°C this century** per UN 2025 Emissions Gap Report; 3–4°C+ if fossil fuel expansion continues
- **High Seas Treaty entered into force 17 January 2026** — first binding deal for the two-thirds of the ocean beyond national borders; 80+ signatories
- **US Ocean Observatories Initiative** (~900 deep-sea sensors, $386m network): NSF ordered shutdown in May 2026; reversed in June after outcry and rare bipartisan revolt in Congress
- **2020 shipping sulphur rule** cut ship fuel sulphur by ~80%; NASA saw reflective "ship tracks" over major shipping lanes drop sharply; some studies suggest the loss of those aerosols may have contributed to the acceleration of ocean warming behind the 2023 records
- **IPCC** estimates sulphate pollution has hidden roughly **0.4°C of warming**
- Clean energy now attracts almost **twice as much investment each year as fossil fuels** per IEA
- Cutting methane and other short-lived climate pollutants could avoid up to **~0.5°C of warming by mid-century**
- **OECD:** ocean supports >3% of world GDP and feeds ~3 billion people
- **High Level Panel for a Sustainable Ocean Economy:** current path could cost **>$8 trillion by 2050**; WRI-associated finding — every **$1 in healthy-ocean projects returns at least $5**

## Notable quotes

- **Kevin Trenberth (NCAR):** ocean heat is "the clearest single sign the planet is warming: unlike the air, it barely wobbles. It just keeps rising."
- **Carlo Buontempo (Copernicus C3S):** the current situation is "uncharted territory."
- **Helen Findlay (Plymouth Marine Laboratory)** on the near-shutdown of the US ocean sensor network: we would be left to "navigate an increasingly volatile ocean with diminishing visibility."
