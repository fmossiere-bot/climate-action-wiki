# Business and policy leaders join forces for nature: Trinity College Dublin

**Source:** Trinity College Dublin, news release, 16 November 2026. https://www.tcd.ie/news_events/articles/2026/business--nature-event/

## What is it

On 16 November 2026, more than 150 business, finance and policy leaders met at Trinity College Dublin for a one-day event called **Business + Nature: The Imperative for Action**. It was hosted by the new Trinity Business + Nature Hub, a joint project of Trinity Business School and the School of Natural Sciences, and was an associated event of Ireland's Presidency of the Council of the EU.

The idea running through the day was that nature is **critical infrastructure**: the base that businesses and societies stand on, not a nice extra. Speakers included former President of Ireland Mary Robinson (by video), Trinity's Provost Linda Doyle, IBEC chief executive Danny McCoy, and two authors of the international IPBES report on business and biodiversity, Matt Jones of UNEP's World Conservation Monitoring Centre and IPBES chair David Obura.

## Why it matters

Many companies now have a climate plan. Very few have one for nature, even though their supply chains depend on pollinators, healthy soil, clean water and working floodplains. Professor Jane Stout pointed out that pollinator loss, poor soil and climate change are already cutting crop yields and disrupting supply chains, and that destroyed floodplains mean flooded properties and lost working days. The event gave Irish firms a practical starting point: a self-assessment tool, built by Trinity researchers, to help a business see where it affects and depends on nature.

Danny McCoy, who leads Ireland's main business lobby, said businesses both depend on and affect nature, and that seeing the scale of that dependence is "an emerging awareness for modern business".

## Key facts

- According to Professor Martha O'Hagan of Trinity Business School, **55% of global GDP**, or $58 trillion, depends on nature, which makes nature loss one of the biggest risks facing the world economy.
- The IPBES business and biodiversity report found that only 2% of the biggest businesses have nature transition plans, against 63% that have climate plans.
- More than 150 leaders took part, from the energy, food, insurance, finance and packaging sectors among others.
- The self-assessment tool was demonstrated by business leaders from those sectors.
- The hub brings together Jane Stout (Vice President for Biodiversity and Climate Action), Siobhan McQuaid, Martha O'Hagan and Catherine Farrell.
- The event was supported by the AIB Trinity Climate Hub and GoNaturePositive!
