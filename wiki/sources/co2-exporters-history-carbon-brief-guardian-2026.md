# Russia, Saudi Arabia and Australia named as biggest CO2 exporters in history

**Source:** The Guardian, Fiona Harvey and Simon Evans, 17 November 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/nov/17/russia-saudi-arabia-and-australia-named-as-biggest-co2-exporters-in-history

## What is it

Most league tables of climate responsibility count emissions where the fuel is burned. A new **Carbon Brief** analysis, reported by the Guardian during COP31, counts them where the fuel was dug up instead. It adds up the CO2 released by burning every country's coal, oil and gas exports since 1850.

On that basis, Russia is the biggest exporter of CO2 in history, responsible for more than 70 gigatonnes (billion tonnes) up to the end of 2025. Saudi Arabia comes second with about 60 gigatonnes. Australia is a distant third with more than 25 gigatonnes, just ahead of Venezuela.

## Why it matters

Exporting countries can look modest in normal emissions rankings, because the fuel they sell is burned somewhere else. This analysis shows how much of the world's warming flows from a handful of producers, and how much money they made from it. Louise Hutchins, convener of the Make Polluters Pay International Coalition, said Australia's "contribution to global heating more than doubles when fossil fuel exports are counted."

The timing made it pointed. Australia was leading the negotiations at COP31 in Turkey, while fossil fuels made up about two-fifths of its exports and it had set no end date for producing or exporting them. Saudi Arabia and Russia, the top two, were among the countries resisting a strong outcome.

## Key facts

- Exported CO2 since 1850: Russia more than 70 gigatonnes, Saudi Arabia about 60, Australia more than 25.
- The US and China, the two biggest emitters today, still carry more historical responsibility than any of the three, because they burned their own fuel as well as importing large amounts.
- On the Monday of COP31's second week, Australia's first draft outcome called for "the immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems". Many observers expected the wording to be weakened in later drafts.
- Saudi Arabia, Russia and allies were opposing even having a cover decision text.
- Climate Analytics found Australia appeared to have no plans to curb its own extraction, with coalmines still being approved that would run until 2088, decades after the world is meant to reach net zero around mid-century.
- Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said the country accounts for about 1% of historical global CO2 emissions within its borders, and that the countries burning the oil decide how it is used.

## Two ways of sharing the blame

The Saudi response sets out the counter-argument: buyers choose how to use the fuel, so blaming the seller is "misleading". Both views hold part of the truth. Consumption-based accounting puts the weight on the countries that burn fuel; extraction-based accounting puts it on those that profit from selling it. Neither replaces the other. The extraction view matters most in debates about who should pay for climate damage and who should stop opening new mines and wells. Bill Hare, chief executive of Climate Analytics, called it "a deep contradiction" to champion climate ambition abroad while approving coal and gas projects that lock in production for decades.
