# Europe's most forested countries (Visual Capitalist, 2026)

**Source:** Visual Capitalist, Srijaa Chatterjee, 10 June 2026. https://www.visualcapitalist.com/cp/europes-most-forested-countries/

## What it is

A ranking of European countries by how much of their land is covered in forest. It uses World Bank data and the State of Europe's Forests 2025 report.

**Finland** comes top, with about 73% of its land under forest. Sweden is close behind, and Montenegro and Slovenia are also near the top. At the other end, Iceland, Ireland and the UK have some of the lowest forest shares in Europe.

## Why it matters

Forests store carbon, shelter wildlife and protect soil and water, so how much forest a country has says a lot about its natural defences. For Ireland in particular, sitting near the bottom of the table is a reminder of how much ground there is to make up.

There is some good news. Europe has generally grown its forest area over the last three decades, which most other regions of the world have not. But a warmer climate and more extreme weather are putting those forests under strain, so keeping them healthy is becoming as important as planting more.

## Key facts

- Finland leads Europe at about 73% forest cover. Sweden is close behind, with Montenegro and Slovenia also in the top group.
- Iceland, Ireland and the UK have some of the lowest forest shares in Europe.
- The northern lead comes from the boreal forest belt, colder climates, fewer people per square kilometre and a history of limited farming.
- Countries such as Denmark and the Netherlands have little forest because their fertile lowlands are given over to farming.
- Europe has generally expanded its forest area over the last three decades, unlike most other regions.
- The State of Europe's Forests 2025 report finds forest area and biodiversity indicators moving upwards, but growth is slowing in some areas.
- The World Resources Institute (WRI) points to logging, natural disturbances such as storms and pests, and climate impacts as growing causes of forest loss. The European Environment Agency (EEA) says forest resilience is becoming a major policy issue as temperatures rise and extreme weather becomes more frequent.
