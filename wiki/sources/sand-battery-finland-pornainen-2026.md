# World's largest commercial sand battery online — Pornainen, Finland

**Source:** OilPrice.com, Alex Kimani, 29 July 2026 — https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/The-Worlds-Largest-Sand-Battery-Is-Now-Online.html
**Company:** Polar Night Energy — https://polarnightenergy.com

## What it is

Announcement that **Finnish startup Polar Night Energy has commissioned the world's largest commercial sand battery** in Pornainen, southern Finland. Built for local district heating company **Loviisan Lämpö**, the **13-metre-tall silo** contains **2,000 tonnes of crushed soapstone** — a byproduct of local fireplace manufacturing — and stores **100 MWh of renewable energy as heat**. The system charges from cheap or negatively priced grid electricity when wind and solar are abundant, and discharges via hot water into the town's district heating network to warm homes, schools and public buildings. **Enough for the 5,000 residents of Pornainen for one week in winter, or nearly one month in summer.**

## Key facts

- **100 MWh storage capacity**
- **13 metres tall**, contains **2,000 tonnes of crushed soapstone**
- Storage temperature: **400-600°C**
- Charges via **resistive heating** (like an industrial-scale hair dryer) using surplus grid electricity
- Discharge: cool air blown through pipes → hot water at **up to 400°C** → district heating network
- Serves **5,000 residents** of Pornainen — 1 week of heating in winter, or ~1 month in summer
- **Fully automated by AI**, charging when spot-market electricity prices are lowest (often near zero or negative) and discharging at peak
- Estimated **~70% cut** in local greenhouse gas emissions
- Estimated **~60% reduction** in wood chip use (older wood-chip plant kept for peak loads / backup)
- **No degradation** over time (unlike lithium-ion)
- **No rare-earth materials** required
- Soapstone from local fireplace manufacturing = readily available byproduct

## Track record and next steps

- **2022:** Polar Night Energy's first pilot in Kankaanpää — 0.1 MW / 8 MWh
- **2026:** Pornainen system online — 100 MWh
- **2027 (summer):** planned **250 MWh system in Vääksy** — will use natural sand (not soapstone) and provide grid-balancing ancillary services alongside district heating

## Notable quote

**Tommi Eronen** (Polar Night Energy CEO): *"We're changing from a world where big power plants were doing the energy production to where solar and wind are producing the energy, then we need a massive amount of storage. Hopefully a big portion of those storages could be sand battery-type of storages where we don't need rare earth materials, like in many chemical batteries, such as lithium-ion batteries."*

## Why it matters

Long-duration seasonal storage is the hardest part of a renewables-dominated grid. Lithium-ion batteries are excellent for minutes-to-hours storage but economically hopeless for weeks or months (see [intermittency-storage-problem](../myths/intermittency-storage-problem.md)). Sand batteries are one of the few technologies actually running commercially that can bridge weeks-to-months at reasonable cost.

Three specific things stand out:

1. **The materials are cheap and non-strategic.** Soapstone and sand — no lithium, cobalt or rare earths. Removes one of the constraints on scaling long-duration storage.
2. **The output is heat.** Not electricity. This makes it best-suited to **district heating** networks (common in Nordic countries, Germany, parts of eastern Europe) and to **industrial heat** users (cement, steel, chemicals). It doesn't replace lithium-ion for electricity storage; it complements it by taking heat load off the electrical grid entirely.
3. **The economics work.** The AI-driven charging strategy (charging on cheap/negative-price hours, discharging at peak) means the operation is economically viable at current market conditions without heavy subsidy.

## Caveat

Sand batteries are a specialised solution for **district heating and industrial heat** markets. They aren't a general-purpose grid battery — you can't get electricity back out at high efficiency. That's fine — different storage technologies fit different needs. But the "sand batteries will solve the storage problem" framing sometimes seen in headlines overstates what this technology is designed for.
