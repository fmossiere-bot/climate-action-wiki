# World's largest commercial sand battery online in Pornainen, Finland

**Source:** OilPrice.com, Alex Kimani, 29 July 2026. https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/The-Worlds-Largest-Sand-Battery-Is-Now-Online.html

**Company:** Polar Night Energy. https://polarnightenergy.com

## What it is

The Finnish start-up Polar Night Energy has switched on the world's largest commercial **sand battery** in Pornainen, in southern Finland. It was built for the local district heating company, Loviisan Lämpö.

The battery is a 13-metre-tall silo filled with 2,000 tonnes of crushed soapstone, left over from local fireplace making. It stores 100 MWh of renewable energy as heat. It charges up when wind and solar power are plentiful and grid electricity is cheap, or even negatively priced. It then releases that heat as hot water into the town's district heating network, warming homes, schools and public buildings. That is enough for Pornainen's 5,000 residents for one week in winter, or nearly one month in summer.

## Why it matters

Storing energy for weeks or months is the hardest part of running a grid on renewables. Lithium-ion batteries are excellent for storing power for minutes or hours, but far too expensive for weeks or months (see [the intermittency myth page](../myths/intermittency-storage-problem.md)). Sand batteries are one of the few technologies already running commercially that can cover weeks to months at a reasonable cost.

The materials are cheap and easy to find. Soapstone and sand need no lithium, cobalt or rare earths, which removes one of the limits on building long-duration storage at scale.

What comes out is heat, not electricity. That makes sand batteries best suited to district heating networks, which are common in the Nordic countries, Germany and parts of eastern Europe, and to industries that need heat, such as cement, steel and chemicals. They do not replace lithium-ion for storing electricity. They work alongside it by taking heating demand off the power grid altogether.

The numbers add up, too. Because the system charges in cheap or negative-price hours and releases heat at peak times, it can pay its way in today's market without heavy subsidy.

## Key facts

- **100 MWh** of storage capacity.
- The silo is 13 metres tall and holds 2,000 tonnes of crushed soapstone.
- Heat is stored at 400-600°C.
- It charges by resistive heating, a bit like an industrial-scale hair dryer, using surplus electricity from the grid.
- To release the heat, cool air is blown through pipes and heats water to up to 400°C, which then flows into the district heating network.
- It serves Pornainen's 5,000 residents: 1 week of heating in winter, or about 1 month in summer.
- The system is run fully by AI, charging when spot-market electricity prices are lowest (often near zero or negative) and releasing heat at peak times.
- Expected results: about a 70% cut in local greenhouse gas emissions, and about 60% less wood chip burned. The older wood-chip plant stays on for peak demand and backup.
- Unlike lithium-ion batteries, it does not wear out over time, and it needs no rare-earth materials.

## Track record and next steps

- **2022:** Polar Night Energy's first pilot in Kankaanpää, at 0.1 MW / 8 MWh.
- **2026:** The Pornainen system goes online, at 100 MWh.
- **Summer 2027:** A planned 250 MWh system in Vääksy. It will use natural sand instead of soapstone, and will help balance the electricity grid as well as supply district heating.

## Voices in the piece

Tommi Eronen, chief executive of Polar Night Energy, explains why he thinks storage like this is needed: *"We're changing from a world where big power plants were doing the energy production to where solar and wind are producing the energy, then we need a massive amount of storage. Hopefully a big portion of those storages could be sand battery-type of storages where we don't need rare earth materials, like in many chemical batteries, such as lithium-ion batteries."*

## Limits of the technology

Sand batteries are a specialist tool for district heating and industrial heat. They are not a general-purpose grid battery, because you cannot turn the heat back into electricity efficiently. That is fine, since different kinds of storage suit different jobs. But headlines suggesting that sand batteries will solve the storage problem overstate what the technology is built to do.
