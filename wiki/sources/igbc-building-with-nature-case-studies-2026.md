# The wetland that saved €100,000: building with nature in Ireland

**Source:** Irish Green Building Council (IGBC), blog post by Irene, October 2026. https://www.igbc.ie/the-wetland-that-saved-e100000-and-other-lessons-from-building-with-nature/

## What is it

The Irish Green Building Council has published a catalogue of **ten Irish case studies** showing how nature can be built into buildings and infrastructure. The council noticed that Ireland already had plenty of good examples, but hardly anyone knew about them. The catalogue was made with support from Construct Innovate and the Land Development Agency, and shaped by building and environmental professionals so it would be practical rather than aspirational.

The projects cover a hospital, a school, social housing, public spaces, a heritage renovation and a large housing development, plus two studies on biodiversity strategy and on the ecological impact of supply chains. The first two were launched in May 2025 by Christopher O'Sullivan, Minister of State for Nature, Heritage and Biodiversity.

## Why it matters

Construction depends on nature for water and raw materials, and trees and plants cool cities, clean the air and hold back floods. Yet the way we build often harms wildlife, both on site and where materials are extracted. These case studies show that protecting nature does not have to cost more. In the best example it saved money.

At the National Forensic Mental Health Services in Portrane, Co. Dublin, the project ecologist suggested sending the site's surface water into a nearby drained wetland instead of building an underground storage tank under the car park. The wetland came back to life, and the project **saved more than €100,000**.

## Key facts

- At **Seven Mills** in Clonburris, Dublin 22, Cairn Homes measured biodiversity net gain. When native plants were hard to find in Irish nurseries, the team harvested wildflower seed on site and grew native trees, such as hawthorn, from local stock.
- At Clúid Housing's **Ballygraigue Court** in Nenagh and Tuath Housing's **Kickham Street** in Carrick-on-Suir, residents turned shared outdoor spaces into gardens for pollinators and birds. One Ballygraigue resident said the project helped neighbours dealing with addiction and mental health problems come out and get involved.
- Across all ten projects, five problems kept coming up: a shortage of ecologists and biodiversity skills, nature measures added too late in design, tight or inflexible budgets with no money set aside for maintenance, people put off by "wild" looking meadows and rain gardens, and the difficulty of sourcing native plants at scale.
- The lessons: bring in ecologists from the start, explain the work to residents with signs and planting days, collaborate early, plan and budget for long-term care, and build evidence through surveys and monitoring, for example by aligning with the All-Ireland Pollinator Plan.
- The case studies have featured at Environment Ireland 2026 and in Engineers Ireland's nature-based solutions training course, and one was picked for a World Green Building Council publication.
