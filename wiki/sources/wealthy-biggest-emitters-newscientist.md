# Wealthy people with environmental ideals are the biggest emitters

**Source:** New Scientist, Alec Luhn.

## What it is

New Scientist reports on a study by Malte Dewies and Micha Kaiser at the University of Cambridge. They surveyed 5,000 people across Canada, France, Germany, Italy, the UK and the US. The survey asked about income, wealth, education and environmental values. The researchers then estimated each person's broad "ecological footprint", covering diet, house size, car use and flying.

For most people, caring more about the environment went with a smaller footprint. The exception was the **top 30%** by income and social status. There, the people with the strongest green values had an even larger footprint than their equally well-off peers, mainly because they flew more often.

## Why it matters

Many people who care about the climate try hard to live by their values, and this finding can feel uncomfortable. The researchers' point is not that green-minded people are hypocrites. It is that good intentions alone do not bring emissions down. Their conclusion is that changing policy matters more than changing attitudes, and that campaigns aimed at people's values will not do the job on their own.

## Key facts

- The study covered 5,000 people across 6 countries: Canada, France, Germany, Italy, the UK and the US.
- In the top 30% by socioeconomic status, people with pro-environment views emitted more than their peers.
- The main reason is frequent flying, one of the most emissions-heavy things an individual can do.
- The term "carbon footprint" was popularised by BP to shift responsibility from producers to consumers.
- The researchers say price and policy measures, such as aviation taxes and bans on short-haul flights, work better than campaigns about values.
- France banned short-haul flights in 2023, but loopholes meant no routes were actually cancelled.

## Other voices

Felix Creutzig of the University of Sussex adds some nuance. People with green values also tend to be open to the world and have friends and family abroad, and that drives flying.

The piece also points to Greta Thunberg's Fridays for Future movement. It helped push German climate legislation, even though most of the young people marching did not give up flying themselves. As the article puts it: "Being a citizen with an active voice matters more than [consumer] behaviour."
