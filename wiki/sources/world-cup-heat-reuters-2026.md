# Volatile summer weather threatens to turn World Cup into test of heat

**Source:** Reuters, Angelica Medina and Janina Rios, 10 June 2026. https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/volatile-summer-weather-threatens-turn-world-cup-into-test-heat-2026-06-10/

## What it is

Reuters looks at how hot the 2026 World Cup in North America could get for players. Research by Climate Central found that climate change has made dangerously hot conditions more likely at 97 of the 104 matches. By dangerous, the researchers mean hot enough to hurt how well players perform.

The measure they use is **wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT)**. It combines air temperature with humidity, sunshine and wind, so it reflects how hot it feels to a body working hard. A thermometer alone misses most of that. The most humid venues include Houston, Miami, Dallas and Monterrey.

## Why it matters

When we exercise, 75% of the energy we use turns into heat rather than movement. The body sheds that heat by sweating, but sweat only cools us when it evaporates. In humid air it evaporates slowly, so the heat builds up. That is why humidity matters so much for players.

Scientists want FIFA to require cooling measures once WBGT reaches 26°C, and to think about postponing matches at 28–30°C. The same risks apply to fans in the stands and to anyone working or playing sport outdoors in a heatwave.

## Key facts

- Climate change raised the risk of performance-impairing heat at 97 of 104 matches.
- The biggest increase is for Uruguay v Spain in Guadalajara on 26 June. It has a **70% chance** of performance-impairing heat, 37 percentage points higher than it would be without climate change.
- Nearly half of all matches have at least a 50% chance of temperatures above 28°C. That level of heat is linked to less sprinting, less distance covered and slower recovery.
- The heat risk is twice what it was at the 1994 World Cup, held in the same country.
- Mexico City's altitude (2,240m) adds another strain on players.
- FIFA has made three-minute drinks breaks compulsory in each half.
