# Excessive wealth, extreme emissions — Sam Wollaston at the Monaco Yacht Show

**Source:** Guardian, Sam Wollaston, 20 October 2026 — https://www.theguardian.com/news/2026/oct/20/excessive-wealth-extreme-emissions-and-ecocide-what-i-learned-at-europes-big-superyacht-show

## What it is

Sam Wollaston's long first-person feature from the **Monaco Yacht Show 2026**, Europe's biggest superyacht trade exhibition. **The core numerical findings: 1,093 superyachts over 24 metres were under construction globally in 2026 (up from 755 in 2016); the Oxfam 2024 study found an average annual carbon footprint of 5,672 tonnes per yacht for 23 yachts owned by 18 billionaires, which would take an average world citizen 860 years to produce.** Wollaston walks through specific yachts (Nixie, Boardwalk V, Leviathan, Koru, Azzam, Launchpad), the fuel use (big ones burn >500 litres of diesel per hour), the ecosystem damage (anchors destroying Mediterranean Posidonia seagrass), and interviews **Grégory Salle** (CNRS Lille, author of *Superyachts: Luxury, Tranquility and Ecocide*) who frames superyachts as the symbol of a transnational "super-bourgeoisie" operating outside borders and rules.

## Key facts

- **2026 Global Order Book (Boat International)**: **1,093 superyachts over 24m under construction** globally, down 3.95% from 2025 but with increased average length and tonnage
- **In 2016, 755 vessels were under construction** — the fleet in build is growing
- **At the Monaco Yacht Show 2026**: 118 superyachts, combined length 5,508 metres, combined value **€4.5bn**
- **Oxfam (2024) carbon-inequality study**: 23 superyachts owned by 18 billionaires produce **~5,672 tonnes of CO2 per year each on average**. **That takes the average world citizen 860 years to produce.**
- **The Walton family** (Walmart heirs, three superyachts): 56,000 nautical miles in a year, combined footprint **~18,000 tonnes CO2** = ~1,700 Walmart employees' worth
- **Fuel use**: bigger superyachts burn **>500 litres of diesel per hour**; releases CO2 plus NOx and SO2
- **Total recreational boating sector**: ~**16 million tonnes CO2 per year**
- **Anchors destroy Posidonia seagrass** on the Mediterranean seabed (a vital marine habitat and oxygen source)
- **Boats under 500 gross tonnage** (GT) escape IMO's stricter safety and environmental rules — a common size category to stay just below
- **Covid caused a superyacht surge**: 2022-2023 saw UHNWI (ultra-high-net-worth-individual) orders soar, as a yacht became "the best way to practise social distancing in a very safe and very comfortable way" (Salle)

## Specific yachts and owners named

- **Koru** (127m) — Jeff Bezos, ~€444m, a sailing yacht but with a 75m support vessel following it
- **Launchpad** (118m) — Mark Zuckerberg, plus support vessels
- **Nixie** (102m) — Nik Storonsky (Revolut co-founder/CEO), **€2.4m per week to hire**
- **Leviathan** (111m) + **Draak** (93m support) — Gabe Newell (Valve, co-founder), **494,000-litre fuel tank, ~€1m to fill**
- **Boardwalk V** (76.5m) — Tilman Fertitta (Trump ambassador to Italy), on sale at €135m
- **Azzam** (180m) — launched for Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan (Abu Dhabi), world's longest superyacht
- **Dilbar** (world's largest by volume) — formerly linked to Alisher Usmanov; German court ruled sanctions no longer apply after insufficient evidence of control
- **Solaris** (140m) and **Eclipse** (162m) — Roman Abramovich, now parked near Istanbul (Turkey didn't join sanctions)
- **Scheherazade** (140m, €620m) — reportedly linked to Putin via Navalny activists; impounded in Italy

## Notable quotes

- **Grégory Salle** (CNRS, author of *Superyachts: Luxury, Tranquility and Ecocide*): the superyacht "has really become the symbol of excessive, unjustifiable wealth. It looks like provocation, especially when all you're hearing and reading about is crisis, austerity, poverty — including child poverty."
- **Salle on "conspicuous seclusion"**: *"On the one hand they're meant to be noticed, they're massive, you can't miss them. But at the same time the interior is hidden and of course it can sail away and escape ordinary people."*
- **Salle's terms for the owners**: "plutocracy", "oligarchy", "global super-bourgeoisie", "transnational capitalist class"
- **Wollaston's Italian friend**: calls superyachts *stronzi di mare* — "turds of the sea"
- **A yacht broker (anonymous)**: *"I don't understand the clients. They just want something that's big and flashy and comfortable, they don't care about anything else."*
- **Wollaston's conclusion**: *"They drink fossil fuels like there's no tomorrow, which there won't be if they're allowed to carry on."*

## Where they are registered

Almost all superyachts are registered in **tax-neutral flag states**: Cayman Islands, Marshall Islands, Isle of Man, Malta. The result is that superyachts operate **outside and above borders and rules** — sanctions dodge via Turkey, sanctions win via Italian courts, trust-and-shell-company ownership that makes legal challenge difficult.

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **The 860-years ratio is the most shareable climate-inequality fact.** One average yacht owner's annual carbon footprint is nearly 10 generations of an average citizen's. When combined with the £30:£1 nature-finance ratio from Earthshot, these are the headline numbers for the "unequal responsibility" story at COP31.
2. **"Conspicuous seclusion" names the structure.** Salle's framing is sharp: the super-rich are visible (massive, famous, named yachts) and simultaneously untouchable (sail away, trust-held, Cayman-flagged). Policy responses that treat superyacht emissions as a tax or regulatory question need to grapple with this geography.
3. **Posidonia seagrass damage is a specific, local, measurable harm.** Beyond the carbon-abstract case, the Mediterranean seabed is being physically destroyed by anchors. That is both a biodiversity issue (Posidonia feeds the whole Mediterranean marine food chain) and a policy lever (anchoring restrictions, mooring buoys, marine protected areas).

## Caveats

- **Oxfam's 23-yacht study (2024) is a specific sample**, not an exhaustive accounting. The 5,672-tonne average will vary a lot across yacht sizes.
- **Boat International's Global Order Book counts yachts under construction**, not the operational fleet. The in-build pipeline matters for forward-looking policy; current emissions depend on the operational fleet.
