# Can everyone live a 'good life' without destroying the planet? — New Scientist on Guimarães

**Source:** New Scientist, April Reese, 14 July 2026 (updated 15 July 2026)

## What it is

A **long-form feature on the Portuguese city of Guimarães** as a working test bed for what "living within the planet's essential limits" actually looks like when a city commits to it — and what it takes politically, financially and culturally. The piece uses Guimarães as the case study to open a wider debate about **planetary boundaries, sufficiency, consumption corridors and social tipping points**, drawing on Johan Rockström (planetary boundaries), Joel Millward-Hopkins (University of Lausanne), Sara Pires (University of Aveiro), Tim Lenton (University of Exeter) and the World Inequality Lab's Global Justice Report.

## Key facts cited

- **Humanity has now crossed seven of the nine planetary boundaries** (climate change, ocean acidification, biosphere integrity, land-system change, freshwater-system change, novel entities, nutrient cycles — with ozone depletion and atmospheric aerosol pollution still inside)
- The planetary boundaries concept was introduced in a **landmark 2009 paper by Johan Rockström** at the Potsdam Institute
- **Total domestic water use has increased sixfold over the past 50 years** — faster than agriculture or industry
- Overall **extraction of natural resources and energy use has tripled** over a similar period
- **Guimarães ecological-footprint study (Sara Pires, University of Aveiro, a decade ago):** if everyone in the world lived like a Guimarães resident, we would need the resources of **almost 2.3 Earths** — significantly more than the global average of 1.7 Earths
- **Millward-Hopkins 2025 study**: growth rate of consumption is **four times higher in countries with surplus consumption** than in countries below decent living standards. At current rates, **decent living standards would not be fully achieved in lower-income countries until the end of the century**
- **Guimarães now has a waste circularity index 2.5× the Portuguese average**
- **Guimarães reduced average waste per person by 18% since 2018**
- **95 hectares of green space restored since 2012** by the Landscape Laboratory and partners
- **>20 tonnes of textiles recovered annually** through circular initiatives
- **Awarded European Green Capital 2026** by the EU
- **Global Justice Report (World Inequality Lab, June 2026)** proposes a global wealth tax on billionaires to fund the transition; calculates that average per-capita income across all countries could rise to **~€5,000 per month by the end of the century** while staying within planetary limits

## Key concepts introduced

- **Safe operating space / planetary boundaries** (Rockström): nine limits that if breached knock us out of the stable Holocene conditions humanity has thrived in for 12,000 years
- **Decent living standards** (Millward-Hopkins): a basic floor for well-being — nutrition, housing, plus a buffer to address inequality
- **Useless overconsumption**: additional wealth above the decent-living-standards level has little impact on well-being but a large environmental footprint
- **Consumption corridor**: a floor of decent living plus a ceiling of maximum consumption, walked collectively
- **Social tipping points** (Tim Lenton, University of Exeter): when changes in attitudes and behaviours trigger a much larger, often rapid and self-sustaining change across a society
- **One Planet City**: designation from Bioregional/WWF for cities committing to shrink their ecological footprint to a "one-Earth" level by 2050

## What Guimarães is actually doing

- **Landscape Laboratory** — research and education facility in an old textile factory, in collaboration with University of Minho and University of Trás-os-Montes e Alto Douro
- **Pay-as-you-throw** waste collection (charges per kg)
- **Regular repair fairs** to extend the life of electronics and other goods
- **Circular textile industry** revival: **RDD Textiles**, **Zouri shoes** using recycled materials and natural dyes
- **Floodwater retention basins** combining wetlands, meadows and retaining walls — held water during Portugal's deadly January 2026 storms while nearby areas flooded
- **"Green brigades"** of citizen volunteers who organise clean-ups, plant trees, run biodiversity surveys
- **Community consultation** — a recent call for project/activity ideas garnered 200 suggestions; 150 are now being implemented
- **Bairro C** (city neighbourhood, UNESCO World Heritage Site) turned into a testing ground for **zero-carbon policies and technologies**
- Sustainability commitments **codified into law** by the previous administration — the new mayor cannot easily undo them

## The uncomfortable finding on wealthy environmentalists

A **recent study found that among the wealthiest 30% of people, those who cared most about the environment had an even larger footprint than their peers**. Millward-Hopkins on why: extreme wealth insulates people from the economic pressures that might otherwise change their behaviour. When fuel prices rise, the poor cut consumption; the very rich do not. This echoes the [High-Income Carbon Paradox](../concepts/behavior-change/high-income-carbon-paradox.md) already on the wiki.

## Notable quotes

- **Carlos Ribeiro** (biologist, head of the Landscape Laboratory): *"Social justice and the principle of leaving no one behind are truly central to our approach."*
- **Joel Millward-Hopkins** (University of Lausanne): *"Any reductions in consumption that are required by regular people in Europe or North America could be more than compensated for by providing things that really improve people's lives: universal healthcare and education; healthy, affordable food; fulfilling work; and secure housing without the threat of eviction or mortgage spikes. All of these things can be provided to all without exceeding ecological limits, if we can direct our labour towards things that matter, not just what's profitable."*
- **Sandra Freitas** (Guimarães resident, runs a vegetarian café): *"We don't need too much food in our freezer or too much money in our pockets, and we don't need to have 10 cars."*
- **Sara Pires** (University of Aveiro): *"It's almost impossible for a new mayor to come and destroy that."*
- **Cornelia Mohren** (environmental coordinator, World Inequality Lab): *"The climate crisis and inequality are very much intertwined. We think that it's necessary to make those pay who caused the climate crisis, which are the global rich."*

## Why it matters

- **First live European case study of a city trying to walk a "consumption corridor"** — a floor of decent living plus a ceiling of maximum consumption — at scale
- Puts specific numbers on **Guimarães's progress**: 18% cut in per-capita waste since 2018, 2.5× the national average for circularity, 95 ha of green space restored, €5m in EU Green Capital funding
- Highlights the **legal codification** of sustainability commitments — a defence against political reversal — as one of the practical lessons other cities can copy
- The **World Inequality Lab's Global Justice Report** framing (June 2026) provides a distributional argument for sufficiency that avoids the "everyone must sacrifice equally" trap

## Tone and angle

Feature journalism. Optimistic in tone but grounded in named researchers, published studies (Millward-Hopkins 2025; Rockström 2009 planetary boundaries; Global Justice Report 2026) and concrete municipal actions with numbers. Honest about limits: many Guimarães residents still opt to drive despite the new bus fleet, and the very rich do not respond to fuel prices.
