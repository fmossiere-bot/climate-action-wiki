# Sixty years of fighting hunger, but another food crisis looms (Arnold & Fowler, Irish Times)

**Source:** Irish Times, Tom Arnold and Cary Fowler, 22 October 2026. https://www.irishtimes.com/environment/climate-crisis/2026/10/22/sixty-years-of-fighting-hunger-yielded-results-but-another-food-crisis-looms/

## What it is

This Irish Times analysis is written by Tom Arnold, an Irish expert on food policy and food security, and Cary Fowler, an American agronomist and former US special envoy for global food security, known for his work on the Svalbard Global Seed Vault.

They argue that six decades of progress against hunger are being undone. Long-running wars, climate change and the collapse of much of the aid system are all hitting at once, and they expect a food crisis in late 2026 and into 2027. They also say the way we judge food security needs to change: the question is now **nutrition quality**, as well as whether people get enough calories.

## Why it matters

The authors bring together two kinds of experience. Arnold has spent decades in Irish and global food policy. Fowler ran the Svalbard Seed Vault and served as US envoy for global food security. Their joint view reflects thinking in both policy and farming science, not a lone voice.

The focus on nutrition changes how aid works. For the last 60 years the fight against hunger focused mostly on calories, through bigger harvests and more bulk food. Putting nutrition first, such as vitamins, minerals and a varied diet, calls for different programmes, different measures of success and different spending.

The piece also treats food, climate and conflict as one connected problem rather than separate ones. That fits the approach of the [IPBES Nexus Assessment](ipbes-nexus-assessment-2024.md) and the Earthshot report on positive tipping points. Behind the figures are families who cannot be sure of their next meal.

## Key facts

- About **300 million people** face severe food insecurity today. The main causes are long-running conflicts, with Sudan the clearest example, and climate change.
- The war between Russia and Ukraine and the conflicts in the Middle East are disrupting supplies of grain, fertiliser and energy. This will hit wheat, maize and rice production in 2027.
- The authors write: *"Nutrition quality rather than calories is now key to food security but conflict and climate change make the task harder"*.
- A photo in the piece shows USAID food aid being delivered in Bentiu, South Sudan, in 2023. The caption notes that USAID has now closed, and that future US governments "may revert to playing a positive role in global food politics".

## Limits of the source

The clipping saved for this wiki covers only the opening section of the article. The full piece may include policy proposals and points specific to Ireland that are not captured here. If Irish policy recommendations from it are quoted elsewhere in the wiki, check them against the Irish Times original.

The comments on aid focus on the US. The closure of USAID reflects the US pulling back during Trump's second term, and the authors present it as something a future government could reverse.
