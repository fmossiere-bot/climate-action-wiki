# Sixty years of fighting hunger — another food crisis looms (Arnold & Fowler, Irish Times)

**Source:** Irish Times, Tom Arnold and Cary Fowler, 22 October 2026 — https://www.irishtimes.com/environment/climate-crisis/2026/10/22/sixty-years-of-fighting-hunger-yielded-results-but-another-food-crisis-looms/

## What it is

Irish Times opinion-analysis by **Tom Arnold** (Irish policy and food-security expert) and **Cary Fowler** (American agronomist and former US special envoy for global food security, known for his work on the Svalbard Global Seed Vault). **Their core argument: six decades of progress against hunger are being reversed by the convergence of protracted conflicts, climate change, and the collapse of the aid system that has supported global food security — a food crisis is looming in late 2026 and into 2027.** The piece emphasises a shift in what food security means: **nutrition quality, not just calories, is now the lens through which to assess the problem**.

## Key facts (per the clipping captured)

- **~300 million people** currently suffer from severe food insecurity, driven by **protracted conflicts** (Sudan is the most notable example) and **climate change**
- The **Russia-Ukraine** war and the **Middle East** conflicts are disrupting flows of **grain, fertiliser and energy** — this will hit wheat, maize and rice production in 2027
- The piece's framing: *"Nutrition quality rather than calories is now key to food security but conflict and climate change make the task harder"*
- Reference image in the piece: USAID food aid delivered in Bentiu, South Sudan, 2023 — with the caption noting USAID is now closed; future US governments "may revert to playing a positive role in global food politics"

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **The expert combination is unusually strong.** Tom Arnold has decades of Irish and global food-policy experience; Cary Fowler ran the Svalbard Seed Vault and served as US envoy for global food security. When these two write together, they are reflecting both policy and agronomic consensus, not an outlier view.
2. **The nutrition-quality framing matters for how aid works.** The last 60 years of hunger-fighting focused on calories (crop yields, bulk food supply). Shifting to nutrition quality (micronutrients, dietary diversity) implies different programmes, different metrics, and different investments.
3. **It links directly to climate change and conflict as compounding drivers.** The piece is not treating food security as a separate silo from climate or geopolitics. That matches the integrated-nexus framing of the IPBES Nexus Assessment and the Earthshot positive-tipping-points report.

## Caveats

- **Clipping captured only the opening section.** The full analysis in the Irish Times — including policy proposals and Ireland-specific angles — may extend further than the captured material. If specific Irish policy recommendations are quoted elsewhere in the wiki, cross-check against the Irish Times original.
- **US-centric framing on aid.** The reference to USAID's closure reflects the Trump second-term retreat; the piece frames this as reversible under future administrations.
