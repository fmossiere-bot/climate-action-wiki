# Data centre boom sees 107 facilities operating in Ireland (Irish Independent, June 2026)

**Source:** Irish Independent, John Burns, 26 June 2026. https://www.independent.ie/irish-news/data-centre-boom-sees-107-facilities-now-operating-in-ireland-with-18.5bn-invested/a/157776762.html

## What it is

A report on the latest Bitpower analysis of Ireland's data centre sector, alongside updates on grid policy and planning. Ireland now has **107 data centres** in operation, with €18.5bn invested so far.

## Why it matters

Data centres already use a large share of Ireland's electricity, and that share is set to grow. Every new facility adds demand to a grid that is still partly run on fossil fuels, which makes Ireland's climate targets harder to reach. New rules from the energy regulator try to tie growth to renewable power, but environmental groups say they leave a gap in the early years. There is more background on the wiki's page about [Ireland's data centre dilemma](../ireland-hub/IRELAND%20Data%20Centre%20Dilemna.md).

## Key facts

- 107 data centres are operating in Ireland, run by 32 operators. Another 9 are under construction and 43 have planning permission, for a possible total of about 160.
- The operating facilities have a combined IT load of 1.3 GW. The big cloud firms (AWS, Microsoft, Google, Meta) hold about 75% of total computing capacity.
- Total investment so far is €18.5bn.
- In EirGrid's middle-growth scenario, data centres will use **31% of all national electricity by 2034**, up from 22% in 2024.
- The Commission for Regulation of Utilities (CRU) lifted its moratorium in December 2025. Under the new rules, new data centres must source 80% of their electricity from renewables and become energy neutral after six years.
- Three environmental groups have won High Court permission to challenge the rules. They argue that fossil fuels are still allowed for the first six years.
- Connections in the Dublin area remain constrained, even under the new rules.
- Imports of AI hardware from Taiwan quadrupled in six months, from €0.5bn (Q3 2025) to €2.2bn (Q1 2026).
- There is no official census of data centres in Ireland.
