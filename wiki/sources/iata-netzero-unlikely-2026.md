# Airline industry chiefs say 2050 net zero goal now unlikely

**Source:** The Guardian, Gwyn Topham, 8 June 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jun/08/airline-industry-chiefs-willie-walsh-2050-net-zero-unlikely

## What it is

Willie Walsh, director general of IATA (the International Air Transport Association, which represents the world's airlines), spoke at the group's annual summit in Rio de Janeiro. He admitted that the industry's shared pledge to reach net zero by 2050 will "probably not now be achieved".

He blamed fuel suppliers, governments and aircraft makers, and called for a "realistic timeline" to replace the current one.

## Why it matters

Airlines have long pointed to **sustainable aviation fuel** (SAF, made from waste oils, crops or captured carbon instead of crude oil) as their main route to cutting emissions. This admission shows how far that plan has fallen behind. Production in 2026 covers less than 1% of what airlines burn.

Campaigners have argued for years that aviation's net zero pledges were greenwashing. Hearing the industry's own leader say the 2050 goal is out of reach gives that criticism more weight. For travellers, it means the emissions from flying are not going to fall quickly on their own.

## Key facts

- SAF production will reach only 2.4 million tonnes in 2026, just **0.8% of airline fuel needs**. The 2050 target is 65%, or 500 million tonnes.
- There is "no path" to reach the target set by ICAO (the UN's aviation body) of a 5% emissions cut by 2030 through SAF.
- Walsh said CORSIA, the UN's carbon offsetting scheme for aviation, is being "undermined" because governments are not acting.
- Aircraft makers have delayed deliveries of more fuel-efficient planes, and promised reforms to air traffic management have not happened.
- The UK only just met its 2% SAF minimum in 2025. Most of that came from recycled cooking oil imported from Asia, not the next generation of e-SAF made with clean electricity.
- IATA's vice-president for sustainability called the EU and UK targets for e-SAF in 2030 "utterly detached from reality".
- Environmental campaigners have long seen aviation's net zero pledges as greenwashing.
