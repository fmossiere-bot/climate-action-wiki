# UN says 1.5°C is over, world on track for 2.8°C — Emissions Gap Report 2026

**Source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 21 October 2026 (updated 20 October 2026) — on the UN Environment Programme's annual Emissions Gap Report 2026

## What it is

New Scientist coverage of the **UN's 2026 Emissions Gap Report**, released ahead of COP31 in Antalya. **The core finding: it is now impossible to limit warming to 1.5°C without "overshoot" — the world will temporarily breach 1.5°C and must then rely on massive mechanical and natural cooling (direct air capture, afforestation, etc.) to bring temperatures back down.** The best realistic scenario — the "lowest possible overshoot" pathway — would see peak warming at **1.7-1.8°C** with a 50% chance of falling back to 1.5°C by 2100. But current pledges fall far short of even this limited-overshoot path: policies as-stated point to **2.8°C by 2100**, and if every pledge were delivered, that falls to **2.1°C** — worse than last year's projection of 1.9°C, driven mainly by the US exit from the UN climate process.

## Key facts

- **Under current climate policies: 2.8°C warming by 2100**
- **If every national pledge is delivered: 2.1°C**, worse than last year's 1.9°C projection — the deterioration is **"driven mainly by the US's exit from the UN climate process"**
- **"Lowest possible" overshoot path**: peak warming at **1.7-1.8°C**, 50% chance of returning to 1.5°C by 2100
- To deliver that path, emissions must fall by:
  - **18% by 2030**
  - **45% by 2035**
  - **69% by 2040** (vs 2025 levels)
- **Current pledges would deliver** only **5-8% cuts by 2030** and **11-15% by 2035** — a factor of 3-4× too slow
- Last year's Emissions Gap report (2025) already said overshooting 1.5°C was inevitable; this year's "overshoot pathways" are explicitly designed to **keep 1.5°C on life support**
- The overshoot scenarios require **"vast amounts of global cooling"** via direct air capture and other technologies — many scientists are sceptical these can be delivered at scale

## Notable quotes

- **Bob Watson** (emeritus professor, University of East Anglia; former chair of the IPCC): *"We don't know whether we can overshoot. We don't know if any of those technologies are real, and so when people assume that we can meet 1.5°C or 2°C, and the assumption is based on overshooting and coming back, I think they're in cuckoo land."*
- **António Guterres** (UN Secretary-General): *"Overshoot is not surrender. We can still keep it as small and as short as possible — and bring temperatures back below 1.5 degrees by the end of the century."*
- **Joeri Rogelj** (Imperial College London): overshoot should be seen *"as a failure that we try to rectify"*. He argues there is still value in using the 1.5°C goal rather than resetting it to a weaker 2°C or 2.5°C target: *"The target is still to limit harm to vulnerable and poor populations... In the public debate, this failure to stay within this most ambitious limit should re-emphasise the need to stay as close to it as possible."*

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **It is now the UN's own official position that 1.5°C has been passed.** The 2024 and 2025 Emissions Gap reports built up to this. The 2026 report calls it directly. That is a significant shift for diplomatic framing: COP31 in Antalya will no longer be arguing about whether 1.5°C is still achievable, but about overshoot pathways.
2. **The 2.1°C projection worsened from 1.9°C in one year — because of the US withdrawal.** This is the single sharpest numerical statement of how much the US exit has cost the global climate trajectory. It makes concrete what Guardian / Patrick Greenfield's biodiversity reporting also hints at (12.1% of global biodiversity funding gone).
3. **The reliance on "mechanical cooling" (direct air capture, etc.) is now explicit policy.** Reaching the lowest-possible-overshoot path requires technologies that scientists like Bob Watson consider politically and technologically implausible at the required scale. This creates a credibility problem for every national climate plan that depends on them.

## Implications for the its-too-late myth

The wiki's existing [its-too-late myth](../myths/its-too-late.md) said the realistic warming range is 1.8-3°C — current policies ~2.5-3°C, 2030 targets met ~2.4°C, net-zero pledges delivered ~1.8°C. **This 2026 UN report sharpens those numbers and shifts the lower bound upward**: current policies now 2.8°C, pledges-delivered 2.1°C. The myth page needs updating to reflect this trajectory.

**But the myth's core argument still stands**: 2.8°C is not 5-6°C. Every tenth of a degree still matters. The debate has shifted from "can we hit 1.5°C" to "how small and short can we keep the overshoot" — but that is still a debate about how much damage to avoid, not whether to try.

## Caveats

- **The report's "lowest possible overshoot" scenario depends on large-scale negative emissions.** Many of those technologies (DAC at Gt scale) do not currently exist. Watson's "cuckoo land" quote captures the central scientific scepticism.
- **The 2.8°C is "central-estimate" under current policies.** Uncertainty bands exist in both directions.
