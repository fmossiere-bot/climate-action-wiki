# Climate change is a growing health emergency, Lancet Countdown 2026 warns

**Source:** World Health Organization, 28 October 2026. https://www.who.int/news/item/28-10-2026-climate-change-is-a-growing-health-emergency--lancet-countdown-warns

## What is it

The **Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change** is a yearly report that tracks how climate change is affecting people's health, using 54 indicators. It is produced by an international group of researchers led by University College London, in collaboration with the World Health Organization (WHO). The 2026 edition brings together 146 researchers from more than 100 institutions in over 30 countries. WHO summarised it ahead of COP31.

Its central finding is that many health harms from climate change are now growing faster than the world's response. Heat deaths, drought and other climate-related risks are rising faster than they were a decade ago. At the same time, clean energy and better health systems are already saving lives.

## Why it matters

Heat is the clearest example. Average yearly heat-related deaths rose by nearly **36%** in the years after the Paris Agreement compared with the decade before. In 2023 alone, heat was linked to a record 710,000 deaths worldwide, and more than 70% of that toll is attributed to human-caused climate change. Behind those numbers are mostly older people, outdoor workers and people in homes that cannot stay cool.

The report also shows the other side. Countries that have cut air pollution from burning fossil fuels are estimated to be sparing thousands of people from early death every year. WHO's Dr Diarmid Campbell-Lendrum, who leads its work on climate change, air quality and health, summed it up: "climate action is a health intervention."

## Key facts

- Yearly heat-related deaths increased by nearly 36% after the Paris Agreement compared with the previous decade.
- Heat was linked to a record 710,000 deaths globally in 2023, with more than 70% attributable to human-caused climate change.
- Drought, exposure to floods and other water extremes, and economic losses from heat are all getting worse.
- Global clean energy investment reached a record US$2.1 trillion in 2025.
- More countries now have national health adaptation plans and climate-informed early warning systems.
- The authors call for climate change to be formally recognised as a global health emergency, with health built into decisions on emission cuts, adaptation and finance.
- Since September 2026, WHO has run a graded emergency response, with a Global Incident Management Team, to the expected health impacts of the current El Niño.

## What WHO is doing

WHO already works on climate and health through disease surveillance, early warning systems, health adaptation planning and emergency response. It hosts the secretariat of the Alliance for Transformative Action on Climate and Health (ATACH), a network of health ministries working on climate-resilient, low-carbon health systems. The WHO statement also notes that, under the International Health Regulations (2005), an event driven by climate change could in principle qualify as a "public health emergency of international concern", the highest alert WHO can declare.

## Limits of the figures

This page is based on WHO's press summary, not the full report. Heat death figures are modelled estimates, since heat is rarely written on a death certificate. WHO is a partner of the Lancet Countdown, so this is a summary by one of the report's own collaborators.
