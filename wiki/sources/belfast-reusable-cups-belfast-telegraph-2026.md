# Belfast's reusable cup scheme

**Source:** Belfast Telegraph, Jonathan McCambridge (Press Association), 15 November 2026. https://www.belfasttelegraph.co.uk/news/northern-ireland/30-tonnes-of-waste-saved-thanks-to-single-use-plastic-cup-project-at-city-venues/a/164573601.html

## What is it

A pilot that swapped single-use plastic cups for **reusable cups** at events in Belfast is being made permanent and expanded. Its organisers describe it as the first multi-venue reusable cup scheme in the UK and Ireland. It started in January 2025 in six venues: the Waterfront Hall, Ulster Hall, the O2 Belfast, the Oh Yeah Centre, Black Box and The MAC.

The idea is simple. Visitors get a cup at the bar, hand it back at a collection point, and an operations partner, North Down Marquees and More, collects, washes and returns the cups to be used again. The scheme is driven by the Venue Sustainability Forum and supported by Visit Belfast. It has since spread to the Balmoral Show and to Northern Ireland men's international football matches at the national stadium.

## Why it matters

Single-use cups are a small, everyday item, which is exactly why they pile up. A shared scheme across several venues means people meet the same cup wherever they go, which makes returning it a habit. It also shows venues sharing the cost of one system rather than each trying alone.

The organisers' consultants found that a reusable cup beats a single-use one on carbon after **just two uses**, and a well-kept cup can be reused hundreds of times. The scheme only works if people return their cups.

## Key facts

- In under two years the scheme avoided **30 tonnes of waste** and 1.5 million single-use plastic cups, and saved 105 tonnes of carbon, according to its organisers.
- The founding venues have signed a longer-term contract, and more large events and arenas have joined.
- The operations partner has bought the washing equipment it used to rent and introduced a new cup design carrying a sustainability message.
- The carbon comparison comes from a life cycle analysis by AD Sustainability, an environmental consultancy working on the scheme. These are the organisers' own figures, not an independent audit.
