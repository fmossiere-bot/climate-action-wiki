# Interview with Anaïs Massé, Fondation de la mer — Écologie 360, June 2026

**Source:** Écologie 360 (French magazine), Anaïs Massé interview, published June 2026. No public URL for the print original.

## What it is

An interview with **Anaïs Massé**, scientific director of the **Fondation de la mer** (a French ocean-protection NGO), in the June 2026 issue of *Écologie 360*. Split in two: a primer on why the ocean matters and what threatens it, then Massé's own commentary on solutions and international momentum. The Fondation de la mer is a well-known French charity that runs the **"Repêchons les océans"** programme with clothing brand Ecoalf, where volunteer fishers keep any waste caught in their nets on board and drop it in containers at 13 partner ports.

## Key facts (Massé's headline numbers)

- Ocean absorbs **25% of human CO2 emissions**
- Ocean absorbs **90% of excess heat** from climate change
- **3 billion people** depend on marine resources for protein and income (out of 8.2 bn population)
- **50% of Earth's oxygen** comes from marine phytoplankton
- **80% of the world's animal biomass** is in the oceans
- Sea surface temperature is **+0.5°C** vs 1981-2010 average; +1°C since the pre-industrial era
- **Mediterranean is warming 20% faster** than the global average
- **Sea level rose 15 cm** over the twentieth century
- **9-14 million tonnes of plastic** enter the ocean each year; **80% of pollutants** come from land-based sources
- **18 tonnes of plastic per minute** enter the ocean
- Plastic carries **~4,000 chemicals** with proven toxicity into the food chain
- **37.7% of fish stocks** are overexploited

## Concrete case: Miquelon relocation (first in France)

Massé cites **Miquelon**, in the Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon archipelago off Newfoundland, as **the first French village that must be relocated because of climate change**. Rising storm and flood risk have made the current settlement no longer safe.

## Nature-based solutions vs geoengineering

Massé's core argument: **nature-based solutions are the most effective way to limit damage.** She points to:

- **Mangroves** — can store **three times more carbon than a tropical forest of the same size**
- **Seagrass meadows** (like Mediterranean Posidonia) — play a key climate role
- **Coral reefs** — habitat, coastal protection, cultural value

On **geoengineering**, she is cautious: the door should not be closed, but extensive testing is required before deployment.

## Marine protected areas — the 2025 effectiveness study

On the international goal of **protecting 30% of the ocean by 2030**, Massé cites a 2025 study identifying three factors needed for MPAs to actually work:

1. **Large surface area**
2. **Strict protection** — no fishing or extraction
3. **Connection between zones** — so species can move between them

Small "paper parks" without enforcement don't count.

## International momentum: BBNJ + Ocean COP 2027

- The **BBNJ / High Seas Treaty** enters into force in 2026 (this is the same event covered in [record-hot-ocean-el-nino-2026](../climate-science/record-hot-ocean-el-nino-2026.md) — signed 17 January 2026)
- The **first Ocean COP will be held in early 2027**
- Massé calls this a **"historic step"** but warns that momentum from the UN Ocean Conference must be sustained

## Notable quotes and framing

- Mediterranean is warming **20% faster** than global average — a specific European lens on the ocean crisis
- **Miquelon**: "a first for France" — the first climate-driven French village relocation
- On plastic: 18 tonnes per minute + 4,000 chemicals into the food chain — a concrete "reaches our plates" framing
- On MPAs: three-factor test explicitly rules out large-but-nominal protected areas

## Why it matters

Two things this interview adds to the wiki that were less well covered before:

1. **A French/European lens on ocean loss.** Existing pages (WRI, UN World Ocean Assessment) come from Anglo-American sources; the Miquelon and Mediterranean facts are specifically European, and the "Repêchons les océans" programme is a citizen-fisher partnership rare in English-language ocean coverage.
2. **The BBNJ → Ocean COP 2027 through-line.** The High Seas Treaty entering into force isn't the endpoint. It's the launchpad for the first Ocean COP in 2027 — a governance milestone worth watching.
