# Interview with Anaïs Massé, Fondation de la mer (Écologie 360, June 2026)

**Source:** Écologie 360 (French magazine), interview with Anaïs Massé, June 2026. There is no public URL for the print original.

## What it is

The June 2026 issue of *Écologie 360* carries an interview with **Anaïs Massé**, scientific director of the Fondation de la mer, a French charity that works to protect the ocean. The piece comes in two parts. The first is a primer on why the ocean matters and what threatens it. The second is Massé's own view on solutions and on where international talks are heading.

The Fondation de la mer is well known in France. With the clothing brand Ecoalf it runs a programme called "Repêchons les océans" ("let's fish the oceans back"). Volunteer fishers keep any rubbish caught in their nets on board and drop it into containers at 13 partner ports.

## Why it matters

Much of the wiki's ocean coverage comes from British and American sources, such as WRI and the UN World Ocean Assessment. This interview adds a French and European view. The Mediterranean is warming faster than the world's oceans as a whole, and a French village is already being moved because of the sea. "Repêchons les océans" is also a rare example of fishers and citizens working together, which seldom features in English-language reporting.

It also links two events that are easy to treat separately. The High Seas Treaty coming into force is a starting point rather than a finish line, because it leads on to the first Ocean COP in 2027. That is a moment worth watching for anyone who cares about how the open ocean is governed.

## Key facts

These are the figures Massé gives.

- The ocean absorbs **25% of human CO2 emissions**.
- It also absorbs 90% of the extra heat trapped by climate change.
- 3 billion people, out of a world population of 8.2 billion, depend on marine resources for protein and income.
- 50% of the Earth's oxygen comes from marine phytoplankton, the tiny plants that drift near the surface.
- 80% of the world's animal biomass (the total weight of all animals) lives in the oceans.
- Sea surface temperature is +0.5°C above the 1981-2010 average, and +1°C above pre-industrial times.
- The Mediterranean is warming 20% faster than the global average.
- Sea level rose 15 cm over the twentieth century.
- 9-14 million tonnes of plastic enter the ocean each year, about 18 tonnes every minute. 80% of pollutants come from land.
- Plastic carries around 4,000 chemicals with proven toxicity into the food chain, and so onto our plates.
- 37.7% of fish stocks are overexploited, meaning they are fished faster than they can recover.

## Miquelon: the first French village to be moved

Massé points to **Miquelon**, in the Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon archipelago off Newfoundland. It is the first French village that has to be relocated because of climate change, "a first for France", as the interview puts it. Rising storm and flood risk mean the current settlement is no longer safe for the people who live there.

## Nature first, geoengineering with care

Massé's view is that **nature-based solutions** are the most effective way to limit the damage. She gives three examples.

- Mangroves can store three times more carbon than a tropical forest of the same size.
- Seagrass meadows, such as the Posidonia meadows of the Mediterranean, play a key role in the climate.
- Coral reefs provide habitat, protect coasts and carry cultural value.

On geoengineering (large-scale deliberate interventions in the climate system), she is cautious. She does not want the door closed, but says it needs extensive testing before anything is deployed.

## What makes a marine protected area work

The international goal is to protect 30% of the ocean by 2030. Massé cites a 2025 study that found marine protected areas only work when three conditions are met. They need to be large. They need strict protection, with no fishing or extraction. And they need to be connected, so that species can move from one to the next. Small "paper parks" that exist on maps but are not enforced do not count, and neither do large areas that are protected in name only.

## The High Seas Treaty and the first Ocean COP

The BBNJ agreement, better known as the High Seas Treaty, enters into force in 2026. It was signed on 17 January 2026, the same event covered in [Record-hot ocean and El Niño](../climate-science/record-hot-ocean-el-nino-2026.md). The first Ocean COP will follow in early 2027. Massé calls this a "historic step", but warns that the energy built up at the UN Ocean Conference has to be kept going.
