# UN takes on AI climate misinformation as COP31 fossil fuel tensions rise

**Source:** The Climate Watch, TCW Report, 14 November 2026. https://theclimatewatch.com/un-takes-on-ai-climate-misinformation-as-cop31-fossil-fuel-tensions-rise/

## What is it

On Friday 13 November 2026, at COP31 in Antalya, a UN-backed group launched the **Priorities for Action on AI and Climate Information Integrity**. The group is the Global Initiative for Information Integrity on Climate Change, co-chaired by the United Nations, its scientific and cultural agency UNESCO, and Brazil. "Information integrity" means people being able to get accurate, reliable information, in this case about climate change.

The priorities ask AI companies to check how their systems affect the quality of climate information, publish what they find, and disclose their energy use and their carbon, water and land footprints. Governments are asked to build information integrity into their national AI strategies and climate plans. The same day saw the launch of a **Charter for Climate Information Integrity in Advertising**, which targets the advertising money that ends up funding climate denial.

## Why it matters

More and more people ask AI chatbots about climate change. Charlotte Scaddan, who advises the UN Department of Global Communications on information integrity, framed the issue this way: "The question isn't simply whether AI is good or bad for the climate. It's whether people can rely on what they're told by AI and about it." A UN scientific panel on AI has warned that "producing fluent text is easier than producing factual text." The risk is highest during disasters, when fake AI-generated images and videos can spread fast and compete with official emergency warnings.

There is a second problem. The electricity demand of AI and data centres is becoming a new argument for keeping or expanding coal, oil and gas. An analysis by the think tank InfluenceMap found that **nearly half** of the statements it collected over the past year about AI and data centres contained arguments in favour of fossil fuels. Rising demand is real, but it can be met with renewables, storage and efficiency. The article draws a line between that real challenge and the political or commercial use of it to justify new fossil fuel investment.

## Key facts

- The Priorities for Action were launched at COP31 by the Global Initiative for Information Integrity on Climate Change, co-chaired by the UN, UNESCO and Brazil.
- They call on AI companies to disclose energy use and carbon, water and land footprints, and to assess and publish their effects on climate information.
- They build on the Belém Declaration on Information Integrity on Climate Change, launched at COP30 in 2025, and the UN Global Principles for Information Integrity.
- Germany has committed more than $2.2 million to research, communication strategies and public awareness campaigns under the initiative.
- InfluenceMap found that nearly half of the statements it collected on AI and data centres over the past year contained pro-fossil fuel arguments.
- The Charter for Climate Information Integrity in Advertising asks for more transparency in the ad supply chain, so advertisers can see where their ads appear and stop unknowingly funding climate disinformation.

## Advertising and the money behind disinformation

Online advertising is largely automated, so a brand's ad can end up on a website that spreads climate denial without anyone at the brand choosing it. That ad still pays the site. Jake Dubbins, co-founder of the Conscious Advertising Network, said the charter aims to bring transparency to the whole advertising industry, letting advertisers see where their ads run while respecting users' privacy. Cutting off that income removes one of the financial incentives to publish misleading climate content.

## Fairness for climate-vulnerable countries

The article stresses that transparency matters most where resources are already stretched. New data centres in climate-vulnerable countries can put extra pressure on electricity grids, water supplies and land. The communities living next to them should know the environmental costs and have a real say in decisions.

## Limits of the figures

The Priorities and the Charter are voluntary. They carry no legal obligation for AI companies, platforms or advertisers. The InfluenceMap figure covers the statements InfluenceMap collected, not every public statement on the subject. Two near-identical copies of this article were clipped; this page covers both.
