# Air conditioning in the UK: can it be green?

**Source:** The Guardian, Chris Baraniuk and Zoe Wood, 28 May 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/may/28/air-conditioning-uk-homes-heatwave

## What it is

A Guardian explainer published during a UK heatwave in May 2026. It looks at the environmental trade-offs of air conditioning at home: the types of cooling on offer, what they cost to run, their carbon emissions, how pairing them with renewable energy helps, and the alternatives to mechanical cooling.

## Why it matters

Summers in the UK are getting hotter, and many homes were built to keep heat in, not out. More people are buying air conditioning, and the question of whether that is bad for the climate is a fair one.

The article's answer is measured. Air conditioning running on today's grid is not green until the grid is fully renewable. The best approach is to keep homes cool without machines first, through shading windows in the day, opening them at night, green roofs and insulation. If you do need cooling, an **air-to-air heat pump** is the most efficient choice, and it can heat the home in winter too. Running it from solar panels, or from a home battery charged with surplus renewable power, cuts its footprint further. Portable units are the least efficient type.

The authors also make a humane point. Nobody should feel guilty for using air conditioning as a last resort during dangerous heat. The health benefits are real.

## Key facts

- About **4 million UK homes** now have an air conditioner, double the figure from three years ago.
- The UK government's climate advisers, the Climate Change Committee (CCC), estimate that 22% of British homes would need active cooling with 2°C of global heating.
- A portable unit (about 1kW) running 8 hours a day produces 4.87kg of CO2 over a summer, roughly the same as driving 18 miles.
- Running a portable unit round the clock costs about £5 a day, against 15–31p a day for a fan.
- A wall-mounted unit (12,000 BTU) costs about £750 plus about £1,150 to install. A portable equivalent costs about £350.
- Air-to-air heat pumps give about 3 units of cooling for every 1 unit of electricity, far more efficient than standard air conditioning.
- The UK government offers a £2,500 grant to replace a fossil-fuel heating system with an air-to-air heat pump.
- A [2021 Lancet study](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanplh/article/PIIS2542-5196(21)00136-4/fulltext), using data from 2007–2019, found that electric fans can be about as good as air conditioning at preventing heat stress on hot days.
