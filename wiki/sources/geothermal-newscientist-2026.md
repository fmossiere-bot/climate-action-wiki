# How to unlock unlimited geothermal energy, anywhere we want

**Source:** New Scientist, Katharine Sanderson, 21 July 2026 (updated 23 July 2026)

## What it is

A long-form New Scientist explainer on the **three new routes to global-scale geothermal energy**: **Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS)**, **Advanced Geothermal Systems (AGS / closed-loop)**, and **supercritical / superhot rock geothermal**. The framing is that geothermal has always been a "spectacular but geographically lucky" energy source (Iceland, New Zealand, Kenya, Indonesia) — and that a decade of drilling advances is now on track to **untether it from geology entirely**.

## Key facts cited

- The IEA estimates geothermal's **technical potential is around 150× current global electricity demand**, assuming heat can be exploited down to about 8 km
- Traditional geothermal plants are limited to **heat from less than 2 km underground**
- **Iceland now generates over 25% of its electricity from geothermal**; the industry took off after the 1970s oil crisis
- **EGS** targets hot but impermeable rock **2–10 km deep**, pumping water at high pressure to open fractures — most mature of the new techniques
- **Advanced geothermal (AGS)** = closed-loop sealed pipe; **Eavor's Geretsried plant in Germany** began construction in 2022 and started producing electricity in 2025
- **China Huaneng Group** has built the first geothermal demonstration plant using **supercritical CO₂** as the working fluid (Zhengzhou, operational May 2026)
- **Iceland Deep Drilling Project**: second well (2017) went to **4.7 km** — first well in the world where **supercritical conditions were confirmed in the geothermal well**; third well planned next year
- **Supercritical temperatures**: **374°C (705°F)** and above under sufficient pressure; typically at depths of **5-20 km+**
- **Quaise Energy** (Houston, Texas): plans to use **conventional drilling for the upper well and high-powered millimetre-wave energy** for deeper hard rock — no drill bit contact; rock vaporised into ash
- **GA Drilling** (Slovakia) trialling laser- and plasma-based systems; first field tests 2023
- **Quaise timeline**: first planned superhot geothermal plant in Oregon by 2030 (400°C / 752°F at 3-5 km); "by 2035, we want the first superhot well producing energy at a depth greater than 10 km" (Matt Houde, Quaise co-founder)

## The serious risks

- **Pohang, South Korea (November 2017)**: a nearby EGS project **triggered a magnitude-5.5 earthquake**; over **80 people injured**; **~300 billion Korean won (~£148 million) in damage** — the most destructive earthquake in South Korean history. The South Korean government's investigation concluded EGS caused it.
- **Fault-slip mechanism**: pumping water into deep rock raises pore pressure, partly propping fault sides apart and reducing the friction holding them together. If a fault is already close to slipping, that can be enough to trigger a quake.
- **Iceland's mitigation**: rather than blasting reservoirs open all at once, engineers pump cold water slowly and at low pressure over months — the cooling rock contracts and cracks naturally (HS Orka).
- **Groundwater contamination risk** from EGS fluid leakage
- **Deep-well integrity**: at supercritical temperatures, steel casings can expand, deform and fail; Iceland engineers are testing flexible connectors between casing sections
- Closed-loop systems (AGS) may be safer but face **rock debris clogging** (Eavor scaled back its planned wells) and **doubts about whether they can circulate fluid and draw heat fast enough** to be commercially useful

## Notable quotes

- **Matt Houde** (Quaise Energy co-founder): *"Ultimately, the whole world is the goal."* Also: *"If you measure in terms of the energy stored within the Earth's crust, geothermal heat exceeds by orders of magnitude all hydrocarbons."*
- **Iain Staffell** (Imperial College London): geothermal is *"really well placed to be the glue that will make low-carbon electricity systems work."*
- **Bill Ellsworth** (Stanford): on Pohang — the drilling connected two wells through faults primed to slip. On deep wells: *"We know that very deep geothermal energy would be fabulous if we could reach it. We just don't yet know if it's possible."*
- **Sanjeev Kumar** (European Geothermal Energy Council): *"It's very, very costly. But once you've drilled that hole in the ground, that lasts for over 100 years."*

## Why it matters

Geothermal is **dispatchable, low-carbon and geographically inexhaustible in principle** — the ideal complement to variable wind and solar. Until now it has been "spectacular but geographically lucky." The three new routes documented here — EGS, AGS, superhot rock — attempt to solve that in different ways, with different risk/cost trade-offs. The Pohang earthquake is the biggest cautionary note, and Iceland's slow-cooling approach is the current best answer.

If Quaise or its equivalents actually reach **superhot rock at 10+ km depth**, the same drilling techniques pioneered to extract oil and gas could unlock a **150× overshoot of current global electricity demand** — clean, dispatchable, and largely local.
