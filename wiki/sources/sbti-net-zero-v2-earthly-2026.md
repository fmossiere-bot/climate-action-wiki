# SBTi's Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2: the OER framework

**Source:** Earthly blog, Faith Sayo, 23 July 2026. https://earthly.org/blog/what-sbti-corporate-net-zero-standard-v2-means-for-business-carbon-credit-strategy

## What it is

An explainer of version 2.0 of the **Science Based Targets initiative's** Corporate Net-Zero Standard. The SBTi is the body that checks whether company climate targets are in line with the science, and this is the biggest redesign of its main rulebook since 2021. The new rules take effect on 1 February 2027, and companies can start having targets approved under them from Q1 2027.

For two years, carbon markets have been asking whether buying carbon credits has any formal place in an SBTi-approved climate plan. The answer is now yes, through a new framework called **Ongoing Emissions Responsibility (OER)**.

The article was written by Earthly, a company that sells verified nature-based carbon credits. Its description of the SBTi rules is accurate and detailed, but its recommendations at the end are sales advice rather than neutral analysis.

## Why it matters

This is a big change to how companies plan for net zero. Carbon credits now have a formal, structured and public role in SBTi-aligned strategies, which settles a debate that ran for two years. Cutting a company's own emissions still comes first: credits still cannot count towards scope 1, 2 or 3 reduction targets.

The largest companies also face real costs ahead. From 2035 they will have to buy credits for carbon removal, meaning carbon actually taken out of the air, and the amount rises every year.

The new reporting rules should also make greenwashing harder to get away with. Companies must report every year, work in five-year cycles, publish their OER declarations, and explain publicly on the SBTi Dashboard if they opt out.

## Key facts

- **BVCM** (Beyond Value Chain Mitigation) is replaced by OER, a structured programme with public recognition tiers, required disclosures and a binding obligation from 2035 for large companies to buy carbon removal credits.
- Companies fall into two groups. **Category A** covers firms with annual turnover above €50m in high-income countries, or above €450m in lower-income countries. They need transition plans, scope 3 targets and independent checks, and are subject to the 2035 removal requirement. **Category B** is mostly small and medium-sized firms, with lighter duties, though larger customers will ask them for emissions data.
- Net-zero targets are now optional. Every company must set near-term five-year targets: scope 1 and 2 for all, plus scope 3 for Category A.
- Scope 1 and 2 must be reported every year, and full scope 1-3 at the end of each five-year cycle.
- Scope 3 rules are more flexible, allowing supplier alignment, product alignment, volume-based and shared-responsibility approaches.
- The old version, V1.3.1, can still be used for new targets until 31 January 2028.
- Removal credit supply is small today: about **5-10 million tonnes a year**, against a voluntary carbon market of about 180 million tonnes ([Carbon Direct](https://www.carbon-direct.com/insights/key-trends-2026-voluntary-carbon-market)). As 2035 approaches, that gap is likely to narrow and prices to rise.

## Three roles for carbon credits under V2.0

- A voluntary OER recognition programme from 2027 to 2035, with three tiers of public recognition.
- A mandatory removal requirement for Category A from 2035. It starts at 1% of ongoing emissions and rises to 100% by the company's net-zero year.
- Neutralising what is left at the net-zero year. Whatever a company still emits at and after that point must be balanced with removal credits that store carbon for as long as the emissions they cancel out would last.

## The OER recognition tiers

Each tier is measured against a company's total scope 1-3 emissions over the five-year target cycle.

- **Engaged**: cover at least 1% of total ongoing scope 1-3 emissions, either tonne for tonne with retired verified credits or through a contribution budget of $20 a tonne.
- **Advanced**: cover 100% of scope 1 and 2 and at least 10% of total scope 1-3, with the same options.
- **Leadership**: cover 100% of all ongoing scope 1-3 emissions with a contribution budget of at least $80 a tonne, and retire verified credits for the full amount. The SBTi calls this "the full internalisation of the cost of climate change".

## What the $80 a tonne figure means

The **$80 a tonne** figure is not a market price, a legal requirement or a minimum price for carbon credits. It is the budget set for the Leadership tier only. A company with 50,000 tonnes of CO2e in ongoing emissions would face a Leadership budget of about $4 million a year. At 500,000 tonnes, that rises to about $40 million a year. The Engaged tier for 500,000 tonnes would cost about $100,000 a year.

## The 2035 mandate

From 2035, OER becomes compulsory for Category A companies, the large firms in high-income countries. They must cover at least 1% of their ongoing scope 1-3 emissions with **carbon removal credits**, and that share rises each year to reach 100% by their net-zero target year.

Long-lasting removals are phased in alongside. They must make up 10% of the removal requirement in 2035, rising to 100% by the net-zero year. Because the SBTi works in five-year cycles, many companies will need to plan for 2035 at their next target cycle rather than waiting until 2035 itself.

## Nature-based solutions under OER

Between 2027 and 2035, while OER is voluntary, credits from nature all qualify, whether they come from reducing, avoiding or removing emissions. These are projects that protect, manage or restore ecosystems.

From 2035, only removals count. Nature-based removals can make up as much as **90%** of the requirement in 2035, and their share then shrinks as durable, long-lived removals are phased in. The SBTi is clear that nature-based solutions and engineered removals work together rather than compete.

## Quality rules for OER credits

Credits used under OER must:

- be measured after the fact, counting only results that have already happened
- be independently verified against a recognised standard
- be permanently retired when claimed
- have been generated within five years of the reporting year
