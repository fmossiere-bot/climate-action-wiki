# SBTi's Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2 — the OER framework

**Source:** Earthly blog, Faith Sayo, 23 July 2026 — https://earthly.org/blog/what-sbti-corporate-net-zero-standard-v2-means-for-business-carbon-credit-strategy

## What it is

A vendor-authored (Earthly) but comprehensive explainer of the **Science Based Targets initiative's Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0**, the biggest redesign of SBTi's flagship framework since 2021. **Takes effect 1 February 2027**, with new-rule target validation opening in Q1 2027. The central question the carbon markets have been asking for two years — do carbon credits have a formal place in SBTi-aligned climate strategies — is now answered **yes**, through a new **Ongoing Emissions Responsibility (OER)** framework.

## Key structural changes

- **BVCM replaced by OER (Ongoing Emissions Responsibility)** — a structured programme with public recognition tiers, mandatory disclosure requirements, and a **binding 2035 obligation** for large companies to buy carbon removal credits
- **Two-tier company classification**:
  - **Category A**: annual turnover >€50m in high-income countries, or >€450m in lower-income countries. Mandatory transition plans, mandatory scope 3 targets, mandatory third-party assurance, subject to 2035 removal requirement
  - **Category B**: mostly SMEs; lighter obligations but their emissions data will be requested up the value chain
- **Net-zero targets are now optional**, but every company must set **near-term five-year targets** (scope 1 and 2 for all; scope 3 for Category A)
- **Annual reporting required** — scope 1 and 2 annually, full scope 1-3 at end of each five-year cycle
- **Scope 3 flexibility**: supplier alignment, product alignment, volume-based and shared-responsibility provisions all allowed
- **Existing V1.3.1 remains available for new target submissions until 31 January 2028**

## Three roles for carbon credits under V2.0

1. **Voluntary OER recognition programme (2027–2035)** — three tiers of public recognition
2. **Mandatory removal requirement (from 2035 for Category A)** — starts at 1% of ongoing emissions and scales to 100% by net-zero year
3. **Neutralisation of residual emissions at net-zero year** — whatever a company still emits at and after its net-zero year must be offset with **removal credits matched to the durability of the emissions being neutralised**

**What has not changed**: carbon credits still cannot be used to meet scope 1, 2 or 3 emissions reduction targets. Reductions remain foundational.

## The OER recognition tiers

Measured against cumulative scope 1-3 emissions across the five-year target cycle:

- **Engaged**: cover at least **1%** of total ongoing scope 1-3 emissions, either tonne-for-tonne with retired verified credits or via a **$20/tonne** contribution budget
- **Advanced**: cover **100% of scope 1 & 2** and **at least 10% of total scope 1-3**, with the same instrument options
- **Leadership**: cover **100% of all ongoing scope 1-3 emissions** with a contribution budget of at least **$80/tonne**, and retire verified credits for the full covered volume — SBTi describes Leadership as "the full internalisation of the cost of climate change"

## The $80/tonne figure — what it is and isn't

$80/tonne is **not a market price, compliance requirement or minimum carbon credit price**. It is the **defined contribution budget for the Leadership tier only**. A company with **50,000 tCO₂e of ongoing emissions** would face a Leadership budget of **~$4 million/year**; **500,000 tCO₂e** = **~$40 million/year**. Engaged tier for 500,000 tCO₂e = **~$100,000/year**.

## The 2035 mandate

From **2035**, the OER programme becomes **mandatory for Category A** (large, high-income) companies:

- From 2035, cover at least **1% of ongoing scope 1-3 emissions** with **carbon removal credits**
- Requirement increases annually, reaching **100% by the company's net-zero target year**
- **Durable removals phased in**: 10% of the removal requirement in 2035, rising to **100% by net-zero year**
- SBTi's five-year review cycle means many companies will need to plan the 2035 transition **at their next target cycle, not in 2035 itself**

## Nature-based solutions under OER

- **2027–2035 (voluntary OER)**: nature-based reduction, avoidance and removal credits all qualify — projects that protect, manage or restore ecosystems
- **From 2035 (mandatory)**: requirement shifts to **removals only**; nature-based removals can account for **up to 90%** of the removals requirement in 2035, then their share gradually declines as **durable, long-lived removals** are phased in
- SBTi is explicit: nature-based solutions and engineered removals are **complementary, not competing**

## Quality requirements for OER credits

Credits used under OER must:

- Be measured **after the fact (ex-post outcomes only)**
- Be **independently verified** against a recognised standard
- Be **permanently retired** when claimed
- Be **generated within five years** of the reporting year

## Broader market signal

Removal credit supply is currently limited: **~5-10 million tonnes/year against a voluntary market of ~180 million tonnes** ([Carbon Direct](https://www.carbon-direct.com/insights/key-trends-2026-voluntary-carbon-market)). As the 2035 mandate approaches, that gap will narrow and prices will rise.

## Why it matters

This is a substantial revision to the corporate net-zero playbook. Three things matter most:

1. **Carbon credits now have a formal, structured, publicly visible place** in SBTi-aligned climate strategy — the two-year debate is settled
2. **Category A companies face binding future costs** for removal credits, phased in from 2035
3. The **transparency architecture** (annual reporting, five-year cycles, published OER declarations, public opt-out justifications on the SBTi Dashboard) makes greenwashing measurably harder

The Earthly explainer is a vendor piece — Earthly sells verified nature-based credits — but the description of the SBTi framework itself is accurate and detailed. The vendor recommendations at the end should be read as commercial guidance, not neutral policy analysis.
