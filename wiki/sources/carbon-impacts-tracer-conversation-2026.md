# We can finally measure the damage each new coal, gas or oil project will do

**Source:** The Conversation, Claudia Stepin and co-authors, 28 September 2026. https://theconversation.com/we-can-finally-measure-the-damage-each-new-coal-gas-or-oil-project-will-do-to-people-and-nature-292128

## What is it

When a new coal mine or gas field is proposed, its backers often argue that its emissions are a **"drop in the ocean"**: tiny next to the global total, and impossible to link to any particular harm. A team of researchers from universities in Australia and the UK has built a free, open-source tool to answer that argument. The **Carbon Impacts Tracer** takes a project's published emissions, works out how much extra warming they cause, and then estimates the damage across eight areas, from lost crops and melting glaciers to heatwave deaths in Europe.

It rests on a simple, well-established relationship. Warming rises almost in a straight line with the total CO2 humans have emitted: about 0.45°C for every trillion tonnes, according to the IPCC. By 2019, cumulative emissions had reached about 2.4 trillion tonnes. So every extra tonne adds a small, calculable amount of warming, and published studies link that warming to specific harms.

## Why it matters

The tool turns an abstract "tiny share" into named consequences: so many more people exposed to dangerous heat, so many square kilometres of glacier lost. The authors expect it to be used by communities and lawyers in court cases, by insurers and by officials deciding whether to approve projects. In 2025 the International Court of Justice found that fossil fuel production and expansion "may constitute an internationally wrongful act", and accepted that a causal link between emissions and climate harm can be established. A tool that makes that link for a single project fits squarely into that legal shift.

The same logic answers the "my share is too small to matter" argument people use about their own emissions: small amounts add up in a predictable way.

## Key facts

- Woodside's Scarborough gas project in Western Australia: 876 million tonnes of CO2 would expose an extra 516,000 people worldwide to unprecedented heat and destroy millions more coral colonies in every future mass bleaching on the Great Barrier Reef.
- The same Scarborough emissions would cause 484 heat-related deaths in Europe by 2100.
- Woodside's proposed Browse to North West Shelf gas project would add 1,597 megatonnes of CO2 over its lifetime and warm the planet by 0.00072°C, enough for almost 200,000 more people to face an extra week of dangerous heat each year.
- ExxonMobil's projected emissions of 5,245 Mt between 2024 and 2050 mean about 0.0024°C of extra warming and an extra 127 km² of glacier melt.
- The tool covers five proposed Australian projects, one UK project and 16 major fossil fuel companies. Users can also enter any project, company or country.
- It only includes damage types backed by robust, reproducible studies, which is why it has eight for now. European heatwave deaths qualify; heatwave deaths in Australia do not yet.

## Limits of the tool

The damage figures are estimates built from published averages, so they show the expected scale of harm, not exact counts. Some damage areas are limited to places with strong research, such as Europe for heat deaths, so the totals understate global harm. The authors include some of the researchers behind the original Scarborough study.
