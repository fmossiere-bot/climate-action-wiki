# From pledges to action: can COP31 become the COP of implementation?

**Source:** We Don't Have Time, Sarah Chabane, November 2026. https://www.wedonthavetime.org/events/cop31

## What is it

A commentary by Sarah Chabane for the climate platform We Don't Have Time, published just before COP31 opened in Antalya. Ten years after the Paris Agreement, she argues, countries have made plenty of commitments but delivery is falling short. Her question is whether COP31 can be the summit that **turns pledges into policies, investment and infrastructure**. She walks through the unusual leadership, the fossil fuel interests of both hosts, and five themes to watch.

## Why it matters

The piece is a readable guide to what was at stake in Antalya, and it does not avoid the awkward parts. After Egypt, the UAE, Azerbaijan and Brazil, COP is again led by countries with large fossil fuel interests. Australia is the world's third-largest fossil fuel exporter, and Türkiye relies heavily on coal. Chabane asks whether such hosts can credibly lead a transition away from fossil fuels, which she calls one of the biggest unresolved political challenges in the talks.

## Two hosts, two tracks

Türkiye hosts the summit and holds the presidency, led by its environment minister Murat Kurum. Australia leads the formal negotiations through its climate and energy minister Chris Bowen, and wants to bring Pacific island voices to the table. The arrangement came about because both countries wanted to host. It also explains the two parallel tracks: the Action Agenda, with voluntary targets and pledges set by the presidency, and the negotiated outcomes, which need consensus and become formal COP decisions.

## Electrification and money

The presidency's flagship is "35 by 35": raising electricity's share of final energy use to 35% by 2035, replacing direct fossil fuel use in buildings, transport and industry. Samuel Guéret, an analyst at Climate Action Tracker, warns it "needs to be one of a suite of complementary actions that also includes scaling up renewables, improving energy efficiency and phasing out fossil fuels to limit the overshoot of 1.5°C and bring warming back below it." Finance is the other big test. COP29 in Baku set the New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG): $300 billion a year for developing countries by 2035, within a wider ambition of **$1.3 trillion a year**. Without that money, Chabane writes, many pledges will stay on paper.

## Zero waste, methane and a "Blue COP"

Türkiye has put zero waste and methane at the centre. Methane is the second-largest cause of global warming after carbon dioxide, responsible for about 30% of it, and the waste sector is its third-largest human source. Because methane breaks down much faster than CO2, cutting it slows warming quickly. COP31 aims to halve the projected growth in global waste by 2035, with a focus on food waste and better waste management. The hosts have also branded COP31 a "Blue COP", with ocean action among the ten priorities and a loggerhead sea turtle called Caretta as mascot. Some expect an Antalya Declaration on the Oceans.

## Roadmaps and coalitions

On 3 November 2026, just before COP31, Brazil's COP30 presidency launched two roadmaps that did not make it into the Belém outcome: one for transitioning away from fossil fuels, and one for halting and reversing deforestation by 2030. The Brazilian climate network Observatório do Clima said the fossil fuel roadmap "makes it clear that there are no longer any justifications for countries to rely on oil, gas and coal". The TAFF coalition, which grew out of the April 2026 Santa Marta conference attended by 57 countries, was expected to announce new members and present national roadmaps at COP31.

## Summary findings

- COP31 runs from 9 to 20 November 2026 in Antalya, with Türkiye holding the presidency and Australia leading negotiations.
- At COP30, 88 countries supported a roadmap away from fossil fuels, but petrostates blocked phaseout language from the final text.
- Australia is the world's third-largest fossil fuel exporter.
- The NCQG aims for $300 billion a year for developing countries by 2035, and $1.3 trillion a year from all sources.
- Methane causes about 30% of global warming, and waste is its third-largest human source.
- Brazil's fossil fuel and deforestation roadmaps were launched on 3 November 2026.
- Seven of the nine planetary boundaries are now crossed, wildlife populations have fallen by 73% in 52 years, and current policies could lead to 2.8°C of warming.
