# Positive Tipping Points, Part I: Urgency — Tim Lenton

## What it is

Plain-language summary of **Part I ("Urgency", Chapters 1-6)** of Tim Lenton's book *Positive Tipping Points*, prepared from photographs of the pages. **Lenton's core argument: step-by-step change on climate is now too slow; only reinforcing feedbacks in society, technology and nature — "positive tipping points" — can move fast enough, and history plus current data show they are real and can be triggered.** He calls himself a "conditional optimist": if enough people act, tipping points can flip; if they don't, the bad ones win. Lenton coined "tipping elements" with John Schellnhuber and originally listed 9 climate tipping points in 2008; the updated list has **16**, and **5 are already in the danger zone at today's ~1.25°C** of warming.

## Why it matters

Lenton offers the most detailed public map of where climate tipping points sit and what could trigger positive ones on the other side. The book's framework has become central to how European scientists and policymakers now talk about "abrupt change" — both in bad ways (AMOC, Greenland, Amazon) and good ones (Norwegian EVs, UK coal). It is the intellectual scaffolding behind campaigns for "five times faster" transitions.

## Tipping points as reinforcing feedback (Chapters 1-2)

A tipping point happens when the forces holding a system stable weaken and a **reinforcing feedback** takes over. After that, change is fast, hard to stop and hard to undo. Human-network shocks (heat dome 2021, Ever Given 2021, COVID, 2008 financial crisis) show small causes producing huge effects when networks amplify them. The same mechanics apply positively: **Norway's EVs went from 0.25% of new sales in 2010 to 84% in 2024** (S-curve growth); **UK coal fell from 40% of electricity in 2012 to 2% in 2019** once a carbon price made plants lose money; **wind and solar double every ~3 years worldwide**, and solar is now forecast to supply >50% of world electricity by mid-century. The three reinforcing feedbacks for new technology are **Wright's Law (learning by doing — costs fall ~20% per doubling)**, economies of scale, and supporting infrastructure. Norway's tax policy tipped EV buyers by removing the price premium.

## The climate tipping points already close (Chapter 4)

Sixteen elements, five in the danger zone at 1.25°C:
- **Greenland ice sheet:** melts, surface drops, warms further; holds **7 m of sea-level rise**
- **West Antarctica (Thwaites, Pine Island):** ice sits below sea level; **1.5°C could lose it all**, giving **>3 m of sea-level rise** over centuries
- **Winter Arctic sea ice** could tip at just over 1.5°C (Barents Sea first)
- **AMOC** slowed ~15% in 50 years; collapse would cool the UK, dry Europe, cut world tropical rain and halve wheat/maize belts
- **Subpolar gyre (North Atlantic)** could switch off within 10 years somewhere between 1.1-3.8°C — Lenton says "almost nobody has heard of it, but it may be one of the nearest tipping points"
- **Amazon:** already 18% cleared; warming plus deforestation could turn ~40% of what is left into dry savanna
- **Permafrost:** holds **twice all human CO2 emissions so far**; large-scale thaw likely at ~1.5°C, full collapse ≥4°C, would add ~10% to warming
- **Coral reefs: 70-90% lost at 1.5°C, almost all at 2°C** — 500 million people depend on them

## The climate niche and its migration (Chapter 5)

Humans have a "climate niche": most people live where the yearly average is ~13°C (London 11, New York 12, Beijing 13), with a second smaller group at 25-27°C (monsoon India). The niche has barely moved in 6,000 years. Warming is pushing it fast, mostly onto the world's poorest. **Every 0.1°C of extra warming puts >100 million more people into extreme heat.** At 1.5°C, ~400 million are exposed; at 2.7°C (current policies), ~2 billion — including >600 million in India and >300 million in Nigeria. **Holding at 1.5°C instead of 2.7°C cuts the exposed count by five** (six for India, seven for Nigeria). The unfairness: the lifetime emissions of just **1.2 average Americans** are enough to push one future person into extreme heat; the future losers emit half the world average.

## Averting disaster: the Titanic warning (Chapter 6)

Lenton compares the climate to the Titanic: **six ice warnings ignored, radio operators busy, ship turning too slowly**. Committed warming is 0.1-2°C even if emissions stop today. **If gas levels froze now, there is >80% chance of passing 1.5°C and >50% of passing 2°C.** Current policies point to ~2.7°C; every announced target met = ~1.8°C. None reach net zero.

- Carbon budget: **~250 Gt CO2 left for a 50:50 chance of 1.5°C at start of 2023**; gone before 2030 at current rate. Staying at 1.5°C requires **8% cuts every year** (COVID gave 5% for one year; only the Great Depression reached 10%)
- **1.7°C is possible but "extremely hard"**: 10%/year cuts across all sectors, halving by 2038, net zero ~2050. Simon Sharpe's *Five Times Faster* (7%/year) gives 1.7°C
- **Methane** could be halved in ten years; half those cuts cost nothing, over half of fossil methane cuts are profitable
- **Sulphur pollution ("a brake")** cools the planet ~as much as methane warms it, but kills ~5 million people/year. Cleaning it up removes the brake — so methane cuts must move faster

## Objections Lenton addresses head-on

- **"Changing fast costs too much."** The economy question is answered in Part II; the ethical question is whether money is the right measure when lives are at stake. On the Titanic, third-class passengers missed the lifeboats
- **"It will cause a financial crash."** Fossil assets losing value is a reason to make finance stronger, not to keep burning
- **"Geoengineering will fix it."** Solar geoengineering "changes the climate in new ways", causes new political disputes, and does not fix the cause. Lenton notes it but leaves it out of the book

## Summary findings

- **5 of 16 climate tipping elements** already in the danger zone at 1.25°C; every reassessment raises the risk
- **AMOC has slowed ~15% in 50 years**; collapse would end farming in the UK and cut world grain belts
- **Every 0.1°C of warming puts >100 million more people into extreme heat**
- **1.5°C vs 2.7°C** cuts the extreme-heat exposed count by 5× globally, 6× for India, 7× for Nigeria
- Norway EVs 0.25% → 84% in 14 years; UK coal 40% → 2% in 7 years; wind and solar **double every 3 years**
- **~250 Gt CO2 budget** for 1.5°C at start of 2023; **8%/year cuts needed** (COVID gave 5%)
- **Sulphur cooling brake:** cleaning air removes ~0.4°C of hidden warming — makes methane cuts more urgent, not less
