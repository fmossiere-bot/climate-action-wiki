# The World Cup Story (Almost) No One Told

**Source:** Covering Climate Now, 23 July 2026 — https://coveringclimatenow.org/from-us-story/the-world-cup-story-almost-no-one-told/

## What it is

A media-criticism piece from the journalism collaborative Covering Climate Now, arguing that most US coverage of the 2026 World Cup's new mid-half hydration breaks failed to explain the climate science behind them, with one exception: Telemundo's environment reporter Vanessa Hauc.

## Key facts cited

- 2026 was the **first World Cup in 96 years** to add mandatory hydration breaks mid-half
- **FIFPRO** (the players' union) commissioned a scientific study on extreme heat and player health/performance after brutal conditions at the 2025 FIFA Club World Cup, where a referee fainted on the field; Argentina's Enzo Fernandez described lying on the ground "really dizzy," calling play in that heat "very dangerous"
- FIFPRO recommended breaks at a **wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) of 26°C (~79°F)** and suspending play at **28°C (~82°F)**; FIFA wanted breaks only at **32°C (~90°F)**; hydration breaks were the eventual compromise
- On-air example: during Argentina vs Cape Verde in Miami (3 July), game-time temperature of 89°F "can feel closer to 101°F" with humidity and sun factored in; Colombia vs Ghana in Kansas City the same evening was 88°F at 70% humidity
- Wet-bulb heat "makes it harder for the body to cool itself, causing core body temperature to rise and increasing the risk of exhaustion, dehydration, and even heat stroke"
- Other sports adapting: professional tennis allows cooling breaks above 30°C WBGT; the 2026 LA Marathon let runners officially finish at mile 18 instead of 26 due to extreme heat
- FIFA president Gianni Infantino denied hydration breaks were commercially motivated, saying "no additional revenue for FIFA" since TV deals were signed in advance — CCNow calls this technically true but incomplete, since the extra ad minutes raise the value of future World Cup TV contracts
- Yale Program on Climate Change Communication (survey released ~July 2026): 74% of Americans are interested in climate change news stories; Latino audiences have long polled as more concerned about climate change than the general US population
- Wider noteworthy items in the same CCNow briefing: rapid-attribution studies found human-caused warming responsible for **40-70% of the death toll** in recent big European heatwaves; wildfire smoke is estimated to cause **1.5 million deaths a year globally**; the 2023 Canadian wildfire season alone was linked to an estimated 80,000 smoke-related deaths, 20,000 of them in Europe

Adds named thresholds (FIFPRO's 26°C/28°C WBGT proposal vs FIFA's 32°C) and specific 2026 World Cup match-day heat data to the wiki's existing World Cup heat coverage, plus a concrete media-literacy example (Telemundo) of how to report the climate connection responsibly.
