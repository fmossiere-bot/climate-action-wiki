# Infantino's private jet at the 2026 World Cup (BBC Verify, 2026)

**Source:** BBC Verify / BBC Sport, Jake Horton, Tom Shiel, Ciaran Varley and Katie Gornall, 28 June 2026. https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/articles/cgev5wy0zg3o

## What it is

BBC Verify tracked a private jet linked to FIFA and its president, Gianni Infantino, during the group stage of the 2026 World Cup. Using FlightRadar24 flight data and published photographs, the team matched the plane's movements to the matches Infantino was confirmed to have attended.

Over 16 days, from 11 to 27 June, Infantino went to 24 matches. The jet made 27 flights that lined up with where he was, covering at least 31,144 miles (50,122 km). The BBC estimates this produced about **516 tonnes of CO2e**, the same as the yearly greenhouse gas footprint of 78 average people.

## Why it matters

Football fans are often asked to travel by train, recycle at stadiums or accept hydration breaks in extreme heat. Seeing the head of the sport fly between matches by private jet sits uneasily with that.

It also tests FIFA's own promises. The organisation has pledged to halve its emissions by 2030 and reach net zero by 2040, and it has already been rebuked once by a Swiss regulator over a "carbon neutral" claim. How its leaders travel is a visible sign of whether those pledges are being taken seriously.

## Key facts

- Infantino attended 24 matches in 16 days (11 to 27 June). The jet made 27 flights matching his confirmed locations.
- Total distance: at least 31,144 miles (50,122 km), with more than 66 hours in the air.
- Estimated emissions: about 516 tonnes of CO2e over the group stage, equal to 78 people's average yearly greenhouse gas footprint.
- Longest flight: Vancouver to Miami, 2,800 miles (4,507 km), on 13 June. Shortest: Philadelphia to Teterboro, 92 miles (148 km).
- The aircraft is believed to be a Gulfstream G650ER, which burns about 1,817 litres of fuel an hour.
- Private jets are 5 to 14 times more polluting than commercial planes and 50 times more than trains, according to Denise Auclair of Transport & Environment.
- A 2025 report by SGR estimated that the overall carbon footprint of the 2026 World Cup could reach 9 million tonnes of CO2e, almost double the average of the past four World Cups.

## FIFA's position and record

FIFA said its president's travel is "sometimes on commercial airlines, sometimes on private charter, depending on which is more efficient". It did not confirm the type of plane, how many passengers were on board, or whether the emissions were offset.

FIFA's own targets are a 50% cut in emissions by 2030 and net zero by 2040. In 2023, a Swiss regulator found that FIFA had made "false statements" when it called the Qatar 2022 World Cup "carbon neutral".

## Voices in the piece

Freddie Daley, of Cool Down and the University of Sussex, works on the climate impact of sport. He described Infantino's private jet use as "symptomatic of FIFA's failings on the environment".
