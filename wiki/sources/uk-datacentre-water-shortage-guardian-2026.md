# Not enough water for UK's datacentre plans, trade body says

**Source:** The Guardian, Pippa Neill, 21 July 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jul/21/not-enough-water-for-uks-datacentre-plans-trade-body-says

## What it is

The Guardian reports on a written submission to MPs from **Water UK**, the trade body for water companies in Britain and Northern Ireland. The submission is public. It calls the government's water forecasts "fatally flawed" because they leave out the water that datacentres will need.

The submission responds to the government's AI growth zones policy. It warns: "There appears to be an assumption that the country will always have enough water for its economic needs. Nothing could be further from the truth."

## Why it matters

In the south-east of England, datacentres and households could end up competing for the same drinking water. Under Environment Agency permits, datacentres currently have to use drinking water rather than recycled water. Some 125 datacentres are planned in a single water company's area. And the government has not yet set a drought hierarchy, a formal order of who gets water first when supplies run short.

Datacentres have also been named critical national infrastructure. That label could put them ahead of households during a drought, without any public debate about whether that is the right choice.

Ireland faces a similar tension, with its [concentration of data centres around Dublin](../ireland-hub/IRELAND%20Data%20Centre%20Dilemna.md). In the UK, the [Slough heat-island research](slough-datacentre-heat-island-2026.md) has already looked at the heat datacentres give off, and this report adds water to the picture.

## Key facts

- Water UK submitted written evidence to MPs in June/July 2026. The Environment Agency's national framework for water resources (June 2025) also left out datacentre demand.
- 125 datacentres are proposed or under construction in the Affinity Water region alone. Affinity serves Slough, which has the UK's highest concentration of datacentres, and much of the region is under a hosepipe ban.
- Single datacentres in the Affinity region are asking for up to **3 million litres of water a day**, roughly the peak demand of 3,500 homes.
- Datacentres in England currently use about 6.6 million litres a day. The government wants to triple datacentre capacity by 2030, which would mean about 19.8 million litres a day.
- A May 2026 House of Lords report found England faces a public water supply shortfall of 5 billion litres a day by 2055. The current shortfall is already 220 million litres a day more than forecasts assumed. It is being covered by "accepting a greater risk of environmental harm and restrictions on water use in the event of drought".
- More than three-quarters of UK datacentres are in the south and east of England, the regions shortest of water.
- Water UK says cutting leaks is not enough on its own, and a new major reservoir takes about 15 years to plan and build. Water companies have cut leakage by about 40% since privatisation and plan a further 17% cut by 2030. Even so, Greenpeace (July 2026) finds they still leak 5× more water than a national hosepipe ban would save.
- During the June 2026 heatwave, an estimated 440 people a day died from heat in England and Wales.

## Voices in the piece

Jon Chappel, deputy director at Water UK, calls the failure to include datacentres in the government's forecasts "unforgivable" and "a genuine failing of government." Water UK has asked ministers for a drought hierarchy, but none has been published.

Oliver Hayes, head of big tech at the campaign group Global Action Plan, points to the critical national infrastructure label. He warns that water companies "may be powerless to restrict their water use as a result". That could leave households lower down the queue than datacentres "running chatbots, or enabling people to do benign and very wasteful, pointless things".

Affinity Water told MPs in May 2026: "The planning framework is built around household growth and per-capita consumption, and has not adapted to commercial users whose water needs can match those of a market town."

## The government's response

A government spokesperson said: "The environment and sustainable development must go hand-in-hand and we will protect our water supply while unlocking growth, including looking at alternatives or ways to minimise impact on supplies and output of wastewater from datacentres." The government points to record investment in infrastructure and nine new reservoirs.

The national policy statement for AI growth, which could settle some of these questions, was expected in February 2026 but has been delayed.
