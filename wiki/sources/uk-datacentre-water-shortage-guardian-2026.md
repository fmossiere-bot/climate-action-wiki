# Not enough water for UK's datacentre plans, trade body says

**Source:** The Guardian, Pippa Neill, 21 July 2026
**URL:** https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jul/21/not-enough-water-for-uks-datacentre-plans-trade-body-says

## What it is

Reporting on a **publicly available written submission to MPs** from **Water UK** — the trade body representing water companies in Britain and Northern Ireland — describing the UK government's forecasts as **"fatally flawed"** because they **explicitly exclude datacentre water demand**. The submission, tied to the government's AI growth zones policy, says: *"There appears to be an assumption that the country will always have enough water for its economic needs. Nothing could be further from the truth."*

## Key facts cited

- **Water UK** submitted written evidence to MPs (parliamentary written evidence, June/July 2026)
- The Environment Agency's **national framework for water resources** (June 2025) also failed to include datacentre demand
- **125 datacentres** are proposed or under construction in the **Affinity Water** region alone (which serves Slough — the UK's highest concentration of datacentres); much of the region is under a hosepipe ban
- Individual datacentres in the Affinity region are requesting up to **3 million litres of water per day** — roughly the **peak water demand of 3,500 homes**
- Because of Environment Agency permitting, **datacentres are currently required to use drinking water only**
- **Current datacentre water use in England: ~6.6 million litres per day**
- Government ambition to triple datacentre capacity by 2030 → **~19.8 million litres per day**
- **May 2026 House of Lords report**: England faces a **5 billion litres per day public water supply shortfall by 2055**
- England's current shortfall is already **220 million litres per day more than water forecasts assumed** — met by "accepting a greater risk of environmental harm and restrictions on water use in the event of drought"
- **More than three-quarters** of UK datacentres are located in the **south and east of England** — the most water-stressed regions
- Water UK: reducing leakage is not enough on its own; a new major reservoir takes **~15 years to plan and build**
- Water companies have **cut leakage by ~40% since privatisation** and plan a further **17% reduction by 2030** — but **Greenpeace (July 2026)** finds water companies still leak **5× more water than a national hosepipe ban would save**
- **June 2026 heatwave**: an estimated **440 people per day** died from heat in England and Wales

## Voices

- **Jon Chappel** (Water UK deputy director): the failure to include datacentres in government water forecasts is *"unforgivable"* and *"a genuine failing of government."* Water UK has asked for a **drought hierarchy** — a formal ordering of which sectors get water first in a shortage — but ministers have not published one
- **Oliver Hayes** (Global Action Plan, head of big tech): datacentres have been designated **critical national infrastructure**, which could mean water companies "may be powerless to restrict their water use as a result" — leaving households at risk of being deprioritised in favour of datacentres *"running chatbots, or enabling people to do benign and very wasteful, pointless things"*
- **Affinity Water** to MPs (May 2026): *"The planning framework is built around household growth and per-capita consumption, and has not adapted to commercial users whose water needs can match those of a market town."*

## Government response

A government spokesperson: *"The environment and sustainable development must go hand-in-hand and we will protect our water supply while unlocking growth, including looking at alternatives or ways to minimise impact on supplies and output of wastewater from datacentres."* Points to record levels of infrastructure investment and nine new reservoirs.

The **national policy statement for AI growth** was expected in February 2026 but has been delayed.

## Why it matters

The story lands three specific policy hooks:

1. **Datacentres and households will compete for drinking water in the south-east of England** — because Environment Agency permitting requires datacentres to use drinking water, not recycled water; because 125 proposed datacentres are in one water company's patch; and because a drought hierarchy has not been set
2. **The "critical national infrastructure" designation may put datacentres ahead of households** in a drought without any public debate about that trade-off
3. **This is not just a UK story** — Ireland has hit similar tension ([Dublin data-centre concentration](../ireland-hub/DRAFT - IRELAND Data Centre Dilemna.md)), and the [Slough heat-island research](slough-datacentre-heat-island-2026.md) already documented the electricity side. Now the water side lines up.

## Tone and angle

Business/environment reporting: direct, quotes-heavy, sourced to written parliamentary evidence rather than press releases. Structural framing: **infrastructure planning is not keeping up with what AI actually requires**, and the government has not yet published the policy statement that would force it to.
