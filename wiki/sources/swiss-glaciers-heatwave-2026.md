# Swiss glaciers facing 'enormous' loss from heatwave

**Source:** RTÉ News / AFP, 27 June 2026. https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0627/1580624-glaciers-heatwave/

## What it is

A short news report on what Europe's June 2026 heatwave did to Swiss glaciers. It draws on Matthias Huss, head of GLAMOS (Glacier Monitoring in Switzerland). He warned that all the snow that built up on the glaciers over the winter of 2025–26 would be gone by 30 June. That makes it the second-earliest **"glacier loss day"** on record, after 2022. The glacier loss day is the date when a glacier has lost its winter snow and starts eating into its own ice.

## Why it matters

The Swiss Alps feed two of Europe's great rivers, the Rhine and the Rhone. Millions of people downstream rely on that water for drinking, farming, shipping and power.

Once the winter snow has gone, the darker bare ice underneath soaks up more sunlight and melts faster. Each summer that starts early makes the next one worse. Huss puts it plainly: a single heatwave does not destroy a glacier on its own. What does the damage is the build-up of long spells of high temperatures, year after year.

## Key facts

- The glacier loss day was expected around 30 June 2026, about three months earlier than in a healthy year, when it usually falls in mid-August.
- The only earlier loss day on record was 26 June 2022.
- The Rhone Glacier lost **1 metre** of ice in height in just 10 days during the heatwave.
- 25% less snow fell to replenish the glaciers this winter than the 2010–2020 average.
- Saharan dust that settled in March sped up the melt. Dust darkens the ice, so it reflects less sunlight and absorbs more heat.
- The volume of Swiss glaciers shrank by 38% between 2000 and 2024.
- Switzerland has already lost 1,200 glaciers in 50 years, and only about 1,300 remain.
- If warming continues at the current rate, only small remnants of Swiss glaciers will be left by 2100.
