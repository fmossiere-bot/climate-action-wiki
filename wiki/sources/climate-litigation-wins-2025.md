# Greenwashing, illegality and false claims: 13 climate litigation wins in 2025

**Source:** The Guardian, Isabella Kaminski, 31 December 2025. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/dec/31/greenwashing-illegality-false-claims-climate-litigation-wins-2025

## What it is

In 2025 it had been ten years since the Urgenda case, when a court first ordered the Netherlands to cut its carbon emissions faster. Since then, climate cases have spread around the world. This Guardian round-up looks back at 13 wins from 2025 alone.

They cover a wide range: companies caught greenwashing, oil, gas and coal projects approved unlawfully, international courts setting out what governments owe their citizens, and states pushed into stronger climate plans. The list in the article actually runs to 14 entries, set out below.

## Why it matters

Courts are becoming one of the places where climate promises get tested. A company can say its watch is "carbon neutral" or that it will reach net zero, and a judge can now ask it to prove it. Governments can approve an oilfield, and a court can send the decision back because nobody counted the emissions from burning the oil.

For ordinary people, this matters in two ways. Marketing claims are getting more trustworthy because they can be challenged. And citizens, from a Peruvian farmer to a group of young people in Hawaii, have found they can use the law to hold big emitters and their own governments to account.

## Key facts

- Two UK North Sea fields, **Rosebank and Jackdaw**, had their approval ruled unlawful because the emissions from burning the oil and gas (called scope 3 emissions) were not counted.
- EnergyAustralia apologised to 400,000 customers and admitted that carbon offsets do not undo greenhouse gas emissions.
- JBS paid $1.1m to the state of New York to settle a meat greenwashing case.
- Hawaii agreed a roadmap to zero-emissions transport by 2045.
- The UK government's revised climate plan commits to decarbonising electricity by 2030.
- 2025 was ten years after the Urgenda ruling in the Netherlands.

## The wins

- **Rosebank and Jackdaw ruled illegal (UK).** A Scottish court ruled the UK government's approval of these two North Sea oil and gas fields unlawful, because it ignored scope 3 (end-use) emissions from burning the fuel.
- **Brazil's largest coal plant scrapped.** Copelmi withdrew its Nova Seival plant and Guaíba mine plans, saying the project had become unfeasible after its licences were suspended on climate grounds.
- **German court opens the door to climate damages (RWE case).** A Peruvian farmer's decade-long case against RWE was rejected, but it set a precedent that big polluters can be liable for their emissions. Pakistani farmers have since launched a similar case.
- **EnergyAustralia greenwashing settlement (Australia).** The company settled with Parents for Climate, accepted that carbon offsets do not undo greenhouse gas emissions and apologised to 400,000 customers. It was the first case of its kind in Australia.
- **Inter-American court: a human right to a stable climate.** The court found that states have a duty to protect the human right to a healthy climate.
- **International Court of Justice advisory opinion.** Countries must prevent harm to the climate system, and failing to do so could mean paying compensation.
- **New South Wales coalmine expansion annulled.** A court blocked the Mount Pleasant Optimisation expansion because it did not account for scope 3 emissions, from coal exported and burnt abroad.
- **Apple banned from calling its Watch "carbon neutral" (Germany).** A Frankfurt court ruled that Apple could not back up the claim. Apple then dropped the label in other markets too.
- **Hawaii transport emissions roadmap.** The state delivered a roadmap to zero-emissions transport by 2045, as part of a settlement with young climate campaigners who had taken it to court.
- **Kenya's Lamu coal plant cancelled.** A decade of litigation ended with Amu Power's licence revoked on environmental assessment grounds.
- **TotalEnergies greenwashing conviction (France).** A Paris court ruled that TotalEnergies's "reinvention" advertising campaign misled people about reaching net zero by 2050 while the company kept producing fossil fuels.
- **JBS and Tyson meat greenwashing settlements (US).** JBS paid $1.1m to New York. Tyson agreed to drop its net zero claims after a lawsuit by the Environmental Working Group.
- **Three Norwegian oilfields ruled illegal.** The Borgarting court of appeal declared the licences for Breidablikk, Yggdrasil and Tyrving unlawful for not accounting for their full climate impact.
- **UK government forced to publish a tougher climate plan.** After the original plan was ruled unlawful, a revised carbon budget and delivery plan were published, committing to decarbonise electricity by 2030.
