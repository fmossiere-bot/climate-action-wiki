# Switzerland has lost 20% of its glaciers in five years — GLAMOS annual report

**Source:** Guardian, Ajit Niranjan, 1 October 2026 — https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/oct/01/switzerland-glacier-loss-fossil-fuel-pollution-europe
**Primary source:** Swiss Glacier Monitoring Network (GLAMOS) annual report, collaboration with the Swiss Commission for Cryosphere Observation. Report: https://www.glamos.ch/en/#/A10g-05

## What it is

Guardian coverage of the **2026 annual report from the Swiss Glacier Monitoring Network (GLAMOS)**, co-authored by lead glaciologist **Matthias Huss (ETH Zurich)**. **The single headline number: Swiss glaciers have lost 19.4% of their ice volume in just five years (since 2021), with 2026 alone contributing a 5.5% drop in the first eight months of the year — just short of the record set in 2022.** The piece pairs the hard GLAMOS numbers with field reportage from the Rhône and Scex Rouge glaciers and introduces a new monitoring finding: **many Swiss glaciers are no longer just retreating from the edges — they are collapsing from inside**, as subglacial streams excavate caverns ("cheese holes") that cause the ice above to cave in.

## Key facts

- **Switzerland has lost 19.4% of its glacier ice since 2021** — about one-fifth in five years
- **2026 alone: 5.5% of ice volume lost in the first eight months** (just under the 2022 record)
- **Mean thickness of individual glaciers fell by 2.5 to 4 metres in 2026**
- **The Plattalvafirn (Griess glacier)** lost **99% of its area in just 20 years** — Huss visited the former site in September 2026, describing it as a "moonlike landscape"
- **Europe is warming twice as fast as the global average** — Alpine regions among the most dramatic
- **MeteoSwiss finding (October 2026):** the April-September 2026 half-year was the **hottest and driest since Swiss records began in 1864**
- **Humanity has emitted 1.8 trillion tonnes of CO2** cumulatively and warmed the planet by **~1.4°C**
- **Why 2026 missed the 2022 record despite worse heat**: limited Saharan dust deposits (dust foul the snow and prevents it reflecting sunlight), slightly more snow at end of winter, more sunlight in summer

## New mechanism: internal collapse

Huss introduces a shift in how glaciers are failing: increasingly, melting is **initiated inside the glacier**, not just at the surface. Subglacial streams excavate caverns under the ice. The roof above those caverns eventually collapses, tearing the glacier apart faster than surface-melt alone would.

- **"You have huge holes like cheese in the middle of the ice that have collapsing roofs. These are processes that tear the glaciers apart and make them retreat even faster."** (Huss)
- This echoes the earlier September 2026 Alec Luhn / New Scientist field report on the Rhône glacier, and the ETH fibre-optic crevasse study (July 2026) which found **8% of a Valais glacier's volume was empty crevasses** hidden up to 25 m down

## Notable quotes

- **Matthias Huss** (ETH Zurich): *"I visited one last week and it's so impressive to stand in this moonlike landscape where there was a big layer of ice — an extensive glacier — just a few years ago. And now it's a rock desert where there's some scattered blocks of old ice still lying around and melting on the hot rocks."*
- **Huss on failure mode:** *"You have huge holes like cheese in the middle of the ice that have collapsing roofs."*
- **Huss on long-term perspective:** *"What was different at that time [the 1950s], is that the rates of retreat were still kind of at a normal level. What we are seeing now is a complete explosion of these melt rates, leading to the disintegration of glaciers. They're really just falling apart."*

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **The 19.4%-in-five-years number is a step-change framing.** Earlier wiki content covered individual-year losses (2022's record, Rhône glacier's 30 Mt in 2026). A one-fifth-of-the-national-ice-in-five-years figure is a cumulative-loss number that reframes the trajectory: Switzerland is not slowly losing glaciers, it is losing them fast enough that the five-year horizon alone is a meaningful denominator.
2. **The "internal collapse" mechanism changes failure modes.** If subglacial-cavern collapse is becoming common, glaciers can fail suddenly in ways that surface-only monitoring will miss. This has consequences for both drought planning (less meltwater downstream than models assume) and disaster planning (more Blatten-type collapses).
3. **MeteoSwiss's "hottest and driest since 1864" line is specific and unambiguous.** 160+ years of records; this is not a rhetorical flourish. It also arrives alongside Europe's 2026 heatwave summer already documented in the wiki.

## Caveats

- **One country.** Swiss glacier loss is dramatic but Switzerland is not representative of global mountain glacier loss — rates in the tropical Andes, Scandinavia and Canada vary.
- **One year within a trajectory.** 2026 came in just below 2022, partly because Saharan dust was limited. The underlying heat was worse; the specific sunlight and dust conditions created a near-record rather than an outright one.
- **The "cheese hole" collapse mechanism is new enough that quantified global implications are still being worked out.** The ETH fibre-optic study showing 8% of one Valais glacier is empty crevasses is suggestive, not yet systematic.

## Tone and angle

Strong Guardian field reporting with Huss as the primary scientific voice. The "moonlike rock desert" framing is Huss's own; the Griess-glacier-99%-in-20-years anchor is the hardest-hitting single case. The piece ends on his "complete explosion of melt rates" quote and the Mauro Werder-adjacent framing ("we really should just reduce CO2 fast") that closed the earlier Luhn piece.
