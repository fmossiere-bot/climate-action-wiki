# Switzerland has lost 20% of its glaciers in five years: GLAMOS annual report

**Source:** Guardian, Ajit Niranjan, 1 October 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/oct/01/switzerland-glacier-loss-fossil-fuel-pollution-europe
**Primary source:** Swiss Glacier Monitoring Network (GLAMOS) annual report, produced with the Swiss Commission for Cryosphere Observation. https://www.glamos.ch/en/#/A10g-05

## What it is

The Guardian reports on the 2026 annual report from the Swiss Glacier Monitoring Network (GLAMOS), co-written by its lead glaciologist, Matthias Huss of ETH Zurich. Swiss glaciers have lost **19.4% of their ice** in just five years, since 2021. In the first eight months of 2026 alone they lost 5.5%, just short of the record set in 2022.

The article mixes these figures with reporting from the Rhône and Scex Rouge glaciers. It also describes a change in how glaciers are breaking up. Many are no longer only shrinking from the edges. They are collapsing from the inside, as meltwater streams under the ice carve out caves that eventually cave in.

## Why it matters

Earlier coverage looked at single years, such as the 2022 record or the 30 Mt the Rhône glacier lost in 2026. Losing about a fifth of the country's ice in five years shows the overall direction more clearly. Switzerland is not losing its glaciers slowly over generations. It is losing them fast enough to see within a few years.

The collapse from inside matters for the people who live below the ice. Glaciers that hollow out can fail suddenly, in ways that monitoring the surface alone will miss. That affects drought planning, as there may be less meltwater downstream than models expect, and disaster planning, as more collapses like the one at Blatten become possible. Mountain glaciers are one of the 16 tipping elements in Tim Lenton's work on climate tipping points.

The weather record adds weight. MeteoSwiss found that April to September 2026 was the hottest and driest half-year since Swiss records began in 1864, more than 160 years ago. It came during a summer of heatwaves across Europe.

## Key facts

- Switzerland has lost 19.4% of its glacier ice since 2021, about one fifth in five years.
- In 2026 alone, 5.5% of the ice was lost in the first eight months, just under the 2022 record.
- The average thickness of individual glaciers fell by **2.5 to 4 metres** in 2026.
- The Plattalvafirn (Griess glacier) lost 99% of its area in just 20 years. Huss visited the site in September 2026 and described a "moonlike landscape".
- Europe is warming twice as fast as the global average, and the Alps are among the hardest-hit regions.
- MeteoSwiss (October 2026): April to September 2026 was the hottest and driest half-year since Swiss records began in 1864.
- Humanity has emitted 1.8 trillion tonnes of CO2 in total and warmed the planet by about 1.4°C.
- 2026 missed the 2022 record despite worse heat for three reasons: less Saharan dust (dust darkens snow so it absorbs more sunlight), slightly more snow left at the end of winter, and more sunlight in summer.

## Glaciers collapsing from inside

Huss describes a shift in how glaciers are failing. More and more, melting starts inside the glacier as well as on its surface. Streams beneath the ice dig out caverns, and in time the roofs of those caverns fall in. This tears the glacier apart faster than surface melting alone would. In his words: "You have huge holes like cheese in the middle of the ice that have collapsing roofs. These are processes that tear the glaciers apart and make them retreat even faster."

This matches earlier findings. In September 2026, Alec Luhn reported for New Scientist from the Rhône glacier. In July 2026, an ETH study using fibre-optic cables found that **8% of the volume** of one glacier in Valais was empty crevasses, hidden up to 25 m below the surface.

## Voices in the piece

Matthias Huss leads Switzerland's glacier monitoring, so he has watched these losses year by year. Describing the Griess glacier site, he said: "I visited one last week and it's so impressive to stand in this moonlike landscape where there was a big layer of ice, an extensive glacier, just a few years ago. And now it's a rock desert where there's some scattered blocks of old ice still lying around and melting on the hot rocks."

Comparing today with earlier periods of retreat, he said: "What was different at that time [the 1950s], is that the rates of retreat were still kind of at a normal level. What we are seeing now is a complete explosion of these melt rates, leading to the disintegration of glaciers. They're really just falling apart."

## Limits of the figures

These are figures for one country. Swiss losses are severe, but Switzerland does not represent mountain glaciers worldwide, and rates differ in the tropical Andes, Scandinavia and Canada. The 2026 total is also one year in a longer trend: it came in just below 2022 partly because there was little Saharan dust, even though the underlying heat was worse. Finally, the collapse-from-inside process is new enough that its wider effects are still being worked out. The ETH finding of 8% empty crevasses in one Valais glacier is a strong hint, not yet a systematic result.
