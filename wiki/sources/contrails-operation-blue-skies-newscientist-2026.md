# Planes flying over the Atlantic will be re-routed to avoid contrails

**Source:** New Scientist, Madeleine Cuff, 18 August 2026
**Companion source:** BBC News, Shiona McCallum, 18 August 2026 — same trial, covered in [contrails-operation-blue-skies-bbc-2026.md](contrails-operation-blue-skies-bbc-2026.md)

## What it is

New Scientist's account of the same **Operation Blue Skies** trial covered by the BBC on the same day, with a sharper technical framing from the research side. Marc Shapiro of Contrails.org, a research organisation participating in the project, explains the forecasting approach: predicting where persistent, climate-warming contrails will form and re-routing planes around those regions.

## Key facts cited (additional detail beyond the BBC piece)

- Contrails reflect solar radiation during the day (a cooling effect) but also trap outgoing heat (a warming effect) — the **net effect is warming**, and is estimated at around a third of aviation's total climate impact
- Trial starts **November 2026**; air traffic controllers in the Shanwick airspace will advise altitude changes to avoid contrail-forming conditions
- The £5m project is described as the **world's first airspace-scale contrail avoidance trial** — previous work informed individual airline flight plans before take-off, but never applied to an entire, non-opt-out airspace
- Confirms the same figures as the BBC piece: ~5% of global contrail warming from Shanwick; 2,000ft/600m typical altitude adjustment; ~100kg extra fuel burn per re-routed aircraft; 50-60% contrail formation cuts in prior single-airline trials
- Researchers from the University of Cambridge and Imperial College London will analyse trial data
