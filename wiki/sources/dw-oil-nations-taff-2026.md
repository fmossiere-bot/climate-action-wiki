# Oil nations on edge in the face of new climate coalition

**Source:** DW (Deutsche Welle), Tim Schauenberg, 18 June 2026. https://www.dw.com/en/oil-nations-on-edge-in-the-face-of-new-climate-coalition/a-77607010

## What it is

A report from the June 2026 Bonn climate talks. Every year, governments meet in Bonn for two weeks of technical negotiations that prepare the agenda for the main UN climate summit, the COP.

This year's talks sat between two big moments. At **COP30 in Belém**, in November 2025, governments failed to agree a roadmap for phasing out fossil fuels. COP31 follows in Turkey in November 2026.

DW's angle is that the war between the US and Iran, and the energy price shock that came with it, has changed the political mood. A new group, the Transition Away From Fossil Fuels (TAFF) initiative, launched at Santa Marta in April 2026, is starting to worry oil-producing states.

## Why it matters

For years the fight at UN summits was over whether the words "fossil fuels" could even appear in the final text. The question now is whether a group of countries can form an alliance that actually phases them out. This piece shows the nervousness that alliance is causing inside the UN process.

It also shows a real disagreement with African negotiators. They worry that a smaller coalition working outside the full UN process could weaken the leverage poorer countries have when everyone negotiates together. Meanwhile, the energy crisis acts as a test. Some governments used it to speed up the switch to electricity. Others used it to keep fossil fuels cheap.

## Key facts

- The Bonn talks in June 2026 ran for two weeks, preparing for COP31 in Turkey in November 2026.
- At COP30 in Brazil, a phaseout roadmap was blocked in particular by Saudi Arabia, Iran, China, Russia, Tanzania and Senegal.
- In response, TAFF met in Colombia in April 2026 with about 60 countries. It was hosted by Colombia and the Netherlands and joined by Brazil, Australia, Norway and Germany. Talks on how TAFF should be organised continued behind closed doors at Bonn.
- A New Climate Institute review found that the EU, Chile, Indonesia and Vietnam used the crisis to speed up electrification. Germany and others fell back on tax relief for fossil fuels.
- COP30 agreed to triple adaptation finance by 2035, but the starting point is disputed. Tripling from about $20bn (the 2019 level) or from about $40bn (the 2025 level) leads to very different totals.

## Voices in the piece

Simon Stiell, the UN's climate chief, says the Iran war has "sparked a fossil fuel cost crisis that's strangling economies everywhere". Fatih Birol, executive director of the International Energy Agency, says the crisis has exposed how vulnerable a system built on fossil fuel markets and fragile supply chains really is.

Antwi Boasiako Amoah, Africa's lead negotiator, warns that TAFF risks splitting the shared UN process. African positions on phaseout are complicated: some countries export fossil fuels, while others rely on them to bring energy to people who still lack it. On money, Amoah is clear that Africa cannot pay for adaptation by taking on more debt. It needs public support and international climate finance, not loans.

André Correa do Lago, the outgoing COP30 President, sums up the shift he sees: "We are moving from COPs focused on negotiations to COPs focused on implementation".
