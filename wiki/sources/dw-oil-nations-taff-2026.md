# Oil nations on edge in the face of new climate coalition

**Source:** DW (Deutsche Welle), Tim Schauenberg, 18 June 2026
**URL:** https://www.dw.com/en/oil-nations-on-edge-in-the-face-of-new-climate-coalition/a-77607010

## What it is

Reporting from the **June 2026 Bonn climate intersessional**, the two-week technical meeting that prepares the agenda for the annual COP. This year's Bonn negotiations sit between two hinge moments: the **COP30 Belém talks in November 2025**, where governments failed to agree a fossil fuel phaseout roadmap, and **COP31 in Turkey in November 2026**. DW's angle is that the war between the US and Iran, and the resulting energy price shock, has changed the political mood — and that a new coalition, the **Transition Away From Fossil Fuels (TAFF) initiative** launched at Santa Marta in April 2026, is starting to worry oil-producing states.

## Key facts cited

- The **Bonn talks (June 2026)** ran for two weeks, preparing for COP31 in Turkey (November 2026)
- UN climate chief **Simon Stiell**: the Iran war has "sparked a fossil fuel cost crisis that's strangling economies everywhere"
- COP30 in Brazil failed to agree a phaseout roadmap; blocked in particular by **Saudi Arabia, Iran, China, Russia, Tanzania and Senegal**
- In response, the **Transition Away From Fossil Fuels (TAFF)** initiative met in Colombia in April 2026 with ~60 countries; hosted by Colombia and the Netherlands and joined by Brazil, Australia, Norway and Germany
- Discussions about future structures for TAFF continued behind closed doors at Bonn
- **African lead negotiator Antwi Boasiako Amoah** warned that TAFF risks fragmenting the multilateral process; African positions on phaseout remain complex because some countries export fossil fuels and others rely on them for energy-access finance
- IEA executive director **Fatih Birol**: the crisis has exposed the vulnerability of a system dependent on fossil markets and fragile supply chains
- New Climate Institute review: EU, Chile, Indonesia and Vietnam used the crisis to accelerate electrification; Germany and others fell back on tax relief for fossil fuels
- Outgoing COP30 President **André Correa do Lago**: "We are moving from COPs focused on negotiations to COPs focused on implementation"
- Adaptation finance debate: COP30 agreed a tripling by 2035, but the baseline is contested — **~$20bn (2019)** vs **~$40bn (2025)** would lead to very different absolute figures
- Amoah on finance: Africa cannot fund adaptation through additional debt — needs public support and international climate finance, not loans

## Why it matters

The piece captures the shift from **"can we say the words fossil fuels?"** to **"can we build an alliance willing to actually phase them out?"** The most interesting part is not the TAFF membership list (already covered in the Santa Marta ingest) but the **strategic anxiety it is now creating** inside the UNFCCC process, and the **live disagreement with African negotiators** who worry about a plurilateral track undermining multilateral leverage. The Iran-driven energy crisis works as a stress test: some governments used it to push electrification, others to keep subsidising fossil fuels.

## Tone and angle

Straight news, structured around three arcs: (1) a new coalition growing under the surface, (2) African negotiators pushing back on plurilateralism, and (3) a debate about whether the current energy crisis will accelerate or delay decarbonisation. DW's editorial framing is calm — no cheerleading, but no false balance either.
