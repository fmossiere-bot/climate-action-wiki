# Ireland's transport and fossil fuel price shocks: CCAC Annual Review 2026

**Source:** RTÉ News, Aaron McElroy, 17 June 2026. https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0617/1578833-climate-change-review/

## What it is

This RTÉ News report covers the transport chapter of the 2026 Annual Review by the Climate Change Advisory Council (CCAC), the independent body that advises the Irish government on climate policy.

The council warns that Ireland's reliance on fossil fuels for transport leaves the country exposed to **repeated fuel price shocks**, because global energy markets are still disrupted. Its answer is to give people real alternatives to the car and to the petrol pump.

## Why it matters

When fuel prices jump, the people hit hardest are those who have to drive and cannot easily switch, often in rural or car-dependent areas and on lower incomes. The council says the emergency help offered during recent price rises was not targeted well enough. Support should go to the people most exposed to fuel costs and least able to avoid them.

It also points to a capacity problem. More people are using some services, yet total public transport journeys have barely moved, which suggests buses and trains are close to full at busy times. Without more capacity, it is hard for anyone to leave the car at home.

## Key facts

- Journeys on the TFI network rose by **6%** in 2025 compared with 2024.
- Local Link journeys, the rural bus service, rose by 19%.
- Total public transport journeys stayed largely the same, which suggests services are near full capacity at peak times.
- The CCAC recommends faster investment in public transport, walking and cycling (active travel), EV charging points and grid capacity.
- Projects it highlights include DART+ South West, Luas Finglas and the NTA Park and Ride Investment Programme.
- The council welcomes the pilot EV scrappage scheme, but wants measures to help lower-income households in car-dependent areas switch to electric cars.
- CCAC Chair Alex White said: "Fossil fuel shocks are not one-off events; giving alternatives is the way to reduce exposure".
