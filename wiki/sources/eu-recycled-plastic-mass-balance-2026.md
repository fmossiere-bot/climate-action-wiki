# Europe's supermarket shelves packed with 'misleading' claims about recycled plastic packaging

**Source:** The Guardian, Stefano Valentino, 27 January 2026 — part of an **IJ4EU cross-border investigation** coordinated by Ludovica Jona with Voxeurop, Mediapart, Altreconomia, Público, Investigative Reporting Denmark, Deutsche Welle and others.
**URL:** https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jan/27/recycled-plastic-packaging-claims-misleading-say-experts

## What it is

A cross-border investigation into how consumer brands — including **Kraft's Heinz Beanz** and **Mondelēz's Philadelphia** — can label plastic packaging as "recycled" or "circular" while the material remains almost entirely fossil-based. The mechanism is a set of **industry-lawful accounting tricks** — chiefly **mass-balance bookkeeping** and **avoided emissions** — combined with **pyrolysis-based chemical recycling** that produces only a small amount of recycled feedstock before it has to be diluted with virgin petroleum. The EU is about to legalise the practice in 2026 and the UK in 2027.

## Key facts cited

- **Saudi Aramco** — via its petrochemical subsidiary **Sabic** — supplies the plastic used by many brands making these claims; Aramco is the world's largest corporate greenhouse gas emitter (over 70 million tonnes to 2023) and opposes production cuts under the UN plastic treaty
- Pyrolysis oil can make up **at most 5% of feedstock** and must be diluted with **95% virgin naphtha** to avoid damaging steam-cracking plants
- **Mass-balance bookkeeping** assigns the recycled input to specific output batches — so 5% pyrolysis oil across 100 tonnes can be credited to certify 5 tonnes as "100% recycled" packaging that in reality contains only fossil feedstock
- **Avoided emissions** — the accounting adds credit for emissions that *would have been released* if an equivalent volume of waste had been incinerated
- Sabic used **2,600 tonnes of pyrolysis oil in 2022** vs **~4 million tonnes of naphtha** fed into its European cracking plants in the Netherlands — recycled content is well under 5%
- Sabic's own LCA admits the process **emits 6–8% more than producing plastic from fossil fuel** — only avoided-incineration credit turns the number positive (about 2 kg less CO₂ per kg of recycled plastic)
- LCAs "critically reviewed" by parties including the co-founder of Sabic's main feedstock supplier (Plastic Energy)
- Certificates are issued by the **industry-led International Sustainability and Carbon Certification (ISCC)** platform
- **EU PPWR** rules that accommodate mass balance take effect in **2026**; **UK plastic packaging tax mass-balance rules** in **2027**
- Petrochemical industry has intensified EU lobbying and is rushing to secure offtake deals with pyrolysis oil suppliers
- The **IEA's World Energy Outlook 2025** sees plastic becoming a critical growth engine for oil majors' future profits as demand for oil-as-fuel declines

## Notable quotes

- **Helmut Maurer** (former senior expert, European Commission environment): *"The whole process is labelled as plastic recycling, while fossil fuel use expands because virgin feedstock must be added."*
- **Lauriane Veillard** (Zero Waste Europe): *"This is unfair to consumers — recycled content should be physically part of the final product."*
- **Peter Quicker** (Aachen University, emission control in waste management): *"LCA documents serve no purpose other than advertising, because companies control the parameters to achieve desired results."*
- **Margaux Le Gallou** (senior programme manager, ECOS): the overestimated carbon savings "follow the downstream value chain, amplified by mass-balance credit, to packaged products, potentially making consumer brands' statements unreliable and misleading."

## Why it matters

Mandatory **recycled-content targets** are one of Europe's main policy levers against plastic pollution and to cut virgin oil demand. If those targets can be met on paper via mass-balance accounting while the physical product remains almost entirely fossil-based, the policy delivers **no material shift** — and, as the IEA notes, **big oil pivots into plastic as its next growth market**. This piece is the clearest recent documentation of how the loophole works, from the chemistry through to the accounting and the lobbying, and who benefits at each step.

## Tone and angle

Investigative and technical, but with a clear editorial line: the investigation calls the practice greenwashing, quotes experts and NGOs on the record, and names specific consumer brands. Notably, both Sabic and Plastic Energy declined to disclose full LCAs and the named brands did not respond.
