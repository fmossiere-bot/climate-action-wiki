# Extreme heat breaks: the climate story behind the 2026 World Cup

**Source:** Outrage+Optimism podcast, June 2026. https://podcasts.apple.com/ie/podcast/outrage-optimism-the-climate-podcast/id1459416461?i=1000772155867

## What it is

In this episode, the Outrage+Optimism hosts treat the 2026 men's football World Cup as climate adaptation happening in plain sight. For the first time in the history of the game, every match will stop for a **three-minute hydration break** in each half. A quarter of the 104 matches are expected to be played in conditions hotter than recommended safety limits.

The hosts ask whether these pauses should be called what they are: "extreme heat breaks" or "climate breaks". Naming them that way could help a global audience of billions see the climate crisis unfolding in real time.

## Why it matters

Football reaches people who may never read a climate report. When players have to stop mid-match to cool down, the effects of a hotter world become visible to fans everywhere. The hosts also voice a familiar frustration: the signs of climate change keep piling up, yet they rarely change how most people think about it.

## Key points

- Every match at the 2026 World Cup includes mandatory three-minute hydration breaks in each half, a first for football.
- A quarter of the 104 matches are projected to be played in conditions above recommended heat safety limits.
- Transport at some US venues struggled. NJ Transit raised the price of return tickets to MetLife Stadium to $150, from under $15, at times of high demand.
- The hosts discuss the boycott Trump's World Cup (BTWC) movement.
