# EU right-to-repair rules enter force in Ireland — RTÉ, August 2026

**Source:** RTÉ Business, Brian O'Donovan, 24 August 2026 — https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0824/1588931-repair-rules/

## What it is

RTÉ reporting on the **EU Right to Repair Directive coming into force in Ireland in August 2026**. The regulation gives consumers **new rights to demand that manufacturers repair technically-repairable products after the seller's guarantee period has expired**, at a **reasonable price and within a reasonable timeframe**. It applies to household and electronic goods such as washing machines, vacuum cleaners, mobile phones and tablets. **A national repair platform — RepairMyStuff.ie — is being developed to help Irish consumers find repair services**. The rules are the EU's answer to the estimated **35 million tonnes of waste** generated across the bloc each year by premature disposal of consumer goods.

## Key facts

- **35 million tonnes** of waste per year across the EU from premature disposal of consumer goods
- **€4.8 billion in growth and investment** expected within the EU from the new rules (European Commission estimate)
- Products covered: **washing machines, vacuum cleaners, mobile phones, tablets** and other categories under the directive
- **Right applies AFTER the seller's guarantee period expires** — consumers can now demand repair, not just replacement, at reasonable cost
- Manufacturers must:
  - Repair technically-repairable products on request
  - Provide **easily accessible information** about repair services
  - Give access to **spare parts at a reasonable price**
- **RepairMyStuff.ie** will be further developed to serve as Ireland's national repair platform
- Directive is part of the EU's broader circular-economy legislation, alongside the ESPR (Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation)

## Notable quotes

- **Peter Burke** (Ireland's Minister for Enterprise, Tourism and Employment): *"These regulations will make it easier for consumers to choose repair over replacement, while also creating opportunities for Irish businesses operating in the repair and refurbishment sector. In implementing the Directive, we have sought to strike the right balance between supporting consumers, encouraging sustainable economic activity and ensuring that businesses are not faced with unnecessary regulatory burdens."*
- **Darragh O'Brien** (Ireland's Minister for Climate, Energy and the Environment): *"These new regulations are important in helping to meet our climate goals by realigning how we produce, consume, and value materials."*

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **Structural shift in consumer rights.** Until now, the manufacturer's job largely ended when the guarantee did. Under the Directive, "repair on request" is a live obligation for years after purchase. That changes product-design incentives at the source, not just at the recycling end.
2. **Repair sector as a growth story.** €4.8bn EU-wide is a genuine investment thesis for repair cafés, small electronic-repair firms and refurbishment startups — the same sector that had been quietly shrinking for two decades as goods got cheaper to replace than to fix.
3. **Materials angle for climate.** Darragh O'Brien's framing (climate goals through material use) matches the [IPBES Nexus Assessment](ipbes-nexus-assessment-2024.md) argument: reducing material extraction and disposal is a climate lever, not just a waste-management one. Every device kept in use another 2-3 years is embedded emissions not re-emitted, and virgin resource extraction avoided.

## Tone and angle

Straight policy news; two Irish government ministers quoted; light on numbers beyond the €4.8bn and 35Mt figures. Doesn't cover implementation questions (which categories are technically repairable? how "reasonable" is defined?) — those will emerge as the Directive is tested.
