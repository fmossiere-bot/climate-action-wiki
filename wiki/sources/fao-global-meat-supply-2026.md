# Average person eats six times more chicken than in 1961, UN report finds

**Source:** The Guardian, Ajit Niranjan, 5 June 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jun/05/global-meat-supply-chicken-pork-fao-report

## What it is

A Guardian article on a new report from the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). The report shows that global meat supply has risen **fourfold since 1961**, driven mostly by chicken and pork.

The article also carries criticism from experts. They point out that the FAO stops short of recommending that wealthy countries eat less meat, even though the evidence of harm to the climate is clear.

## Why it matters

What we eat is one of the biggest levers most people have over their own footprint, and meat is the heaviest part of it. This report shows the trend is still going the wrong way, with farming emissions forecast to keep rising and livestock behind most of that rise. The data now sits on the wiki's page on [agriculture and the planetary footprint](../sectors/agriculture-food/agriculture-planetary-footprint.md).

## Key facts

- Average global meat supply rose from 25 kg per person in 1961 to 47 kg per person in 2022.
- Chicken and other poultry rose from below 3 kg per person to 17 kg. Pork doubled to 15 kg. Beef stayed level at 9 kg.
- Agriculture is the second most polluting economic sector. Its emissions are forecast to rise 7.6% over the next decade.
- Livestock is responsible for an estimated 80% of that increase.
- About 14% of meat and milk is lost during production or wasted before anyone eats it.
- Animal farming causes 12–20% of planet-heating emissions and is a leading cause of biodiversity loss.
- The IPCC names a shift to plant-rich diets as one of the most effective things people can do on the demand side to cut emissions.
