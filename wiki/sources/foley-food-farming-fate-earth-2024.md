# How Food and Farming Will Determine the Fate of Planet Earth

**Source:** Project Drawdown, Jonathan Foley PhD, 30 March 2024. https://drawdown.org/insights/how-food-and-farming-will-determine-the-fate-of-planet-earth

## What it is

A long essay by Jonathan Foley, Executive Director of Project Drawdown. He argues that farming has changed the planet more than any other human activity, burning fossil fuels included.

Foley walks through the ways agriculture leaves its mark: the land it takes up, the wildlife it displaces, the fresh water it uses, the chemicals it releases and its share of climate change. He ends by pointing to Project Drawdown's work on food systems as a way forward.

## Why it matters

Climate conversations tend to focus on power stations, cars and planes. Foley's essay is a reminder that what we eat, and how it is grown, shapes the planet just as much. Farming covers more than a third of the world's land and uses most of its fresh water.

It also points to where change would make the biggest difference. Most farmland goes to feeding animals rather than people directly, so **meat and dairy** sit at the heart of food's footprint. None of this is a criticism of farmers, who feed the world. It is a question of how the whole system is set up.

## Key facts

- Cropland (16 million km²) and pasture (34 million km²) together cover **37% of Earth's land surface**, more than Asia and Europe combined.
- 75% of all farmland is used to graze animals or grow animal feed. That makes meat and dairy the biggest driver of the land that food takes up.
- Farming accounts for 70% of the world's freshwater use, mostly to irrigate crops, which is about 2,800 km³ a year.
- Fertiliser use has more than doubled the natural flows of nitrogen and phosphorus across Earth's surface. The run-off creates "dead zones" in coastal waters where little can live.
- Farming and the land use linked to it directly cause about 22% of global greenhouse gas emissions. Counting the whole food system, including transport, packaging and waste, the share rises to about one-third.
- Clearing forests for farming (for pasture, soybeans and oil palm) accounts for about 10–11% of global greenhouse gas emissions, roughly as much as the entire US economy.
