# Are manure digesters a real solution to dairy farm emissions? (New Scientist, April 2026)

**Source:** New Scientist, Alec Luhn, 7 April 2026 (updated 20 May 2026)
**Journal reference:** Environmental Research Letters, DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/ae4fe4

## What it is

A New Scientist look at the evidence on **anaerobic digesters** for dairy manure. A digester is a sealed tank where manure breaks down without air, producing biogas that can be captured and used instead of escaping as methane.

Under good conditions, a digester can cut methane from stored manure by up to 91%. But a California study of 98 dairies found that leaks, disruption during construction and badly designed subsidies make the real-world picture much messier.

## Why it matters

Methane from farming is a big and fast-acting part of the climate problem, and digesters are often presented as a tidy fix. This piece shows they can help a lot, but only when they are built and run well, and only when the money behind them does not reward farms for keeping more cows. The findings were added to the wiki's page on [methane digesters](../solutions/carbon-removal/Methane%20Digesters%20-%20Turning%20Farm%20Waste%20into%20Energy%20and%20Fertiliser.md).

## Key facts

- Agriculture causes about 1/3 of human-caused emissions. In the US, about 1/3 of farm emissions come from cattle belching and 14% from manure.
- More than 17,000 digesters are installed in the EU, about 400 each in the US and the UK, and millions in China (mostly small brick digesters).
- Manure that goes through a digester releases **91% less methane** while stored. This is the figure usually quoted.
- In the California study of 98 dairies, digesters cut methane from the main sources on site from 91 kg/h to 68 kg/h at 2/3 of dairies.
- Some leaks were larger than 1,000 kg/h, which turned some digesters into net emitters. Emissions also rose briefly during construction.
- Under a California notification scheme in 2023, 20% of farms with leaks fixed them after being told.
- Across most research, digesters cut manure emissions by roughly half overall.
- California's Low Carbon Fuel Standard gives $389m in grants for building digesters and issues credits that farms can sell. One study found that farms receiving these incentives grew their herds by 860 cows on average, the opposite of what the climate needs.
- Digesters speed up the release of ammonia from manure, raising fears of "pollution swapping", where one problem is traded for another.
- Burning off biogas that cannot be sold can release sulphur dioxide, which harms the lungs.

## Voices in the piece

Rebecca Larson of the University of Wisconsin-Madison sees digesters as "one of the highest performing mitigation measures we have" for livestock. Brent Kim of Johns Hopkins University is more critical of the subsidies, saying "taxpayer dollars are being used to inflate the value of manure".
