# Countries reach COP17 deal, but money still short: Reuters

**Source:** Reuters, Simon Jessop and Virginia Furness, 31 October 2026. https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/cop/countries-reach-cop17-deal-speed-up-biodiversity-action-money-still-short-2026-10-31/

## What is it

A short Reuters news report on the close of the UN biodiversity conference (COP17) in Yerevan, Armenia, after talks ran through the night. Governments adopted a decision nicknamed the **Yerevan Pathway**. It admits that action so far has not been enough to meet the 2030 nature goals and sets priorities to speed things up: cutting harmful subsidies, raising finance for developing countries, and lining up national budgets with biodiversity goals.

The conference was the first global review of the Global Biodiversity Framework agreed in Montreal four years earlier. That framework aims to protect 30% of land, inland waters, coasts and seas by 2030, restore 30% of damaged ecosystems, reform harmful subsidies and raise $200 billion a year for nature. Delegates agreed the world is off track on almost all of its targets.

## Why it matters

The deal shows countries can still agree on nature at a tense political moment, but it does not answer the question of who pays. Reuters notes that the money to deliver the plan "remains elusive". Anita de Horde, executive director of the Finance for Biodiversity Foundation, said the real test is "what governments implement before COP18". Catherine Weller of the charity Fauna & Flora put it more bluntly: "We cannot just plod on to 2030 without a course correction."

## Key facts

- COP17 lasted two weeks and ended on Saturday 31 October 2026.
- Nearly 90% of countries in the biodiversity convention have set national targets, but fewer than half of those assessed are on track, according to the UN.
- Organisers said more than **$1.8 billion** was announced during the conference by governments, philanthropies and investors.
- Governments agreed to speed up work towards the existing goal of cutting harmful incentives by at least $500 billion a year by 2030.
- Delegates agreed to review current funding arrangements, set criteria for a future permanent financing mechanism, and voluntarily widen the group of countries that contribute.
- COP18 will be held in Quito, Ecuador, in 2028.
- The UN climate talks (COP31) opened in Turkey on 9 November, nine days later.
