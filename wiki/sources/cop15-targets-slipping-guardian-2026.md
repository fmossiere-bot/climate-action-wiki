# Four years after COP15, our biodiversity targets are slipping away — Patrick Greenfield, Guardian

**Source:** Guardian, Patrick Greenfield, 16 October 2026 — https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/oct/14/four-years-after-the-promises-of-cop15-our-biodiversity-targets-are-slipping-away

## What it is

Guardian commentary from biodiversity reporter Patrick Greenfield, written days before COP17 opens in Yerevan, Armenia. **The core framing: the optimism of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework at COP15 (Montreal, 2022) has faded, pre-COP17 reports show the world off track on all 23 targets, and the geopolitical collapse of multilateralism — particularly the US withdrawal under Trump's second term — has gutted biodiversity funding at exactly the wrong moment.** The pre-COP17 report traffic-light system "is awash with yellow, orange and red", and there is "a massive gap between countries agreeing to do something and actually doing it".

## Key facts

- **COP15 (Montreal, December 2022)** adopted the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework with 23 targets, including: protect **30% of the planet** by 2030 (30×30); restore **30% of degraded ecosystems**; tackle **$500bn** of environmentally damaging subsidies
- **Nearly every country signed**, except the US (which does not ratify the CBD) and the Vatican
- **Pre-COP17 progress report**: *"Unless collective implementation accelerates rapidly, the 2030 targets and mission will not be achieved."* All 23 targets have gaps; **no target presents a fully positive picture**
- **US biodiversity funding has been gutted**: USAID and partners provided an average of **12.1% of the world's biodiversity funding between 2015 and 2022** (OECD); "almost all of that has gone" under Trump's second term, hitting anti-rhino-poaching in East Africa and anti-illegal-fishing in the Galápagos
- Positive counter-examples: **Kazakhstan** has used the agreement to reintroduce the **Caspian tiger**; the EU passed the **Nature Restoration Law** — though it became "a culture-wars issue"
- Reporter's own witness: Greenfield was in the hall in Montreal in December 2022 and remembers the excitement

## Notable quotes

- **Pre-COP17 report**: *"Unless collective implementation accelerates rapidly, the 2030 targets and mission will not be achieved. Parties and other actors have taken action to address all 23 targets; however, no target presents a fully positive picture."*
- **Zac Goldsmith** (UK environment minister when COP15 deal was reached, 2022): *"There's a massive gap between countries agreeing to do something and actually doing it. That's where we are today."*
- Greenfield: *"The world has lost its way on nature once again... this time, humans are the asteroid."*

## Why it matters

Three overlapping reasons:

1. **A clear-eyed diagnosis before COP17 opens.** Greenfield is one of the Guardian's longest-serving biodiversity reporters and his pessimism matters — this is not an opening-week scramble, it is a journalist with four years of continuity flagging the trajectory before delegates arrive.
2. **The 12.1% USAID number is the specific scale of the US retreat.** When general accounts talk about "Trump cuts to climate", this number lets a reader see what left the system: an eighth of all global biodiversity funding, over seven years, disappearing between 2024 and 2026.
3. **The EU Nature Restoration Law as a precedent for culture-wars resistance.** The piece flags that even in a country that did try to implement COP15 at home (EU), the political backlash was severe. That changes the baseline expectation of what COP17 can realistically achieve.

## Caveats

- **Guardian commentary, not peer-reviewed research.** The 12.1% USAID share is attributed to OECD and is credible; the "almost all of that has gone" is journalistic. Specific programmes affected (Galápagos, East African anti-poaching) are stated as examples.
- **US-centric framing.** The piece gives relatively little space to what BRICS countries, oil-state blocs, or Indigenous-led initiatives are doing to compensate for the US retreat.
