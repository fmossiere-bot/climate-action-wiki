# World's largest banks pledged $906bn to fossil fuel companies in 2025

**Source:** The Guardian, Oliver Milman, 9 June 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/jun/09/world-banks-pledge-billions-fossil-fuel-industry-2025

## What it is

Each year a coalition of environmental groups publishes the Banking on Climate Chaos report, which tracks how much money the world's biggest banks lend to and raise for the fossil fuel industry. The Guardian reported on the latest edition.

It found that the world's 65 largest banks committed **$906 billion** to fossil fuel companies in 2025. That is $64 billion more than in 2024, a rise of nearly 8%. Since the Paris Agreement was signed in 2015, the same banks have channelled a total of $8.7 trillion into fossil fuel production.

## Why it matters

Banks decide which projects get built. When lending to oil, gas and coal goes up rather than down, new wells, pipelines and terminals become easier to finance, and those can keep running for decades.

The trend also runs against what many banks promised. The UN-backed Net-Zero Banking Alliance, where banks pledged to align their lending with climate goals, has been wound up after a wave of departures, many of them prompted by political pressure in the US. Over half the money pledged in 2025 went into expanding existing fossil fuel sites rather than simply keeping them running.

There is some movement in the other direction. 26 of the 65 banks cut their fossil fuel financing, which shows that reducing it is a choice banks can make.

## Key facts

- The 65 largest banks committed $906bn to fossil fuels in 2025, up 8% on 2024.
- Total fossil fuel financing since the Paris Agreement (2015 to 2025): **$8.7 trillion**.
- JPMorgan Chase is the world's leading fossil fuel financier, at $58bn, up 13% from 2024.
- The top five are JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, MUFG, Mizuho Financial and Citigroup. Barclays is the highest-placed British bank, in 8th place.
- The "dirty dozen" banks provide 40% of all fossil fuel funding.
- $508bn went to expanding existing fossil fuel sites in 2025, up 27% on 2024.
- The three largest recipients were Venture Global, Enbridge and Energy Transfer, all US oil and gas companies.
- 26 of 65 banks reduced their fossil fuel financing, with BNP Paribas, UBS and La Caixa making the biggest cuts.
- Almost all the financing comes from six places: the US, Canada, Japan, China, the UK and the EU.
