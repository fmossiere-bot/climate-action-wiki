# Hometree's fundraiser to restore Ireland's rainforest

**Source:** Hometree, "Help restore Ireland's rainforests" fundraiser page, accessed 17 November 2026. https://www.hometree.ie/fundraiser

This is a charity's own appeal page, so its figures and plans are Hometree's own description of the project.

## What is it

Hometree is an Irish nature restoration charity based in Ennistymon, Co. Clare, that started as a small tree-planting project. Its **Wild Atlantic Rainforest Project** aims to protect and restore 4,000 acres across the west of Ireland. Half will be land Hometree buys, and half will be on neighbouring farms, where the charity pays for fencing and planting and gives landowners a financial return for restoring habitat.

The plan rests on restoration hubs: sites the charity owns and restores, which then act as a base for work on surrounding land and as places for schools, volunteers and researchers. Hometree has four hubs so far and plans eight or nine along the western seaboard. This first phase is part of a ten-year plan to bring 57,500 acres into restoration.

## Why it matters

When most people hear "rainforest", they picture somewhere hot and far away. But **temperate rainforest** grows where rain falls little and often, on more than 200 days a year, and Ireland's west coast is one of the few places on Earth where it can. It once covered much of the west. Today less than 2% of Ireland is native woodland, and only fragments of this rainforest remain. Hometree describes it as one of the rarest habitats on Earth, one that protects water, stores carbon and shelters a great deal of life.

Restoration here is about more than planting trees. Depending on the site, it can mean letting woodland regrow on its own, clearing invasive plants, rewetting bog or protecting what is already there.

## Key facts

- In summer 2026 Hometree secured access to **€14.7 million** for the project through the Immigrant Investor Programme, a government scheme that let overseas investors fund approved social and environmental projects. Hometree stresses it is investor money, not taxpayers' money. The scheme closed to new applications in 2023.
- The condition: raise €3.9 million by 19 November 2026, or lose the €14.7 million. Hometree aimed to raise €2.9 million from philanthropists, foundations and businesses, and, for the first time, €1 million from the public.
- More than 9,000 people had given by the time the page was clipped. The page does not say whether the target was reached.
- During the appeal, each euro given unlocked more than three more. Examples given: €50 helps grow native saplings in the Ennistymon nursery; €5,000 could secure and restore a full acre of Irish rainforest.
- Money raised goes to buying land, restoring native woodland and peatland, clearing invasive species, supporting farmers and building community nature hubs.

## How individuals can help
<!-- tag: individual-actions -->

Donations of any size go to the project. In Ireland, when an individual gives €250 or more in a calendar year, the charity can claim tax relief under the Charitable Donation Scheme, which raises a €250 gift to about €362.32 at no extra cost to the donor, if the donor pays enough Irish income tax or capital gains tax. Companies giving €250 or more can deduct the donation as a business expense. Hometree is a registered charity and a member of 1% for the Planet.
