# Wildfires 2026, first lessons (Écologie 360)

**Source:** Écologie 360 magazine (French), summer 2026 issue
**Reference cited:** J. Kirschner, J. Clark, G. Boustras, "Governing Wildfires: Toward a Systematic Analytical Framework", *Ecology and Society*, 2023

## What it is

An overview from the French magazine *Écologie 360* of the summer 2026 wildfire season in Europe and around the world, one of the worst on record. It draws out the first lessons. Megafires are becoming more frequent, more intense and harder to control. The case is growing for European countries to share firefighting planes and crews through **rescEU**, the EU's joint reserve. And the FAO and the UN have long proposed a dedicated international fire brigade.

The piece also covers new thinking on how to manage forests so they burn less: mixing tree species, restoring wetlands, cutting firebreaks, and bringing back grazing animals (sheep, goats and cattle in Andalusia) to keep vegetation down.

## Why it matters

For the people living near these forests, 2026 felt like a change of scale rather than one bad summer. France lost 122,000 hectares, a record. [2025](europe-wildfires-2025-newscientist.md) was already Europe's worst fire year on record, and 2026 went beyond it. Canada's 4.1 million hectares continues several years of unprecedented fires in its northern forests. Fires release carbon that heats the planet further, which dries out more land and feeds more fires. This year's figures suggest that loop is running faster than models expected.

rescEU offers a practical response. The idea is not new, but this season makes the case for growing it. Twelve new water-bombing planes, delivered from 2028 to six southern European countries, are a concrete commitment. Fires ignore borders, and sharing equipment across them is one of the cheapest ways to adapt.

The return of grazing and mixed forests is a reminder that some answers are old ones. Forests were managed this way for centuries before industrial forestry brought dense plantations of a single species.

## Key facts

Figures up to mid-August 2026:

- France: **122,000 hectares** of forest and vegetation burned, a record.
- Spain: 240,000 hectares.
- Canada: 4.1 million hectares.
- United States: 2.9 million hectares.
- Russia and Brazil also had very large fires.

## rescEU: the EU's shared response

- rescEU was used during the Gironde megafire. It now holds a European reserve of water-bombing planes, helicopters and hundreds of expert firefighters, placed in advance in the countries most at risk.
- The European Commission is paying for 12 new water-bombing planes, to be shared between France, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece and Croatia. The first will arrive in 2028.
- The magazine calls it "the first permanent response to a risk that is now continent-wide".

## Building more resilient forests

- Mix tree species rather than planting stands of just one.
- Restore wetlands, which act as natural firebreaks and hold moisture.
- Widen firebreaks.
- Clear brush around homes, still one of the most effective measures.
- Bring back grazing, especially in Andalusia, where sheep, goats or cattle keep vegetation short and leave less fuel to burn.

## A world fire brigade?

Since the early 2000s, the FAO and the UN have floated the idea of a dedicated international force that could act quickly anywhere in the world during a major fire, much as emergency aid works after other natural disasters. Scientists support it, but it has never happened. rescEU shows that permanent cooperation across borders is possible, and as fires become a global problem, the idea of an international brigade may gain ground.

## In the magazine's words

*Écologie 360* sums up the season plainly: "The fires of summer 2026 were more frequent, more intense and harder to control." And: "Megafires are no longer isolated events. They are becoming a recurring risk on several continents."
