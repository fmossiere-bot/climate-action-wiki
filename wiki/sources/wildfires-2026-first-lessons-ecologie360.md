# Wildfires 2026, first lessons — Écologie 360 summary

**Source:** Écologie 360 magazine (French), summer 2026 issue
**Reference cited:** J. Kirschner, J. Clark, G. Boustras, "Governing Wildfires: Toward a Systematic Analytical Framework", *Ecology and Society*, 2023

## What it is

A synthesis piece from *Écologie 360* on the **summer 2026 wildfire season in Europe and worldwide** — one of the worst on record — and the first policy lessons emerging: **more frequent, more intense, harder-to-control megafires; growing case for shared European firefighting resources through the rescEU system; and a longer-running proposal from FAO and the UN for a dedicated international fire brigade**. Also covers the shift in forest-management thinking toward more resilient forests (mixed species, wetland restoration, firebreaks) and the return of grazing (sheep, goats, cattle in Andalusia) as a strategic fuel-reduction tool.

## Key facts (up to mid-August 2026)

- **France: 122,000 hectares** of forest and vegetation destroyed — a record
- **Spain: 240,000 hectares**
- **Canada: 4.1 million hectares**
- **United States: 2.9 million hectares**
- Also very large fires in **Russia and Brazil**

## rescEU: the EU's shared response

- The **rescEU system** — used during the Gironde megafire — now pools **a European reserve of water-bombing planes, helicopters and hundreds of expert firefighters**, pre-positioned in the most exposed countries
- **12 new water-bombing planes** paid for by the European Commission will be shared between **France, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece and Croatia**, with the first delivered in **2028**
- Described as **"the first permanent response to a risk that is now continent-wide"**

## Building more resilient forests

- **Mix of tree species** — reduce single-species stands
- **Restore wetlands** — natural fire breaks and moisture reservoirs
- **Wider firebreaks**
- **Clearing brush around homes** — still one of the most effective measures
- **Grazing returning as strategic tool** (particularly Andalusia) — sheep, goats or cattle keep vegetation down and reduce fuel load

## A world fire brigade?

- The **FAO and UN** have raised the idea since the early 2000s of a **dedicated international force**, able to act quickly anywhere in the world during major crises, modelled on how emergency aid works after natural disasters
- **Scientists are in favour, but the project has never happened**
- The European rescEU experience shows that **permanent cross-border cooperation is possible**
- With fires becoming a global challenge, the international brigade concept could gain credibility

## Notable framing

*"The fires of summer 2026 were more frequent, more intense and harder to control."* … *"Megafires are no longer isolated events. They are becoming a recurring risk on several continents."*

## Why it matters

Three things:

1. **The 2026 season is a step change**, not just a bad year. France's 122,000 ha is a record; Canada's 4.1 M ha continues a several-year pattern of unprecedented boreal fire seasons. When the wildfires-climate-feedback loop (page already on wiki) has this much fuel to work with, the models' emissions estimates are being outpaced by reality.
2. **rescEU is the practical answer.** Not a new idea, but the 2026 season is the case for scaling it. The 12 new water-bombers delivered from 2028 for six southern European states is the concrete commitment. Fires ignore borders; sharing the equipment across them is the cheapest form of adaptation.
3. **Grazing and mixed-species forests as fire management.** Both were how forests were managed for centuries before the industrial-forestry model of dense monocultures. Their return as fire-adaptation strategy is a reminder that some of the answers are old.

## Tone and angle

*Écologie 360* practical-adaptation register — no doomism, no denial, just what's changing and what's being tried. Cites Kirschner, Clark & Boustras (2023) for the governance framework, which is a solid academic anchor. Ends with a policy-forward pitch (world fire brigade) rather than a lament.
