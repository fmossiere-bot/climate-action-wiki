# Record heat and political denial: Jonathan Freedland on the June 2026 heatwave

**Source:** The Guardian (comment), Jonathan Freedland, 26 June 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/jun/26/climate-sceptics-record-temperatures-heatwave-ed-miliband-net-zero

## What it is

This is an opinion column. Jonathan Freedland uses the June 2026 European heatwave to look at what he sees as a "Don't Look Up" pattern, after the film about a world that ignores an approaching comet. Even as the effects of climate change become impossible to escape, he argues, politicians keep distracting, deflecting and denying.

While the UK sweltered, a conference in London billed as an "anti-woke Davos" mocked net zero indoors in heat above 35°C. Freedland sets the two scenes side by side.

## Why it matters

The facts in the column are hard to dismiss. For the first time in Met Office history, the UK issued **red heat warnings on three days in a row**. A wildfire was burning in Derbyshire in June, which is rare for a country with a mild climate. These are the kinds of events scientists have long warned would become more common.

At the same time, support for net zero is weakening across politics, from the right to some trade unions and centrist leaders. The column captures how hard it is to keep climate policy on track when people are living through the effects and still arguing about the cause.

## Key facts

- The UK issued red heat warnings for three consecutive days in June 2026, a first in Met Office history.
- Temperatures went above 35°C, and nights were hot enough to meet the technical definition of "tropical".
- A wildfire was burning in Derbyshire, unusual for a temperate country in June.
- Across Europe, Germany and Poland were bracing for 40°C.
- The "anti-woke Davos" conference (Alliance for Responsible Citizenship) in London was backed by the owner of GB News and by fossil fuel companies. Speakers included Trump's energy secretary Chris Wright, a former fossil fuel executive.
- Attendees criticised UK net zero policies and Ed Miliband while reportedly sweltering indoors in heat of 35°C+. Chris Wright called UK green policies a "tragic mistake".

## The wider politics

Freedland notes that net zero has also been criticised by Kemi Badenoch, some trade unions, Tony Blair and Mark Carney, who has dismantled several Canadian green measures. He describes the European Commission as "easing the pressure" on environmental policy too.

He also links the heat to geopolitics. In his reading, Trump backed down on an Iran deal in June 2026 partly to bring petrol prices down, which shows how dependence on fossil fuels shapes foreign policy.

## Limits of the source

This is a comment piece, not a news report. The factual claims, such as the Met Office warnings, the wildfire and the conference details, are well sourced. The political commentary is the author's own view.
