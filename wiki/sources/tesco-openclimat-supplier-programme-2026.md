# Tesco asks 400 suppliers to share climate data through OpenClimat

**Source:** Green Retail World, Ben Sillitoe, 13 November 2026. https://greenretail.world/2026/11/13/data-sharing-tesco-aims-for-supplier-decarbonisation-via-openclimat-partnership/

## What is it

<!-- tag: uk, ireland -->
Tesco, the UK's largest retailer, has joined **OpenClimat**, a platform where retailers and manufacturers share and check emissions data. Tesco is inviting about 400 key suppliers into a new Climate Supplier Programme. Together they make up more than 80% of its grocery supply chain by value in the UK and the Republic of Ireland. Suppliers to its Booker wholesale business are included too.

Suppliers will first fill in a questionnaire and submit climate data. From February 2027 the programme moves into an engagement stage, with tailored guidance, practical resources and chances to work with other companies on cutting emissions. The aim is to help Tesco reach its target of net zero across its full value chain by 2050.

## Why it matters

For a supermarket, most emissions come from what it sells rather than from its own shops and lorries. These indirect emissions, called **scope 3**, come from farms, factories and packaging owned by other companies. A retailer cannot cut them alone, and it cannot track them without good data from suppliers.

The difficulty is that every retailer tends to ask suppliers for the same information in a slightly different format, which turns climate reporting into a paperwork burden. OpenClimat's model is one shared exchange that many retailers and manufacturers use, so a supplier reports once. OpenClimat says the goal is for climate data to become "a practical tool" for cutting emissions rather than extra admin.

## Key facts

- About 400 suppliers invited, covering more than 80% of Tesco's grocery supply chain by value in the UK and Ireland.
- Tesco targets net zero across its full value chain by 2050.
- OpenClimat was founded in 2022 and already links more than 15 retailers and 300 manufacturers across Europe.
- Other retail members include Carrefour, Metro and E.Leclerc. Manufacturers include General Mills, JDE Peet's, Beiersdorf, McCain, Ontex and Emmi.
- Tesco is the first UK retailer to join the network.

## What to watch

The announcement is about collecting data, not about cutting emissions yet. The real test comes after February 2027: whether suppliers' emissions actually fall, and whether Tesco reports those results openly. Claire Lorains, Tesco's group quality and sustainability director, said reaching net zero across the value chain "will require collaboration with all our suppliers."
