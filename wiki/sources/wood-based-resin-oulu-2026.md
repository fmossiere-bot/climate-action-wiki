# New wood-based resin beats fossil resin strength by 76%

**Source:** New Atlas, Etiido Uko (with Juha Heiskanen / University of Oulu), 8 March 2026
**URL:** https://newatlas.com/materials/bio-based-wooden-plastic-resins/
**Peer-reviewed study:** Composites Part B: Engineering — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1359836825011722

## What it is

A New Atlas write-up of research from the **University of Oulu, Finland**, on **bio-based replacements for the two most widely used fossil resins in high-performance composites**: polyester and epoxy. The team, including doctoral researcher Mikko Salonen and research team member Juha Heiskanen, has developed resins from **furfural produced from lignocellulosic biomass** (sawdust, straw and other forestry and agricultural sidestreams) that not only match but **outperform their petroleum-based equivalents** on strength and toughness — and can also be chemically recycled.

## Key facts cited

- Bio-based polyester resin exhibited **up to 76% higher tensile strength than a commercial fossil-based polyester**
- Bio-based epoxy composites showed **improved toughness and significantly higher tensile and flexural strengths** than conventional DGEBA (diglycidyl ether of bisphenol A) resins
- The furfural-based diepoxide has a **degradable chemical structure**: composites can be broken down and the components extracted as raw materials — the first reported combination of high mechanical performance and chemical recyclability in bio-based epoxy composites
- Feedstock: **lignocellulosic biomass** — sawdust, straw and other agricultural/forestry sidestreams
- **Production compatible with existing chemical industry lines** — no factory retooling required, so no price premium expected once platform chemicals are produced at scale
- 90-day harbour weathering test: panels stood up to water, UV, temperature and humidity
- Three patents filed; team seeking industrial partners for pilot-scale production
- Strategic angle for Europe: EU has **<2% of global oil reserves**, so displacing petroleum-based resins supports material self-sufficiency and the circular economy

## Why it matters

Composite resins are ubiquitous and problematic. Epoxy and polyester composites make up the matrix of **wind turbine blades, boat hulls, car body panels, roofing sheets, aircraft structures, high-end sporting goods and civil engineering reinforcements**. Two things have held bio-based alternatives back until now: mechanical performance and end-of-life. This work reports the first combination that improves on both — including a chemistry that enables **closed-loop recycling** of the whole composite, not just recovery of the fibres. If it scales, it could remove one of the awkward fossil inputs in the wind and marine industries, and cut the wider industrial resin market's carbon and waste footprint at the same time.

## Tone and angle

Straight science-and-tech reporting. Cautious on timing ("may be a while before we see yachts and turbines made from renewable, recyclable composites") and honest that the work is at the experimentation/validation stage, but confident on the mechanical results. Frames the innovation as a **European strategic autonomy** play as well as a climate one.
