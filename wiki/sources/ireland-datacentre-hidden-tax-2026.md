# 'Hidden datacentre tax' costing Irish households millions, report says

**Source:** The Guardian, Rory Carroll, 28 May 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/may/28/irish-datacentres-household-bills-electricity

## What it is

A report commissioned by Friends of the Earth Ireland and Beyond Fossil Fuels tries to put a price on what data centres cost Irish households. The Guardian covered its findings.

Data centres need a lot of electricity, and they need it around the clock. The report finds that this steady, high demand means gas power stations set the price of electricity more often. Over a decade, that has added hundreds of euros to the average household's bills.

## Why it matters

Most people never see a data centre, but they may be paying for one. In Ireland's electricity market, the most expensive power station needed at any moment sets the price for everyone, and that is often a gas plant. The more hours gas sets the price, the more households pay, and the more the country stays tied to fossil fuels.

Ireland already has some of the strictest rules in Europe on new data centres, yet the report suggests the costs are still landing on ordinary bills. Beyond Fossil Fuels calls the Irish case "a warning for Europe".

## Key facts

- Ireland's data centres used **22% of the country's electricity** in 2025, more than all urban homes combined, according to the Central Statistics Office (CSO).
- The report says data centres "drained" €715m from the Irish economy and raised household bills by an average of €360 in total over 2015–2023.
- Looking ahead, each household could pay an extra €295–€644 in total over 2025–2034, or €633m–€1.43bn across the country.
- The cause: data centres increase the number of hours in which gas sets the electricity price.
- New data centres in Ireland must get 80% of their energy from additional renewable capacity, the strictest rule in Europe.
- The industry disputed the findings, pointing to €18bn of investment and saying it pays network charges in proportion to its use.
