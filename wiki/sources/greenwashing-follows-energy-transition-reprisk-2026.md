# Greenwashing is following the energy transition

**Source:** Sustainable Brands, Antonio Vizcaya Abdo, November 2026 (clipped 3 November). https://sustainablebrands.com/read/greenwashing-is-following-the-energy-transition

## What is it

Antonio Vizcaya Abdo, a sustainability writer, uses the latest **RepRisk Greenwashing Report** to argue that greenwashing is moving with the money. RepRisk is a data company that tracks reports of corporate misconduct. Its figures show greenwashing incidents rose by 112% between 2022 and 2026, and 1,594 companies were linked to greenwashing in 2026.

The change is in who is being accused. Alternative energy, mining, industrial metals, utilities and software companies are taking a growing share of greenwashing concerns. These are the industries building the energy transition.

## Why it matters

A wind farm, a lithium mine or a data centre can help cut emissions and still damage a habitat, drain local water or push out a community. Being **low carbon is not the same as being low impact**. For the first time in five years, RepRisk found that ecosystems and biodiversity were the environmental issue most often linked to greenwashing, ahead of climate and emissions. In alternative energy, biodiversity concerns outnumbered climate concerns between 2022 and 2026.

This does not weaken the case for clean energy. It means words like "green", "clean" and "sustainable" now need evidence on nature, water and people as well as carbon. For readers, it is a reminder to ask what a climate claim leaves out.

## Key facts

- Greenwashing incidents rose by 112% between 2022 and 2026 (RepRisk).
- 1,594 companies were linked to greenwashing in 2026.
- Ecosystems and biodiversity overtook climate as the issue most linked to greenwashing, for the first time in five years.
- Banks and financial services together remain the largest group of companies linked to greenwashing, mostly through the companies and projects they finance, invest in or underwrite.
- Nearly one in four greenwashing incidents now involves severe allegations, against roughly one in six in 2022.
- Three quarters of greenwashing incidents in 2026 involved a company already linked to greenwashing in earlier years.

## From marketing problem to business problem

Vizcaya argues that greenwashing can no longer be fixed by rewording a campaign. If a project's risks were badly assessed, no legal review of the press release will make the claim defensible. He also notes that rules are moving in different directions: some places are tightening rules on green marketing while others are cutting reporting duties. A company can meet its legal reporting requirements and still be criticised for what it says voluntarily.

He suggests three steps for companies: check projects for their effects on water, nature and communities as well as their climate benefit; keep a record behind every environmental claim, with its scope, source, limits and a named owner; and involve the people running the project, not only communications and legal teams, when approving a claim.
