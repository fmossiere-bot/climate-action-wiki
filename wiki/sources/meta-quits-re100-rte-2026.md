# Meta quits clean energy pledge amid data centre expansion

**Source:** RTÉ News, 25 July 2026 — https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0725/1585032-meta-pledge/
Originally reported by Recharge News.

## What it is

RTÉ report that **Meta has abandoned RE100** — the UK-based Climate Group's initiative that asks major companies to source 100% of their electricity from renewable sources. Meta joined in 2016 (confirmed via a Wayback Machine archive) and is no longer listed among the 444 members. The exit comes as Meta ramps up **fossil gas contracts to power its data centre buildout**, including **10 new natural gas power plants for a single Hyperion data centre in Louisiana**.

## Key facts cited

- **RE100** has 444 members, including Apple, Google, Microsoft
- **Meta joined RE100 in 2016**; no longer listed on the Climate Group website
- Climate Group's statement to Recharge News: *"After several in-depth conversations between Meta and Climate Group, Meta has withdrawn from the RE100 initiative, as it is no longer able to meet the technical criteria due to investments made in new gas power."*
- Meta has struck deals paying utilities to bring **new natural gas power plants** online, including **10 to power its Hyperion data centre in Louisiana**
- **Microsoft signed a deal with Chevron** last month to supply natural gas electricity to a data centre in West Texas
- **Google** has also been linked to similar partnerships in press reports
- Meta says it will continue to scale investments in renewable energy and match its electricity use with 100% clean energy on an **annualised basis** (via long-term PPAs) — but Climate Group says this **no longer meets the RE100 technical criteria**

## Why it matters

Three points stand out:

1. **RE100 was one of the largest voluntary corporate clean-energy commitments in the world.** A supermajor tech company quitting rather than tightening its accounting is a significant credibility hit.
2. Meta's exit lines up with a **broader tech-industry pivot to gas for AI data centres** — Microsoft/Chevron in West Texas, similar Google deals in press reports. Natural gas has long been marketed as a "bridge fuel," but drilling and extraction leak methane and burning it emits nitric oxides, sulphur, mercury and particulates. It is inconsistent with a **100% clean electricity** claim.
3. **Annualised matching versus hourly (24/7) matching** is now the real fault line in corporate clean-energy accounting. Meta's exit essentially concedes it cannot hit 24/7 matching once its gas plants come online.
