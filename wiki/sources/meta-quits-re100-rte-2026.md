# Meta quits clean energy pledge amid data centre expansion

**Source:** RTÉ News, 25 July 2026. https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0725/1585032-meta-pledge/ (originally reported by Recharge News)

## What it is

RTÉ reports that Meta has left **RE100**, an initiative run by the UK-based Climate Group in which big companies commit to getting 100% of their electricity from renewable sources. Meta joined in 2016, which an archived copy of the Climate Group website on the Wayback Machine confirms. It no longer appears among the 444 members.

The exit comes as Meta signs more deals for fossil gas to power its growing number of data centres. For a single data centre, Hyperion in Louisiana, it is paying for 10 new natural gas power plants.

## Why it matters

RE100 is one of the biggest voluntary clean energy commitments made by companies anywhere. When one of the largest tech firms in the world leaves instead of changing how it works to stay in, it weakens trust in these pledges more widely.

Meta is not alone. Tech companies are turning to gas to run the data centres behind AI. Microsoft has a deal with Chevron in West Texas, and press reports link Google to similar arrangements. Gas has long been sold as a "bridge fuel" towards clean energy. But drilling for it and extracting it leaks methane, and burning it releases nitrogen oxides, sulphur, mercury and fine particles. That does not sit with a claim of **100% clean electricity**.

The case also shows a quieter argument about how companies count clean power. Meta says it will match its use with clean energy over a whole year. The stricter test is hourly, or 24/7, matching: clean power supplied in the same hour it is used. Leaving RE100 amounts to accepting that Meta cannot meet that test once its gas plants are running.

## Key facts

- RE100 has 444 members, including Apple, Google and Microsoft.
- Meta joined RE100 in 2016 and is no longer listed on the Climate Group website.
- The Climate Group told Recharge News: *"After several in-depth conversations between Meta and Climate Group, Meta has withdrawn from the RE100 initiative, as it is no longer able to meet the technical criteria due to investments made in new gas power."*
- Meta has struck deals paying utility companies to build new natural gas power plants, including 10 to power its Hyperion data centre in Louisiana.
- Last month, Microsoft signed a deal with Chevron to supply electricity from natural gas to a data centre in West Texas.
- Google has also been linked to similar partnerships in press reports.
- Meta says it will keep increasing its investment in renewables and will match its electricity use with 100% clean energy on a **yearly basis**, through long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs). The Climate Group says this no longer meets the RE100 technical criteria.
