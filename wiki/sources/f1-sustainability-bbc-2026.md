# Formula 1 cuts its carbon emissions by 35% since 2018

**Source:** BBC Sport, Andrew Benson, 17 June 2026. https://www.bbc.com/sport/formula1/articles/cvgjp4rj2p5o

## What it is

BBC Sport reports on Formula 1's yearly sustainability review for the 2025 season. The review tracks progress against F1's pledge to reach **Net Zero by 2030**.

## Why it matters

Formula 1 is a travelling circus. Cars, crews and equipment move around the world for every race, so most of the sport's footprint comes from logistics rather than from the cars on track. The review shows that the biggest savings so far have come from fairly ordinary changes: moving freight by ship instead of plane, buying cleaner aviation fuel and arranging the race calendar so teams travel less.

F1's own definition of net zero matters too. It means cutting emissions by at least half compared with 2018, then offsetting what is left. The figures are F1's own, calculated through a third-party reporting platform.

## Key facts

- Total CO2 emissions fell from 228,793 tonnes in 2018 to 148,805 tonnes in 2025. That is a 35% reduction, and 12% lower than the year before.
- Emissions from factories and facilities are down 64% compared with 2018.
- Logistics emissions are down 21% on the previous year and 29% compared with 2018.
- The main levers are shifting from air to sea freight, investing in sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) and grouping races by region on the calendar.
- From 2026, F1 requires sustainable fuels in races. These produce 80% fewer greenhouse gas emissions than the fossil fuels they replace.
- Reshuffling the calendar, for example moving Japan to the spring alongside Australia and China, cuts travel emissions by about 3% for some sequences of races.
- The Net Zero by 2030 pledge means a minimum 50% cut in absolute emissions compared with 2018. The emissions that cannot be avoided will be offset through what F1 calls "credible programmes".
