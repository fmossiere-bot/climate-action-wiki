# Global atmospheric water vapour record in August 2026 — Copernicus C3S

**Source:** Guardian, Graham Readfearn, 17 September 2026 — https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/sep/18/planets-atmosphere-breaks-record-for-water-vapour-levels-increasing-risk-of-extreme-heat-and-rainfall
**Primary source:** Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S), ERA5 monthly bulletin, August 2026

## What it is

Guardian reporting on a **new global record: August 2026 held more atmospheric water vapour than any month on record**, per Europe's Copernicus Climate Change Service. **Global total column water vapour hit 27.35 kg/m² for the month — 0.04 kg/m² above the previous record set in July 2024**. Multiplied across the planet's surface, that 0.04 kg/m² equals roughly **20 trillion litres** (about **41 Sydney Harbours** or "20 cubes of water, each a km wide, high and deep") of extra atmospheric water. The two drivers: **August was also the joint-hottest month in the ERA5 record (going back to 1940) and the hottest in NOAA's dataset (going back to 1850)**, and the current **historic El Niño** in the tropical Pacific — expected to be the strongest on record — is dumping vast quantities of moisture from evaporating tropical oceans into the atmosphere.

## Key facts

- **August 2026: 27.35 kg/m² global mean water vapour** — the highest monthly value in the ERA5 record (starts 1979)
- Beats the previous record (July 2024) by **0.04 kg/m² = ~20 trillion litres = 41 Sydney Harbours**
- August was also the **joint-hottest month in ERA5 (since 1940)** and **hottest month in NOAA's record (since 1850)**
- **A warmer atmosphere can hold ~7% more water vapour per degree of global warming** (the Clausius-Clapeyron relationship)
- The current **El Niño is forecast to be the strongest on record**; provisional analysis by Australian scientists finds record Pacific ocean temperatures were made **6× more likely by global heating**
- Water vapour is estimated by combining humidity data from satellites, weather balloons and other sources with a weather model

## The feedback loop

Water vapour is itself a greenhouse gas. More warming → more evaporation → more water vapour → more warming. Dr Kimberley Reid (Melbourne): *"Water vapour is also part of the climate feedback loop – water vapour is a greenhouse gas and so the atmosphere will warm even further, and then take up even more water vapour. The root cause is the extra CO2 and methane."*

## What extra atmospheric water does

- **Intensifies rainfall**, including from cyclones
- **Adds energy to storms** — Julien Nicolas (C3S): "heat is released when water vapour condenses into cloud droplets, potentially helping to intensify them"
- **Moves heat to higher latitudes** — Prof Steven Sherwood (UNSW): "when you move this water vapour to higher latitudes, it puts heat into the air, and so a lot of extreme heat that's experienced there is indirectly from the water vapour"
- **Stops night-time cooling** — Sherwood: "we know high humidity has effects during heatwaves as it stops night-time cooling, so it gets harder for people to recover from very hot days"

## Notable quotes

- **Dr Andrew Dessler** (Texas A&M): *"unimaginable that any month in the last few thousand years, maybe 100,000 years"* had had more water vapour in the atmosphere than August 2026. *"We are, as the proverb says, living in interesting times."*
- **Dr Julien Nicolas** (C3S senior scientist): *"consistent with the record high global temperature in August"* and *"the exceptionally high sea surface temperatures seen across these regions in August, partly linked to the presence and strengthening of El Niño conditions, increased evaporation and were likely an important contributor."*
- **Prof Steven Sherwood** (UNSW): *"An extra 0.04 kg m² of water vapour across the planet does not sound like a lot … when this rise is continuous, it starts to have effects. A lot of the impacts of climate change actually happen because of water vapour."*

## Why it matters

Two things:

1. **Water vapour is the mechanism behind a lot of what people feel as "climate change".** Heatwaves that don't cool at night, rainstorms that dump 100mm in an hour, cyclones that intensify faster than they used to — these are all downstream of extra atmospheric moisture. The 7%/°C rule (Clausius-Clapeyron) turns temperature records into rainfall and humidity records automatically.
2. **Direct evidence of the water-vapour feedback loop.** This isn't projection; it's the observation. As CO2 and methane warm the atmosphere, the atmosphere holds more water vapour, and water vapour then adds more warming. Every record like August 2026 makes the loop harder to ignore in models and policy.

The Dessler quote — "unimaginable any month in maybe 100,000 years had more water vapour" — is the honest edge case. We are now in a state of the atmosphere with no clear analogue in the observational or paleoclimate record.
