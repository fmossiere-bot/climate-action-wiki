# At COP17, the pressure is on to rescue a deal that could end the destruction of nature: Rebecca Ratcliffe in the Guardian

**Source:** Guardian, Rebecca Ratcliffe, 23 October 2026. https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/oct/23/cop17-deal-that-could-end-the-destruction-of-nature-deadline-armenia

## What it is

Rebecca Ratcliffe reports from Yerevan, Armenia, during the first week of COP17, the UN biodiversity summit. She opens with a hopeful scene. Just before the conference began, a camera trap in the Gndasar Community Conserved Area caught a **Persian (Caucasian) leopard**, in a country where no more than 10 are thought to survive.

The wider picture is harder. Nearly 200 countries are attending, but the targets they set four years ago at COP15 are "woefully off track". Many governments arrived with tight national budgets and attention pulled away by conflict and the energy crisis.

The main news from the first week was a new group, the Coalition of Action to Recover Endangered Species (CARES), led by Armenia and Colombia. It aims to help governments decide which species need focused help.

## Why it matters

CARES is one of the few concrete results from the first week. Under the global nature deal (the Kunming-Montreal Framework), countries have targets but no shared way of deciding which species to prioritise. CARES tries to fill that gap, and 17 countries, including the UK, have signed up.

Alongside it, 9 private funders pledged **$500 million over five years** for threatened species. That is a serious sum. But the ecologist Benoit Guénard warns that money of this kind tends to follow the animals people find appealing, like leopards, tigers and elephants. Fungi, algae and amphibians often need it more and get less.

## Key facts

- CARES was launched by Armenia and Colombia. 17 countries, including the UK, have joined to share good practice. Reverse the Red, co-chaired by Dr Kira Mileham, will run its secretariat.
- 9 private funders, including Leonardo DiCaprio's Re:wild, committed $500 million over five years to help threatened species recover.
- Only about 10 Persian leopards are thought to remain in Armenia. The leopard caught on camera was a new individual.
- At COP15, governments agreed 23 targets for 2030. They are not on track to meet them.
- Benoit Guénard (University of Hong Kong) says most conservation money goes to large, charismatic species, while fungi, algae and amphibians are neglected.
- On the ground, the Gndasar Social-Environmental NGO (led by Syuzi Karapetyan) and WWF have rebuilt the population of bezoar goats, which leopards hunt.

## Voices in the piece

Dr Kira Mileham of Reverse the Red explains why countries struggle to know where to start: "there might be hundreds of species at risk within a country's borders; which should they prioritise?" She says CARES will help them see which species needing specific support are already being looked after, and which are "falling through the cracks".

Benoit Guénard, an ecologist at the University of Hong Kong, makes the case for following the evidence: "Decisions need to be taken based on the scientific information that is available, and not just because some are cute, which unfortunately is what happens".
