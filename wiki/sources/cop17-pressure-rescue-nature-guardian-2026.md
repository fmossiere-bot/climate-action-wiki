# At COP17, the pressure is on to rescue a deal that could end the destruction of nature — Rebecca Ratcliffe, Guardian

**Source:** Guardian, Rebecca Ratcliffe, 23 October 2026 — https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/oct/23/cop17-deal-that-could-end-the-destruction-of-nature-deadline-armenia

## What it is

Guardian field reporting from Yerevan during the first week of COP17, framed around a hopeful case study: **a Persian (Caucasian) leopard spotted on a camera trap in the Gndasar Community Conserved Area** just before the conference began, in a country with no more than 10 leopards left. Ratcliffe uses the leopard as a counterpoint to the broader bleakness: **nearly 200 countries are present, targets set four years ago at COP15 are "woefully off track", and governments arrived with tight national budgets and distractions from conflict and the energy crisis**. Key announcement from the first week: the **Coalition of Action to Recover Endangered Species (CARES)**, led by Armenia and Colombia, to help governments decide which species need focused interventions.

## Key facts

- **CARES — Coalition of Action to Recover Endangered Species** launched by Armenia and Colombia; **17 countries including the UK** have joined to share best practice. Reverse the Red (co-chaired by Dr Kira Mileham) will serve as the secretariat
- **$500 million over five years** committed by **9 private funders**, including Leonardo DiCaprio's **Re:wild**, for threatened species recovery
- Only **~10 Persian leopards** are thought to remain in Armenia; the camera-trap sighting was a new individual
- Previous commitment: COP15's **23 targets** for 2030 — governments are not on track to meet them
- **Benoit Guénard** (University of Hong Kong): most conservation funding goes to large, charismatic species (leopards, tigers, elephants) — fungi, algae and amphibians are neglected
- Ratcliffe notes active conservation partnership on the ground between the **Gndasar Social-Environmental NGO** (Syuzi Karapetyan) and **WWF**, who rebuilt the bezoar goat population (a key leopard prey)

## Notable quotes

- **Dr Kira Mileham** (Reverse the Red): governments don't know where to focus — "there might be hundreds of species at risk within a country's borders; which should they prioritise?" CARES will help countries assess which species in need of specific support are already receiving attention and which are "falling through the cracks"
- **Benoit Guénard**: "Decisions need to be taken based on the scientific information that is available, and not just because some are cute, which unfortunately is what happens"

## Why it matters

Two things:

1. **CARES is a concrete multilateral outcome from the first week.** It is a species-level governance mechanism that fills a gap in the Kunming-Montreal Framework: countries have targets but no shared system for triaging species-specific efforts. 17 countries signed on, including the UK.
2. **The $500m private commitment is significant but biased.** 9 private funders putting up half a billion over five years is a serious private-biodiversity-finance signal, but the Guénard point about charismatic-species bias is a persistent problem: fungi, algae and amphibians need the money more and get less of it.
