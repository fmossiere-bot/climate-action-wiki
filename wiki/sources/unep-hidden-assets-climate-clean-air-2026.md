# Hidden assets: the economic and health case for climate and clean air action

**Source:** United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), *Hidden assets: The economic and health case for climate and clean air action*, September 2026. 189 pages. https://wedocs.unep.org/handle/20.500.11822/49870

## What it is

This UNEP assessment looks at climate change and air pollution together, rather than as two separate problems. They share the same main sources: burning fossil fuels, some farming practices and some industrial processes. The report tests a package of 25 proven solutions across energy, industry, transport, farming, cooking and heating, and waste. Its central finding is that **every dollar invested returns around 15 dollars**, with benefits that start paying back within ten years.

## Why it matters

Climate action is often sold as a cost today for a benefit decades away. Cleaner air changes that picture. Fewer people get sick or die early within a few years, and that saves money on hospitals and lost work. Counting those health gains turns climate action into one of the best investments a government can make, at a time when public budgets are tight.

## Air pollution is already a health crisis

In 2025, outdoor fine particles (PM2.5, tiny specks of soot and dust that reach deep into the lungs) and ozone caused an estimated **6.4 million premature deaths** worldwide. Smoke from cooking and heating indoors caused about 2 million more, 300,000 of them children. Outdoor PM2.5 is also linked each year to 5.5 million new cases of childhood asthma, 4.2 million heart attacks, 3.8 million cases of COPD (a long-term lung disease) and millions of cases of diabetes, stroke, dementia and lung cancer. Without new policies, the share of people breathing the most dangerous air would rise from 27% in 2025 to 34% by 2050.

## What the 25 solutions deliver

The package includes four long-term climate measures aimed at deep cuts in carbon dioxide, and 21 measures aimed at **"super pollutants"** such as methane and black carbon, which clear the air and slow warming quickly. By 2050 the solutions would halve global CO2 emissions, cut methane by 60%, and cut black carbon and other particle-forming pollution by more than 70%. Cumulatively to 2050 they would avoid an estimated 144 million premature deaths, including 62 million cases of childhood asthma, 87 million heart attacks and 36 million cases of dementia. They would also avoid about 0.34°C of warming by 2050 and about 1.4°C by 2100.

## The economics

Benefits would reach about 2.8% of global GDP by 2035, 4.5% by 2050 and about 11.4% by 2100. Costs are around 0.7% of global GDP in the coming decade, falling to about 0.5%. That gives a benefit-cost ratio of 15 to 1 and an **internal rate of return of 60%**. Even counting only money that shows up in markets, such as lower health spending and higher productivity, each dollar returns about $4. For comparison, the report notes that governments spent 2.18% of GDP on explicit fossil fuel subsidies in 2022. Clean air makes up nearly 45% of the total benefit even in 2100, so judging action on climate damage alone misses almost half the gains.

## Delay is the main enemy

The biggest obstacles are institutional rather than technical or economic: ministries working in silos, weak enforcement and poor coordination. On average, solutions are expected to arrive 7.5 to 8 years late, and institutional barriers alone account for about 2.4 years of that. Each year of delay gives up more than 0.5% of global GDP in benefits, more than $1.5 trillion. Fixing the enabling conditions could bring full implementation forward by up to a decade, worth up to $10 trillion in savings by 2040, roughly one year of global health spending.

Delhi shows what delay costs. Meeting India's current air quality standards on schedule from 2026 would cut the city's cumulative PM2.5 exposure by 20% by 2040. With an eight-year delay, the global average, the cut is only 10%.

## Summary findings

- Every $1 invested in the 25 solutions returns around $15; the internal rate of return is 60%.
- 144 million premature deaths avoided by 2050 from cleaner outdoor and indoor air and lower ozone.
- By 2050: CO2 halved, methane down 60%, black carbon and particle-forming pollutants down more than 70%.
- About 0.34°C of warming avoided by 2050 and about 1.4°C by 2100.
- Each year of delay gives up more than $1.5 trillion in benefits.
- Benefit-cost ratios range from 3 in high-income regions such as the EU and UK to 26 in Southern Africa, where health and climate risks are concentrated. About 95% of air pollution deaths occur in lower-income countries.
- Top near-term gains differ by region: diet change and shipping controls in rich regions, clean cooking in South Asia, Latin America and Sub-Saharan Africa, methane leaks and flaring in oil and gas regions, and power and household energy in fast-growing Asia.

## Limits of the source

The economic figures come from models and carry wide ranges: GDP benefits by 2100 run from 8% to 14%, for example. The 15-to-1 ratio includes "non-market" benefits such as quality of life, which are valued in money terms by assumption. The more conservative market-only return of about $4 per dollar is a useful cross-check.
