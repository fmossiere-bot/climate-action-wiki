# Emission Gap Report 2024 - UNEP

## What it is

The **Emissions Gap Report 2024: No More Hot Air... Please!** is the 15th edition of an annual assessment published by the **UN Environment Programme (UNEP)**, bringing together many of the world's leading climate scientists to track how far national climate pledges fall short of what is needed to limit global warming. **Its central warning is that nations must deliver dramatically stronger ambition in the next round of Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), or the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C goal will be gone within a few years.**

## Why it matters

This report is the annual scorecard for how the world's actual climate policies compare with what scientists say is needed. It matters to anyone trying to understand whether current pledges, including those made at COP30, are enough, and by how much they fall short.

## 2023 emissions and the top emitters

Global greenhouse gas emissions grew by **1.3%** across all sectors in 2023, excluding land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF). The report finds that the **top six emitters account for 63% of global greenhouse gas emissions**, a group that includes China, the United States, India, the European Union and Russia among the largest contributors.

## Why current policies and NDCs fall short

The report concludes that current policies and pledges are not being matched by sufficiently stringent implementation. **Full implementation of countries' unconditional NDCs would reduce expected 2030 emissions by only 4% compared with 2019 levels, and full implementation of conditional NDCs would reduce them by 10%.** Neither comes close to what is required: a **28% reduction is needed by 2030 to keep the world aligned with 2°C**, and a **42% reduction is needed for 1.5°C**.

## Why immediate action matters

Based on current policies, the report estimates that global warming is on track to reach a maximum of **3.1°C** by the end of the century, with a likely range of **1.9°C to 3.8°C**. The report is nonetheless optimistic about the technical potential to close the gap: an assessment of sectoral emission reduction options found that techno-economic mitigation potential available at a cost below **US$200 per tonne of CO2 equivalent** is sufficient to bridge the emissions gap identified for both 2030 and 2035. That potential is assessed at **31 GtCO2e per year in 2030** (range 25 to 35) and **41 GtCO2e per year in 2035** (range 36 to 46).

## Summary findings

- Global GHG emissions grew 1.3% in 2023 across all sectors excluding LULUCF.
- The top six emitters, including China, the United States, India, the European Union and Russia, account for 63% of global emissions.
- Full implementation of unconditional NDCs cuts expected 2030 emissions by only 4% versus 2019 levels; conditional NDCs, by 10%.
- A 28% cut by 2030 is needed for 2°C alignment; a 42% cut is needed for 1.5°C.
- Current policies put the world on track for up to 3.1°C of warming (range 1.9 to 3.8°C).
- Available low-cost mitigation potential (below $200/tCO2e) is technically sufficient to close the gap: 31 GtCO2e/year by 2030, 41 GtCO2e/year by 2035.
