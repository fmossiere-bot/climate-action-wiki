# Emission Gap Report 2024 - UNEP

## What it is

The **Emissions Gap Report 2024: No More Hot Air... Please!** is the 15th edition of a yearly assessment by the UN Environment Programme (UNEP). It brings together many of the world's leading climate scientists to measure how far national climate pledges fall short of what is needed to limit global warming. Its central warning is that countries must be far more ambitious in the next round of Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), their national climate plans. Otherwise the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C goal will be out of reach within a few years.

## Why it matters

This report is the yearly scorecard comparing the world's actual climate policies with what scientists say is needed. It is useful for anyone trying to judge whether current pledges, including those made at COP30, are enough, and how far short they fall.

## 2023 emissions and the top emitters

Global greenhouse gas emissions grew by **1.3%** across all sectors in 2023, not counting land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF). The report finds that the top six emitters account for **63% of global greenhouse gas emissions**. The five largest are China, the United States, India, the European Union and Russia.

## Why current policies and NDCs fall short

The report concludes that current policies and pledges are not being carried out strictly enough. If countries fully delivered their unconditional NDCs (the cuts they promise to make on their own), expected 2030 emissions would fall by only **4%** compared with 2019 levels. Fully delivering their conditional NDCs (the cuts they promise if they get outside support) would bring that to 10%. Neither comes close to what is needed: a 28% cut by 2030 to stay in line with 2°C, and a **42% cut** for 1.5°C.

## Why immediate action matters

On current policies, the report estimates that global warming is on track to reach up to **3.1°C** by the end of the century, with a likely range of 1.9°C to 3.8°C. Even so, the report sees enough technical potential to close the gap. Looking across sectors, it found that the emission cuts available at a cost below **US$200 per tonne of CO2 equivalent** would be enough to bridge the gap for both 2030 and 2035. That potential is put at 31 GtCO2e (billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent) per year in 2030 (range 25 to 35) and 41 GtCO2e per year in 2035 (range 36 to 46).

## Summary findings

- Global greenhouse gas emissions grew 1.3% in 2023 across all sectors, excluding LULUCF.
- The top six emitters account for 63% of global emissions. The five largest are China, the United States, India, the European Union and Russia.
- Full implementation of unconditional NDCs cuts expected 2030 emissions by only 4% compared with 2019 levels. Conditional NDCs cut them by 10%.
- A 28% cut by 2030 is needed to stay in line with 2°C. A 42% cut is needed for 1.5°C.
- Current policies put the world on track for up to 3.1°C of warming (range 1.9 to 3.8°C).
- Emission cuts available below $200/tCO2e would be technically enough to close the gap: 31 GtCO2e/year by 2030, 41 GtCO2e/year by 2035.
