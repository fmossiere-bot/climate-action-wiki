# Carbon Emissions and the Carbon Cycle

Also see [Emissions and the Global Carbon Cycle](../../climate-science/Emissions%20and%20the%20Global%20Carbon%20Cycle.md) for a deeper look at greenhouse gas comparisons and carbon sinks.

Carbon is different from CO2 emissions. Carbon **stock** means the accumulation of carbon from CO2 emissions over time: 40 gigatonnes (GT) of CO2 emissions increases the carbon stock by around 4.9 GT once the roughly 55% removed by natural sinks is taken into account. Today's total carbon stock is around 880 GT, corresponding to an atmospheric concentration of 412 parts per million (ppm).

**We have a budget of around 420 GT of CO2 over roughly the next 10 years to keep a 66% chance of staying under 1.5°C of warming.**

A useful way to think about this is a bathtub. Carbon stays in the atmosphere for a very long time, often hundreds of years, so reaching net-zero does not mean removing existing carbon: it means we stop adding more, and let natural sinks slowly draw down what is already there to mitigate the warming effect.

Not all of the extra carbon humans emit stays in the air. About a quarter of it ends up in the ocean, and roughly 30% is absorbed by plants and trees. This leaves around [44%](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/14/4811/2022/#:~:text=trend%2C%20remaining%20at-,around%2044%E2%80%89%25,-%2C%20albeit%20showing%20a) of CO2 emissions piling up in the atmosphere, netting out to more than 5 billion tonnes of carbon added every year on average over the last decade. The rate of absorption by vegetation and oceans has actually increased over time as more carbon has been added to the atmosphere.
