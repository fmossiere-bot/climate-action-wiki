# Climate TRACE: Real-time Emissions Data Goes Live

## What is it
**Climate TRACE** is a coalition of nonprofits, tech companies, universities and researchers, co-founded by former US Vice President Al Gore. It tracks global greenhouse gas emissions with satellites, sensors and artificial intelligence, instead of relying on countries or companies to report their own figures. On 28 March 2025 the coalition released the world's first monthly update on global greenhouse gas emissions. It is built from direct, checked observation rather than self-reported pledges or slow government inventories, and there is a lag of just **60 days** between an emission happening and it showing up in the data.

## Why it matters
Until now, estimates of global emissions have relied heavily on figures supplied by the polluters themselves. These are gathered into inventories that can be years behind what is actually happening, so we only had a rough idea of what was going into the atmosphere at any given time. Climate TRACE's monthly, satellite-checked figures aim to replace that guesswork with something much closer to real-time accounting. That makes it far harder for emissions to go unnoticed or unreported.

## How the monitoring works
Climate TRACE's network watches more than **660 million individual emission sources** around the world, including power plants, factories, farms and ships. AI programs analyse heat signatures picked up by satellites, images that show which gases are present (spectral imagery) and operating data to produce monthly updates. The satellite and machine-learning results are then checked independently against data collected on the ground. The coalition says the accuracy is comparable to quarterly financial reporting. The method is not perfect, but it is a big step up from the self-reported inventories that global emissions tracking used to rely on.

As Al Gore put it: "With Climate TRACE, we're making the invisible visible."
