# Climate TRACE — Real-time Emissions Data Goes Live

## What is it
**Climate TRACE** is a coalition of nonprofits, tech companies, universities and researchers, co-founded by former US Vice President **Al Gore**, that tracks global greenhouse gas emissions using satellites, sensors and artificial intelligence rather than relying on countries or companies to report their own figures. On 28 March 2025 the coalition released the world's first-ever monthly update on global greenhouse gas emissions, built from direct, verified observation rather than self-reported pledges or slow-moving government inventories, with a lag of just 60 days between an emission occurring and it appearing in the data.

## Why it matters
Historically, estimates of global greenhouse gas emissions have relied heavily on data supplied by the polluters themselves, compiled into inventories that can lag years behind real-world emissions, leaving only a vague approximation of what is currently being released into the atmosphere. Climate TRACE's monthly, satellite-verified figures are intended to replace that guesswork with something closer to real-time accounting, making it much harder for emissions to go unnoticed or unreported.

## How the monitoring works
Climate TRACE's network monitors more than 660 million individual emission sources worldwide, including power plants, factories, farms and shipping vessels. AI-powered algorithms analyse satellite heat signatures, spectral imagery and operational data to produce accurate monthly updates, cross-referencing satellite imagery and machine-learning analysis with independent validation against ground-based data sources. The coalition describes the resulting accuracy as comparable to quarterly financial reporting, a significant step up from the self-reported inventories that previously formed the backbone of global emissions tracking, even though the methodology is not perfect.

As Al Gore put it: "With Climate TRACE, we're making the invisible visible."
