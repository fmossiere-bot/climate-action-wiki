# World Resources Institute 2023 — State of Climate Action

## What it is
The **State of Climate Action 2023**, published by the World Resources Institute (WRI) ahead of the final phase of the Paris Agreement's Global Stocktake, offers a roadmap for the world to avoid increasingly dangerous and irreversible climate impacts while minimising harm to biodiversity and food security. It translates the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C temperature limit into 2030 and 2050 targets across the sectors that together account for roughly 85% of global greenhouse gas emissions: power, buildings, industry, transport, forests and land, and food and agriculture, alongside targets for scaling up technological carbon removal and climate finance. **The report's headline finding is that global efforts to limit warming to 1.5°C are failing across the board**, with progress on every indicator except electric passenger car sales lagging well behind the pace and scale needed.

## Why it matters
This report functions as an annual scorecard for how far the world's real-world climate progress falls short of what the science says is necessary, sector by sector. It gives a concrete sense of both how big the remaining gap is and where the fastest progress is actually happening.

## Progress is falling short almost everywhere
Of the 42 indicators the report assesses, **41 are not on track to hit their 2030 targets**, and progress on more than half of these remains so far off course that recent efforts would need to at least double this decade just to catch up. A further six indicators are heading in the wrong direction entirely. The steepest single-year setbacks were in ending public financing for fossil fuels, cutting deforestation, and expanding carbon pricing systems. In 2021, public financing for fossil fuels increased sharply, with government subsidies nearly doubling from 2020 to reach their highest level in almost a decade; in 2022, deforestation increased slightly to 5.8 million hectares (Mha) worldwide, an area of forest loss larger than Croatia in a single year.

## Where progress is genuinely encouraging
Not every indicator is moving the wrong way. Over the past five years, electric vehicles' share of passenger car sales has grown at an average annual rate of 65%, rising from 1.6% of sales in 2018 to 10% in 2022; for the first time in this report series, that indicator is now on track for its 2030 target. Six further indicators are heading in the right direction, albeit still too slowly, and could see faster progress with the right support; among these, mandatory corporate climate risk disclosure, electric truck sales, and the EV share of the total passenger car fleet saw the most significant single-year gains.

## How much faster the world needs to move
Even with these bright spots, the report finds an enormous acceleration in effort is required across every sector to get on track for 2030. Growth in solar and wind power's share of electricity generation needs to nearly double, from an annual average of 14% in recent years to 24%. Coal needs to be phased out of electricity generation **seven times faster** than current rates, equivalent to retiring roughly 240 average-sized coal-fired power plants every year through 2030. Rapid transit infrastructure needs to expand **six times faster**, equivalent to building public transit systems roughly three times the size of New York City's subway, bus lane and light-rail network every year this decade. Deforestation, running at the equivalent of 15 football (soccer) pitches per minute in 2022, needs to slow **four times faster** over the decade. And the shift to healthier, more sustainable diets needs to happen **eight times faster**, by lowering per-capita consumption of beef, goat and lamb to roughly two servings a week or less across the Americas, Europe and Oceania by 2030; this shift does not require cutting consumption for populations already below that level, especially in low-income countries where modest increases can boost nutrition.

## Who produced the report
The *State of Climate Action* series is published under Systems Change Lab, a joint effort of the Bezos Earth Fund, Climate Action Tracker (a project of Climate Analytics and NewClimate Institute), the ClimateWorks Foundation, the UN Climate Change High-Level Champions, and the World Resources Institute.

## Summary findings
- 41 of 42 indicators assessed are not on track to hit their 2030 targets; six are moving in the wrong direction entirely.
- Fossil fuel subsidies nearly doubled from 2020 to 2021, reaching their highest level in almost a decade.
- Deforestation rose to 5.8 million hectares worldwide in 2022, an area larger than Croatia.
- EV share of passenger car sales grew from 1.6% (2018) to 10% (2022), an average annual growth rate of 65%, the only indicator on track for 2030.
- Coal needs to be phased out of power generation seven times faster, equivalent to retiring around 240 coal plants a year through 2030.
- Rapid transit infrastructure needs to expand six times faster than current rates.
- Diet shifts towards lower meat consumption need to happen eight times faster to meet 2030 targets.
