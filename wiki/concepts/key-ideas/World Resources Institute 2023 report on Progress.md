# World Resources Institute 2023: State of Climate Action

## What it is
The **State of Climate Action 2023** was published by the World Resources Institute (WRI) ahead of the final stage of the Paris Agreement's Global Stocktake, the five-yearly check on how the world is doing. It sets out a route for avoiding ever more dangerous and irreversible climate impacts while doing as little harm as possible to wildlife and food security. It turns the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C limit into targets for 2030 and 2050 across the sectors that together cause roughly 85% of global greenhouse gas emissions: power, buildings, industry, transport, forests and land, and food and farming. It also sets targets for scaling up technology that removes carbon and for climate finance. Its main finding is that global efforts to limit warming to 1.5°C are failing across the board. On every indicator except sales of electric cars, progress is well behind the pace and scale needed.

## Why it matters
The report works as a yearly scorecard, sector by sector, of how far real-world climate progress falls short of what the science says is needed. It gives a clear sense of how big the remaining gap is, and of where progress is moving fastest.

## Progress is falling short almost everywhere
Of the 42 indicators the report looks at, **41 are not on track to meet their 2030 targets**. More than half of these are so far off course that recent efforts would need to at least double this decade just to catch up. Six more indicators are heading in the wrong direction altogether. The biggest setbacks in a single year were in ending public money for fossil fuels, cutting deforestation, and expanding carbon pricing. In 2021, public financing for fossil fuels rose sharply, with government subsidies nearly doubling from 2020 to their highest level in almost a decade. In 2022, deforestation rose slightly to 5.8 million hectares (Mha) worldwide, an area of forest bigger than Croatia lost in a single year.

## Where progress is encouraging
Not every indicator is going the wrong way. Over the past five years, the share of new passenger cars sold that are electric has grown by an average of 65% a year, from **1.6% of sales in 2018 to 10% in 2022**. For the first time in this report series, that indicator is on track for its 2030 target. Six more indicators are heading in the right direction, though still too slowly, and could speed up with the right support. Of these, mandatory reporting of climate risk by companies, sales of electric lorries, and the electric share of all passenger cars on the road made the biggest gains in a single year.

## How much faster the world needs to move
Even with these bright spots, the report finds that every sector needs to speed up enormously to get on track for 2030. The growth in solar and wind's share of electricity needs to nearly double, from an average of 14% a year recently to 24%. Coal needs to be phased out of electricity **seven times faster** than it is now, which means closing roughly 240 average-sized coal power plants every year until 2030. Rapid public transport needs to expand six times faster, the same as building public transport roughly three times the size of New York City's subway, bus lane and light-rail network every year this decade. Deforestation, which in 2022 ran at the equivalent of 15 football pitches every minute, needs to slow four times faster over the decade. The shift to healthier, more sustainable diets needs to happen eight times faster. That means cutting how much beef, goat and lamb each person eats to roughly two servings a week or less across the Americas, Europe and Oceania by 2030. People who already eat less than that do not need to cut back, especially in low-income countries, where eating a little more can improve nutrition.

## Who produced the report
The *State of Climate Action* series is published by Systems Change Lab, a joint effort of the Bezos Earth Fund, Climate Action Tracker (a project of Climate Analytics and NewClimate Institute), the ClimateWorks Foundation, the UN Climate Change High-Level Champions, and the World Resources Institute.

## Summary findings
- 41 of the 42 indicators assessed are not on track to meet their 2030 targets, and six are moving in the wrong direction altogether.
- Fossil fuel subsidies nearly doubled from 2020 to 2021, reaching their highest level in almost a decade.
- Deforestation rose to 5.8 million hectares worldwide in 2022, an area larger than Croatia.
- The electric share of passenger car sales grew from 1.6% (2018) to 10% (2022), an average growth of 65% a year. It is the only indicator on track for 2030.
- Coal needs to be phased out of power generation seven times faster, the same as closing around 240 coal plants a year until 2030.
- Rapid public transport needs to expand six times faster than it is now.
- The shift towards eating less meat needs to happen eight times faster to meet 2030 targets.
