_What they are, why they exist, and how to use them well_

---

## What is it?

A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gas that comes from your way of life in a given year: how you travel, what you eat, how you heat your home and what you buy. It is usually measured in **tonnes of CO₂ equivalent (CO₂e)**.

Carbon footprint calculators are online tools that estimate this number from your answers to a series of questions about your habits. Most take between 5 and 15 minutes to complete.

> ⚠️ **A word of warning on origins.** The term "carbon footprint" was not invented by environmentalists. It was popularised in **2004 by BP**, the oil company, as part of a large PR campaign designed by the advertising firm Ogilvy & Mather. The aim was to move the conversation about climate change away from the fossil fuel industry and towards individuals. BP even launched one of the first carbon footprint calculators as part of the campaign. That context matters. Climate change is mainly a systemic problem caused by large corporations and governments, not by people's recycling habits.

Even so, the tool itself has become useful, whatever its manipulative origins. Used with the right mindset, a calculator can give you a rough idea of where your personal impact is biggest. The word that matters is _rough_. These calculators rely on national averages and broad assumptions, so they give approximations, not precise measurements. Think of them as a compass rather than a GPS.

---

## Why it matters

The main value of a calculator is less the number it gives you than the perspective. Most of us have a surprisingly distorted picture of what really drives our personal emissions.

> 💡 Research consistently shows that people tend to **underestimate the impact of flying** and overestimate the impact of everyday actions like recycling or switching off lights. A single long-haul return flight can produce more emissions than several months of average car use.

So if you are vegetarian, cycle to work and buy second-hand, but fly somewhere on holiday every few months, your flights are where to focus, not which bin to use. A calculator helps you see that.

It also helps you sort out which parts of your life you _do_ control and which depend on wider circumstances, such as public transport, housing options or your country's energy grid. That distinction shapes where you put your energy, both in your own choices and in pushing for bigger change.

## Key Facts

### Benchmarks by country

To make sense of your own result, it helps to know where different countries stand.

|Country / Region|Tonnes CO₂e per person/year|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|🌍 Global average|~4.7|Average across all countries|
|🇮🇪 Ireland|~14.0|2nd highest in the EU (consumption-based, 2023)|
|🇪🇺 EU average|~9.0|Consumption-based, 2023|
|🇺🇸 USA|~17.6|Nearly four times the global average|
|🇮🇳 India|~2.2|Below global average|
|🌍 Sub-Saharan Africa|~0.1|Chad, Niger, CAR: around 175 times lower than the US|

**The target:** climate scientists say we need to get to around **2 tonnes per person per year** to limit global warming to 1.5°C. For most people in Ireland, that means cutting emissions by roughly 85%.

### Production vs consumption: an important distinction

Most country figures you see are **production-based**. They count only the emissions released inside a country's borders, which can be misleading. If Ireland imports goods made in Asia, the emissions from making them don't show up in our national statistics.

**Consumption-based figures** give a fuller picture. They count emissions wherever they happened, as long as they came from making things we consume. Ireland's consumption-based footprint is much higher than its production-based one. Eurostat data shows that the EU's total footprint is about 21% higher when measured on a consumption basis.

### Where do individual emissions actually come from?

Research from Our World in Data, the IEA and the UN shows a consistent pattern for people in wealthier countries:

|Sector|Approximate share|
|---|---|
|🚗 Transport (car + flights)|~30–35%|
|🥩 Food & diet|~20–25%|
|🏠 Home energy|~15–20%|
|🛍️ Goods & shopping|~10–15%|
|🔧 Services & other|~10%|

In Ireland, **transport** is the biggest single factor: the average Irish person drives roughly 16,000–17,000 km a year. Food, beef in particular, also counts for much more than in many other European countries. For many households, flying adds a large amount on top.

## The main carbon footprint calculators

There are several good tools available, and they vary in depth and approach. These are the main ones worth trying.

### ⭐ ClimateHero _(recommended)_

**[climatehero.me](https://climatehero.me/)**

Takes about 5 minutes and covers housing, travel and consumption. It draws on over 30 public data sources and works well for Ireland and the UK. Rather than fussing over exact decimal points, it places you in one of four clear types: Climate Hero, Friend, Consumer or Villain. You can also commit to a reduction pledge and see how your score compares with other people's. Over 2 million people have used it.

### Carbon Footprint Ltd

**[carbonfootprint.com/individuals.html](https://www.carbonfootprint.com/individuals.html)**

One of the more detailed tools. It goes further into household energy, travel, shopping and your secondary footprint (the emissions from things you buy), and also covers businesses and events. A good choice if you want more detailed results and advice on cutting emissions.

### WWF Footprint Calculator

**[footprint.wwf.org.uk](https://footprint.wwf.org.uk/#/)**

Run by the World Wildlife Fund. A simple questionnaire, aimed at the UK but useful elsewhere. Good for a quick first look: less detailed than ClimateHero, but friendly and easy to use.

### SEAI (Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland)

**[seai.ie](https://www.seai.ie/)**

Not a full personal calculator, but the best Irish source for understanding your home's energy use and where you can make changes. It is useful for heating, insulation and electricity, which together make up a large part of most Irish households' footprint.
