Green Digest is built around the principle of double materiality, which recognises that a company's impact runs in two directions: it affects both the environment and society at large, and those external factors in turn affect the company's own financial and operational performance. While traditional ESG assessments focus mainly on the latter, Green Digest aims to examine companies' direct impacts on people and planet, using a scoring system that quantifies that impact. More on the methodology is available on [Green Digest's site](https://www.greendigest.co/c/impactmethodology).

## A deep dive into Glencore's environmental and social impact

Glencore is one of the world's largest globally diversified natural resource companies, with a strong presence in mining, metals, energy and agriculture.

**Some facts:** Glencore operates some of the oldest mines in the world; for example, the Mount Isa Mines in Australia, which Glencore owns, have been operational since the early 20th century and remain among the largest producers of copper and zinc globally. The company has also been embroiled in numerous controversies over the years, including accusations of environmental degradation, human rights violations and corruption, and its operations are closely watched by regulators and environmental groups given the scale of its mining activities.

## What is the company's impact?

Glencore has a general impact score of -2.59 on Green Digest's scale, which runs from -5 to +5. Its impact is spread across 12 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 10 topics, split between positive and negative analyses.

**In the socio-economic sphere,** Glencore's operations are crucial to the global supply of essential raw materials. The company's mining and resource extraction activities provide critical inputs for industries ranging from technology to construction. Glencore's extensive supply chain supports thousands of jobs, and the company plays a significant role in the economies of the regions where it operates. It is the second-largest mining company globally by brand value, providing over 60 commodities and operating in more than 35 countries. Glencore employs 150,000 people, offering salaries above the country and industry average, though the company has not achieved gender parity at any level of its workforce.

However, Glencore's socio-economic impact is not without serious controversy. The company has faced numerous allegations of corruption, unethical practices and human rights abuses, particularly in developing countries, including accusations of exploiting labour, causing environmental degradation, and complicity in fuelling conflicts over resource-rich areas. Glencore was found guilty of bribery and corruption in Cameroon, Guinea, Ivory Coast, Nigeria, South Sudan, Brazil, Venezuela and the Democratic Republic of Congo, spanning roughly 2007 to 2019, resulting in billions of dollars in fines. According to a 2023 investigation report, its mines have also adversely affected the health of at least 336,000 people in nearby communities by contaminating their water supplies.

**Environmentally,** Glencore emits over 433 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent annually, equal to the emissions of 111 coal-fired power plants for a year. The company also uses more than 558 million cubic metres of water annually, 44% of it in medium-high, high or extremely high water-stressed areas. It generates approximately 2.31 billion tonnes of waste, of which it sustainably manages only around 1%. Glencore's operations span over 2 million hectares of land; as of 2021, only 37,000 hectares of this had been rehabilitated, leaving around 93,000 hectares, roughly 71% of disturbed land, still in need of restoration.

The impact score is current as of August 2024 and may change as it is continuously updated.

## ESG rating versus impact score

ESG ratings from MSCI ESG Research are designed to measure a company's resilience to financially material ESG risks: they measure how effectively a company manages ESG risks to itself, not the company's impact on the wider world. This is why Glencore's Green Digest impact score and its MSCI ESG rating can diverge significantly.

## Impact scores by SDG

Positively, and weighted by significance, Glencore scores highest on Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure (+2.75) and Partnership for the Goals (+2.45). Negatively, it scores worst on Responsible Consumption and Production (-3.15), followed by Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions (-4.47) and Climate Action (-3.68).

## Conclusion

Glencore's key social and environmental impact lies in its critical role in the global supply of essential raw materials that drive industries from technology to construction. As the second-largest mining company globally, it supports economic growth and provides livelihoods for thousands of people across its supply chain in more than 35 countries, and its extensive operations contribute significantly to regional economies.

However, Glencore's operations come with substantial socio-economic and environmental costs. The company has faced serious allegations of corruption, unethical practices and human rights abuses, particularly in developing countries, resulting in billions of dollars in fines. Its mining activities have also had devastating effects on local communities, including contaminating water sources that have negatively affected the health of hundreds of thousands of people. Environmentally, Glencore is a major greenhouse gas emitter, releasing over 433 million tonnes of CO2 annually, generates vast amounts of waste with only a small fraction managed sustainably, and leaves a significant portion of its disturbed land unrestored.

Its overall score of -2.59 reflects the severity of these factors, highlighting the considerable challenges Glencore faces in balancing its essential role in the global economy with the pressing need for ethical practices and environmental stewardship.
