Green Digest rates companies using **double materiality**. The idea is that a company's impact runs both ways: it affects the environment and society, and those outside factors in turn affect the company's own finances and operations. Traditional ESG assessments mostly look at the second direction. Green Digest focuses on the first, a company's direct impact on people and planet, and gives it a score. You can read more about the method on [Green Digest's site](https://www.greendigest.co/c/impactmethodology).

## A deep dive into Glencore's environmental and social impact

Glencore is one of the world's largest diversified natural resource companies, active in mining, metals, energy and agriculture.

**Some facts:** Glencore runs some of the oldest mines in the world. The Mount Isa Mines in Australia, which it owns, have been working since the early 20th century and are still among the largest producers of copper and zinc anywhere. The company has been caught up in many controversies over the years, including accusations of environmental damage, human rights violations and corruption. Given the scale of its mining, regulators and environmental groups keep a close eye on it.

## What is the company's impact?

On Green Digest's scale, which runs from -5 to +5, Glencore has an overall impact score of **-2.59**. Its impact touches 12 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 10 topics, some scored positively and some negatively.

**On the social and economic side,** Glencore matters to the global supply of raw materials. Its mines feed industries from technology to construction, its supply chain supports thousands of jobs, and it plays a big part in the economies of the regions where it works. It is the second-largest mining company in the world by brand value, supplies over 60 commodities and operates in more than 35 countries. Glencore employs 150,000 people and pays above the national and industry average, though it has not reached gender parity at any level of its workforce.

That picture comes with serious controversy. Glencore has faced many allegations of corruption, unethical practices and human rights abuses, particularly in developing countries, including exploiting workers, damaging the environment and helping to fuel conflicts over resource-rich areas. It was found guilty of bribery and corruption in Cameroon, Guinea, Ivory Coast, Nigeria, South Sudan, Brazil, Venezuela and the Democratic Republic of Congo, over a period running roughly from 2007 to 2019, and paid billions of dollars in fines. A 2023 investigation report found that its mines had also harmed the health of at least 336,000 people in nearby communities by contaminating their water.

**On the environmental side,** Glencore emits over 433 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent a year, the same as 111 coal-fired power plants running for a year. It uses more than 558 million cubic metres of water a year, 44% of it in areas where water is under medium-high, high or extremely high stress. It produces approximately 2.31 billion tonnes of waste and manages only around 1% of it sustainably. Its operations cover over 2 million hectares of land. As of 2021, only 37,000 hectares had been rehabilitated, leaving around 93,000 hectares, roughly 71% of the land it had disturbed, still waiting to be restored.

The score is current as of August 2024 and is updated continuously, so it may change.

## ESG rating versus impact score

ESG ratings from MSCI ESG Research measure how well a company copes with ESG risks that could hurt its finances. In other words, they measure the risk the world poses to the company, not the harm or good the company does to the world. That is why Glencore's Green Digest impact score and its MSCI ESG rating can tell very different stories.

## Impact scores by SDG

Weighted by significance, Glencore's best scores are for Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure (+2.75) and Partnership for the Goals (+2.45). Its most significant negative scores, which are not listed in order of score, are for Responsible Consumption and Production (-3.15), Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions (-4.47) and Climate Action (-3.68).

## Overall picture

Glencore's main contribution is its role in supplying raw materials that industries from technology to construction depend on. As the second-largest mining company in the world, it supports economic growth and livelihoods for thousands of people across its supply chain in more than 35 countries, and it is a large part of many regional economies.

The costs are heavy, socially and environmentally. The company has faced serious allegations of corruption, unethical practices and human rights abuses, particularly in developing countries, and has paid billions of dollars in fines. Its mines have contaminated water sources and harmed the health of hundreds of thousands of people living nearby. It is a major greenhouse gas emitter, releasing over 433 million tonnes of CO2 a year, it produces huge amounts of waste and manages only a small fraction sustainably, and much of the land it has disturbed is still unrestored.

Its overall score of -2.59 reflects how serious these problems are, and how far Glencore still has to go to match its role in the global economy with ethical practice and care for the environment.
