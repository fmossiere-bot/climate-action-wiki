Green Digest is built around the principle of double materiality, which recognises that a company's impact runs in two directions: it affects both the environment and society at large, and those external factors in turn affect the company's own financial and operational performance. While traditional ESG assessments focus mainly on the latter, Green Digest aims to examine companies' direct impacts on people and planet, using a scoring system that quantifies that impact. More on the methodology is available on [Green Digest's site](https://www.greendigest.co/c/impactmethodology).

**Some interesting facts:** L'Oréal began in 1909, when its founder Eugène Schueller created the first-ever synthetic hair dye, marking the beginning of modern hair colouring. L'Oréal was also among the first companies to ban animal testing for its products, in 1989, 14 years before it became a legal requirement in the European Union.

## Why L'Oréal has a negative impact score

L'Oréal has a general impact score of -0.92 on Green Digest's scale, which runs from -5 to +5. Its impact is spread across 11 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 11 topics, split between positive and negative analyses.

The company's overall score reflects its position as the world's leading beauty brand, serving over 1 billion customers annually with products that support hygiene, self-care and personal expression. Through its global workforce of over 94,600 employees and partnerships with 50,000 suppliers, L'Oréal contributes to economic activity and job creation, and supports local development in the regions where it operates.

However, cases of harmful chemicals in its products, including toxic substances such as lead and PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances), have raised serious concerns about consumer safety, potentially affecting hundreds of millions of customers. Criticism regarding sourcing practices tied to regions associated with child and forced labour also highlights gaps in supply chain oversight. Environmentally, while L'Oréal shows strong performance in waste recovery and packaging innovation, significant challenges remain, including its continued reliance on non-recyclable materials, substantial CO2 emissions, and water usage, particularly in water-stressed regions.

L'Oréal's overall impact score of -0.92 reflects the balance between its significant contribution to global beauty and well-being and the critical ethical and environmental issues the company still needs to address.

**In the socio-economic sphere,** L'Oréal is the number one beauty brand worldwide, with a presence in 150 countries. It provides around 7 billion products to over 1 billion customers annually, across a portfolio of skincare, haircare, makeup, perfumes and other cosmetics that support personal hygiene, self-care routines and individual expression. The company employs over 94,600 people globally and works with over 50,000 suppliers worldwide, supporting local economies by contributing to job creation and economic activity in the regions where its suppliers operate.

However, L'Oréal has faced significant allegations concerning the use of harmful chemicals in its products, potentially affecting an estimated 700 million customers. Investigations have uncovered toxic substances, including lead, formaldehyde-releasing agents, PFAS and other potential carcinogens, raising concerns about product safety. The company has also been criticised for sourcing raw materials from regions associated with child labour, forced labour and exploitative working conditions.

**Environmentally,** L'Oréal has an annual carbon footprint of around 11.5 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent, comparable to the annual electricity usage of approximately 2.4 million homes. It generates around 93,500 tonnes of operational waste, including 88,000 tonnes at industrial sites, of which 97% is recovered through reuse, recycling or energy recovery. The company also uses around 170,800 tonnes of plastic packaging, of which 44% is recyclable, reusable and/or compostable, meaning a majority, 56%, remains non-recyclable. L'Oréal also consumes over 1 million cubic metres of water annually, around 33% of it drawn from water-stressed regions.

## ESG rating versus impact score

ESG ratings from MSCI ESG Research are designed to measure a company's resilience to financially material ESG risks: they measure how effectively a company manages ESG risks to itself, not the company's impact on the wider world. This is why a company's Green Digest impact score and its MSCI ESG rating can diverge significantly.

## Impact score by SDG

Positively, and weighted by significance, L'Oréal scores highest on No Poverty (+3.01), followed by Partnership for the Goals (+2.88). Negatively, it scores worst on Good Health and Well-being (-0.21), followed by Responsible Consumption and Production (-2.32) and Decent Work and Economic Growth (-1.49).

L'Oréal positively impacts SDG 3, Good Health and Well-being, by providing products that support personal hygiene, self-care and confidence, contributing to overall well-being. However, it also impacts the same SDG negatively, due to cases of harmful chemicals in its products, such as lead and PFAS, which raise concerns about consumer safety and health risks.
