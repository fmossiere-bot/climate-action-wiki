Green Digest rates companies using **double materiality**. The idea is that a company's impact runs both ways: it affects the environment and society, and those outside factors in turn affect the company's own finances and operations. Traditional ESG assessments mostly look at the second direction. Green Digest focuses on the first, a company's direct impact on people and planet, and gives it a score. You can read more about the method on [Green Digest's site](https://www.greendigest.co/c/impactmethodology).

**Some interesting facts:** L'Oréal started in 1909, when its founder Eugène Schueller created the first synthetic hair dye, the start of modern hair colouring. It was also one of the first companies to stop testing its products on animals, in 1989, 14 years before the European Union made it a legal requirement.

## Why L'Oréal has a negative impact score

On Green Digest's scale, which runs from -5 to +5, L'Oréal has an overall impact score of **-0.92**. Its impact touches 11 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 11 topics, some scored positively and some negatively.

The score reflects L'Oréal's position as the world's leading beauty brand. It serves over 1 billion customers a year with products for hygiene, self-care and personal expression. With more than 94,600 employees and 50,000 suppliers, it creates jobs and economic activity and supports local development where it operates.

Against that, harmful chemicals have been found in some of its products, including toxic substances such as lead and PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, sometimes called "forever chemicals"). That raises serious safety concerns that could affect hundreds of millions of customers. Criticism of its sourcing from regions linked to child and forced labour also points to gaps in how it checks its supply chain. On the environment, L'Oréal does well on waste recovery and new packaging, but problems remain: it still relies on non-recyclable materials, its CO2 emissions are large, and it uses a lot of water, some of it in places already short of water.

The overall score of -0.92 weighs L'Oréal's large contribution to beauty and well-being worldwide against the ethical and environmental problems it still has to fix.

**On the social and economic side,** L'Oréal is the number one beauty brand in the world, present in 150 countries. It sells around 7 billion products a year to over 1 billion customers, across skincare, haircare, make-up, perfumes and other cosmetics. It employs over 94,600 people and works with over 50,000 suppliers worldwide, which supports jobs and economic activity in the regions where those suppliers are based.

L'Oréal has also faced serious allegations about harmful chemicals in its products, which could affect an estimated 700 million customers. Investigations have found toxic substances including lead, formaldehyde-releasing agents, PFAS and other possible carcinogens. The company has also been criticised for sourcing raw materials from regions linked to child labour, forced labour and exploitative working conditions.

**On the environmental side,** L'Oréal's carbon footprint is around 11.5 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent a year, about the same as the yearly electricity use of approximately 2.4 million homes. It produces around 93,500 tonnes of operational waste, including 88,000 tonnes at industrial sites, and 97% of that is recovered through reuse, recycling or energy recovery. It uses around 170,800 tonnes of plastic packaging. Only 44% of it is recyclable, reusable and/or compostable, so the majority, 56%, is not. L'Oréal also uses over 1 million cubic metres of water a year, around 33% of it from regions under water stress.

## ESG rating versus impact score

ESG ratings from MSCI ESG Research measure how well a company copes with ESG risks that could hurt its finances. In other words, they measure the risk the world poses to the company, not the harm or good the company does to the world. That is why a company's Green Digest impact score and its MSCI ESG rating can tell very different stories.

## Impact score by SDG

Weighted by significance, L'Oréal's best scores are for No Poverty (+3.01), followed by Partnership for the Goals (+2.88). Its most significant negative scores, which are not listed in order of score, are for Good Health and Well-being (-0.21), Responsible Consumption and Production (-2.32) and Decent Work and Economic Growth (-1.49).

SDG 3, Good Health and Well-being, shows both sides of the company. L'Oréal helps it by selling products for hygiene, self-care and confidence. It also harms it, because harmful chemicals such as lead and PFAS found in its products raise concerns about safety and health.
