## Google's emissions rise 48% as AI demand grows

Source: The Guardian, "Google's emissions climb nearly 50% in five years as AI demand upends climate pledges," 2 July 2024 — https://www.theguardian.com/technology/article/2024/jul/02/google-ai-emissions

Google's goal of reducing its climate footprint is in jeopardy as it relies on more and more energy-hungry data centres to power its new artificial intelligence products. The company revealed in July 2024 that its greenhouse gas emissions had climbed 48% over the previous five years. Its [2024 environmental report](https://www.gstatic.com/gumdrop/sustainability/google-2024-environmental-report.pdf) also showed that emissions in 2023 had risen 13% compared with the previous year, reaching 14.3 million metric tonnes. Google's emissions have risen by nearly 50% since 2019, the baseline year for its goal of reaching net zero, a target that requires the company to remove as much CO2 as it emits.

**Data centre energy demand more broadly:** the International Energy Agency estimates that data centres' total electricity consumption could double from 2022 levels to 1,000 terawatt-hours (TWh) by 2026, roughly equivalent to Japan's total electricity demand. AI is expected to push data centres to use 4.5% of global energy generation by 2030, according to calculations by the research firm SemiAnalysis.

## Turning to nuclear power

Because of this surging energy demand, most large tech companies, Google included, are now turning to nuclear power. In May 2025, nuclear developer Elementl Power announced it had signed an agreement with Google to develop three project sites for advanced reactors. Google will commit early-stage development capital to the three projects, each of which is expected to generate at least 600 megawatts. This is the latest example of a tech giant partnering with the nuclear industry to help meet the vast energy needs of its data centres.

This is not Google's only nuclear investment. In October 2024, Google teamed up with small modular reactor company Kairos Power, agreeing to buy power from its planned fleet of reactors. At the time, Google said the first reactor would enter service by 2030, with more coming online through 2035.

## Turning landfill waste into biochar for carbon capture

Source: We Don't Have Time — https://app.wedonthavetime.org/posts/e4989d8d-9c22-4fcd-a63b-ee133d5f73d2

Google is also backing a project converting landfill waste into biochar as a carbon capture measure, with the aim of capturing around 200,000 tonnes of carbon. Biochar is a charcoal-like material produced by heating organic waste in a low-oxygen environment; once spread over agricultural fields, it locks carbon into the soil for long periods while also improving soil quality, making it a promising carbon capture option.
