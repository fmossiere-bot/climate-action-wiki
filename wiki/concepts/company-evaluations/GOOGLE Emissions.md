## Google's emissions rise 48% as AI demand grows

Source: The Guardian, "Google's emissions climb nearly 50% in five years as AI demand upends climate pledges," 2 July 2024. https://www.theguardian.com/technology/article/2024/jul/02/google-ai-emissions

Google's plan to shrink its climate footprint is at risk because its new artificial intelligence products run on more and more energy-hungry data centres. In July 2024 the company said its greenhouse gas emissions had climbed **48% over the previous five years**. Its [2024 environmental report](https://www.gstatic.com/gumdrop/sustainability/google-2024-environmental-report.pdf) also showed that emissions in 2023 were 13% higher than the year before, reaching 14.3 million metric tonnes. That puts Google's emissions nearly 50% above 2019, the baseline year for its net zero goal. Net zero means the company has to remove as much CO2 as it emits, so the gap is growing in the wrong direction.

**Data centre energy demand more broadly:** the International Energy Agency estimates that the electricity used by data centres could double from 2022 levels to 1,000 terawatt-hours (TWh) by 2026, about as much as the whole of Japan uses. According to calculations by the research firm SemiAnalysis, AI could push data centres to 4.5% of global energy generation by 2030.

## Turning to nuclear power

With demand rising this fast, most large tech companies, Google included, are now looking to nuclear power. In May 2025 the nuclear developer Elementl Power announced an agreement with Google to develop three sites for advanced reactors. Google will put in early-stage development money for the three projects, each expected to generate at least 600 megawatts. It is one more example of a tech giant teaming up with the nuclear industry to power its data centres.

It is not Google's only nuclear bet. In October 2024 Google signed up with Kairos Power, a company building small modular reactors, to buy power from its planned fleet. At the time, Google said the first reactor would start supplying power by 2030, with more following through to 2035.

## Turning landfill waste into biochar for carbon capture

Source: We Don't Have Time. https://app.wedonthavetime.org/posts/e4989d8d-9c22-4fcd-a63b-ee133d5f73d2

Google is also backing a project that turns landfill waste into biochar, with the aim of capturing around 200,000 tonnes of carbon. **Biochar** is a charcoal-like material made by heating organic waste with very little oxygen. Spread on farmland, it locks carbon into the soil for a long time and improves the soil too, which makes it a promising way to store carbon.
