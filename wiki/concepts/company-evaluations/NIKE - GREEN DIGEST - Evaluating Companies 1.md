Green Digest is built around the principle of double materiality, which recognises that a company's impact runs in two directions: it affects both the environment and society at large, and those external factors in turn affect the company's own financial and operational performance. While traditional ESG assessments focus mainly on the latter, Green Digest aims to examine companies' direct impacts on people and planet, using a scoring system that quantifies that impact. More on the methodology is available on [Green Digest's site](https://www.greendigest.co/c/impactmethodology).

## Why Nike has a positive impact score

Nike is a leading global designer, marketer and distributor of athletic footwear, apparel, equipment and accessories.

**Some facts:** Nike's Swoosh logo was designed by graphic design student Carolyn Davidson in 1971. She was initially paid $35 for the work, but later received Nike stock now worth a significant sum. The company's famous slogan "Just Do It" was inspired by the last words of convicted murderer Gary Gilmore, "Let's do it," and was introduced in 1988. Nike revolutionised athletic footwear in 1979 with the introduction of Nike Air technology, featuring air-filled bags in the soles of shoes for added cushioning and comfort.

## What is the company's impact?

Nike has a general impact score of +0.58 on Green Digest's scale, which runs from -5 to +5. Its impact is spread across 10 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 13 topics, split between positive and negative analyses.

**In the socio-economic sphere,** Nike holds a 38.7% market share of the global footwear market and sells over 900 million products annually. As a sports industry pioneer, Nike has consistently addressed physical inactivity and played a key role in shaping global fitness culture. The company promotes inclusivity through various initiatives, including designing products for people with disabilities, creating maternity sportswear for all stages of pregnancy and post-birth, pioneering the sale of sports hijabs for female athletes, and spending millions on advertising to increase women's access to sports participation. It has also helped more than 20 million children over the past four years engage in physical activity and play, through campaigns such as "Made to Play," based on the premise that sport should be safe, fun and designed for all children.

Nike employs roughly 83,700 people and pays salaries above the industry average, and has achieved gender parity at all levels except its board of directors. It also indirectly employs over 1.3 million workers in its supply chain, most of them in developing countries.

However, Nike received a complaint from 20 unions in the garment industry for violating OECD guidelines in its treatment of 450,000 garment supply chain workers across 94 factories in Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.

**Environmentally,** Nike emits 16.7 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent annually (2023 figure), including indirect emissions, equal to the energy use of over 3.2 million homes for a year. Annually, the company consumes 18.5 million cubic metres of water (2023), 42% of it in water-stressed areas. Nike also generates approximately 200,000 tonnes of waste every year, 97.5% of which it manages sustainably through recycling, waste-to-energy and composting. Despite 100% of Nike's leather being certified sustainable by the Leather Working Group, some of its suppliers have been linked to Amazon rainforest deforestation. The company sources over 500,000 tonnes of raw materials annually, requiring hundreds of thousands of hectares of land.

The impact score is current as of May 2024 and may change as it is continuously updated.

## ESG rating versus impact score

ESG ratings from MSCI ESG Research are designed to measure a company's resilience to financially material ESG risks: they measure how effectively a company manages ESG risks to itself, not the company's impact on the wider world.

## Impact scores by SDGs

Positively, and weighted by significance, Nike scores highest on Good Health and Well-being (+3.33), followed by Partnership for the Goals (+3.19) and Reduced Inequalities (+2.18). Negatively, it scores worst on Responsible Consumption and Production (-1.93), followed by Decent Work and Economic Growth (-2.42) and Climate Action (-3.79).

The company also positively and negatively influences SDG 1, No Poverty, in ways that broadly balance each other out: negatively, because its products are more expensive than those of its competitors, and positively, because it employs more than 1.3 million workers in its supply chain, most of them in developing countries.

## Conclusion

Nike's key social and environmental impact lies in its essential role enabling hundreds of millions of people worldwide to engage in physical activity and express themselves through its products. The company also contributes to economic growth and social stability by employing millions of people directly and indirectly, and by contributing billions of dollars in taxes.

Conversely, Nike has a negative impact through its substantial emissions, water usage, materials sourcing and waste production. The company has also been criticised for the quality of jobs in its supply chain, many of which do not meet labour standards and could involve forced labour.

Its positive score of +0.58 is a balance of all of these factors.
