Green Digest rates companies using **double materiality**. The idea is that a company's impact runs both ways: it affects the environment and society, and those outside factors in turn affect the company's own finances and operations. Traditional ESG assessments mostly look at the second direction. Green Digest focuses on the first, a company's direct impact on people and planet, and gives it a score. You can read more about the method on [Green Digest's site](https://www.greendigest.co/c/impactmethodology).

## Why Nike has a positive impact score

Nike designs, markets and sells athletic footwear, clothing, equipment and accessories all over the world, and leads that market.

**Some facts:** the Swoosh logo was designed in 1971 by Carolyn Davidson, then a graphic design student. She was first paid $35 for it, and later received Nike shares that are now worth a great deal. The "Just Do It" slogan, introduced in 1988, was inspired by the last words of the convicted murderer Gary Gilmore, "Let's do it". In 1979 Nike changed sports shoes with Nike Air, which put air-filled bags in the soles for extra cushioning and comfort.

## What is the company's impact?

On Green Digest's scale, which runs from -5 to +5, Nike has an overall impact score of **+0.58**. Its impact touches 10 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 13 topics, some scored positively and some negatively.

**On the social and economic side,** Nike holds a 38.7% share of the global footwear market and sells over 900 million products a year. As a pioneer in sport, it has long pushed back against physical inactivity and helped shape fitness culture around the world. It works on inclusion in several ways: products for people with disabilities, maternity sportswear for every stage of pregnancy and after birth, being one of the first to sell sports hijabs for female athletes, and spending millions on advertising to get more women into sport. Over the past four years it has also helped more than 20 million children get active and play, through campaigns such as "Made to Play", built on the idea that sport should be safe, fun and designed for all children.

Nike employs roughly 83,700 people and pays above the industry average. It has reached gender parity at every level except its board of directors. It also employs over 1.3 million workers indirectly in its supply chain, most of them in developing countries.

There is a darker side. A group of 20 garment industry unions filed a complaint against Nike for breaking OECD guidelines in how 450,000 supply chain workers were treated across 94 factories in Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.

**On the environmental side,** Nike emits 16.7 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent a year (2023 figure), including indirect emissions. That equals the energy used by over 3.2 million homes in a year. It uses 18.5 million cubic metres of water a year (2023), 42% of it in areas short of water. It produces approximately 200,000 tonnes of waste a year and manages 97.5% of it sustainably through recycling, waste-to-energy and composting. Although 100% of Nike's leather is certified sustainable by the Leather Working Group, some of its suppliers have been linked to deforestation in the Amazon rainforest. The company buys over 500,000 tonnes of raw materials a year, which takes hundreds of thousands of hectares of land to produce.

The score is current as of May 2024 and is updated continuously, so it may change.

## ESG rating versus impact score

ESG ratings from MSCI ESG Research measure how well a company copes with ESG risks that could hurt its finances. In other words, they measure the risk the world poses to the company, not the harm or good the company does to the world.

## Impact scores by SDGs

Weighted by significance, Nike's best scores are for Good Health and Well-being (+3.33), followed by Partnership for the Goals (+3.19) and Reduced Inequalities (+2.18). Its most significant negative scores, which are not listed in order of score, are for Responsible Consumption and Production (-1.93), Decent Work and Economic Growth (-2.42) and Climate Action (-3.79).

Nike affects SDG 1, No Poverty, in two directions that roughly cancel out. On the negative side, its products cost more than its competitors'. On the positive side, it employs more than 1.3 million workers in its supply chain, most of them in developing countries.

## Overall picture

Nike's biggest contribution is helping hundreds of millions of people around the world be active and express themselves through what they wear. It also supports economic growth and social stability by employing millions of people directly and indirectly, and by paying billions of dollars in taxes.

On the other side are its large emissions, its water use, the way it sources materials and the waste it produces. It has also been criticised for the quality of jobs in its supply chain, many of which fall short of labour standards and could involve forced labour.

Its positive score of +0.58 is the balance of all these factors.
