# BP's Triple Climate Rebellion: Shareholders Push Back at the April 2026 AGM

## What is it

In April 2026 the oil and gas company BP held its Annual General Meeting, the first under its new chair, Albert Manifold, and new CEO, Meg O'Neill. Its own shareholders handed it a **triple defeat**. More than half of the votes cast opposed the company's plan to scrap its existing climate reporting. More than half opposed a plan to replace in-person AGMs with online-only meetings. And 18% of votes cast opposed the chair's re-election. Observers called all three results "unprecedented" for an oil supermajor.

The two rejected resolutions needed a simple majority, so BP cannot go ahead with either. Manifold stays on as chair, but with a badly weakened mandate less than a year into the role. Meg O'Neill, the **first external hire to BP's top job and the first woman to lead any oil major**, inherits the situation.

## Why it matters

Shareholder revolts this large at a supermajor are rare, and this one was driven by **mainstream institutions**, not a small group of activists. Legal & General Investment Management, the UK's largest asset manager, publicly opposed both the climate-reporting rollback and Manifold's re-election. The two most influential proxy advisers (firms that advise investors how to vote), Glass Lewis and ISS, recommended voting against the rollback. Glass Lewis went further and recommended voting against Manifold personally, because he was responsible for keeping a shareholder resolution from the campaign group Follow This off the ballot.

That resolution asked BP to explain how its plan to grow oil and gas output fits a world moving away from fossil fuels. By blocking the question, BP gave its answer of sorts, and shareholders made clear they were tired of being brushed off.

## What was on the ballot

Three items were at the centre of the rebellion:

- **Climate reporting rollback.** BP wanted to scrap two earlier resolutions that required company-specific climate disclosures. Losing this vote means BP must keep reporting under the existing rules.
- **In-person AGMs.** BP proposed moving to online-only shareholder meetings. In-person AGMs have become "a lightning rod for climate protest", so the vote to keep them is in effect a vote to keep that pressure point.
- **Chair re-election.** 18% of shareholders opposed Manifold, largely because of how he handled the Follow This resolution.

## The wider strategic backdrop

Through 2025 and early 2026, BP has been rolling back the green targets set under former CEO **Bernard Looney**. It has raised planned oil and gas output and watered down its transition commitments, arguing that this is needed to close the gap in market value with rivals such as Shell. The AGM revolt shows that a sizeable group of investors doubts this will pay off in the medium term, especially when it comes with weaker disclosure and less scrutiny.

Mark van Baal of Follow This put the underlying question like this: *"How does BP plan to create value for shareholders as oil and gas demand declines? BP would rather antagonise its shareholders than answer it."*

Nick Mazan of the Australasian Centre for Corporate Responsibility (ACCR), a shareholder advocacy group, added: *"Today's result is unprecedented and demonstrates that investors are fed up with BP's lack of capital discipline and its approach to shareholder rights."*

## Key facts

- **>50% of votes** opposed BP's plan to scrap its existing climate reporting
- **>50% of votes** opposed replacing in-person AGMs with online-only meetings
- **18% of votes** opposed the re-election of chair Albert Manifold, described as "unprecedented" less than a year into his tenure
- LGIM, the UK's largest asset manager, publicly voted against the climate-reporting rollback and against Manifold
- Both proxy advisers, Glass Lewis and ISS, urged shareholders to oppose the rollback
- Glass Lewis also recommended voting against Manifold because BP excluded the Follow This resolution
- Meg O'Neill is the first external hire to BP's top job and the first woman to lead any major oil company
