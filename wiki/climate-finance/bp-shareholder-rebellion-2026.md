# BP's Triple Climate Rebellion — Shareholders Push Back at the April 2026 AGM

## What is it

At its April 2026 Annual General Meeting — the first under new chair **Albert Manifold** and new CEO **Meg O'Neill** — the oil and gas major BP suffered a **triple defeat** at the hands of its own shareholders. More than half of the votes cast opposed the company's plan to **scrap its existing climate reporting**, more than half opposed a plan to **replace in-person AGMs with online-only meetings**, and **18% of votes cast opposed the re-election of the chair**. All three results are described by observers as "unprecedented" for an oil supermajor.

Because the two rejected resolutions carried on a simple majority, BP is now blocked from enacting either. Manifold remains as chair but with a badly damaged mandate less than a year into the role. Meg O'Neill — the **first external hire to BP's top job and the first woman to lead any oil major** — inherits the situation.

## Why it matters

Shareholder rebellions of this size at a supermajor are rare. **Mainstream institutions**, not activist minorities, drove the outcome: **Legal & General Investment Management**, the UK's largest asset manager, publicly opposed both the climate-reporting rollback and Manifold's re-election. The two most influential proxy advisers — **Glass Lewis** and **ISS** — recommended voting against the climate-reporting rollback, and Glass Lewis went further, recommending against Manifold personally on the grounds that he was responsible for excluding a shareholder resolution from campaign group **Follow This**.

That excluded resolution asked BP to explain how its plan to grow oil and gas output aligns with a world shifting away from fossil fuels. In blocking the question, BP produced its answer: shareholders decided they were done being brushed off.

## What was on the ballot

Three items lie at the heart of the rebellion:

- **Climate reporting rollback.** BP wanted to scrap two previous resolutions that required company-specific climate disclosures. Losing this vote means BP must keep reporting under the existing rules.
- **In-person AGMs.** BP proposed moving to online-only shareholder meetings. In-person AGMs have become "a lightning rod for climate protest" — the vote to keep them is effectively a vote to preserve that pressure point.
- **Chair re-election.** 18% of shareholders opposed Manifold, in large part because of his handling of the Follow This resolution.

## The wider strategic backdrop

BP has spent 2025 and early 2026 walking back the green targets set under former CEO **Bernard Looney**. It has raised planned oil and gas output and diluted transition commitments, arguing this is needed to close a market-value gap with rivals like Shell. The AGM revolt shows that a meaningful bloc of investors is not convinced this delivers value in the medium term, especially if it comes bundled with weaker disclosures and less scrutiny.

Mark van Baal of Follow This framed the underlying question sharply: *"How does BP plan to create value for shareholders as oil and gas demand declines? BP would rather antagonise its shareholders than answer it."*

Nick Mazan of the Australasian Centre for Corporate Responsibility (ACCR) added: *"Today's result is unprecedented and demonstrates that investors are fed up with BP's lack of capital discipline and its approach to shareholder rights."*

## Key facts

- **>50% of votes** opposed BP's plan to scrap its existing climate reporting
- **>50% of votes** opposed replacing in-person AGMs with online-only meetings
- **18% of votes** opposed the re-election of chair Albert Manifold — described as "unprecedented" less than a year into his tenure
- **LGIM**, the UK's largest asset manager, publicly voted against the climate-reporting rollback and against Manifold
- Both **Glass Lewis** and **ISS** proxy advisers urged shareholders to oppose the rollback
- Glass Lewis also recommended voting against Manifold because BP excluded the **Follow This** resolution
- **Meg O'Neill** is the first external hire to BP's top job and the first woman to lead any major oil company
