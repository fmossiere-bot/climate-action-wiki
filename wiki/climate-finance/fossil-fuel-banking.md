# Fossil Fuel Banking

## What is it

Every year since the Paris Agreement, a coalition of environmental groups has tracked the world's largest banks in a report called **Banking on Climate Chaos**. The picture it paints is worrying and consistent. Instead of cutting fossil fuel lending in line with their climate commitments, the world's 65 largest banks have together increased their financing year after year.

> In 2025, those banks committed **$906 billion** to fossil fuel companies. That is an increase of $64 billion, nearly 8%, on 2024. Since 2015 the total has reached $8.7 trillion in new fossil fuel financing, even as the same institutions made voluntary pledges to align their lending with net zero by 2050.

## Why it matters

Banks do not drill for oil or burn coal themselves. But the money they lend, and the bonds they help companies sell, make new fossil fuel projects possible. Without cheap money, the expansion of coal mines, gas fields and LNG terminals would stall. So the amount of fossil fuel financing is a direct measure of how seriously the financial system takes the climate emergency.

The 2025 report found that the world's largest banks are "making decisions incompatible with international agreements to restrain rising global temperatures." The UN's own Net-Zero Banking Alliance, set up to change this, effectively collapsed in 2025 after a wave of departures, many driven by political pressure in the United States under the Trump administration.

Switching to a greener bank is one of the biggest things you can do, whether you are an individual, a company or a pension fund.

## Who is lending what

JPMorgan Chase is the world's largest fossil fuel financier, committing $58 billion in 2025, up 13% from 2024. The top five lenders are all American or Japanese: JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, MUFG, Mizuho Financial and Citigroup. Barclays is the highest-placed British bank, in eighth place.

A "dirty dozen" of the largest institutions account for 40% of all fossil fuel funding worldwide. Almost all of the money comes from six places: the United States, Canada, Japan, China, the United Kingdom and the European Union.

Not every bank is heading the same way. Twenty-six of the 65 banks tracked cut their fossil fuel financing in 2025. European banks led the cuts, with BNP Paribas, UBS and La Caixa reducing their fossil fuel lending the most.

## Where the money goes

In 2025, $508 billion was pledged specifically to expand existing fossil fuel sites, a 27% increase on 2024. The three biggest single recipients were all US-based: Venture Global (LNG exports), Enbridge (pipelines) and Energy Transfer (gas transport and processing). Big oil's record profits, pushed up in 2025 by soaring energy prices after US and Israeli military action against Iran, have made fossil fuel companies attractive borrowers.

## May 2026: French banks fund EPH's gas expansion

Here is how the pattern plays out in a single deal. On 21 May 2026, all four big French banks (**BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole, Société Générale and BPCE**) took part in a €2.2 billion revolving credit facility (RCF, a flexible credit line a company can draw on) for Energetický a průmyslový holding (EPH). EPH is a Czech coal and gas group controlled by the billionaire Daniel Křetínský. Its parent, EP Group, is Europe's largest coal producer and its second-largest coal power operator. EPH itself runs 11.1 GW of fossil gas power across 8 European countries and plans to add 5.5 GW more. It has just formed a joint venture, TTEP, with TotalEnergies.

Two things about the deal stand out. First, EPH has moved its coal assets into a new subsidiary, EP Energy Transition (EPETr), also owned by Křetínský, so it can claim a "coal exit" by 2030 without actually closing any plants. Reclaim Finance calls this "a sleight of hand." Second, one of the banks described the RCF as a **"green loan"** under the LMA Green Loan Principles. The deal was later removed from the database provider's records "for confidentiality reasons." S&P's own second-party opinion notes that EPH's Green Finance Framework "includes significant investments in fossil fuel-based assets and infrastructure."

BNP Paribas is the same bank that led European cuts in fossil fuel financing in 2025. The EPH deal shows that a falling overall total can sit alongside big new deals for individual polluters. See the [French banks EPH source summary](../sources/french-banks-eph-reclaim-finance-2026.md).

## Accountability parallels beyond climate

The same pattern, banks ignoring the findings of voluntary accountability processes, shows up outside climate too. In **August 2026**, the Swiss National Contact Point for Responsible Business Conduct (NCP) issued a Follow-up Statement. It found that UBS had failed to act on two of three recommendations from a private-prisons complaint filed by Worth Rises and BankTrack. UBS had also refused to enter NCP mediation, a first among European commercial banks. Both the process (the OECD Guidelines and NCP system) and the underlying issue (due diligence on passive investment portfolios) mirror the climate accountability gap: voluntary rules with no enforcement produce voluntary compliance. See the [UBS private prisons source summary](../sources/ubs-private-prisons-swiss-ncp-2026.md).

## The collapse of voluntary commitments

Many banks had announced net zero lending targets and joined voluntary initiatives. The break-up of the Net-Zero Banking Alliance in 2025 shows that voluntary commitments have not brought change on the scale needed. As one of the report's editors put it: "The era of voluntary commitments has not worked at the scale that we need and so this points to a much more active role for financial regulators, legislators and policymakers, especially in those big six financial centres."

## Key facts

- $906bn committed by the 65 largest banks to fossil fuels in 2025, up 8% on 2024.
- $8.7 trillion in total since the Paris Agreement (2015–2025).
- JPMorgan Chase: $58bn in 2025, up 13% from 2024.
- $508bn specifically for expanding fossil fuel sites in 2025, up 27%.
- "Dirty dozen" banks: 40% of all industry funding.
- 26 of 65 banks reduced fossil fuel financing, with BNP Paribas leading the cuts.
- Net-Zero Banking Alliance disbanded in 2025 after mass departures.
- May 2026: €2.2bn RCF to the Czech coal and gas group EPH from all four big French banks, plus ING, Lloyds and CaixaBank, labelled a "green loan" under LMA principles.
- Aug 2026: the Swiss NCP finds UBS failed on 2 of 3 human-rights recommendations, a parallel accountability failure to the climate case.
