## The climate choice most people overlook

Most people who care about climate change have already changed some things about how they live: fewer flights, less meat, better energy choices at home. These steps matter. But there is one choice most people have never thought about, and it is sitting in their wallet.

It is their bank.

Where you keep your money has a direct and measurable effect on the climate. Your deposits are very likely being used, right now, to finance oil fields, gas pipelines and other carbon-heavy industries, on a larger scale than most people realise.

> **Did you know?** 
> Changing to a greener bank could be the most effective climate action you take. 10,000€ in a "dirty" bank could generate the equivalent of 2.4 tCO2e a year.

---

## How banking and climate are connected

Banks do not simply hold your money. They put it to work. Every euro or pound you deposit can be lent out, many times over, to businesses and projects across the economy. What a bank can lend is not limited to the money sitting in your account at a given moment. It depends on the total flow of deposits passing through.

A large share of that lending goes to industries that drive climate change. On average, eleven of the largest US banks direct around 19% of their lending to carbon-intensive industries. **For some, the figure reaches 30%.**

This adds up at a personal level. According to Project Drawdown, every 1,000 euros or dollars held in a carbon-intensive bank is linked to roughly the same greenhouse gas emissions as a one-way flight from New York to Seattle, every year. For most people with a current account and some savings, their banking produces more indirect emissions than their flights.

---

## Fossil fuels are only part of it

Banks also finance other kinds of environmental damage that get less attention. In 2024, Santander provided more than 600 million dollars to companies linked to beef, palm oil and soya supply chains that drive deforestation in South America. Since 2011, the bank has co-arranged over 1.3 billion dollars of financing for a single Argentine farming company, Cresud. Cresud has been linked to large-scale forest clearance in the Gran Chaco, one of South America's most important and biodiverse ecosystems.

This happened even though Santander brought in a policy to limit deforestation in 2018 and committed to net-zero emissions across its portfolio by 2050. It shows the gap that often exists between what banks promise and what they do.

## The impact of switching

The good news is that switching banks is one of the most effective climate actions you can take, and once it is done it needs no further effort.

> Project Drawdown estimates that moving from a carbon-intensive bank to a climate-responsible one could reduce the climate impact of your personal banking by up to 76%. That is a bigger yearly reduction than most people achieve through years of changes to diet or travel.

You decide once, and the effect lasts.

---

## What to look for in a greener bank

Not every bank that calls itself sustainable takes it equally seriously. When comparing options, a few questions are worth asking.

On climate commitments:

- Has the bank committed to stop financing fossil fuel expansion?
- Does it provide more financing to climate solutions than to carbon-intensive sectors?
- Does its net-zero commitment cover the emissions it finances, as well as its own operations?
- Does it publish short-term targets alongside its 2050 goal?
- Does it measure and disclose its full emissions in line with recognised accounting standards?

On practical matters:

- Does it offer the services you need day to day?
- Are you happy with its app and online banking?
- How do existing customers rate it?

[bank.green](https://bank.green/), [BankTrack](https://www.banktrack.org/) and [Banking on Climate Chaos](https://www.bankingonclimatechaos.org/) give independent assessments of major banks and how much they lend to fossil fuels and other high-risk sectors. In the UK, [Make My Money Matter](https://makemymoneymatter.co.uk/) campaigns on the issue and offers practical guidance.
If you are in France, [Team for the Planet](https://team-planet.com/fr/bank-for-the-planet) now partners with Credit Cooperatif on a credit card that funds climate innovation, and with Good Vest on green pensions.

---

## Why it is worth doing

Switching banks does not mean giving anything up. It costs nothing and barely changes your daily routine. Yet over the years its climate impact can be greater than many of the sacrifices people make elsewhere in their lives.

Campaigners spend a lot of effort encouraging people to fly less or eat differently, and those things matter. Where your money sits overnight matters too, perhaps more than most people know.
