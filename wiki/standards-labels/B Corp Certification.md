[B Corp](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/certification/) has become one of the best-known marks of responsible business. This page looks at what being certified means, and who it is for.


**1. What is B Corp?**

B Corp certification is given to companies that meet **verified standards** of social and environmental performance, accountability and transparency. It is run by B Lab, a global non-profit founded in the United States in 2006.

> Traditional ESG reporting often relies on figures that companies report about themselves. B Corp instead requires verified, measurable results in five areas: **governance, workers, community, environment and customers**. That makes it more demanding than most voluntary sustainability frameworks, and it is a large part of why it has the reputation it has.

It helps to be clear about what B Corp is not. **It does not certify products or services as sustainable.** It assesses how the business as a whole is run, and it requires companies to write their accountability to stakeholders into their legal structure, as well as into what they say publicly.


**2. Why is B Corp considered a reference standard?**

Several things set B Corp apart from other sustainability labels.

- It is **holistic**. Instead of focusing on a single issue such as carbon emissions or fair trade, it looks at the whole business: how it treats employees, how it works with its community, how open it is with customers, and how it is governed.
- It requires **independent verification**. Companies cannot certify themselves. B Lab reviews their answers and supporting documents before giving a score.
- It requires a **legal commitment**. To get and keep B Corp certification, companies must write their commitments to all stakeholders into their governing documents, and they must re-certify every three years. That builds the commitment into the company's structure, rather than leaving it as a matter of image.

The standards are also changing. In June 2026, a first group of companies was certified under B Lab's newly revised global standards, described as the biggest overhaul of the certification in its history. The new framework requires companies to meet independently verified performance thresholds on seven impact topics, from climate action to human rights. The tougher standards respond directly to concerns that the original system was too flexible. They also reflect B Lab's aim to keep the certification credible as more large corporations seek it.


**3. Who has adopted it?**

> The B Corp community now includes over **10,800 certified companies** across 102 countries and 163 industries, employing more than one million people between them.

They range from small independent businesses to well-known global brands. Large international companies with certification include Nespresso, San Pellegrino and Innocent Drinks. Patagonia, the outdoor clothing company, has been a certified B Corp since 2012 and is one of the most cited examples of what the certification means in practice.

The **fastest growth in recent years has been in Europe**. Consumers are more aware, and regulation is tightening, in particular through the EU's CSRD and the Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive. Together these have pushed sustainability up the agenda for many businesses.


> **About B Corp Certification**
> B Corp Certification is a designation that a business is meeting high standards of verified performance, accountability, and transparency on factors from employee benefits and charitable giving to supply chain practices and input materials. In order to achieve certification, a company must: 
> - Demonstrate **high** **social and environmental** **performance** by achieving a B Impact Assessment score of 80 or above and passing our risk review. Multinational corporations must also meet baseline requirement standards. 
> - Make a **legal commitment** by changing their corporate governance structure to be accountable to all stakeholders, not just shareholders, and achieve benefit corporation status if available in their jurisdiction. 
> - Exhibit **transparency** by allowing information about their performance measured against B Lab’s standards to be publicly available on their B Corp profile on B Lab’s website.



**4. The certification process**

The process is built around the B Impact Assessment, an online questionnaire covering five areas: governance, workers, community, environment and customers. Companies answer questions and submit supporting evidence for all five.

> The minimum score to qualify is **80 out of 200**. Companies that reach 80 points go through an assessment review, in effect a verification call and a check of their documents, before being certified. The median score for companies that complete the assessment without seeking certification is around 50, which shows how high the bar is.

The route to certification depends on the size of the company. Smaller businesses follow a simpler process, while larger companies with more complex structures face extra requirements and standards specific to their sector.

Certification lasts three years. After that, companies must apply again and show they have kept improving. Annual fees are based on company revenue.


**5. B Corp in Ireland**

B Corp has grown quickly in Ireland. Ireland's first B Corp was certified in 2018. By 2025, more than 90 Irish companies had joined the global B Corp community.

- B Lab Ireland was founded in 2023 to support the growth of the movement in Ireland. Over 500 companies have used B Lab's impact measurement tools in the past 12 months, and many more are working through the certification process now.
- State support is available. Enterprise Ireland, LEO and IDA clients can apply for the GreenStart grant to hire an experienced B Corp consultant. The grant covers 80% of the cost, up to 5,000 euros.
- Irish B Corps today range from coffee roasters and food producers to distilleries and professional services firms. The community is still fairly small, but the pace of growth suggests it is moving from niche to mainstream.



**Summary**

B Corp is one of the few sustainability certifications that looks at the whole business, requires a legal commitment and insists on independent verification. Its credibility comes from the fact that it is hard to get. For companies that want to show they run their business responsibly, it remains one of the best-known frameworks available, and in Ireland it is growing quickly.
