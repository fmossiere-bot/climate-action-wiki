If you pick up a packet of fish in a supermarket and see a small blue label with a fish on it, that is the [MSC certification](https://www.msc.org). Here is what it guarantees, and what it does not.


**1. What is the MSC?**
The Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) is a non-profit that sets standards for sustainable fishing. It uses its ecolabel and certification programme to recognise and reward sustainable **fishing practices**. It was founded in 1996, after the collapse of the Grand Banks cod fishery off Canada. That collapse wiped out one of the world's most productive fishing grounds and showed clearly what unchecked overfishing can do.

> The MSC Fisheries Standard is grounded in the United Nations Code of Conduct for Responsible Fisheries and has been developed through a process involving fisheries, scientists, environmental NGOs, and the seafood industry.



**2. What does the blue label cover?**
The MSC label is about the environmental side of fishing: keeping fish stocks productive, ecosystems healthy and fisheries well managed. It does not certify social or economic conditions, such as how crews are treated, and it does not cover the packaging or the carbon footprint of the final product.

> The MSC Fisheries Standard has three core principles that every fishery must meet: first, are enough fish left in the ocean; second, what are the environmental impacts of the fishing activity; and third, are operations well managed and compliant with relevant laws.

The label also covers the full chain of custody. Chain of Custody certification tracks fish and seafood from MSC-certified fisheries through each step of the supply chain, from the boat to your plate. Every company that handles the product along the way, from processor to retailer, must be certified too.



**3. How does the certification process work?**

- Certification to the MSC Fisheries Standard is voluntary and open to any fishery that catches sea or freshwater species in the wild. Fisheries are assessed by accredited independent certifiers, not by the MSC itself.
- During the assessment, fisheries are scored on 28 performance indicators under the three principles. A score of 80 to 100 means full certification. A score of 60 to 79 means conditional certification, with improvements required. A score below 60 is a fail.
- An assessment usually takes around 12 to 18 months and costs from around 15,000 to 120,000 US dollars, depending on how complex the fishery is. Once certified, fisheries are audited by independent assessors every year and must go through a full reassessment every five years.



**4. How widely is it adopted?**

**Over 600 fisheries** take part in the MSC programme, supplying more than 20,000 sustainable seafood products around the world. Major retailers across Europe, North America and beyond recognise the label, and more and more supermarkets require it before they will stock a seafood product.

> It is not without criticism. The MSC has faced scrutiny over its ties to the fishing industry and a potential conflict of interest, given that the industry pays for its own certification. Critics have also raised concerns about some certified fisheries having high levels of bycatch, the unintended capture and killing of non-target species. The MSC has updated its standards over time in response to these concerns, including a revised Fisheries Standard published in 2024.



**In short**

The MSC blue label is one of the best-known sustainability marks in food. It is an independently checked sign that wild seafood was caught with the environment in mind. Like any certification it has flaws, but it is still the most rigorous and widely adopted standard for wild-caught fish available today.
