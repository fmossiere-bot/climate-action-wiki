Long Name: EFRAG Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for non-listed SMEs

> With CSRD scope narrowed, most irish companies now sit outside mandatory EU sustainability reporting - but data demands from larger costumers and finance providers remain. The VSME offers a clear framework to answer these requests and repot performance in a proportionate way. 

## What is VSME?

The **VSME Standard** is a voluntary framework written by EFRAG, the body that drafts the EU's sustainability reporting standards. It helps micro, small and medium-sized enterprises that are not listed on a stock market report on their sustainability performance. The aim is to help these businesses answer the data requests they get from investors, banks and larger corporate clients, and to manage their own environmental and social issues better.

The standard has two parts. The Basic Module is the starting level, suitable for micro-businesses. The Comprehensive Module adds more detailed disclosures about strategy and the impacts along the value chain. The metrics cover environmental factors, such as greenhouse gas emissions and energy use; social factors, such as the health and diversity of the workforce; and governance factors, such as anti-corruption measures.

Because it follows the wider European standards, the VSME gives smaller businesses a way of reporting that is in proportion to their size but still consistent with what larger companies do. It includes detailed guidance and technical definitions, so the information reported can be compared, checked and easily understood.

## Who does it apply to?

It applies to businesses whose securities are **not traded on a regulated market** in the European Union. The standard encourages three categories of business to use it, based on the thresholds in EU Directive 2013/34/EU:

|**Category**|**Balance Sheet Total**|**Net Turnover**|**Avg. Number of Employees**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Micro**|$\le$ €450,000|$\le$ €900,000|$\le$ 10 employees|
|**Small**|$\le$ €5 million|$\le$ €10 million|$\le$ 50 employees|
|**Medium**|$\le$ €25 million|$\le$ €50 million|$\le$ 250 employees|

(Note: a business falls into a category if it does not exceed at least two of the three thresholds.)

## What about smaller businesses (micro-undertakings)?

Micro-businesses are very welcome to use the standard. Rather than the full framework, it suggests a lighter approach for them:

- **Target approach:** micro-businesses are encouraged to fill in only the Basic Module.
- **Omission flexibility:** they can choose to apply only the parts that are clearly relevant to their day-to-day activities.


    

## Why should an SME adopt it voluntarily?

The standard has **no direct legal force**, and these businesses are entirely outside the mandatory scope of the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). There are still good commercial reasons to use it:

- **Supply chain pressure:** large corporate clients that face mandatory EU reporting are asking their suppliers for a lot of sustainability data. VSME helps SMEs answer those requests.
- **Access to finance:** banks and investors increasingly want an ESG risk profile before granting a loan or investing. VSME puts this data in a standard form, which helps when raising finance.
- **Competitive growth:** managing issues like energy, pollution and workplace safety in a structured way makes a business more resilient over the medium to long term.

## What does the framework involve?

The framework is built from modules. Once a business chooses a module, it must complete all of it.

1. The Basic Module (minimum requirement)
- **General information (B1–B2):** legal structure, NACE industry sector codes, basic company figures, and a description of any existing sustainability practices, transition policies or targets.
- **Environmental metrics (B3–B7):** energy use (MWh), gross Scope 1 and location-based Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions (tCO2eq), pollution (where it is already monitored by law), sites affecting land use or biodiversity, water withdrawal, and a breakdown of circular economy practices and waste.
- **Social metrics (B8–B10):** workforce make-up (gender, contract type), rate of work-related accidents and deaths, alignment with the minimum wage, training hours, and the unadjusted gender pay gap.
- **Governance metrics (B11):** court convictions and regulatory fines related to corruption and bribery.


2. The Comprehensive Module

This is a more advanced layer built on top of the Basic Module. It targets the standard disclosures that financial markets, banks and major clients ask for, and adds:

- **Value chain carbon:** measuring Scope 3 emissions (the indirect emissions in the value chain) and setting absolute short and long-term reduction targets.
- **Climate risks (C4):** identifying physical hazards (for example floods or extreme weather) and transition events that could affect the business financially.
- **Human rights (C6–C7):** recording codes of conduct on child labour, forced labour and discrimination, and tracking formal complaints or human rights incidents.
- **Sector exclusions and diversity (C8–C9):** disclosing revenue from sectors such as fossil fuels, controversial weapons or tobacco, along with the gender balance of the board.


## Timelines

- **Publication of the standard:** the final text of this voluntary standard was published in **December 2024**.
- **The second year of reporting:** from a business's second consecutive year of using the standard, it must include comparison figures from the previous financial year.
- **Gender pay gap threshold:** for the social metrics (B10), businesses with fewer than 150 employees may leave out the gender pay gap breakdown entirely. From 7 June 2031, this exemption shrinks to businesses with fewer than 100 employees.

> The December 2024 date marks a specific step in the administrative process:


- **Final draft submission:** on 17 December 2024, EFRAG finished the technical standard and formally submitted it to the European Commission. It was published then and could be used voluntarily straight away, but it had no official regulatory status.

 Later milestones:

- **Official Recommendation (July 2025):** on 30 July 2025, the European Commission formally endorsed EFRAG's framework as an **official Commission Recommendation**. This encouraged banks, investors and large companies to base their ESG data requests to suppliers on the VSME framework.
- **Delegated Act process (2026):** because the VSME standard is entirely voluntary, it does not "come into effect" with compliance deadlines the way a directive does. However, the European Commission is running a public consultation (until June 2026) on formally adopting a revised version of the standard as a delegated act under EU law.

In short, December 2024 was when the standard was technically finished and handed to the EU, and its life as an officially recognised framework began in mid-2025.
