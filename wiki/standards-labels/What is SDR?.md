The UK's Sustainability Disclosure Requirements (SDR) are the Financial Conduct Authority's response to a long-running problem in sustainable investing: too many products sold as "green", "ESG" or "sustainable" with very little behind the label. SDR is not a certification like the Gold Standard or FSC, and it is not a broad corporate reporting law like the EU's CSRD. It is a set of rules for financial services, aimed first at asset managers and the products they sell. Wider corporate reporting is still some way off.

## 1. What is SDR?

SDR is a package of FCA rules with three main parts, plus one rule that sits above all of them.

**The anti-greenwashing rule.** This is the broadest part of SDR, and it applies to every FCA-authorised firm, asset manager or otherwise. Any sustainability claim a firm makes about a product or service, whether in marketing, official statements or messages to clients, must be fair, clear and not misleading, and must be backed by evidence. The rule has been in force since 31 May 2024, and unlike the labels it is not optional.

**A voluntary labelling system.** Asset managers can choose to give a fund one of four labels:

- **Sustainability focus**, for products whose assets are mainly chosen for their sustainability characteristics.
- **Sustainability improvers**, for products investing in assets that could improve their sustainability performance over time.
- **Sustainability impact**, for products aiming for a measurable environmental or social impact.
- **Mixed goals**, for products combining more than one of the above.

To use a label, a fund manager must tell the FCA, meet specific criteria on investment strategy, reporting, governance and stewardship, and then keep meeting them and keep records. Funds that use sustainability terms in their marketing without taking a label are not let off disclosure. They still face reporting requirements before and after a client invests, and they must explain why they chose not to take a label.

**Sustainability entity reports.** Larger asset managers must publish these every year. They cover the firm's overall approach to sustainability risks and opportunities across the whole business, rather than a single product. They follow the same four pillars as TCFD and ISSB: governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets.

## 2. Who needs to comply, and when

SDR is being phased in, and the timeline matters because different rules apply to firms of different sizes:

- **The anti-greenwashing rule**: in force for all FCA-authorised firms since 31 May 2024.
- **Labelling, naming and marketing rules**: in force since 31 July 2024 for firms using labels, and since December 2024 for the wider naming and marketing restrictions.
- **Ongoing product-level disclosures**: in force since 2 December 2025, for both labelled and unlabelled products that use sustainability terms.
- **Entity-level reports for the largest firms**: firms with £50 billion or more in assets under management have had to report since 2 December 2025.
- **Entity-level reports for mid-size firms**: firms with between £5 billion and £50 billion under management have until 2 December 2026.
- **Distributor rules**: platforms and advisers selling labelled or sustainability-marketed products have had to pass on labels and disclosures since 31 July 2024.

**Listed companies specifically.** Separately from the asset manager rules, the FCA is consulting on making listed companies report against the UK Sustainability Reporting Standards (UK SRS), a UK standard closely modelled on IFRS S1 and S2. UK SRS S1 and S2 were published in February 2026 and can only be used voluntarily for now. The FCA's proposal would make reporting mandatory for listed companies from **1 January 2027**, with final rules expected in autumn 2026.

**Other non-financial companies.** Wider reporting duties for private companies, beyond the listed company proposal, are being looked at through the UK government's Modernising Corporate Reporting programme, within the existing Companies Act framework. A further consultation is expected later in 2026, so mandatory reporting for private companies is not close yet.

So SDR is live now, but for the moment its force falls mainly on financial services. Everyone else, including most unlisted companies, does not yet have to report against a UK standard by law, though that is expected to change from 2027 onward.

## 3. Latest developments

Several things have moved since SDR's rules were finalised in November 2023:

- **FCA guidance on good and poor labelling practice, published February 2026.** Based on what it saw while authorising funds, the regulator found that disclosures made before a client invests have generally improved as firms got used to the rules. But it also said it was not always clear whether firms really met the criteria for the label they used, or whether disclosures matched what a fund actually invests in.
- **Minor rule amendments from December 2025**, after a consultation, tidying up some of the original 2023 rules.
- **A revised FRC Stewardship Code**, in effect from 1 January 2026, which brought the word "sustainable" into its own definition of stewardship. The FCA has said this does not clash with SDR's naming and marketing rules, because the two apply in different settings (stewardship reporting on one side, product marketing on the other).
- **The listed company consultation**, described above, which is the biggest change coming soon. If adopted as proposed, it would be the first time SDR-related reporting becomes mandatory outside asset management.
- **No formal alignment with the EU's SFDR.** The UK deliberately built SDR as a separate framework from the EU's Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation, rather than a copy of it. Asset managers working in both markets therefore have to run two separate compliance processes instead of using one to satisfy the other.

## 4. Credibility and limitations

SDR is generally well regarded as a response to a real market problem, but it has some practical limits.

**The labels are voluntary, which limits their reach.** A fund can keep using sustainability language in its marketing without ever taking one of the four labels, as long as it meets the separate naming and marketing conditions and explains why it opted out. So the labels are useful where they are used, but they do not guarantee that every fund using green language has met the full labelling criteria.

**Self-assessment is at the heart of the labelling system.** Fund managers check and record their own compliance with a label's criteria. There is no fully independent certification comparable to, say, the Gold Standard's third-party verification. The FCA's February 2026 finding, that it is not always clear whether firms meet the requirements they claim to meet, points straight at this gap.

**Coverage is narrow compared with the wider economy.** SDR's mandatory rules fall mainly on asset management and, in future, listed companies. Private, unlisted companies make up most of the UK economy, and they are not yet covered by mandatory UK sustainability reporting. That will not change until at least the outcome of the Modernising Corporate Reporting consultation later in 2026.

**Divergence from the EU adds complexity.** Because SDR is not aligned with the EU's SFDR, asset managers selling products in both places cannot reuse one set of disclosures for the other. That works against the wider push for international convergence covered in the CSRD and ISSB pages.

None of this undermines the basic idea of SDR. The anti-greenwashing rule in particular is a strong, broad safety net, because it applies whether or not a label is used. The main caveat is that SDR today is a financial services regime first, and a general corporate reporting standard only in the future.

## 5. What it means for you

For now, SDR is the UK's answer to greenwashing in investment funds, and not yet a general corporate sustainability reporting law. If you are looking at an investment product, check first whether it uses one of the four FCA labels. If it does not, check whether it has given the required explanation. Either way, the anti-greenwashing rule means any sustainability claim attached to it has to be backed by evidence. If you are a listed company, the date to watch is 1 January 2027, when UK SRS reporting may become mandatory. If you are a private company, SDR does not yet apply to you directly, though UK policy looks set to change that eventually.
