# SBTi: Science Based Targets initiative

## What is it

The **Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi)** is a UK-based non-profit that sets the rules for how companies can credibly say they are heading for net-zero emissions. It is widely seen as the gold standard for corporate climate commitments. A company with an SBTi-approved target has had its plan to cut emissions checked against climate science, specifically against whether its path is consistent with limiting global warming to 1.5°C.

In June 2026, SBTi published a major update to its corporate net-zero standard. It describes the new framework as a **"best-efforts" approach**: companies must do everything within their control, but the standard now accepts that they cannot control everything.

## Why it matters

Thousands of large companies around the world have signed up to SBTi, so its rules help decide what counts as real progress and what counts as greenwashing. The 2026 update matters because it widens what companies can count towards their targets. Business has welcomed it as more practical. Some environmentalists see it as a weakening of the standard.

## What changed in the 2026 standard

Until 2026, SBTi asked companies to focus on cutting emissions in their own supply chains. The new rules widen what companies can count in two ways, but carbon offsets bought from outside the value chain still do not count towards emission reduction targets (see the V2.0 section below).

First, companies may now count **purchased environmental attribute certificates** in their footprint calculations. These include sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) certificates, renewable energy certificates and similar market-based tools that certify a cleaner fuel or power supply, even when it is not delivered directly to the company. They are different from carbon offset credits, and they come with integrity and reporting rules.

Second, **carbon removal technologies**, such as direct air capture and reforestation credits, will be allowed from 2035 onward. They are meant to cover the small share of emissions that really cannot be eliminated by then.

The reasoning is that companies face structural barriers they cannot overcome alone, and penalising them for those barriers helps no one. CEO David Kennedy called the transition "a continuous improvement journey."

## Power purchase agreements and electricity matching

**Power purchase agreements (PPAs)**, long-term contracts that directly pay for new renewable energy, can now count towards a company's clean electricity claims. Where structural barriers rule out local PPAs, companies can claim credit for PPAs in other regions.

Hourly electricity matching means checking, hour by hour, that the electricity a company uses is matched by clean power, rather than adding it up over a year. SBTi says hourly matching is better but will not make it compulsory for setting targets. Companies that use a lot of electricity, such as data centre operators, must at least report what share of their use is matched with low-carbon electricity on an hourly basis.

## Key facts

- SBTi's 2026 update lets **environmental attribute certificates** (such as SAF and renewable energy certificates) count towards targets for the first time. Carbon offset credits still cannot be used to meet reduction targets.
- Carbon removals (reforestation, DAC) are allowed from **2035** for leftover emissions that are hard to cut.
- PPAs now count. Local ones are preferred, but PPAs in other regions are allowed where barriers exist.
- Hourly electricity matching is described as "generally preferable" but is not required.
- Physically cutting emissions within the supply chain is still the main expectation.
- Companies must be open about the barriers that limited what they could do.

## July 2026: SBTi publishes the final Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0

In late July 2026, SBTi published the final **Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0**, the biggest redesign of the framework since 2021. It takes effect on **1 February 2027**, and validation of targets under the new rules opens in Q1 2027. The existing V1.3.1 can still be used for new target submissions until 31 January 2028.

That settles a two-year debate about whether carbon credits belong in an SBTi-aligned strategy. The answer is yes, through a new structured programme called the **Ongoing Emissions Responsibility (OER)** framework, which replaces the earlier Beyond Value Chain Mitigation (BVCM) guidance.

### What actually changed

**A two-tier company classification.** Companies are now split into two groups:

- **Category A**: annual turnover above €50m in high-income countries or above €450m in lower-income countries. They face mandatory transition plans, mandatory scope 3 targets, mandatory third-party assurance and, from 2035, mandatory purchases of carbon removals.
- **Category B**: mostly SMEs and companies in lower-income countries below those thresholds. Their obligations are lighter, but Category A customers further up the value chain will still ask them for emissions data.

**Reporting cadence.** Annual reporting is now required: scope 1 and 2 every year, and the full scope 1-3 at the end of each five-year cycle. Long-term net-zero targets are optional. Near-term five-year targets are not (scope 1 and 2 for everyone, scope 3 for Category A).

**Three formal roles for carbon credits.** For the first time, SBTi gives credits a defined place along the whole road to net zero:

1. **Voluntary OER recognition (2027–2035)**: three public tiers (Engaged / Advanced / Leadership).
2. **Mandatory removal purchases (from 2035 for Category A)**: starting at 1% of ongoing emissions and rising to 100% by the company's net-zero year.
3. **Neutralisation of residual emissions at net-zero year**: leftover emissions are balanced with removal credits that last as long as the emissions being neutralised.

**What has not changed:** carbon credits still cannot be used to meet scope 1, 2 or 3 emission reduction targets. Cutting emissions remains the foundation.

### The OER recognition tiers

The tiers are measured against a company's total scope 1-3 emissions over the five-year target cycle:

| Tier | Coverage | Contribution budget |
|---|---|---|
| **Engaged** | ≥1% of total scope 1-3 | tonne-for-tonne with retired verified credits, OR **~$20/tonne** contribution budget |
| **Advanced** | 100% of scope 1 & 2 + ≥10% of scope 1-3 | same options |
| **Leadership** | 100% of all ongoing scope 1-3 emissions | **≥$80/tonne** contribution budget + retired verified credits for full covered volume |

The **$80/tonne** figure has drawn a lot of press attention, but it is not a market price, a compliance requirement or a minimum credit price. It is the contribution budget for the Leadership tier only, which SBTi describes as "the full internalisation of the cost of climate change." For a sense of scale, a company with 500,000 tCO₂e of ongoing emissions that chose Leadership would face an annual budget of about $40m. The Engaged tier for the same volume would cost about $100,000.

### The 2035 mandate

From 2035, the OER programme becomes **mandatory for Category A** companies:

- 2035: cover at least 1% of ongoing scope 1-3 emissions with carbon removal credits
- The requirement rises every year to 100% by the company's net-zero target year
- Durable removals (those that lock carbon away for a very long time) are phased in: 10% of the removal requirement in 2035, rising to 100% by the net-zero year

Because SBTi works in five-year review cycles, many businesses will actually face the 2035 change **at their next target validation**, well before 2035 itself. Removal supply is small for now, about 5-10 million tonnes a year against a voluntary market of about 180 million tonnes, so prices are expected to rise as the mandate gets closer.

### Nature-based solutions under OER

Under the voluntary OER programme (2027-2035), nature-based credits for reductions, avoided emissions and removals all qualify. From 2035 onward, the mandatory requirement switches to removals only, but **nature-based removals can still make up to 90%** in 2035. That share falls gradually as durable engineered removals grow.

### Quality bar

Credits used under OER must be:

- Measured after the fact (ex-post outcomes only)
- Independently verified against a recognised standard
- Permanently retired when claimed
- Generated within five years of the reporting year

### Transparency architecture

Every company must **declare its OER position** when its target is validated, including an explanation if it opts out. The decision is published on the SBTi Dashboard, where investors, customers and regulators can see it. This openness may matter as much as the tier structure itself.
