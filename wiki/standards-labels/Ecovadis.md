Companies are under growing pressure to show that they source responsibly. [EcoVadis](https://ecovadis.com/ ) has become one of the most widely used tools for rating and communicating sustainability performance across global supply chains.

---

**1. What is EcoVadis?**

EcoVadis is a SaaS (online software) platform that gives companies sustainability ratings. Its main job is to help organisations spot ESG risks in their supply chains. That matters because a company's supply chain accounts, on average, for around 90% of its total environmental impact.

The rating covers four areas: environment, labour and human rights, ethics, and sustainable procurement. Companies are scored on how strong their sustainability management systems are, as well as on their results.

EcoVadis is not a certification body in the usual sense. It does not certify products or services as sustainable. Instead, it rates how well a company manages sustainability across its operations and supply chain, compared with the other companies in the EcoVadis database.

---

**2. Who is it for?**

EcoVadis serves two kinds of users.

The first is companies asked by a client or partner to complete an assessment, usually as part of supplier qualification or a buying process. An EcoVadis rating has become a standard requirement in many industries, especially manufacturing, retail and technology.

The second is companies that want to check and manage the sustainability performance of their own suppliers. Here EcoVadis works as a supply chain risk tool, giving buying teams a structured way to keep an eye on ESG risks across their suppliers.

Providers such as Sustainabl-IT offer access to both the EcoVadis certification process and the wider SaaS platform, and support companies through the assessment and afterwards.

---

**3. What is the process?**

There are a few steps.

A company is invited to fill in an EcoVadis questionnaire, either by a client asking for proof of its sustainability performance or because it chooses to be assessed. The questions cover policies, actions and results across the four areas above, and they are adjusted to the company's sector and size.

The company submits supporting documents with its answers. EcoVadis analysts review them and produce a scorecard with a rating out of 100.

Depending on the score, a company may win a medal. There are four levels, Bronze, Silver, Gold and Platinum, which go to companies in the top 50%, 25%, 5% and 1% of all assessed companies respectively.


> **An EcoVadis medal or badge is an acknowledgement of achievement _relative_ to other assessed companies across the EcoVadis database.**

So a medal shows how a company compares with others. It is not a guarantee that the company is sustainable. EcoVadis itself says a medal reflects good intent and the quality of a management system, and that there is always room to improve.

Assessments are renewed every year. Subscription prices depend on the size of the company and the features it wants, from basic ratings to premium plans that include displaying a badge and more detailed analytics.


> **What are Ecovadis medals and Badges**
> EcoVadis Medals and Badges recognize eligible companies that have completed the EcoVadis assessment process and demonstrated a relatively strong management system that addresses sustainability criteria, as outlined in the **[EcoVadis methodology](https://support.ecovadis.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002531507)**.
> An EcoVadis medal or badge is **NOT** a certification or an endorsement of a company or its products or services, and it does not indicate that the company’s products or services are specifically sustainable or more sustainable than another company’s products or services.
> An earned medal or badge does not mean that a company has no room for improvement or any potential weaknesses in their sustainability business practices. It serves as an indicator of positive intent as assessed companies strive to improve their sustainability management performance and drive impact.


---

**Summary**

EcoVadis has become a practical standard for checking sustainability in global supply chains. For many companies a rating is no longer optional: larger buyers, particularly in Europe, increasingly expect one before they will do business.
