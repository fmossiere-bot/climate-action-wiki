# The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

## What is it

The **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**, also known as the Global Goals, were adopted by the United Nations in 2015 as a universal call to action to end poverty, protect the planet, and ensure that by 2030 all people enjoy peace and prosperity. There are 17 SDGs, and they are designed to be integrated: action in one area affects outcomes in others, so development has to balance social, economic and environmental sustainability together. Countries have committed to prioritise progress for those who are furthest behind, and the goals are aimed at ending poverty, hunger, AIDS, and discrimination against women and girls. **Sustainability, in this framing, is not first and foremost about the environment or about technical solutions and engineering; it is a layered concept with justice and equity as foundational elements, about changing the fundamental way we live.** Achieving the SDGs is described as requiring the creativity, knowhow, technology and financial resources of all of society.

## Why it matters

The SDGs are the closest thing the world has to a shared, agreed checklist for what a fairer and more sustainable future looks like. They give governments, businesses and campaigners a common language and common targets, and progress against them (or the lack of it) is one of the clearest signals of whether global development is heading in the right direction.

## The 2024 progress report

On 28 June 2024, the UN published **The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2024**, which set out the significant challenges the world faces in making substantial progress towards the goals, based on the latest available data and estimates. It highlighted areas of setback alongside areas of tangible progress, including reductions in global child mortality, progress preventing HIV infection, and expanding access to energy and mobile broadband. It also flagged where action urgently needs to accelerate, particularly on climate change, peace and security, and inequalities within and between countries.

With only six years left to 2030 at the time of the report, current progress was found to fall far short of what is required to meet the SDGs. Without massive investment and scaled-up action, the report warned, achieving the goals, described as the blueprint for a more resilient and prosperous world and the roadmap out of current global crises, will remain elusive. The lingering impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic, escalating conflicts, geopolitical tensions and growing climate chaos have all severely hindered progress. The report sets out the urgent priorities needed for stronger, more effective action to honour the 2030 promise to end poverty, protect the planet and leave no one behind.

## Ireland's SDG progress

The UN Statistics Division publishes country-level data tracking each nation's progress against the SDGs, including a full breakdown of Ireland's commitments and progress by thematic area. This is a useful reference point for readers who want to see how Ireland is performing goal by goal, rather than relying on national headline figures alone.

## Key facts

- The 17 SDGs were adopted by the UN in 2015 as a universal call to action to end poverty, protect the planet and secure peace and prosperity for all by 2030.
- The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2024 was published on 28 June 2024, six years before the 2030 deadline.
- The report found progress on child mortality reduction, HIV prevention, and access to energy and mobile broadband, but urgent shortfalls on climate change, peace and security, and inequality.
- Without massive additional investment and scaled-up action, the UN says the 2030 goals will remain out of reach.
- Ireland's goal-by-goal SDG progress is tracked by the UN Statistics Division's country data portal.
