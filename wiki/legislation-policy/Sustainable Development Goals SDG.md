# The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

## What is it

The **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**, also called the Global Goals, were adopted by the United Nations in 2015. They are a universal call to action to end poverty, protect the planet and make sure that by 2030 everyone lives in peace and prosperity. There are 17 SDGs, and they are designed to work together: action in one area affects the others, so development has to balance social, economic and environmental needs at the same time. Countries have committed to put those furthest behind first, and the goals aim to end poverty, hunger, AIDS and discrimination against women and girls.

In this framing, sustainability is not mainly about the environment, or about technical and engineering fixes. It has several layers, with justice and fairness at its base, and it is about changing the way we live. Reaching the SDGs, the UN says, needs the creativity, know-how, technology and money of the whole of society.

## Why it matters

The SDGs are the closest thing the world has to a shared, agreed checklist for a fairer and more sustainable future. They give governments, businesses and campaigners a common language and common targets. Progress against them, or the lack of it, is one of the clearest signs of whether global development is heading the right way.

## The 2024 progress report

On **28 June 2024**, the UN published The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2024. Using the latest data and estimates, it set out the serious obstacles to making real progress on the goals. It pointed to setbacks, and also to real gains: fewer children dying worldwide, progress in preventing HIV infection, and wider access to energy and mobile broadband. It also flagged where action needs to speed up most, especially on climate change, peace and security, and inequality within and between countries.

With six years left to 2030 when the report came out, progress was falling far short of what the goals require. The report warned that without massive investment and much more action, the goals will stay out of reach. The UN describes them as the blueprint for a more resilient and prosperous world, and the way out of today's global crises. The lasting effects of the COVID-19 pandemic, worsening conflicts, geopolitical tensions and growing climate chaos have all held progress back. The report lists the urgent priorities for stronger action to keep the 2030 promise to end poverty, protect the planet and leave no one behind.

## Key facts

- The 17 SDGs were adopted by the UN in 2015 as a universal call to action to end poverty, protect the planet and secure peace and prosperity for all by 2030.
- The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2024 was published on 28 June 2024, six years before the 2030 deadline.
- The report found progress on child mortality, HIV prevention, and access to energy and mobile broadband, but urgent shortfalls on climate change, peace and security, and inequality.
- Without massive extra investment and much more action, the UN says the 2030 goals will stay out of reach.
- Ireland's goal-by-goal SDG progress is tracked on the UN Statistics Division's country data portal.
