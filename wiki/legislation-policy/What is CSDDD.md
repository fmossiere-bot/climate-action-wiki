# What is CSDDD? The EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive

## What is it

The **Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)** is an EU law that sets a standard of "due diligence" on sustainability for businesses operating in the EU. In plain terms, companies must find, prevent and deal with harm to people and the environment, both in their own operations and along their value chains. It was originally meant to be phased in, with large companies going first and all covered organisations complying by 2028.

As first agreed, and already watered down by last-minute concessions during negotiations, the directive covered companies with more than **1,000 employees and €450 million in turnover**. That was an estimated 5,000 companies across the EU. The accountancy firm PwC found that companies' biggest worry about the new sustainability rules was whether good-quality data would be available.

Stephen Noonan, head of the accountancy body Acca Ireland, urged companies that had not yet reviewed their environmental footprint to do so "as a matter of urgency as it will increasingly influence the growth of their business in the years ahead." Reporting professionals also pointed to the practical difficulty of complying: "the breadth and depth of reporting presents a massive challenge as teams work to collect, verify and consolidate many new types of data. Organisations need a plan for how they are going to define, source, govern and process the data in a manner to ensure that it is capable of being assured." Because the directive in effect puts sustainability reporting on a par with financial reporting, senior executives increasingly accept that sustainability information has to be available, accurate and checkable by auditors every year, rather than as a one-off.

## Why it matters

CSDDD is one of the EU's biggest attempts to make companies legally responsible for harm across their whole value chain, including suppliers outside the EU. Disclosing their impacts is no longer enough: they have to act on them. It sits alongside the [Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)](What%20is%20CSRD%3F.md) as one of the two pillars of the EU's corporate sustainability agenda.

## October 2025 update: CSDDD scaled back under the omnibus deal

Under the EU's omnibus reform package, the scope of CSDDD was cut back sharply. Only companies with at least **5,000 employees and €1.5 billion in turnover** now have to carry out environmental and human rights due diligence across their value chains, up from the original threshold of 1,000 employees and €450 million. The requirement to assess future risks, and the plan for a common EU-wide system of civil liability (the right to sue companies for harm), were also removed. What remains is a much weaker version of what was once presented as a cornerstone of the EU's sustainable business agenda.

## Key facts

- CSDDD requires companies to find, prevent and deal with harm to people and the environment across their own operations and value chains.
- As originally agreed, it covered companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450 million turnover, an estimated 5,000 companies EU-wide.
- Under the 2025 omnibus reform, the threshold rose to 5,000 employees and €1.5 billion turnover.
- The requirement to assess future risks and a proposed common civil liability framework were removed under the omnibus deal.
- Companies were due to comply in phases, with full compliance originally required by 2028.
