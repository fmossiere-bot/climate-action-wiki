# What is CSDDD? The EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive

## What is it

The **Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)** is an EU directive that establishes a corporate due diligence standard on sustainability issues for businesses operating in the EU, requiring firms to identify, prevent and address human rights and environmental harms across their own operations and value chains. It was originally set to be phased in with large companies required to comply first, and all covered organisations required to comply by 2028.

As first agreed, and while watered down by last-minute concessions during negotiation, the directive covered companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450 million in turnover, affecting an estimated 5,000 companies across the bloc. Accountants PwC identified the availability and quality of data as companies' biggest concern about the new sustainability requirements.

Acca Ireland head Stephen Noonan urged companies that had not yet reviewed their environmental footprint to do so "as a matter of urgency as it will increasingly influence the growth of their business in the years ahead." Reporting professionals also flagged the practical challenge of compliance: "the breadth and depth of reporting presents a massive challenge as teams work to collect, verify and consolidate many new types of data. Organisations need a plan for how they are going to define, source, govern and process the data in a manner to ensure that it is capable of being assured." As the directive essentially requires sustainability reporting to be on par with financial reporting, leading executives increasingly recognise that sustainability information must be available, accurate and capable of being assured, not just once, but annually.

## Why it matters

CSDDD is one of the EU's most significant attempts to make companies legally accountable, not just for disclosing their impacts, but for actively addressing harm across their entire value chain, including suppliers outside the EU. It sits alongside the [Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)](What%20is%20CSRD%3F.md) as one of the two pillars of the EU's corporate sustainability agenda.

## October 2025 update: CSDDD scaled back under the omnibus deal

Under the EU's omnibus reform package, CSDDD's scope was significantly narrowed. **Only companies with at least 5,000 employees and €1.5 billion in turnover are now required to conduct environmental and human rights due diligence across their value chains**, up from the original 1,000-employee, €450 million threshold. Critically, forward-looking risk assessment requirements and efforts to establish a common EU-wide civil liability framework were also stripped out of the directive. What remains is a significantly weakened version of what was once billed as a cornerstone of the EU's sustainable business agenda.

## Key facts

- CSDDD requires companies to identify, prevent and address human rights and environmental harms across their own operations and value chains.
- As originally agreed, it covered companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450 million turnover, an estimated 5,000 companies EU-wide.
- Under the 2025 omnibus reform, the threshold rose to 5,000 employees and €1.5 billion turnover.
- Forward-looking risk assessment requirements and a proposed common civil liability framework were removed under the omnibus deal.
- Companies were due to comply in phases, with full compliance originally required by 2028.
