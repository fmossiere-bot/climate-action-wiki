# COP31 in Antalya: dates, structure and main topics

> **Editorial summary:** Original overview by Fabien Mossière, built from the UNFCCC COP31 Presidency's third Letter to Parties (24 August 2026) and cross-checked against COP31.tr, IISD SDG Knowledge Hub, edie, and Australia's DCCEEW. **COP31 is unusual for two reasons: it is the first COP hosted jointly by two countries plus a region (Türkiye hosts and holds the Presidency, Australia leads the negotiations, Fiji hosts the Pre-COP), and it is framed as an "implementation COP" — less about new promises, more about delivering what was agreed at Belém (COP30) and Baku (COP29).** The Presidency has set ten priorities, five with measurable 2035 targets. But those targets sit in the voluntary Action Agenda, not the negotiated text — so the harder tests are still national climate plans, the delivery of the $300bn / $1.3tn finance goals, adaptation, and whether the fossil-fuel transition enters the formal outcome.

## 1. Key facts at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Event | 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) |
| Dates | Monday 9 November to Friday 20 November 2026 |
| Place | Antalya, on the Mediterranean coast of Türkiye |
| Host and COP Presidency | Türkiye |
| President-Designate | Murat Kurum, Turkish Minister of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change |
| President of Negotiations | Chris Bowen, Australian Minister for Climate Change and Energy |
| Leaders' Summit | 11 and 12 November 2026 |
| Pre-COP meeting | Fiji, in partnership with Pacific island states |
| Main framing | An "implementation COP": less focus on new promises, more on delivering existing ones |

COP stands for Conference of the Parties. It is the yearly meeting of the almost 200 countries that signed the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). It is where the rules of the Paris Agreement are negotiated and reviewed.

## 2. An unusual shared arrangement

COP31 is organised in a way that has not been tried before. Both Türkiye and Australia wanted to host the 2026 summit, and the question was only settled at COP30 in Belém, Brazil, in November 2025. The compromise splits the roles:

- **Türkiye** hosts the conference in Antalya, holds the formal Presidency and leads the Action Agenda (the voluntary, non-negotiated part of the COP).
- **Australia** leads the negotiation process. Chris Bowen acts as President of Negotiations.
- **The Pacific** hosts the Pre-COP meeting in Fiji. Australia has also named Pacific Climate Envoys to bring the region's priorities into the talks.

The Presidency describes this as the first time that two countries and a region deliver a COP together. It is also an untested model. How well the host and the negotiation lead coordinate will matter during the difficult final days.

## 3. What COP31 inherits

COP31 does not start from a blank page. Several open questions come from previous summits:

- **National climate plans (NDCs).** Countries were due to submit new plans covering the period to 2035. Taken together, the plans submitted so far do not put the world on track to limit warming to 1.5°C. Some countries have still not submitted. The Presidency's letter asks them to do so, along with their Biennial Transparency Reports.
- **Climate finance.** COP29 in Baku set a goal of at least USD 300 billion per year by 2035 for developing countries, with a wider aspiration of USD 1.3 trillion per year from all sources. The question now is how this money will actually be delivered.
- **Adaptation.** COP30 called for a tripling of adaptation finance by 2035 and adopted a first set of indicators to measure progress on adaptation. Both need follow-up.
- **Fossil fuels.** COP30 did not agree a formal roadmap for the transition away from fossil fuels. The topic continues through voluntary initiatives outside the negotiated text and is likely to return in Antalya.
- **Just transition.** COP30 agreed to develop a mechanism to support a fair transition for workers and communities. Its design is still to be worked out.

## 4. The ten priorities of the Presidency

On 24 August 2026, Murat Kurum published the Presidency's third Letter to Parties. It sets out ten priority themes for the COP31 Action Agenda. Five of them carry a measurable target for 2035. The other five are described as directions without a number.

| # | Priority | Target or aim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clean energy transition and electrification | Raise electricity's share of global final energy use to 35% by 2035 (from around 23% today) |
| 2 | Zero waste and methane reduction | Cut the projected growth in yearly municipal solid waste by at least 50% by 2035 |
| 3 | Climate-resilient cities and built environment | Reduce energy-use intensity in buildings by at least 25% by 2035 |
| 4 | Green industrialisation | Raise the global circular material-use rate to at least 15% by 2035 |
| 5 | Youth and education | Climate action education for all by 2035 |
| 6 | Food security | Speed up the move to climate-resilient farming and better water management |
| 7 | Ocean and seas | Strengthen marine and coastal resilience |
| 8 | Dynamic and resilient health systems | Build health systems that are climate-resilient and fair |
| 9 | Rio synergies | Restore ecosystems, protect carbon sinks, manage land sustainably and improve cooperation between the three Rio Conventions (climate, biodiversity, desertification) |
| 10 | Climate Implementation Bridge | Help countries turn national climate and development plans into project portfolios that are ready for finance |

The letter names finance, technology and capacity building as enabling conditions that run across all ten themes.

Zero waste stands out as a theme that is specific to this Presidency. It reflects a long-running national programme in Türkiye and has its own thematic day.

## 5. Thematic days

As at previous COPs, each day has a theme. These days host events, announcements and partnerships. They run in parallel to the formal negotiations and are not part of them.

| Date | Theme |
|---|---|
| Mon 9 Nov | Food, Agriculture and Health |
| Tue 10 Nov | Energy and Transport |
| Wed 11 Nov | Zero Waste |
| Thu 12 Nov | Resilient Cities and Built Environment |
| Fri 13 Nov | Finance and Trade |
| Sat 14 Nov | Children, Youth, Education and Skills |
| Sun 15 Nov | "A Breath in Antalya": social and cultural programmes (rest day) |
| Mon 16 Nov | Science, Industry and Technology |
| Tue 17 Nov | Ocean, Seas, Nature, Land Use and Rio Synergy |
| Wed 18 Nov | Human and Social Development |
| Thu 19 Nov | Accelerating Implementation |
| Fri 20 Nov | Final Negotiations |

The Leaders' Summit on 11 and 12 November falls on the Zero Waste and Resilient Cities days. COPs often run past their official closing date, so an end on 20 November should be seen as a plan rather than a certainty.

## 6. Link with the Global Climate Action Agenda

The Global Climate Action Agenda is the framework, renewed at COP30, that organises voluntary climate action by cities, regions, businesses, investors and civil society. The COP31 Presidency has committed to give dedicated space to all six of its axes:

1. Transitioning energy, industry and transport
2. Stewarding forests, the ocean and biodiversity
3. Transforming agriculture and food systems
4. Building resilience for cities, infrastructure and water
5. Fostering human and social development
6. Unleashing enablers and accelerators (finance, technology, capacity)

The ten Turkish priorities fit inside these six axes. This gives some continuity from one Presidency to the next, which has often been missing in the past.

## 7. Milestones on the road to Antalya

The letter lists several meetings before the summit where initiatives will be prepared:

- Climate Week NYC (September)
- Istanbul Climate Finance Summit
- Ocean and Seas Summit
- UNFCCC Climate Week
- Pre-COP meeting in Fiji
- The UN biodiversity COP in October and the UN desertification COP17 in Mongolia, which both feed into the "Rio synergies" theme

## 8. Main topics to watch

Based on the agenda and on what was left open in Belém, these are the areas where COP31 will most likely be judged:

1. **The ambition gap.** How the summit responds to national plans that remain, together, too weak for the Paris goals.
2. **Finance delivery.** Whether there is a credible path from the USD 300 billion goal towards USD 1.3 trillion, and whether the Climate Implementation Bridge attracts real money.
3. **Adaptation finance and indicators.** Whether the tripling goal gets a clear baseline and timeline.
4. **Fossil fuel transition.** Whether the topic enters the formal outcome or stays in voluntary side processes.
5. **Electrification.** The 35% target is the headline number of the Action Agenda. The test is which countries and companies sign up and how progress will be tracked.
6. **Waste and methane.** A less common COP topic, with potential for quick gains because methane has a strong short-term warming effect.
7. **Pacific priorities.** Loss and damage, the 1.5°C limit and ocean protection, carried by Australia and the Pacific envoys.
8. **Trade.** "Finance and Trade" shares a thematic day. Carbon border measures such as the EU CBAM remain a point of tension between countries.

## 9. Credibility and limitations

- **Action Agenda targets are voluntary.** The 2035 targets in the letter are not negotiated or binding. They are collective aims set by the Presidency. No country is individually accountable for them.
- **Half of the priorities have no number.** Food, ocean, health, Rio synergies and the Implementation Bridge are described in general terms. Progress will be hard to measure.
- **"Implementation COP" is not a new label.** Several recent COPs used similar wording. The label on its own says little about results.
- **The host's own record is questioned.** Türkiye has a net zero target for 2053, but independent analysts have criticised its national plan because it allows emissions to keep rising this decade, and coal remains an important part of its energy mix. Australia is a major exporter of coal and gas. Both points are likely to be raised by observers.
- **The shared model is untested.** A split between host Presidency and negotiation lead could bring complementary strengths, or create confusion at decisive moments.
- **Information may change.** This article is based on the third Letter to Parties (24 August 2026) and public information available in mid-September 2026. Programmes and dates can still be adjusted.

## 10. Bottom line

COP31 takes place in Antalya from 9 to 20 November 2026, hosted by Türkiye with Australia leading the negotiations and the Pacific hosting the Pre-COP. The Presidency has set out ten priorities, led by electrification, zero waste, resilient cities and circular industry, with 2035 targets for five of them. These targets are voluntary. The harder questions sit in the formal negotiations: weak national plans, the delivery of climate finance, adaptation, and the transition away from fossil fuels. The summit will be judged less on its themes than on whether it produces money, tracking and accountability behind them.
