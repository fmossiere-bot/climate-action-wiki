# Paris Agreement - NDC - Where Are We 10 Years Later?

## What is it

**Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)** are the national climate action plans that countries develop under the Paris Agreement, laying out how each intends to cut greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to climate impacts. COP30 fell exactly ten years after the Paris Agreement was adopted, and the state of countries' NDCs, most of which were due for renewal in 2025, was a central theme of the summit. Discussions focused on the severe lack of global ambition in the pledges submitted, widespread failure to meet submission deadlines, and political disagreement over how to review and strengthen them.

## Why it matters

NDCs are the mechanism through which the Paris Agreement's abstract temperature goals become concrete national commitments. Ten years on, the gap between what has been pledged and what is needed to stay within 1.5°C remains the single biggest measure of whether the Paris Agreement is working.

## The NDC deadline and status of submissions

The formal deadline for countries to submit new NDCs to the UN was **10 February 2025**, but **95% of countries missed it**. Nations continued submitting pledges through the year and during the talks: on the first day of COP30, the UNFCCC executive secretary noted that 22 new NDCs had been submitted since the previous synthesis report, and by the end of the summit, **some 122 countries** had submitted their new pledges.

Among the major emitters, **China**, the world's biggest annual emitter, announced its NDC at a September event ahead of the summit. There was speculation over whether **India**, the world's third-biggest emitter, would submit in time; its climate minister clarified this would happen "by December". The **US NDC was effectively voided** after President Trump signed an order to withdraw the country from the Paris Agreement, though the pledge remains officially listed with the UNFCCC. The final overarching agreement, the "global mutirao" decision, praised the 122 submitted pledges and "urges" the remaining countries to submit "as soon as possible".

## The ambition gap

The NDCs submitted were deemed **"grossly inadequate"** to meet the Paris Agreement's temperature goal. Based on the available plans, the world remains on track for **2.3 to 2.5°C of warming by 2100**. A UN NDC synthesis report, based on plans submitted up to October, found that the latest NDCs would achieve a global emissions drop of only about **10% by 2035** from 2019 levels. After the 22 additional NDCs submitted at the start of COP30, this projected drop rose slightly to **12%**, still considered "drastically short" of the **60% cut needed to keep 1.5°C in sight**. Sources at the summit said outstanding NDCs should align with 1.5°C, drive bold sectoral shifts in food, forests, ocean, energy and transport, and prioritise resilience.

## Political conflict over review mechanisms

The **Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS)** proposed a "dedicated space" on the COP agenda to "assess collective progress... and drive ambition toward 1.5C." Palau's ambassador, who chaired AOSIS, argued: "The 1.5C target is our north star." The proposal faced opposition from the **Like-Minded Developing Countries (LMDCs)** group, which includes China, India and Saudi Arabia, and prefers to interpret the Paris Agreement goal as allowing temperatures to rise toward 2°C.

An early draft of the "global mutirao" text included an option for **annual NDC discussions** linked to a "transition away from fossil fuels", but this was opposed by the LMDCs and the African group and dropped from the final text. Instead of explicit annual reviews, the final decision "calls on" countries to accelerate the full implementation of their pledges and encourages them to draw up "implementation and investment plans" aligning climate strategy with economic development.

## NDC content on specific sectors

A review found that **93% of new NDCs** included at least one measure on agriculture or food systems, though an assessment ranked Brazil and New Zealand's NDCs as "weak" in this area, while those from Somalia and Switzerland were rated "very strong". On oceans, more than 90% of new NDCs submitted by coastal and island countries included ocean-based climate actions. Brazil and France had previously launched the "blue NDC challenge" to encourage countries to integrate ocean measures into their pledges.

Overall, the picture on NDCs reflects a political reality: most countries recognise the need to align with 1.5°C, but consensus has proven impossible on a binding or mandatory system to rapidly accelerate and enforce those commitments.

## A country ranking on Paris Agreement performance

A separate study compares how 23 developed countries are performing against their Paris Agreement commitments, analysing hundreds of parameters to produce a single score covering CO2 emissions, adaptation measures, transparency and international cooperation. **Austria ranks first overall.** **Germany ranks second** despite unremarkable progress on CO2 reduction, largely on the strength of its adaptation performance, where it ranks first, just ahead of France. **Ireland ranks 16th out of the 23 countries assessed**, and performs particularly poorly on adaptation specifically. The study, limited to wealthy countries, was carried out by the University of Liège together with the fund manager Degroof Petercam (DPAM).

## What are NDCs, in plain terms

NDCs are the climate action plans that countries develop under the Paris Agreement, laying out how each intends to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to the effects of climate change. They matter because they reflect the global effort to limit warming to well below 2°C, ideally 1.5°C, and have prompted countries to set more ambitious targets and adopt cleaner technologies. Progress has been uneven, but there is evidence NDCs are steering investment and policy shifts toward sustainability. Much more still needs to be done to meet global goals and protect vulnerable communities and ecosystems.

## Key facts

- 95% of countries missed the formal 10 February 2025 NDC submission deadline; 122 countries had submitted by the end of COP30.
- Current NDCs put the world on track for 2.3 to 2.5°C of warming, achieving only a 10 to 12% emissions cut by 2035 versus 2019 levels, against a 60% cut needed for 1.5°C.
- The US NDC was effectively voided after Trump ordered withdrawal from the Paris Agreement; China and India, the world's first and third-largest emitters, submitted or confirmed pledges around the summit.
- AOSIS pushed for a dedicated COP agenda item on NDC ambition; the LMDC bloc (China, India, Saudi Arabia) and the African group blocked annual review mechanisms.
- 93% of new NDCs address agriculture or food systems; over 90% of coastal and island countries' NDCs include ocean-based actions.
- In a 23-country Paris Agreement performance ranking by the University of Liège and Degroof Petercam, Austria ranks first, Germany second, and Ireland 16th, with particularly weak adaptation performance.
