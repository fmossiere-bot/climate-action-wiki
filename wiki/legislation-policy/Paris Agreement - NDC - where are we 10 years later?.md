# Paris Agreement - NDC - Where Are We 10 Years Later?

## What is it

**Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)** are the climate action plans that countries draw up under the Paris Agreement. Each one sets out how a country intends to cut its greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to climate impacts. COP30 took place exactly ten years after the Paris Agreement was adopted, and most NDCs were due for renewal in 2025, so the state of these plans was a central theme of the summit. Talks focused on how little ambition the new pledges showed, how many countries missed the deadline, and disagreement over how to review and strengthen them.

## Why it matters

NDCs are how the Paris Agreement's temperature goals turn into national commitments. Ten years on, the gap between what countries have pledged and what is needed to stay within 1.5°C is still the clearest measure of whether the agreement is working.

## The NDC deadline and status of submissions

The formal deadline for new NDCs was **10 February 2025**, and 95% of countries missed it. Countries kept submitting through the year and during the talks. On the first day of COP30, the UNFCCC executive secretary said 22 new NDCs had arrived since the previous synthesis report. By the end of the summit, some 122 countries had submitted new pledges.

Among the big emitters, **China**, the world's largest annual emitter, announced its NDC at a September event before the summit. There was uncertainty over whether **India**, the third-largest emitter, would submit in time. Its climate minister said this would happen "by December". The US NDC was effectively voided after President Trump signed an order to withdraw the country from the Paris Agreement, although the pledge is still officially listed with the UNFCCC. The final overall agreement, the "global mutirao" decision, welcomed the 122 pledges and "urges" the remaining countries to submit "as soon as possible".

## The ambition gap

The submitted NDCs were judged "grossly inadequate" to meet the Paris Agreement's temperature goal. Based on the plans available, the world is on track for **2.3 to 2.5°C of warming by 2100**. A UN synthesis report covering plans submitted up to October found that the latest NDCs would cut global emissions by only about 10% by 2035 compared with 2019. With the 22 extra NDCs submitted at the start of COP30, the expected cut rose slightly to 12%. That is still "drastically short" of the 60% cut needed to keep 1.5°C within reach. Voices at the summit said the outstanding NDCs should line up with 1.5°C, drive big changes in food, forests, ocean, energy and transport, and put resilience first.

## Political conflict over review mechanisms

The **Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS)** proposed a "dedicated space" on the COP agenda to "assess collective progress... and drive ambition toward 1.5C." Palau's ambassador, who chaired AOSIS, said: "The 1.5C target is our north star." The proposal was opposed by the Like-Minded Developing Countries (LMDCs), a group that includes China, India and Saudi Arabia. They prefer to read the Paris goal as allowing temperatures to rise towards 2°C.

An early draft of the "global mutirao" text included an option for annual NDC discussions linked to a "transition away from fossil fuels". The LMDCs and the African group opposed it, and it was dropped from the final text. Instead of annual reviews, the final decision "calls on" countries to speed up the full delivery of their pledges. It also encourages them to draw up "implementation and investment plans" that tie climate strategy to economic development.

## NDC content on specific sectors

A review found that **93% of new NDCs** included at least one measure on agriculture or food systems. One assessment rated the NDCs of Brazil and New Zealand as "weak" in this area, and those of Somalia and Switzerland as "very strong". On oceans, more than 90% of new NDCs from coastal and island countries included ocean-based climate action. Brazil and France had earlier launched the "blue NDC challenge" to encourage countries to build ocean measures into their pledges.

Overall, the NDC picture reflects a political reality. Most countries accept the need to line up with 1.5°C, but they have not been able to agree on a binding system to speed up and enforce those commitments.

## A country ranking on Paris Agreement performance

A separate study compares how 23 developed countries are performing against their Paris Agreement commitments. It analyses hundreds of measures to produce a single score covering CO2 emissions, adaptation, transparency and international cooperation. Austria ranks first overall. Germany ranks second despite average progress on cutting CO2, mainly thanks to its adaptation work, where it ranks first, just ahead of France. **Ireland ranks 16th out of the 23 countries**, and does particularly badly on adaptation. The study, which covers only wealthy countries, was carried out by the University of Liège with the fund manager Degroof Petercam (DPAM).

## What are NDCs, in plain terms

NDCs are the climate plans that countries draw up under the Paris Agreement, setting out how each will reduce its greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to the effects of climate change. They matter because they add up to the global effort to limit warming to well below 2°C, ideally 1.5°C. They have pushed countries to set more ambitious targets and take up cleaner technologies. Progress has been uneven, but there is evidence that NDCs are steering investment and policy towards sustainability. Much more is still needed to meet global goals and protect vulnerable communities and ecosystems.

## Key facts

- 95% of countries missed the formal 10 February 2025 NDC deadline. 122 countries had submitted by the end of COP30.
- Current NDCs put the world on track for 2.3 to 2.5°C of warming, with emissions only 10 to 12% lower by 2035 than in 2019, against a 60% cut needed for 1.5°C.
- The US NDC was effectively voided after Trump ordered withdrawal from the Paris Agreement. China and India, the world's first and third-largest emitters, submitted or confirmed pledges around the summit.
- AOSIS pushed for a dedicated COP agenda item on NDC ambition. The LMDC group (China, India, Saudi Arabia) and the African group blocked annual reviews.
- 93% of new NDCs address agriculture or food systems, and over 90% of NDCs from coastal and island countries include ocean-based action.
- In a 23-country Paris Agreement performance ranking by the University of Liège and Degroof Petercam, Austria ranks first, Germany second and Ireland 16th, with particularly weak adaptation.
