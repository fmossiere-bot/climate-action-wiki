# Why Sustainability Reporting Is Important

## What is it

Sustainability reporting is the practice of companies publicly disclosing their environmental, social and governance performance, and it has moved from a voluntary, occasional exercise to something regulators, investors and employees increasingly expect as standard. Reporting maturity tends to be evolutionary rather than immediate: Irish companies that are now seen as leaders in this space, such as An Post, took roughly a decade of consistent effort to reach their current level of reporting practice. That trajectory matters because it shows sustainability reporting is a capability companies have to build over time, not a document they can produce from a standing start.

## Why it matters

Reporting matters to several audiences at once. It matters to **stakeholders, especially investors**, who increasingly use ESG information to assess financial risk and steer capital, as covered in more detail in [Why Investors Want Companies to Focus on ESG](Why%20Reporting%20-%20Investors%20want%20companies%20to%20focus%20on%20ESG.md). It also matters to **employees**, for whom a company's credible sustainability commitments and transparent reporting increasingly factor into where they choose to work and how engaged they feel once there.

## More Irish companies are reporting, and reporting more thoroughly

KPMG's Survey of Sustainability Reporting tracks how the largest 100 companies in each country, Ireland's "N100", report on sustainability. The share of Irish N100 companies producing a sustainability report rose from **78% in 2020 to 95% in 2022**, bringing Ireland roughly into line with the global average. Within those reports, **86% of Irish N100 companies include carbon reduction targets**, with **62% of those targets aligned with the Paris Agreement's goal of limiting global warming to well below 2°C**. Only **23% of Irish companies identify biodiversity as a risk to their operations**, roughly half the global average, showing biodiversity remains a weaker spot in Irish corporate reporting than climate.

Independent assurance of these reports, third parties checking that what companies say is accurate, rose from 32% in 2020 to 37% in 2022 among Irish N100 companies, though this still lags the global average of 47%. At the time of the 2022 survey, only 58% of Irish N100 companies issued Ireland-specific reports, something that has since become mandatory for companies in scope of the CSRD.

## Key facts

- Irish N100 sustainability reporting rose from 78% (2020) to 95% (2022), broadly matching the global average.
- 86% of Irish N100 companies report carbon reduction targets; 62% of those are aligned with the Paris Agreement's well-below-2°C goal.
- Only 23% of Irish N100 companies identify biodiversity as a business risk, about half the global average.
- Independent assurance of Irish N100 reports rose from 32% (2020) to 37% (2022), still behind the 47% global average.
- Only 58% of Irish N100 companies issued Ireland-specific sustainability reports as of the 2022 survey, a gap the CSRD is closing by making this mandatory.
