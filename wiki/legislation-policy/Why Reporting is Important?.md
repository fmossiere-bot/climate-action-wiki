# Why Sustainability Reporting Is Important

## What is it

Sustainability reporting means companies publicly disclosing their environmental, social and governance performance. It has moved from a voluntary, occasional exercise to something regulators, investors and employees increasingly expect as standard. Companies usually get good at it gradually. Irish companies now seen as leaders, such as **An Post**, took roughly a decade of steady effort to reach their current level. That matters because it shows reporting is a skill companies build over time, and not a document they can produce from scratch overnight.

## Why it matters

Reporting matters to several groups at once. It matters to stakeholders, especially **investors**, who increasingly use ESG information to judge financial risk and decide where money goes, as explained in [Why Investors Want Companies to Focus on ESG](Why%20Reporting%20-%20Investors%20want%20companies%20to%20focus%20on%20ESG.md). It also matters to employees. A company's credible sustainability commitments and open reporting increasingly shape where people choose to work and how engaged they feel once they are there.

## More Irish companies are reporting, and reporting more thoroughly

KPMG's Survey of Sustainability Reporting tracks how the largest 100 companies in each country report on sustainability. In Ireland these are called the "N100". The share of Irish N100 companies producing a sustainability report rose from **78% in 2020 to 95% in 2022**, roughly in line with the global average. Within those reports, 86% of Irish N100 companies include carbon reduction targets, and 62% of those targets are aligned with the Paris Agreement's goal of limiting global warming to well below 2°C. Only 23% of Irish companies see biodiversity as a risk to their business, roughly half the global average, so nature is still a weaker spot than climate in Irish corporate reporting.

Independent assurance means an outside party checking that what a company says is accurate. Among Irish N100 companies it rose from 32% in 2020 to 37% in 2022, still behind the global average of 47%. At the time of the 2022 survey, only 58% of Irish N100 companies published reports specific to Ireland. That has since become mandatory for companies covered by the CSRD.

## Key facts

- Sustainability reporting by Irish N100 companies rose from 78% (2020) to 95% (2022), broadly matching the global average.
- 86% of Irish N100 companies report carbon reduction targets, and 62% of those are aligned with the Paris Agreement's well-below-2°C goal.
- Only 23% of Irish N100 companies see biodiversity as a business risk, about half the global average.
- Independent assurance of Irish N100 reports rose from 32% (2020) to 37% (2022), still behind the 47% global average.
- Only 58% of Irish N100 companies published Ireland-specific sustainability reports at the time of the 2022 survey, a gap the CSRD is closing by making this mandatory.
