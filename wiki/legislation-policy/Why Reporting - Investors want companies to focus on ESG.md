# Why Investors Want Companies to Focus on ESG Reporting

## What is it

Big institutional investors, such as pension funds and insurers, increasingly expect the companies they invest in to report on their environmental, social and governance (ESG) performance. They treat it as core information for judging risk and long-term value, rather than a side project. EY's Global Institutional Investor Survey found that **88% of investors** had somewhat or substantially increased their use of ESG information over the past year. Over half of the investors surveyed now build ESG considerations into 50% or more of their investment mandates.

The same survey found that 77% of investors focus on ESG issues with a clear financial impact, rather than treating ESG as a general reputation matter. Among investors who have used ESG criteria for more than seven years, 71% believe it leads to better returns once risk is taken into account.

## Why it matters

Investor demand is one of the biggest forces pushing companies towards better sustainability reporting, alongside regulation such as the [CSRD](What%20is%20CSRD%3F.md). Without reliable ESG information, investors cannot properly factor climate and sustainability risk into their decisions. That is part of why rules like the EU's Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR) exist. Investors need companies to report so they can meet their own disclosure duties, and so money goes to activities that are sustainable in practice, not only in intention.

This demand has limits. The same research also shows real scepticism. Many investors worry that some ESG initiatives can hurt a company's short-term performance, and the share of investors formally building ESG into their decisions has gone up and down from year to year rather than rising steadily. What investors want from companies is ESG information that has a real financial impact and can be compared across firms, not vague sustainability messaging.

## Key facts

- 88% of institutional investors increased their use of ESG information over the past year (EY Global Institutional Investor Survey).
- Over half of the investors surveyed build ESG considerations into 50% or more of their mandates.
- 77% of investors prioritise ESG issues with a clear financial impact.
- 71% of investors who have used ESG criteria for over seven years report better risk-adjusted returns.
- Formal use of ESG in investment decisions has fluctuated, from a high in 2021 down to 35% in 2022 and 38% in 2024, so investor interest is real but not rising every year.
