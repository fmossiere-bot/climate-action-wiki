# Why Investors Want Companies to Focus on ESG Reporting

## What is it

Institutional investors increasingly expect the companies they invest in to report on environmental, social and governance (ESG) performance, not as a side project, but as core information for assessing risk and long-term value. EY's Global Institutional Investor Survey found that **88% of investors had somewhat or substantially increased their use of ESG information over the past year**, and that over half of investor respondents now integrate ESG considerations into 50% or more of their investment mandates. The same survey found that **77% of investors are prioritising ESG issues with clear financial relevance**, rather than treating ESG as a general reputational concern, and that among investors who have used ESG criteria for more than seven years, 71% believe doing so leads to improved risk-adjusted returns.

## Why it matters

Investor demand is one of the biggest forces pushing companies toward better sustainability reporting, alongside regulation such as the [CSRD](What%20is%20CSRD%3F.md). If investors cannot get reliable ESG information, they cannot price climate and sustainability risk into their decisions properly, which is part of why disclosure regulation like the EU's Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR) exists: investors need companies to report so they can meet their own disclosure obligations, and so capital can be steered toward genuinely sustainable activity rather than good intentions alone.

This demand is not unconditional. The same body of investor research shows real scepticism too: many investors worry that some ESG-related initiatives can harm short-term corporate performance, and adoption rates of formal ESG integration into investment decisions have fluctuated year to year rather than rising in a straight line. The practical takeaway for companies is that investors want ESG information that is financially material and comparable, not vague sustainability messaging.

## Key facts

- 88% of institutional investors increased their use of ESG information over the past year (EY Global Institutional Investor Survey).
- Over half of investor respondents integrate ESG considerations into 50% or more of their mandates.
- 77% of investors prioritise ESG issues with clear financial relevance.
- 71% of investors using ESG criteria for over seven years report improved risk-adjusted returns.
- Formal ESG adoption in investment decisions has fluctuated, from a high in 2021 down to 35% in 2022 and 38% in 2024, showing investor appetite is real but not steadily rising every year.
