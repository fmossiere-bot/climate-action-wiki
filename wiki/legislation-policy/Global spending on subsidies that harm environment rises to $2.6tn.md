# Global Spending on Subsidies That Harm the Environment Rises to $2.6tn

## What is it

Governments around the world still hand out billions of dollars a year in tax breaks, subsidies and other spending that works against their own climate and nature promises. Those promises include the 2015 Paris Agreement and the 2022 Kunming-Montreal agreement to halt biodiversity loss. An analysis of environmentally harmful subsidies by the organisation **Earth Track** found an $800 billion increase in direct government support for activities such as deforestation, water pollution and burning fossil fuels. That takes the global total to **$2.6 trillion**.

The study is described as the first assessment of harmful subsidies across all sectors in more than a decade. It covers everything from tax breaks for beef production in the Amazon to money for unsustainable groundwater pumping in the Middle East.

## Why it matters

Subsidies are meant to help societies. When they prop up fossil fuels, deforestation or unsustainable farming, they work against the goals governments have signed up to. Every dollar spent on a polluting activity is a dollar not spent on the transition, and it tilts the market towards the most damaging choices.

## Where the money goes

According to the report, direct support breaks down as $620 billion for the fossil fuel industry, $520 billion for farming, $320 billion for water and $155 billion for forestry. Together these make up most of the **$1.8 trillion** in direct subsidies the researchers could identify. They could not produce a reliable estimate for mining, even though it is thought to cause billions of dollars of damage to ecosystems every year. Governments and the recipients of this money are not very open about it, so the true figure is probably much higher. The same goes for the hidden cost of these subsidies once indirect costs are counted.

A separate 2021 report from the **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** found that the fossil fuel industry alone benefited from subsidies worth $5.9 trillion in 2020, far more than the direct subsidy figures above. Most of the IMF figure is not direct government spending. It is the hidden cost of letting polluters off paying for the deaths and global heating their emissions cause. A related [analysis on clean electricity](../sectors/energy/Clean%20Electricity%20-%20Why%20It%20Has%20to%20Come%20First.md) uses the same IMF figure when making the case for ending fossil fuel subsidies.

## Farm subsidies mostly miss smallholders

Farm subsidies are a large share of the total. A UN report found that almost 90% of the subsidies paid to farmers every year are harmful: they damage people's health, fuel the climate crisis, destroy nature and drive inequality by leaving out smallholder farmers. In the European Union, more than **80% of farm subsidies go to just 20% of farmers**, so support is concentrated on the largest operations rather than the small farms most likely to need it. ## Key facts

- Environmentally harmful subsidies rose by $800 billion, according to Earth Track, taking the global total to $2.6 trillion.
- Direct subsidies break down as fossil fuels $620bn, agriculture $520bn, water $320bn and forestry $155bn, together most of an identified $1.8 trillion.
- No reliable estimate could be made for harmful mining subsidies.
- A 2021 IMF report found fossil fuel industry subsidies worth $5.9 trillion in 2020, mostly unpriced hidden costs such as deaths from pollution and global heating.
- Almost 90% of subsidies paid to farmers worldwide are harmful, according to a UN report, damaging health, the climate, nature and fairness.
- In the EU, more than 80% of farm subsidies go to just 20% of farmers.
