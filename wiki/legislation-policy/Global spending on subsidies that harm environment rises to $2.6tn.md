# Global Spending on Subsidies That Harm the Environment Rises to $2.6tn

## What is it

Governments around the world continue to hand out billions of dollars a year in tax breaks, subsidies and other spending that works directly against their own climate and nature commitments, including the 2015 Paris Agreement and the 2022 Kunming-Montreal agreement to halt biodiversity loss. Research from the organisation **Earth Track** found that an analysis of environmentally harmful subsidies shows an **$800 billion increase in direct government support for activities including deforestation, water pollution and fossil fuel consumption**, taking the global total to **$2.6 trillion**.

The findings are described as the first cross-sector assessment of harmful subsidies in more than a decade, covering support ranging from tax breaks for beef production in the Amazon to financial support for unsustainable groundwater pumping in the Middle East.

## Why it matters

Subsidies are supposed to help societies, but when they prop up fossil fuels, deforestation or unsustainable farming, they actively work against the goals governments have signed up to. Every dollar spent subsidising a polluting activity is a dollar not spent on the transition, and it distorts markets in favour of the most environmentally damaging choices.

## Where the money goes

According to the report, direct support breaks down as **$620 billion for the fossil fuel industry, $520 billion for the agricultural sector, $320 billion for water, and $155 billion for forestry**, together accounting for the majority of a combined $1.8 trillion in identified direct subsidies. No reliable estimate could be derived for mining, despite it being believed to cause billions of dollars of damage to ecosystems every year. Because of a lack of transparency between governments and the recipients of this spending, the true figure is likely to be considerably higher, as is the implicit cost of these harmful subsidies once indirect and hidden costs are included.

A separate 2021 report from the **International Monetary Fund (IMF)** found that the fossil fuel industry alone benefited from subsidies worth **$5.9 trillion in 2020**, a substantially higher figure than the direct subsidy numbers above. The vast majority of that IMF figure comes not from direct government spending but from the hidden costs of failing to make polluters pay for the deaths and global heating their emissions cause. A related [analysis on clean electricity](../sectors/energy/Clean%20Electricity%20-%20Why%20It%20Has%20to%20Come%20First.md) references this same IMF figure when discussing the case for ending fossil fuel subsidies.

## Farm subsidies mostly miss smallholders

Agricultural subsidies are a major share of the total, but a UN report found that almost 90% of the subsidies given to farmers every year are harmful, damaging people's health, fuelling the climate crisis, destroying nature and driving inequality by excluding smallholder farmers. In the European Union specifically, more than 80% of farm subsidies go to just 20% of farmers, concentrating support among the largest operations rather than the smallholders most likely to need it. ## Key facts

- Environmentally harmful subsidies rose by $800 billion, according to Earth Track, taking the global total to $2.6 trillion.
- Direct subsidy breakdown: fossil fuels $620bn, agriculture $520bn, water $320bn, forestry $155bn, together the majority of an identified $1.8 trillion.
- No reliable estimate could be derived for harmful mining subsidies.
- A 2021 IMF report separately found fossil fuel industry subsidies worth $5.9 trillion in 2020, mostly reflecting unpriced hidden costs such as pollution deaths and global heating.
- Almost 90% of subsidies paid to farmers globally are harmful, according to a UN report, damaging health, the climate, nature and equity.
- In the EU, more than 80% of farm subsidies go to just 20% of farmers.
