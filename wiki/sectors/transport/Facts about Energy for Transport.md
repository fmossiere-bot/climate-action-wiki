# Facts about Energy for Transport

## What is it

Transport is one of the largest single uses of energy in the global economy. **Energy demand for transport accounts for around a third of total final energy consumption worldwide**, according to [REN21's 2022 Global Status Report](https://www.ren21.net/gsr-2022/pages/keymessages/keymessages/#key-power). Despite this scale, attention to shifting that energy demand towards renewables is rising only slowly.

## Why it matters

How transport gets its energy, whether from oil, electricity or alternative fuels, will determine much of whether the world hits its climate targets. Because transport touches nearly every part of daily life and global trade, progress (or the lack of it) here has an outsized effect on overall emissions.

## Policy attention is lagging the scale of the problem

Despite transport's huge share of energy use, only 1 in 10 countries included specific emission reduction measures for transport in their Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) under the Paris Agreement, per REN21. This gap between the size of the problem and the policy response aimed at it is one of the sector's defining weaknesses.

## Electric vehicles: real progress, with a catch

2021 was a record year for vehicle electrification: 6.6 million electric cars were sold, a 109% increase on the year before. That is genuine progress, but electric vehicles still made up only around 1% of the global vehicle fleet, underlining how long fleet turnover takes even during a sales boom. The trend is also being partly offset by a shift towards heavier, less energy-efficient vehicles: more than half of the electric car models on the market in 2021 were SUVs. Despite the EV sales boom, biofuels, not electricity, remain the largest renewable energy contributor within the transport sector overall. For more on why bigger EVs undercut their own climate benefit, see [Car Life Cycle Emissions: EV vs Combustion](Car%20Life%20Cycle%20Emissions%20-%20EV%20vs%20Combustion.md).

## The hard-to-decarbonise sub-sectors

Aviation, shipping and other long-haul transport remain the most difficult parts of the sector to clean up. In principle, they could run on renewable electricity, renewable electricity-based synthetic fuels, hydrogen, biofuels or biomethane, but making that shift at scale requires substantial new infrastructure investment that has not yet materialised. See [Long-Haul Shipping](Long-Haul%20Shipping.md) and [Decarbonising Aviation and Shipping](../../solutions/electrification/Decarbonising%20Aviation%20and%20Shipping.md) for more detail on how these sub-sectors could get there.

## Key facts

- Transport accounts for roughly a third of total final energy consumption globally (REN21, 2022).
- Only 1 in 10 countries set out specific transport emission reduction measures in their Paris Agreement NDCs.
- A record 6.6 million electric cars were sold in 2021, up 109% on the previous year.
- Electric vehicles still made up only around 1% of the global vehicle fleet as of 2021.
- More than half of electric car models on the market in 2021 were SUVs.
- Biofuels, not electricity, remain the largest renewable energy source within transport overall.
- Aviation, shipping and long-haul transport are the hardest sub-sectors to decarbonise, needing large infrastructure investment in renewable electricity-based fuels, hydrogen or biomethane.
