# Facts about Energy for Transport

## What is it

Transport is one of the largest single uses of energy in the global economy. **Energy demand for transport accounts for around a third of total final energy consumption worldwide**, according to [REN21's 2022 Global Status Report](https://www.ren21.net/gsr-2022/pages/keymessages/keymessages/#key-power). Even so, efforts to move that demand onto renewable energy are picking up only slowly.

## Why it matters

Where transport gets its energy, whether oil, electricity or alternative fuels, will decide a lot about whether the world meets its climate targets. Transport touches almost every part of daily life and global trade, so progress here (or the lack of it) has a big effect on overall emissions.

## Policy attention is lagging the scale of the problem

Transport uses a huge share of the world's energy, yet only 1 in 10 countries included specific measures to cut transport emissions in their Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), the climate plans each country submits under the Paris Agreement, according to REN21. The gap between the size of the problem and the policy aimed at it is one of the sector's main weaknesses.

## Electric vehicles: real progress, with a catch

2021 was a record year for electric cars: 6.6 million were sold, a 109% increase on the year before. That is real progress. Still, electric vehicles made up only around 1% of all vehicles on the road worldwide, which shows how long it takes to replace a whole fleet, even during a sales boom. Part of the gain is also lost to heavier, less efficient vehicles: more than half of the electric car models on sale in 2021 were SUVs. And for all the growth in EV sales, biofuels, rather than electricity, are still the largest source of renewable energy in transport overall. For more on why bigger EVs undercut their own climate benefit, see [Car Life Cycle Emissions: EV vs Combustion](Car%20Life%20Cycle%20Emissions%20-%20EV%20vs%20Combustion.md).

## The hard-to-decarbonise sub-sectors

Aviation, shipping and other long-haul transport are still the hardest parts of the sector to clean up. In principle they could run on renewable electricity, synthetic fuels made with renewable electricity, hydrogen, biofuels or biomethane. Making that switch at scale needs large investment in new infrastructure, and that investment has not happened yet. See [Long-Haul Shipping](Long-Haul%20Shipping.md) and [Decarbonising Aviation and Shipping](../../solutions/electrification/Decarbonising%20Aviation%20and%20Shipping.md) for more detail on how these sub-sectors could get there.

## Key facts

- Transport accounts for roughly a third of total final energy consumption globally (REN21, 2022).
- Only 1 in 10 countries set out specific transport emission reduction measures in their Paris Agreement NDCs.
- A record 6.6 million electric cars were sold in 2021, up 109% on the previous year.
- Electric vehicles still made up only around 1% of the global vehicle fleet as of 2021.
- More than half of electric car models on the market in 2021 were SUVs.
- Biofuels, not electricity, remain the largest renewable energy source within transport overall.
- Aviation, shipping and long-haul transport are the hardest sub-sectors to decarbonise, needing large infrastructure investment in renewable electricity-based fuels, hydrogen or biomethane.
