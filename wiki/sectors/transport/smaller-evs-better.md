# Smaller EVs Are Better — Why Bigger Batteries Undermine the Transition

## What is it

Electrification is essential for decarbonising transport. But inside that success story there is a quieter and awkward pattern: **the EVs on offer are getting bigger, fast**. In 2023, **two-thirds of battery-electric models on the major EV markets were SUVs, pickups or large cars**, up from **one-third in 2017**. Only about a quarter of new EV models expected through 2028 will be small or medium sized. Bigger electric vehicles need bigger batteries, more critical minerals, more energy per kilometre, and take up more space in the same cities. They are also more dangerous to people walking and cycling around them. This page draws on a **WRI analysis by Yiqian Zhang-Billert, Sarah Cassius and Cristina Albuquerque** to make the case that **smaller EVs, not just any EVs, are what the transition needs.**

## Why it matters

Every large EV that replaces a small ICE car cuts tailpipe emissions — that is real progress. But every large EV that replaces what could have been a small EV **doubles or triples the battery**, pulls harder on lithium, cobalt and nickel supply, and consumes up to **20% more electricity per mile**. Multiplied across a market moving at scale, that turns into a **mineral-demand and grid-capacity problem the transition does not need**. It also turns city streets into places where a 45%-higher risk of fatal pedestrian collision meets communities already dealing with congestion and heat.

## The size trend, in numbers

- **Two-thirds of BEV models in 2023** were SUVs, pickups or large cars — up from **one-third in 2017**
- **Europe**: fewer than **40% of BEVs** are small or medium; **US: only 25%**; **China: 50%**
- **European ICE trend mirrors this**: SUV sales tripled since 2011; small car sales halved
- Only about **one-quarter** of new EV models expected through 2028 will be small or medium

## What bigger EVs actually cost

- **Energy: up to 20% more electricity** per vehicle, straining grid capacity and increasing lifecycle emissions
- **Critical minerals**: prioritising smaller EVs in Europe could **cut battery-mineral demand by 25%** ([Transport & Environment](https://www.transportenvironment.org)). The IEA finds targeted large-vehicle demand curbs could cut **global mineral demand for EV batteries by 18–20% by 2050**
- **Safety**: larger vehicles raise fatal pedestrian collision risk by **45%**; every extra 300 kg of vehicle mass raises fatal injury risk for cyclists, motorcyclists and pedestrians by **30%** (European study)
- **City space**: SUVs and pickups take more parking, contribute more to congestion, and are less compatible with dense urban centres

## Why the trend keeps growing

- **Consumer preferences**: buyers associate larger vehicles with safety, space and versatility, even when smaller models suit daily use better
- **Model availability**: manufacturers cater to that preference, so smaller models are underrepresented on showroom floors
- **US tax credits** of up to $7,500 are often more advantageous for electric SUVs
- **EU fleet-wide CO₂ targets** allow manufacturers to offset heavier vehicles with cleaner smaller ones — this was designed to lower compliance costs but effectively subsidises larger-vehicle production

## The mismatch with actual travel

- **Average daily travel distance in Europe: 12.4 km per person**
- The smallest EV models typically have a range of **at least 135 km**
- European cities are compact and densely populated; historic centres, narrow streets and mixed-use zoning **naturally discourage larger vehicles**
- Range anxiety is a poor argument for buying a large SUV when the actual journey is a school run

## Policy tools that are working

- **France**: **weight-based tax** on ICE and plug-in hybrid vehicles over **1,600 kg**
- **City of Paris**: **tripled parking fees for SUVs**
- **Norway**: EV purchase taxes based on **weight and emissions** — larger, high-emission models cost significantly more
- **>320 European cities** have introduced **low-emission zones** restricting the largest, most polluting vehicles from city centres
- **China**: BYD, Wuling and others produce budget-friendly subcompacts (**BYD Seagull, Wuling Bingo** — both priced under €10,000) that are entering Europe
- **VW, Renault, Stellantis** are now rolling out sub-€25,000 smaller EVs in response

## The bigger point

Even the perfect smaller EV rollout does not solve the wider problem of car dependency itself. Smaller EVs **reduce energy use, cut mineral demand and improve street safety** — but they still create congestion, urban sprawl and induced demand. In cities, sustainable urban mobility will require a broader shift toward **public transport, cycling and walking**. Smaller EVs have an important role in general car use, but they need to **complement, not replace**, efforts to reduce overall reliance on cars.

## Key facts

- SUVs, pickups and large cars: **1/3 of BEV models in 2017 → 2/3 in 2023**
- Larger EVs consume up to **20% more electricity** than smaller EVs
- Prioritising smaller EVs in Europe could **cut critical-mineral demand by 25%**
- Larger vehicles raise fatal pedestrian collision risk by **45%**
- Average European daily travel: **12.4 km/person** — well within compact EV range
- **>320 European cities** now have low-emission zones
