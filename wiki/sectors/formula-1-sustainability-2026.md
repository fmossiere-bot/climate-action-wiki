# Formula 1: On Track for Net Zero by 2030

## What is it

Formula 1 has committed to reaching net zero carbon by 2030. Its annual sustainability review for the 2025 season shows real, measurable progress. **Total CO₂ emissions have fallen 35% from the 2018 baseline**, from 228,793 tonnes to 148,805 tonnes, including a 12% drop in a single year between 2024 and 2025. The figures cover F1's own operations as well as the factories, operations and travel of all ten teams.

## Why it matters

Sport as a whole has a sizeable carbon footprint. Formula 1, which travels the world to race on five continents, is one of the most complicated sports to move around. F1 is showing that even a sport with this much travel and infrastructure can cut its emissions a lot by reorganising how it works, while keeping the show. That could be useful evidence for other sports and for large businesses that travel.

## Biggest emissions levers

Most of the cuts have come from three areas: logistics, facilities and fuel.

**Logistics** is the biggest variable. Moving freight from air to sea, and grouping races by region so equipment and people travel shorter distances between venues, has cut logistics emissions by 21% year on year and 29% since 2018. For example, holding the Japanese, Australian and Chinese rounds together in spring, instead of spreading them across the calendar, avoids needless long-haul freight. One calendar change cut logistics emissions for a run of races by almost 3%.

**Factories and facilities** have seen the biggest drop: 64% lower than in 2018 and 14% lower than the year before. This comes from buying renewable energy and making team headquarters more energy efficient.

**Sustainable fuels** reached the races themselves in 2026. F1 now requires advanced sustainable fuels in competition, which produce 80% fewer greenhouse gas emissions than the fossil fuels they replace.

## What "net zero by 2030" actually means

F1's commitment requires emissions to fall by at least 50% in absolute terms compared with the 2018 baseline. Any emissions that cannot be avoided must be offset using "credible programmes aligned with best practice guidance." Emissions are calculated through an independent (third-party) reporting platform, to keep the figures accurate and transparent.

The sport is on track. It has achieved a 35% cut in seven years, with four years to go, so closing the remaining gap of 15 percentage points looks achievable if progress continues at the current rate.

## Key facts

- Total CO₂ emissions: 228,793 tonnes (2018) down to 148,805 tonnes (2025), a 35% reduction ([BBC Sport, 2026](https://www.bbc.com/sport/formula1/articles/cvgjp4rj2p5o))
- 12% reduction in a single year (2024 to 2025)
- Factory and facilities emissions down 64% since 2018
- Logistics emissions down 21% year on year, 29% since 2018
- From 2026: sustainable fuels mandatory in races, cutting greenhouse gas emissions from race fuel by 80%
- Net zero target means at least a 50% absolute reduction compared with 2018, with remaining unavoidable emissions offset
