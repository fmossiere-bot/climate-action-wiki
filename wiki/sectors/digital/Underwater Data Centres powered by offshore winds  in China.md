## What is it

In May 2026, China switched on the world's first commercial **underwater data centre (UDC)**, off the coast of Shanghai. It was built by a subsidiary of China Communications Construction and runs mainly on offshore wind. It is a new way of dealing with the land, water and energy problems that ordinary data centres create.

The facility holds 192 server racks across four levels and sits on the seabed in the Lin-gang Special Area. It uses the surrounding sea to carry away heat, so it needs no freshwater for cooling at all.

## Why it matters

Data centres are among the fastest-growing users of electricity and water. Most of the world's 8,808+ data centres are on land, in places chosen for good connections and power supply rather than for environmental efficiency. Going underwater changes several things at once: this facility uses over 90% less land, uses no freshwater, and gets most of its power from offshore wind.

It also arrives just as data centres in the UK and Ireland are turning to fossil gas because the grid cannot supply power fast enough. The UDC model points to another way.

## How it works

The facility uses a **circulating copper-pipe heat exchange system**. Heat passes from the servers into the surrounding seawater without the two ever mixing. This reportedly cuts the electricity used for cooling by 22.8% compared with conventional systems. Professor Li Zhen of Tsinghua University estimates that an underwater facility of the same size would use roughly one-tenth of the cooling electricity of a land-based centre.

Offshore wind farms supply an estimated **95% of the electricity** the facility needs. It currently runs at 2.3 MW but has a planned capacity of 24 MW, enough to power around 20,000 homes. That spare room is meant to allow future hardware upgrades without building new infrastructure.

## What remains uncertain

The UDC idea has not yet been tested at full commercial scale. Two questions are still open:

- Ecological impact. Releasing heat into the local sea all the time could change water temperature and affect marine life. Research on this is at an early stage.
- Maintenance and lifespan. Repairs are much harder under water than on land, and nobody yet knows how these facilities will hold up over years of use.

## Key facts

- World's first commercial underwater data centre, off Shanghai, running since May 2026.
- Uses **seawater cooling**, with no freshwater needed.
- Uses **more than 90%** less land than centres above ground.
- Copper-pipe heat exchange cuts cooling electricity by **22.8%**.
- **95% of power from offshore wind**.
- Current capacity: **2.3 MW**. Planned capacity: **24 MW**.
- Possible global saving if the idea scaled up: **50 billion kWh a year** of cooling electricity.
- Questions remain about the effects on marine life. ([New Atlas, 2026](https://newatlas.com/energy/china-underwater-data-center-opens/))
