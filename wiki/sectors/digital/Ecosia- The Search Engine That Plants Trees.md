**What is Ecosia?**

Ecosia is a search engine based in Berlin, founded in 2009 by Christian Kroll. Its aim is simple: use the money it makes from online searches to plant trees around the world. Rather than keeping its profits, Ecosia puts all of them into reforestation projects. It also runs its searches on renewable energy and does not track or sell user data.

It works like Google or Bing. You type a search and you get results. The difference is what happens to the money your search earns.

---

**How does it actually work?**

When you search on Ecosia, ads appear in the results, as they do on Google. Those ads bring in a small amount of money. Ecosia spends at least 80% of that advertising income on planting trees. You pay nothing: the advertisers fund the trees, through your clicks.

Ecosia plants a tree roughly every 0.8 seconds.

---

**Why is it greener than Google?**

There are two main reasons.

The first is energy. Ecosia runs its search servers on renewable sources such as wind and solar power, which gives it a smaller environmental footprint than search engines that still rely on more carbon-heavy energy.

The second is impact. Unlike Google, Ecosia describes itself as carbon negative, which means it makes up for more CO₂ than it emits. It says its own solar plants produce about twice the electricity its searches use, and its trees take more CO₂ out of the air. These are Ecosia's own figures and have not been independently checked.

One thing worth knowing: the search results you see on Ecosia come from Microsoft Bing, not from Ecosia's own index. So Ecosia does not produce its own web results independently. The money still goes to the trees, though.

On privacy, Google builds user profiles to target ads. Ecosia does not track individual users, sell information to third parties or keep permanent search histories.

---

**Where do they plant trees?**

Ecosia has funded tree planting in over 35 countries. It focuses on places where deforestation does the most damage, and where trees can do the most for local ecosystems and communities.

Key regions include Nicaragua and Peru, Burkina Faso and Malawi, Indonesia and Australia, and Madagascar, where Ecosia built a tree nursery for 200,000 trees in 2018.

More recently it has expanded into Togo, Vietnam and the Netherlands. In October 2025, the top five countries receiving funding were Madagascar, Brazil, Togo, Uganda and Mali.

Ecosia works with over 200,000 tree planters and 125 organisations worldwide, and focuses on native species, including many that are endangered or vulnerable.

---

**Progress so far**

Ecosia recently planted its **250 millionth tree**, making it the world's largest planter of native trees.

In 2024 alone, Ecosia planted and protected 18.4 million trees across around 1,600 different species.

To stay transparent, Ecosia publishes a detailed monthly financial report showing its income, its costs and how much money went to tree planting.

---

**Can I use it?**

Yes. Ecosia is available as a browser extension for Chrome, Firefox and Safari, and as mobile apps for iOS and Android. In April 2024, it also launched its own desktop browser, built on Chromium.

Switching takes about two minutes, and searching feels very close to what you are used to. It is one of the easiest climate actions you can take.

## Going Further: The Campaign for a Climate Nobel Prize

In December 2025, Ecosia's CEO Christian Kroll went beyond tree-planting. He went to a Berlin notary and committed **€1 million** as the starting endowment for a new Nobel Prize category: Climate and Planetary Health. He then wrote to the Nobel Foundation asking it to adopt the prize.

The Nobel Prize currently has six categories: Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economics. The most recent addition was Economics, in 1969. Kroll puts his case like this: "When Alfred Nobel created the prizes, he wrote that they must serve the greatest benefit to humankind. Today, this means protecting the planet we all depend on."

The campaign website is climatenobelprize.org. Ecosia proposes three areas the prize would recognise:

- **Pragmatic Governance**: smart, effective policies that speed up proven solutions
- **Scaling Prosperity and Markets**: innovations and business models that create sustainable prosperity at scale
- **Building Common Ground**: bringing different perspectives together to find shared, practical solutions

Ecosia would have no say over nominations or winners. The money is committed, and the Nobel Foundation decides. A symbolic blue chair was handmade and placed among the laureates' seats at the Nobel ceremony, waiting to be filled.

The campaign is backed by climate activist Luisa Neubauer and energy economist Dr Claudia Kemfert, who called a Climate Nobel "a global signal that climate protection secures the future and deserves the highest recognition."
