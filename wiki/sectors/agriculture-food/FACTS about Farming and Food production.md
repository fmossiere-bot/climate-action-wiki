# FACTS about Farming and Food Production

## What is it

Farming and the wider food system, once every stage is counted, are responsible for around a third of global greenhouse gas emissions. Meat makes up a large share of that. Livestock need a lot of land, their feed has to be grown, the animals themselves give off gases, and all of this takes a wider toll on the health of the soil. Climate change is already cutting yields, especially of wheat, and it is changing where crops can be grown at all. A [Guardian report on Europe's climate-driven drought crisis](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2022/aug/08/the-new-normal-how-europe-is-being-hit-by-a-climate-driven-drought-crisis) described how, in parts of Switzerland in 2022, mountain pastures were already too dry by summer, so herds had to be moved down to valley meadows normally kept for later in the year. Further afield, coffee farmers in Colombia are being pushed ever higher up the mountains to keep their crops alive (see [The Environmental Cost of Coffee](The%20Environmental%20Cost%20of%20Coffee.md)).

## The GHG footprint of the food supply chain

According to [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food), farming affects the environment in three main ways. It uses large amounts of fresh water, which puts pressure on regions that are already short of it, and the fertiliser running off fields pollutes rivers, lakes and seas. It is a major driver of climate change: food production is responsible for around a quarter (26%) of global greenhouse gas emissions, and the whole food system, including processing, transport, packaging and waste, for around a third. And it takes up an enormous amount of space: half of the world's habitable land (land that is not ice or desert) is used for farming.

A few more figures show the scale:

- Food production accounts for [26% of global greenhouse gas emissions](https://ourworldindata.org/food-ghg-emissions).
- [70% of global freshwater withdrawals](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food) are used for agriculture.
- [78% of global ocean and freshwater eutrophication](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food) is caused by agriculture. Eutrophication is when excess nutrients get into water, feed algae blooms and starve the water of oxygen.
- [94% of non-human mammal biomass](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food) on Earth is livestock. Weighed together, farm animals outweigh wild mammals by 15 to 1, and the share rises to 97% when only land mammals are counted.
- [71% of bird biomass](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food) is farmed poultry, which outweighs wild birds by more than 3 to 1.

## Most of what we grow feeds animals, not people

According to [National Geographic's analysis of feeding nine billion people](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/foodfeatures/feeding-9-billion/), only 55% of the world's crop calories feed people directly today. The rest go to livestock (about 36%) or are turned into biofuels and industrial products (roughly 9%). Turning feed into food is very wasteful. For every 100 calories of grain fed to animals, we get back only around 40 calories of milk, 22 of eggs, 12 of chicken, 10 of pork, or just 3 of beef, by far the lowest return of any major animal product. Feeding nine billion people would be much easier if more of the crops we grow went straight into human diets.

People in developing countries are unlikely to eat less meat soon, as they become better off. So the more immediate opportunity lies in countries that already eat a lot of meat. Moving from grain-fed beef to chicken, pork or grass-fed beef, producing meat more efficiently, and using fewer food crops for biofuels could all free up a lot of food. See [Why Eating Less Meat Is One of the Most Powerful Things You Can Do for the Climate](../../solutions/nature-based-solutions/Why%20Eating%20Less%20Meat%20Is%20One%20of%20the%20Most%20Powerful%20Things%20You%20Can%20Do%20for%20the%20Climate.md) for more.

## We need to cut food waste

An estimated 25% of the world's food calories, and up to 50% of total food weight, are lost or wasted before anyone eats them. In rich countries, most of that waste happens in homes, restaurants and supermarkets. In poorer countries, food is more often lost between the farm and the market, because storage and transport are unreliable. In wealthier countries, fairly simple steps would make a real difference: smaller portions, eating leftovers, and getting restaurants and supermarkets to waste less. Of all the ways to make more food available worldwide, cutting waste is one of the most effective. See [FACTS About Food Waste](../../circularity-waste/FACTS%20About%20Food%20Waste.md) for more detail.

## What about fish farming?

Fish farming is often assumed to be the greener alternative to catching wild fish, but the evidence is mixed. Research led by scientist Jennifer Hayek and colleagues, [reported by New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2452261-why-farming-fish-is-more-unsustainable-than-catching-them-in-the-wild/), found that the number of wild fish killed to feed farmed fish is between 27% and 307% higher than earlier estimates suggested. It takes roughly 4 to 5kg of wild-caught fish to produce just 1kg of farmed salmon, because farmed carnivorous fish eat several times more fish, by weight, than they end up providing as food. As fish farming grows, more and more of the world's wild catch goes to feed farmed fish instead of people. That is a serious problem when wild fish populations are already falling, and it also pushes up the price of fish for people in south-east Asia and other poorer regions who rely on it for food.

Switching to plant-based alternatives is not a simple fix either, because growing the substitute crops would need more land and could mean more deforestation. As Hayek put it, "you can't get away from impacts somewhere as long as you're eating animals. Farming animals requires more resources to raise and grow their bodies than you can get from eating them. That's a basic fact of biology."
