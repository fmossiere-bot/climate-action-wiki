# Is the Meat Industry Following the Oil Industry's Climate Playbook?

## What is it

A [study published in the journal *Climatic Change*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-024-03690-w) finds that, much like the fossil fuel industry before it, the animal agriculture industry has been [creating and funding academic research institutions](https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-environment/2024/03/08/livestock-industry-cows-climate-impact/) that argue the world does not need to consume less meat and dairy to preserve a safe, liveable climate. Independent, non-industry-funded research says the opposite: [Oxford University research covered by the New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/07/21/climate/diet-vegan-meat-emissions.html) finds that people who follow a plant-based diet have substantially lower food-related emissions than those who eat a lot of meat.

## Why it matters

The IPCC, whose findings are compiled by hundreds of climate scientists and signed off on by 195 countries, has [warned](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2018/02/ar4-wg3-chapter8-1.pdf) that a strategy of "technical fixes only" for animal agriculture is not moving fast enough to prevent climate catastrophe. Despite "significant technical potential" for reducing emissions from animal agriculture, the IPCC says "there is evidence that little progress has been made in the implementation of mitigation measures at the global scale." This matters because, even if fossil fuel emissions were eliminated immediately, [emissions from agriculture alone](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aba7357) would still be enough to push global warming past safe levels, and meat and dairy [make up most of agriculture's emissions](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/23738600/un-fao-meat-dairy-livestock-emissions-methane-climate-change).

This is why a growing number of scientists and activists argue that [wealthy nations need to reduce their meat and dairy consumption](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2022/4/22/23036010/eat-less-meat-vegetarian-effects-climate-emissions-animal-welfare-factory-farms). The IPCC itself reportedly wanted to say as much in its most recent assessment report, but the [animal agriculture industry successfully lobbied for that language to be removed](https://qz.com/ipcc-report-on-climate-change-meat-industry-1850261179). Ireland offers a local example of the same dynamic: an [Irish Times opinion piece from March 2024](https://www.irishtimes.com/opinion/2024/03/12/why-is-climate-misinformation-going-unchallenged-among-farmers/) noted growing climate disinformation being spread among some farmers, echoing the same "no need to change" framing found in the wider industry-funded research.

## The case for litigation, not imitation

It is perhaps understandable, from a narrow business perspective, that parts of the meat industry would choose the route of scientific obfuscation: it is what the fossil fuel industry did for decades, and doing so allowed it to keep growing and profiting despite clear scientific warnings. But Jennifer Jacquet, a professor of environmental science and policy at the University of Miami and a co-author of the *Climatic Change* study, argues there is a more lucrative and creative option available to animal agriculture: suing the fossil fuel industry for damages. Her reasoning is that meat and dairy would not be under this pressure at all if fossil fuel companies had acted on climate science decades ago. "Meat and dairy should be suing oil and gas," Jacquet has said. "Because part of the reason that we're in this position is because we did nothing about oil and gas."

Jacquet is the author of two related books: *The Playbook*, which examines how corporations lie about climate change, and *Is Shame Necessary?*, about using public shame to push corporations toward better environmental policy.
