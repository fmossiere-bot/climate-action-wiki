# Is the Meat Industry Following the Oil Industry's Climate Playbook?

## What is it

A [study published in the journal *Climatic Change*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-024-03690-w) finds that the animal farming industry has been doing what the fossil fuel industry did before it. It has been [setting up and funding academic research centres](https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-environment/2024/03/08/livestock-industry-cows-climate-impact/) that argue the world does not need to eat less meat and dairy to keep the climate safe and liveable. Independent research, not funded by the industry, says the opposite. [Oxford University research covered by the New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/07/21/climate/diet-vegan-meat-emissions.html) found that people on a plant-based diet have much lower food emissions than people who eat a lot of meat.

## Why it matters

The IPCC, whose reports are written by hundreds of climate scientists and approved by 195 countries, has [warned](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2018/02/ar4-wg3-chapter8-1.pdf) that relying on "technical fixes only" in animal farming is not cutting emissions fast enough to avoid climate catastrophe. Despite "significant technical potential" to cut emissions from animal farming, the IPCC says "there is evidence that little progress has been made in the implementation of mitigation measures at the global scale." This matters because, even if fossil fuel emissions stopped tomorrow, [emissions from agriculture alone](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aba7357) would still be enough to push global warming past safe levels. Meat and dairy [make up most of agriculture's emissions](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/23738600/un-fao-meat-dairy-livestock-emissions-methane-climate-change).

That is why more and more scientists and campaigners argue that [wealthy countries need to eat less meat and dairy](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2022/4/22/23036010/eat-less-meat-vegetarian-effects-climate-emissions-animal-welfare-factory-farms). The IPCC reportedly wanted to say this in its latest assessment report, but the [animal farming industry successfully lobbied to have that wording removed](https://qz.com/ipcc-report-on-climate-change-meat-industry-1850261179). Ireland has its own example. An [Irish Times opinion piece from March 2024](https://www.irishtimes.com/opinion/2024/03/12/why-is-climate-misinformation-going-unchallenged-among-farmers/) described climate disinformation spreading among some farmers, repeating the same "no need to change" message found in the industry-funded research.

## The case for litigation, not imitation

From a narrow business point of view, it is perhaps understandable that parts of the meat industry would choose to muddy the science. The fossil fuel industry did it for decades, and it let them keep growing and making money despite clear scientific warnings. Jennifer Jacquet, a professor of environmental science and policy at the University of Miami and a co-author of the *Climatic Change* study, suggests a more profitable and creative option for animal farming: suing the fossil fuel industry for damages. Her reasoning is that meat and dairy would not be under this pressure at all if oil and gas companies had acted on climate science decades ago. "Meat and dairy should be suing oil and gas," Jacquet has said. "Because part of the reason that we're in this position is because we did nothing about oil and gas."

Jacquet has written two related books. *The Playbook* looks at how corporations lie about climate change, and *Is Shame Necessary?* is about using public shame to push companies towards better environmental behaviour.
