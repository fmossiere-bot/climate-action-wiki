# The 'Recycled' Plastic That Isn't: Mass-Balance Accounting and the Pyrolysis Loophole

## What is it

Walk down any European supermarket aisle and you will see brands proudly labelling their plastic packaging as "recycled," "circular" or "climate-friendly." A **January 2026 cross-border investigation led by The Guardian's Stefano Valentino** (with Voxeurop, Mediapart, Altreconomia, Público, Deutsche Welle and others under the IJ4EU banner) found that in many of the biggest brands — including **Kraft's Heinz Beanz** and **Mondelēz's Philadelphia** — those labels rest on **accounting rules, not chemistry**. The plastic in your hand is almost entirely still made from petroleum.

Two techniques do the heavy lifting: **mass-balance bookkeeping** and **avoided emissions**. Both are lawful today, both are heavily backed by the petrochemical industry, and both are about to be legalised further by the **EU's PPWR rules coming into effect in 2026** and by the **UK plastic packaging tax rules taking effect in 2027**.

## Why it matters

Mandatory recycled-content targets are one of Europe's main policy levers against plastic pollution and to cut virgin-oil demand. If a company can hit those targets on paper without physically incorporating recycled material, the policy stops delivering the shift it was designed for. The IEA's *World Energy Outlook 2025* now sees plastic becoming a **critical growth engine for oil majors' future profits** as demand for oil-as-fuel declines. In other words: the loophole is not accidental. It is the mechanism by which the fossil fuel industry stays large.

## How the pyrolysis pipeline actually works

Most "chemical recycling" schemes running at scale use **pyrolysis** — heating plastic waste in the absence of oxygen to produce a hazardous liquid called **pyrolysis oil**. The technical constraint is unforgiving: pyrolysis oil can make up **at most 5% of feedstock**, and the remaining **95% has to be virgin naphtha** (a petroleum derivative) or the steam-cracking plants downstream get damaged.

**Helmut Maurer**, a former senior expert in the European Commission's environment department, sums it up bluntly: *"The whole process is labelled as plastic recycling, while fossil fuel use expands because virgin feedstock must be added."*

## Trick one: mass-balance bookkeeping

Mass balance is the accounting rule that lets a company assign the recycled input to specific output batches. Imagine 100 tonnes of plastic where 5 tonnes worth of pyrolysis oil (diluted in 95 tonnes of naphtha) was fed in. Under mass-balance rules, the producer can pick any 5 tonnes of the final product and certify it as "100% recycled" — even though those 5 tonnes physically contain only fossil feedstock.

**Lauriane Veillard**, policy officer at NGO **Zero Waste Europe**: *"This is unfair to consumers — recycled content should be physically part of the final product."*

The certificates that make this legal come from the **industry-led International Sustainability and Carbon Certification (ISCC)** platform, and travel down the supply chain to consumer brands.

## Trick two: avoided emissions

The **carbon accounting** side of the story uses **life-cycle assessments (LCAs)**. Sabic's own LCA admits that the whole pyrolysis-to-cracking process **emits 6 to 8% more than making plastic from fossil fuel in the first place**. The net numbers only turn positive when the accounting **credits the emissions that would have been released if the equivalent volume of waste had been incinerated instead** — creating "avoided emissions."

That single accounting move flips a slightly worse process into a headline number of "about 2 kg less CO₂ per kg of recycled plastic." As Maurer says: *"What matters is not hypothetical emissions from incineration that are 'avoided' on paper, but what is actually emitted in reality."*

Sabic's LCA claims a "rigorous critical review" by experts including the co-founder of **Plastic Energy**, which is Sabic's main feedstock supplier — a business relationship that raises obvious impartiality questions. Sabic and Plastic Energy declined to disclose full LCAs to the investigation. The named brands did not respond.

Peter Quicker, professor of emission control at Aachen University, is blunter: *"LCA documents serve no purpose other than advertising, because companies control the parameters to achieve desired results."*

## Who benefits

The story sits inside a bigger corporate ownership picture. The plastic manufacturing arm behind many of these products is **Sabic**, a **subsidiary of Saudi Aramco** — the world's largest corporate greenhouse gas emitter, at over 70 million tonnes CO₂e up to 2023, and one of the loudest opponents of production cuts under the UN plastic treaty. Public records suggest **Sabic used 2,600 tonnes of pyrolysis oil in 2022** against roughly **4 million tonnes of virgin naphtha** at its Netherlands cracking plants. Recycled content is a rounding error.

Behind the scenes, petrochemical firms have intensified lobbying of EU institutions to ensure the upcoming rules accommodate mass-balance accounting, and have rushed to sign offtake deals with pyrolysis oil suppliers. So the timing lines up: as the regulatory train pulls in, the industry sits ready with pre-built compliance paths that require **no material change in what actually goes into the plastic**.

## Key facts

- Pyrolysis oil = **at most 5% of feedstock**; **95% must be virgin naphtha**
- Sabic's own LCA: the pyrolysis-to-cracking process **emits 6–8% more than fossil plastic**; only "avoided incineration" credit flips the number positive
- Sabic 2022: **2,600 tonnes of pyrolysis oil** vs **~4 million tonnes of naphtha** at its Dutch plants
- **EU PPWR** rules that accommodate mass balance take effect in **2026**; **UK plastic packaging tax mass-balance rules** in **2027**
- Certificates issued by industry-led **ISCC**
- **Saudi Aramco** (Sabic's parent) is the world's largest corporate GHG emitter and opposes UN plastic-treaty production cuts
- IEA *World Energy Outlook 2025*: plastic set to become a critical growth engine for oil majors' future profits
- Named consumer brands: **Kraft Heinz Beanz, Mondelēz Philadelphia** — did not respond to the investigation

## What good looks like

Two contrasting benchmarks:

- **Physical recycled content**: recycled material is actually and traceably present in the final product, e.g. **[Seaqual ocean plastic yarn](seaqual-ocean-plastic-fashion.md)** using 10% upcycled marine plastic + 90% land-based rPET, with an LCA showing 37% GWP reduction and third-party certification
- **Genuine chemical recyclability**: bio-based composites like the new [wood-based epoxy/polyester resins](../sectors/industry/bio-based-resins-composites.md) from the University of Oulu, which are designed to be chemically broken down into their original raw materials
