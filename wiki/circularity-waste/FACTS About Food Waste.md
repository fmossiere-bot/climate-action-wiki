###### 1/3 of food is wasted, along with the water, energy, fertilizer and labor used to produce it. 

> Low-Income regions must improve storage, processing, and transport. High-Income regions meed major retails & consumer interventions. This reduces methane emissions from decomposing food and deforestation for new farmland

Roughly one-third of all food produced worldwide is wasted. Where income is low, waste is generally unintentional and occurs on farms or during storage or distribution. In regions of higher income, food waste dominates further along the supply chain. Retailers and consumers reject food based on bumps, bruises, and coloring, or simply order, buy, and serve too much.

When food is wasted, all the energy, resources, and money that went into producing, processing, packaging, and transporting it are wasted, too.  Producing uneaten food squanders a whole host of resources—seeds, water, energy, land, fertilizer, hours of labor, financial capital—and generates greenhouse gases at every stage. 

###### The food we waste is responsible for roughly 8 percent of global emissions.
#### GOAL: 50% food waste reduction
###### "If food waste was a country, it would be the 3rd largest emitter. 87% of the impact comes from preventing deforestation for new farmland"


> 
> ## Reducing Food-waste provide multiple benefits
> - Reducing food waste can improve nutrition and reduce food insecurity by keeping more food available and accessible for human consumption.
> - Improved nutrition and food security are associated with improved cardiovascular health, reduced obesity, sufficient nutrient consumption, and improved mental health.
> - Using fewer resources to produce food has the co-benefits of saving land and resources for other uses and reducing emissions generated from food production.

###### In Ireland, we know that Waste in general is responsible for 1.4% of all GHG emissions. 

> Project Drawdown ranks Reducing Food Loss and Waste as a highly recommended climate solution. Over a third of food produced is lost or wasted before it's eaten, wasting the emissions used to grow, process and transport it too, around 4.9 Gt CO2-eq per year. Losses happen early in the supply chain (production, storage, processing), while waste happens later (distribution, retail, consumer). Fixes include better storage and packaging, smarter retail inventory and forecasting, food donation, clearer date labels, and better consumer habits like meal planning. The right fix depends on the food, the supply chain stage and the location. Waste disposal itself is covered by other Drawdown solutions.

###### The EPA estimates that Ireland generated 753,000 tonnes of food waste in 2021, and the government has agreed to reduce food waste by 50% by 2030 (https://www.epa.ie/our-services/monitoring--assessment/waste/national-waste-statistics/food/)

### Is it possible?
>  Is it possible to eliminate food waste through composting and recycling? South Korea says yes. They’ve virtually eliminated [food waste](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=14b07a1f6b&e=9ea8d6ef4b) thanks to a compulsory curbside composting program.
> - In Geneva, a [nonprofit called Free-Go](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=e1be2e3de6&e=9ea8d6ef4b) is putting fridges on streets that members of the public can use to share food from their own homes and commercial kitchens that might otherwise go to waste.
> - In the U.S., for-profit companies like [Imperfect Foods](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=1fdd42c948&e=9ea8d6ef4b) and [Ugly Food](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=a40db6a17b&e=9ea8d6ef4b) allow consumers to make sure they are buying perfectly good food that would otherwise be thrown out because it doesn’t look quite right. In Canada, non-profits like [Food Stash](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=23c60b970b&e=9ea8d6ef4b) in Vancouver, [Rescue Food](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=924af51634&e=9ea8d6ef4b) in the Prairies, and [Second Harvest](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=da86e0ac75&e=9ea8d6ef4b) across the country collect and redistribute food free of charge that would otherwise go to a landfill.
> - You can compost your produce scraps, either at your own home or through a composting service like [Turn Compost](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=2e29666d9c&e=9ea8d6ef4b), which serves Dallas-Fort Worth, where I live. You can also download an app called [Too Good To Go](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=d58eb10b50&e=9ea8d6ef4b) that matches consumers concerned about cost and climate with restaurants that have surplus food to sell.
> - And if you live in the UK, you can use [this website](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=a76a6beae5&e=9ea8d6ef4b) to locate a local “gleaning” group, which connects volunteers with local farmers to help harvest excess fruits and vegetables that would otherwise not be harvested.
> - https://www.toogoodtogo.com/en-us
