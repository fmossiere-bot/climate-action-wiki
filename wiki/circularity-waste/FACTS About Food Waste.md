###### 1/3 of food is wasted, along with the water, energy, fertiliser and labour used to produce it. 

> Low-income regions need to improve storage, processing and transport. High-income regions need major changes from retailers and consumers. Both cut methane emissions from rotting food and reduce the deforestation that clears land for new farms.

Roughly one-third of all food produced worldwide is wasted. In low-income regions, waste is usually accidental and happens on farms or during storage and distribution. In higher-income regions, most waste happens further along the supply chain. Shops and shoppers reject food because of bumps, bruises and colour, or simply order, buy and serve too much.

When food is wasted, all the energy, resources and money that went into growing, processing, packaging and transporting it are wasted too. Producing food that nobody eats uses up a long list of resources (seeds, water, energy, land, fertiliser, hours of work, money) and releases greenhouse gases at every stage. 

###### The food we waste is responsible for roughly 8 percent of global emissions.
#### GOAL: 50% food waste reduction
###### "If food waste was a country, it would be the 3rd largest emitter. 87% of the impact comes from preventing deforestation for new farmland"


> 
> ## Reducing food waste has several benefits
> - Wasting less food can improve nutrition and reduce food insecurity, because more food stays available and affordable for people to eat.
> - Better nutrition and food security are linked to better heart health, less obesity, getting enough nutrients, and better mental health.
> - Using fewer resources to produce food frees up land and resources for other uses and cuts the emissions from food production.

## Global food systems are responsible for 34% of global GHG emissions
### Agriculture and related land use are responsible for 24% of global GHG emissions
###### In Ireland, the share is even bigger: **38.4%** 

- Much of Irish farming is driven by meat production, and most of it is exported, with China one of the major importers of meat products.

This has many knock-on effects, and it is why farming is at the centre of climate action.

## 1/3 of all food produced goes to waste

This matters, because wasted food is:
- not feeding people who need it {include latest hunger data}
- generating methane when it is sent to landfill
- generating CO2 when it is incinerated


###### In the US, 50% of food waste ends up in landfill, where it produces greenhouse gases equal to those of 12 million cars.
- Methane from US landfills makes up 15% of US methane emissions
- The US composting rate is 38%, well below the EU's (57%)
###### In Ireland, waste as a whole is responsible for 1.4% of all GHG emissions. 

> Project Drawdown ranks reducing food loss and waste as a highly recommended climate solution. Over a third of food produced is lost or wasted before it is eaten, which also wastes the emissions from growing, processing and transporting it: around 4.9 Gt CO2-eq per year. Losses happen early in the supply chain (production, storage, processing), while waste happens later (distribution, retail, consumers). Fixes include better storage and packaging, smarter stock management and forecasting in shops, food donation, clearer date labels, and better habits at home such as meal planning. The right fix depends on the food, the stage of the supply chain and the location. Disposing of waste is covered by other Drawdown solutions.

###### The EPA estimates that Ireland produced 753,000 tonnes of food waste in 2021, and the government has agreed to cut food waste by 50% by 2030 (https://www.epa.ie/our-services/monitoring--assessment/waste/national-waste-statistics/food/)

### Is it possible?
>  Is it possible to eliminate food waste through composting and recycling? South Korea says yes. They’ve virtually eliminated [food waste](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=14b07a1f6b&e=9ea8d6ef4b) thanks to a compulsory curbside composting program.
> - In Geneva, a [nonprofit called Free-Go](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=e1be2e3de6&e=9ea8d6ef4b) is putting fridges on streets that members of the public can use to share food from their own homes and commercial kitchens that might otherwise go to waste.
> - In the U.S., for-profit companies like [Imperfect Foods](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=1fdd42c948&e=9ea8d6ef4b) and [Ugly Food](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=a40db6a17b&e=9ea8d6ef4b) allow consumers to make sure they are buying perfectly good food that would otherwise be thrown out because it doesn’t look quite right. In Canada, non-profits like [Food Stash](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=23c60b970b&e=9ea8d6ef4b) in Vancouver, [Rescue Food](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=924af51634&e=9ea8d6ef4b) in the Prairies, and [Second Harvest](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=da86e0ac75&e=9ea8d6ef4b) across the country collect and redistribute food free of charge that would otherwise go to a landfill.
> - You can compost your produce scraps, either at your own home or through a composting service like [Turn Compost](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=2e29666d9c&e=9ea8d6ef4b), which serves Dallas-Fort Worth, where I live. You can also download an app called [Too Good To Go](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=d58eb10b50&e=9ea8d6ef4b) that matches consumers concerned about cost and climate with restaurants that have surplus food to sell.
> - And if you live in the UK, you can use [this website](https://katharinehayhoe.us14.list-manage.com/track/click?u=fa37a090438ee00d891b2a776&id=a76a6beae5&e=9ea8d6ef4b) to locate a local “gleaning” group, which connects volunteers with local farmers to help harvest excess fruits and vegetables that would otherwise not be harvested.
> - https://www.toogoodtogo.com/en-us
