> 6.6% of the household Carbon Footprint is linked to the furniture, painting and so on that we use inside our houses

### What is it

When we think about our carbon footprint at home, we usually think of heating, electricity or the car. A less obvious part comes from what is inside our homes: the furniture, the paint on the walls, the floor under our feet, the cushions and curtains. All of these things have to be made, transported and eventually thrown away, and each step has an environmental cost.

Around **6.6% of a household's total carbon footprint** comes from furniture, decoration and home fittings. That may sound small, but it adds up across millions of homes. Unlike energy use, which you can cut by switching tariff or improving insulation, this part of your footprint is usually invisible.

It is harder to see because many of these products contain plastic, often more than people realise. Around 90% of all plastics are made from fossil fuels, and plastic turns up well beyond packaging: in sofas, paint, flooring, shampoo bottles and synthetic textiles. Choosing natural or second-hand alternatives is one of the simplest ways to reduce that hidden impact.

---

### Why it matters

Most people do not think twice about buying a new sofa or repainting a room. But the materials in everyday home products, such as synthetic paints, particle board, foam cushions and plastic flooring, often carry hidden costs. Some release chemicals into the air inside your home. Others travel thousands of kilometres before they reach you.

Alternatives are becoming more common. The European bio-composite materials market (materials made partly from plant fibres such as wood) was worth around **$1.86 billion in 2023** and is expected to keep growing through 2029, helped by EU rules that push towards a circular economy. Eco-friendly paints, natural fibre flooring and plant-based cleaning products are no longer niche. They are easier to find and more affordable than they used to be.

In France, for example, second-hand furniture markets have grown a lot, and more manufacturers now use natural or recycled materials instead of plastics or synthetic foams. If you want to reduce your impact without changing your whole lifestyle, this is a good place to start. You do not have to redo your home all at once. Small steps, like buying second-hand, choosing a natural paint or replacing a synthetic rug with a wool one, add up over time.

---

### Key facts

- Around 6.6% of a household's carbon footprint is linked to furniture, decoration and home fittings.
- Around 90% of all plastics are made from fossil fuel derivatives, and plastic is found in many home products beyond packaging, including furniture, flooring and personal care items.
- In Europe, wood fibre is now the main bio-based composite material, making up 58.7% of the European biocomposites market in 2024.
- The European bio-composite materials market is growing by around 11% a year, partly because of EU circular economy policy.
- Eco-friendly paints, such as clay or lime-based products, can greatly reduce volatile organic compounds (VOCs, chemicals that evaporate into the air) inside the home, a common source of indoor air pollution.
- In France, the second-hand furniture market has grown fast, with platforms such as Leboncoin, Vinted and Selency reporting strong annual growth.
