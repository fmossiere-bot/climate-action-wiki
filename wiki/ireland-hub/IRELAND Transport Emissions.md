# Ireland Transport Emissions

## What is it

Transport is Ireland's second-largest source of greenhouse gas emissions after agriculture, accounting for around 20% of the state's overall emissions. Unlike some other sectors, transport emissions in Ireland are still growing rather than falling: according to the EPA's most recent annual report, transport emissions rose by 6.7% in 2022, more than double the EU average increase of 2.7% for that year. This makes Ireland's targets of halving carbon output by 2030 and reaching net zero by 2050 look increasingly difficult without a significant course correction.

## Why it matters

Ireland is heavily dependent on private cars, and that dependency is proving hard to shift even as fuel prices hit record highs. Even during price spikes, demand for petrol and diesel remained stable, showing how few alternatives are available for most drivers. Electric vehicles will play a substantial role in decarbonising the sector, but experts stress that more needs to be done to make active travel and public transport safer and more viable options for citizens.

## Ireland Lags Behind the EU

Road transport accounts for 95% of transport emissions in Ireland, well above the EU average of around 75%. Ireland repeatedly ranks near the bottom of the EU for sustainable transport uptake. Rail is known to be far more sustainable than road, yet in 2022 just 2.6% of Irish railway lines were electrified, one of the lowest figures in Europe, compared with an EU average of around 57%.

Industry experts argue these figures show Ireland is not doing enough to meet its stated transport emissions targets and is not on track to reach them. Priorities identified include increasing active travel and public transport use, with more investment needed in the reliability and accessibility of public transport, as well as investment in sustainable goods transport, particularly rail and short sea shipping.

## 2026 Update: Fossil Fuel Dependence Leaves Ireland Exposed

The Climate Change Advisory Council (CCAC) 2026 Annual Review issued a stark warning: Ireland's dependence on fossil fuels in transport is leaving the country exposed to repeated fuel price shocks as global energy markets stay volatile.

**The CCAC's core recommendations:**
- Accelerated investment in public transport, active travel, EV charging infrastructure and grid capacity.
- Targeted financial support for those most exposed to fuel costs and least able to avoid them, including lower-income households and car-dependent areas without public transport alternatives.
- Wider deployment of real-time EV charging point mapping to give drivers confidence to switch.

**What is working:**
- Journeys across the Transport for Ireland (TFI) network rose 6% in 2025 compared with 2024.
- Local Link, Ireland's rural bus service, saw a 19% jump in journeys.
- The government's pilot EV scrappage scheme was welcomed.

**What is not enough:**
- Total public transport journeys were largely unchanged year on year, suggesting some routes are running at near-full capacity at peak times.
- The CCAC called for accelerated delivery of DART+ South West, Luas Finglas, and the NTA Park and Ride Investment Programme to add capacity.

CCAC Chair Alex White put it bluntly: "Fossil fuel shocks are not one-off events. Giving alternatives is the way to reduce exposure. That means sustained investment in public transport, a charging network people can rely on, and the grid capacity needed to support the switch to electric across cars, buses and commercial fleets. This transition also has to be fair."
