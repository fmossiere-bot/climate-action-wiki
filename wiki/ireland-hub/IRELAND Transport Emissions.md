# Ireland Transport Emissions

## What is it

Transport is Ireland's second-largest source of greenhouse gas emissions after agriculture, making up **22.0%** of the state's total in 2025, according to the EPA's provisional figures, published in July 2026. After rising in earlier years, transport emissions decreased slightly in 2025, by 1.5%, according to the same EPA publication. Even so, Ireland's targets of halving carbon output by 2030 and reaching net zero by 2050 remain hard to meet without a sustained change of course.

## Why it matters

Ireland relies heavily on private cars, and that habit is proving hard to change even with fuel prices at record highs. During price spikes, demand for petrol and diesel held steady, which shows how few alternatives most drivers have. Electric vehicles will do a lot of the work of cutting emissions from the sector. But experts say more is needed to make walking, cycling and public transport safer and more practical for people.

## Ireland Lags Behind the EU

Road transport accounts for **95% of transport emissions** in Ireland, well above the EU average of around 75%. Ireland regularly ranks near the bottom of the EU for sustainable transport. Rail is far cleaner than road, yet in 2022 just 2.6% of Irish railway lines were electrified, one of the lowest figures in Europe, against an EU average of around 57%.

Industry experts say these figures show Ireland is not doing enough and is not on track to meet its own transport emissions targets. They point to more walking, cycling and public transport use as priorities. That means more investment in making public transport reliable and accessible, and in cleaner ways to move goods, especially rail and short sea shipping.

## 2026 Update: Fossil Fuel Dependence Leaves Ireland Exposed

The Climate Change Advisory Council (CCAC) 2026 Annual Review gave a clear warning. Ireland's reliance on fossil fuels for transport leaves the country open to repeated fuel price shocks while global energy markets stay unsettled.

**The CCAC's main recommendations:**
- Faster investment in public transport, active travel, EV charging infrastructure and grid capacity.
- Targeted financial help for people most exposed to fuel costs and least able to avoid them, including lower-income households and car-dependent areas with no public transport alternative.
- Wider use of real-time maps of EV charging points, to give drivers the confidence to switch.

**What is working:**
- Journeys across the Transport for Ireland (TFI) network rose 6% in 2025 compared with 2024.
- Local Link, Ireland's rural bus service, saw a 19% jump in journeys.
- The government's pilot EV scrappage scheme was welcomed.

**What is not enough:**
- Total public transport journeys were largely flat year on year, which suggests some routes are close to full at peak times.
- The CCAC called for faster delivery of DART+ South West, Luas Finglas and the NTA Park and Ride Investment Programme to add capacity.

CCAC Chair Alex White put it plainly: "Fossil fuel shocks are not one-off events. Giving alternatives is the way to reduce exposure. That means sustained investment in public transport, a charging network people can rely on, and the grid capacity needed to support the switch to electric across cars, buses and commercial fleets. This transition also has to be fair."
