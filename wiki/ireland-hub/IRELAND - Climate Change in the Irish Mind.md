# Ireland - Climate Change in the Irish Mind

## What is it

The **Climate Change in the Irish Mind Wave 2 Report**, published by the EPA, asked 1,355 people in Ireland about their attitudes to climate change. The results are broadly encouraging: 95% accept that climate change is happening, and only 5% deny it outright. But the report also finds a big gap between what people think and how much they feel they can do themselves. That gap helps explain why widespread concern does not always lead to widespread action.

**Main findings:**
- 95% accept climate change is real; only 5% deny it outright.
- 53% see climate change as mostly caused by human activity, while 39% see it as partly human and partly natural.
- 79% want the government to treat climate action as a priority, though support is stronger for measures that do not affect them directly.
- 33% talk about climate change often with friends and family, and a further 45% at least occasionally.
- 28% believe other people need to do more, while only 16% believe they themselves should be doing more.

## Why it matters

To design communication and policy that works, it helps to understand how people actually think about climate change, beyond what they say they believe. This survey uses the **theory of planned behaviour**, one of the most widely used and tested models of human behaviour. It says people act when they see a behaviour as positive, as something others around them do, and as within their own control. Looking at the results this way shows where progress is being made and where the gaps still are.

## Attitudes, norms and the agency gap

Overall, Irish respondents are worried about climate change and support action to stop it getting worse. Most believe climate change is already harming people in Ireland (54%), that it will harm future generations a great deal (**83%**), and that it is very or extremely important to them personally (62%). So it is no surprise that most also want climate change to be a government priority (79%).

When it comes to specific measures, though, support leans towards those that ask little of respondents themselves, such as more investment in greener transport or funding to help people buy electric cars. People are much more sceptical about banning oil or peat for home heating, or raising taxes on diesel. Climate change has become part of daily news for most respondents: **74%** hear about it on the news at least once a week, and a third talk about it often with friends and family.

Taken together, Irish respondents show two of the three things the theory of planned behaviour says people need before they act: a positive attitude towards acting, and a sense that acting is what others do. What seems to be missing is the third, a sense of personal agency. Asked who should do more, 28% say other people should do much more, while only 16% say they themselves should. This is an example of **pluralistic ignorance**, where people underestimate how much others already care and act, and so hold back from acting themselves. It shows clearly in behaviour: only 12% of respondents regularly boycott companies that damage the environment, and 17% make a point of buying from environmentally friendly companies.

The report sees scientists as well placed to close this gap, since **61%** of respondents trust scientists more than any other source on climate change.
