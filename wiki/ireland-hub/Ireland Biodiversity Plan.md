# Ireland Biodiversity Plan

## What is it

**Ireland's 4th National Biodiversity Action Plan (2023-2030)** is the country's main roadmap for stopping and reversing the loss of nature by 2030. It was published in January 2024 by the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage, and contains 194 actions to tackle biodiversity loss. It is the first such plan to be published on a statutory basis: the Wildlife Amendment Act 2023 now legally requires public bodies to build biodiversity into their own plans and policies, giving this plan legal teeth that its predecessors lacked.

The two official sources for the plan are gov.ie, the government's publication page, and npws.ie, the National Parks and Wildlife Service, the body that runs the plan and hosts the full document for download in both English and Irish.

## Why it matters

The situation the plan is responding to is serious. Almost a third of EU-protected species and 85% of EU-protected habitats in Ireland are in poor condition. More than half of native Irish plant species have declined over the past 20 years, and a fifth of breeding bird species are also in decline. Biodiversity loss is not a side issue to climate change: healthy ecosystems support clean water, food production, flood protection and carbon storage, all of which are also climate priorities.

## What the Plan Includes

The plan is built around five main goals: a "whole of government, whole of society" approach; meeting urgent conservation and restoration needs; protecting nature's contributions to people, such as clean water and food; building a better evidence base through data and monitoring; and strengthening Ireland's contribution to international biodiversity efforts.

Five of its headline actions illustrate the scope of the 194 total measures:

1. **Restore and rewet peatlands.** Restoring and rehabilitating Ireland's peatlands, including rewetting raised bogs damaged over decades, is one of the plan's flagship actions.
2. **Create Marine Protected Areas.** The government will develop legislation on Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) and progress implementation rapidly, targeting 30% protection of the marine environment by 2030.
3. **Tackle invasive species more aggressively.** A dedicated invasive alien species unit will speed up enforcement, with on-the-ground work to remove species such as Japanese knotweed and rhododendron from native woodlands and peatlands.
4. **Make farming more nature-friendly.** Ireland's agricultural schemes will introduce more nature-friendly farming measures, with a target to cut pesticide use by 50% by 2030.
5. **Expand and protect National Parks.** This includes expanding National Parks, strengthening efforts against wildlife crime, and working with local communities to protect habitats.

These five are the broad headline actions; the full 194-action plan covers everything from urban green spaces to ocean protection.

## Pollinators on the Ground: Bees and Ponds

Two 2026 articles illustrate how the plan's ambitions are playing out in practice, at the level of individual farms and estates.

**Industrial estates as bee habitat.** Over 30 of Ireland's 100 native bee species are now threatened with extinction, making action urgent. Industrial estates, which cover a large share of Ireland's developed land, have been largely absent from national biodiversity conversations, yet they could form a network of pollinator corridors across the country. Researchers at Maynooth University have produced a practical guide for industrial estates with three levels of action: quick wins such as bee hotels and no-mow patches, a mid-level "building up" phase, and more ambitious rewilding efforts designed to work in real-world conditions rather than ideal ones.

**Farm ponds and pollinators.** A new four-year research project is examining an overlooked feature of Irish farmland, the humble pond, and its potential to support struggling pollinator species. The severity of pollinator decline has already led County Wicklow to establish Ireland's first dedicated Bee Sanctuary. The Ponds for Pollinators Project will assess how pond size, age and surrounding vegetation affect pollinator numbers and diversity on working farms, and will explore why some farmers choose to create ponds while others do not.
