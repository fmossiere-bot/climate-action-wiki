# Cool Irish Companies

## What is it

**Silicate** is an Irish start-up based in Sligo. It is testing whether spreading crushed concrete dust on farm fields can help tackle climate change. The company is running trials in the United States and hopes the approach brings three benefits at once: it absorbs carbon, it improves soil health, and it puts construction waste to use instead of sending it to landfill.

## Why it matters

The cement and concrete industry produces around **8%** of global greenhouse gas emissions, a large share of it driven by recent building booms, particularly in China (see [Cement and Concrete: A Hidden Climate Problem](../sectors/industry/Cement%20and%20Concrete%20-%20A%20Hidden%20Climate%20Problem.md)). Turning waste from that industry into a way of removing carbon, rather than simply burying it in landfill, is a promising example of circular climate innovation in Ireland that works with natural processes.

## Silicate's approach

Silicate spreads concrete dust on farm fields with three aims. The first is **carbon absorption**. Cement can absorb a lot of CO2 from the air, and breaking down the rock-based parts of cement, such as limestone, releases more material that absorbs CO2. The second is better soil: early data from the company's trials shows its concrete dust mix improving soil quality. The third is using up waste, since the company works with waste concrete from demolished buildings. In the company's words, this approach addresses "the need to remove CO2 from the atmosphere while utilising a highly alkaline low-value construction waste product that is frequently landfilled or used for low-value filler applications."

Silicate says it is the first company in the world to use this material to store carbon at scale. It has developed ways to measure and model, in the field, how much carbon it removes. Its business model is to spread the material on farmland free of charge, measure the carbon removed, and then sell the resulting carbon credits. The company is still at trial stage.

## What is enhanced weathering

**Enhanced weathering** speeds up the Earth's natural weathering of rock, a slow process that removes carbon dioxide from the air, so that it can help tackle the climate crisis. It usually involves crushing rocks and minerals rich in calcium and magnesium to give them more surface area, then spreading them on farm soils. CO2 levels in soil can be up to ten times higher than in the open air. Materials with high levels of calcium or magnesium (in the form of charged particles called cations) can remove the most carbon.

## Concrete as a waste stream

Around **2 billion tonnes** of concrete waste are created worldwide when buildings are demolished. Companies like Silicate take this plentiful waste from the construction sector, process it so it removes carbon more effectively, and then work with farmers to spread it on their fields.
