# Cool Irish Companies

## What is it

**Silicate** is a Sligo-based Irish start-up testing whether spreading crushed concrete dust on farm fields can help fight climate change. The company is trialling its approach in the United States and hopes it delivers three benefits at once: carbon absorption, improved soil health, and a productive use for construction waste that would otherwise go to landfill.

## Why it matters

The cement and concrete industry is responsible for around 8% of total global greenhouse gas emissions, with a large share driven by recent construction booms, particularly in China (see [Cement and Concrete: A Hidden Climate Problem](../sectors/industry/Cement%20and%20Concrete%20-%20A%20Hidden%20Climate%20Problem.md)). Finding ways to turn waste from that industry into a carbon removal tool, rather than simply landfilling it, is a promising example of circular, nature-adjacent climate innovation happening in Ireland.

## Silicate's Approach

Silicate spreads concrete dust on farm fields with the aim of achieving three impacts. First, **carbon absorption**: cement itself has a high capacity to absorb CO2 from the atmosphere, and breaking down the rock-derived components of cement, such as limestone, releases further material with CO2 absorption properties. Second, **enhanced soil**: initial data from the company's trials shows its concrete dust mix improving soil quality. Third, **removing waste**: the company uses waste concrete generated from building demolition. As the company describes it, this approach simultaneously addresses "the need to remove CO2 from the atmosphere while utilising a highly alkaline low-value construction waste product that is frequently landfilled or used for low-value filler applications."

Silicate says it is the first company in the world to use this material to sequester carbon at scale, and has developed in-situ measurement and modelling capabilities to verify the carbon removal rates it achieves. The company's business model is to spread the material on farmland free of charge, measure the carbon removed, and then sell the resulting carbon credits. The company remains at trial stage.

## What is Enhanced Weathering

Enhanced weathering is a strategy that greatly accelerates the Earth's natural rock weathering process, which removes atmospheric carbon dioxide, at a rate intended to help mitigate the current climate crisis. It typically involves crushing calcium- and magnesium-rich rocks and minerals to increase their surface area before applying them to agricultural soils, where CO2 concentrations can run up to ten times higher than in the open atmosphere. Materials with high concentrations of calcium or magnesium cations have the greatest carbon removal capacity.

## Concrete as a Waste Stream

Around 2 billion tonnes of concrete are created globally through the demolition of buildings. Companies like Silicate take this abundant waste stream from the construction sector and process it to better harness its carbon removal properties, then work with farmers to apply it to their fields.
