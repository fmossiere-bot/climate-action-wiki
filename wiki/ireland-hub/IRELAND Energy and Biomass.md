# Ireland Energy and Biomass

## What is it

Biomass is an integral part of Ireland's climate plan for energy production, despite the risks and controversies that surround it. This page covers Ireland's transition away from peat-fired power, the growing role of bioenergy in the country's renewable supply, and the debate over biomethane, gas made from farming and food waste, as a partial replacement for natural gas.

## Why it matters

Ireland historically relied heavily on burning peat for heating and power, a policy that dates back to national energy security decisions made during the Second World War. That reliance degraded Ireland's peatlands, which the country is now working to restore as part of its climate action plan. At the same time, Ireland is trying to build up sustainable bioenergy and biomethane production domestically rather than relying on imports, since not all biomass is equally sustainable, and getting that balance right matters for whether biomass actually helps or hinders Ireland's climate goals.

## The End of the Peat-Fired Era

Ireland traditionally relied heavily on burning peat for heating homes and generating power, thanks to its abundance of peatlands. Peat-fired power became national policy during the Second World War. That reliance degraded Ireland's peatlands significantly, which is why restoring them is now a major commitment within Ireland's climate action plan. The country's last peat-fired power station has since closed, with the Edenderry power plant switching fully to burning biomass.

## The Rise of Bioenergy

Bioenergy already supplies around 40% of Ireland's renewable energy. Despite this large share of supply and consumption, Ireland has significant untapped potential as a biomass *producer*: much of what is used today is imported rather than produced locally. Developing domestic biomass production, and making better use of what is already produced locally, is therefore an important goal. A legacy of former peat-fired plants has been transitioning to biomass in the process. This shift is not without controversy, since some imported biomass and biofuels can add to climate problems rather than solve them, depending on how sustainably the feedstock is sourced.

Ireland's future energy system is expected to lean heavily on wind energy and bioenergy, alongside other renewable sources.

## Biomethane's Potential

Biomethane, which is carbon-neutral and renewable, could replace around a quarter of Ireland's natural gas use. According to RTÉ, this could reduce Ireland's total emissions by almost 4 million tonnes a year, representing 6.5% of the country's total emissions. Currently, 176 projects across Ireland could, if delivered, produce 14.8 terawatt hours of biomethane a year.

As one industry figure put it: while the first Irish policy support for biomethane is in development, it is essential that it provides a framework delivering long-term price certainty for producers, with planning and permitting aligned to the specific needs of the sector. Friends of the Earth Ireland has expressed scepticism about Gas Networks Ireland's projections, cautioning that increasing biomethane use must not come at the expense of sustainability or Ireland's climate commitments to rapidly reduce fossil fuels. Stable pricing for producers is also seen as important to the sector's growth.

## Gas Networks Ireland's Biomethane Plan

Gas Networks Ireland, a state company, is working in consultation with the Commission for the Regulation of Utilities (CRU) on a formal process to procure Irish-produced biomethane, following publication of the government's biomethane strategy. The government's strategy aims to replace 10% of the natural gas the Republic uses with biomethane made by processing farming and food waste.

Ireland has large quantities of farming waste suitable for biomethane production, yet its production still trails EU countries such as Denmark. The government hopes its strategy will support the construction of up to 200 biomethane production plants, known as anaerobic digesters, in rural communities.

## Ireland's Draft Biomethane Strategy

Ireland's biomethane action plan sets out an integrated strategy for the collective delivery of bioenergy resources across the agriculture, enterprise, transport, environment and energy sectors.
