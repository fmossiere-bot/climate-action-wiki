# Ireland Wind Energy

## What is it

Wind energy is central to how Ireland plans to meet its 2030 and 2050 emissions reduction goals, alongside a supporting role for bioenergy in substituting natural gas (see [Ireland Energy and Biomass](IRELAND%20Energy%20and%20Biomass.md)). Ireland's targets are to cut energy industry emissions by 60% and to source 80% of energy from renewables by 2030. Today, Ireland has 4.5GW of renewable energy capacity; by 2050 it will need around 25GW. Electricity demand itself is expected to grow three to four times over, from 29TWh today to somewhere between 84 and 122TWh. Greenhouse gas emissions from Ireland's energy system, currently around 60 million tonnes, must fall close to zero over that same period, even as the system today remains dominated by fossil fuels.

## Why it matters

As of 2019, wind supplied around 10TWh of Ireland's electricity, with renewables making up around 12% of total energy use and 37% of electricity specifically. Closing the gap between where Ireland is now and where it needs to be by 2050 will require a large and rapid scale-up of wind capacity, both onshore and offshore, alongside the energy storage needed to make an increasingly wind-powered grid reliable.

## Path to 90% of Energy Supplied by Wind

Ireland's roadmap envisions multiplying wind output from around 10TWh today to more than 77TWh by 2050, which would make wind the majority renewable source in the country's energy mix. Reaching that scale would require between 41GW and 46GW of wind deployment. Currently, Ireland has just under 1GW of offshore wind capacity, with plans from EDF to bring that to around 3GW by 2030, a target that looks modest next to the 93GW of new wind capacity added globally in 2022 alone. By 2050, Irish wind could contribute around 2.5% of total EU electricity demand, and over 5% of all EU wind energy generation.

## Ireland's Offshore Wind Efforts

**EDF's partnership with Simply Blue on floating wind.** EDF Renewables and Simply Blue have agreed a deal to develop two offshore wind parks using floating turbine technology: the Emerald Floating Wind project south of Cork, with a capacity of 1.3GW, and the Western Star project, located 35km off the Clare coast, with a capacity of 1.35GW. Together these projects would support the government's plan to bring an additional 2GW of offshore wind online by 2030.

For more detail on Ireland's wind energy capacity and plans, see [FACTS about Wind Energy](../solutions/renewables/FACTS%20about%20Wind%20Energy.md).

## The Storage Challenge

Scaling wind to the levels Ireland's targets require will also depend on building out sufficient energy storage capacity, since wind output is variable and a much larger share of the grid running on wind makes storage essential for reliability.
