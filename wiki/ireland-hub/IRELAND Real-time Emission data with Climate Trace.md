# Ireland Real-Time Emission Data with Climate Trace

## What is it

[Climate TRACE](../concepts/key-ideas/The%20Al-Gore%20CLIMATE%20TRACE%20is%20live.md) is a global tool that tracks emissions in near real time using satellites. It was co-founded by Al Gore and went live with monthly data updates in 2025. Ireland's figures on the tool tell us a few things about where the country's emissions come from.

## What Climate TRACE Shows for Ireland

Agriculture is still Ireland's largest source of emissions, at **38.7%** of the total for 2025, according to the EPA's provisional greenhouse gas emissions figures for 1990-2025, published in July 2026. That figure leaves out land use and forestry.

Ireland's emissions may also have been underestimated in the official inventories. Climate TRACE puts them at around 78 million tonnes, against the roughly 60 million tonnes reported for 2021 under the older accounting.

Transport is another big contributor, at around 20% of emissions.

## Why it matters

Traditional emissions accounting has mostly relied on figures reported by the polluters themselves, gathered into inventories years after the event. Climate TRACE works differently. It uses satellites, sensors and artificial intelligence to produce checked emissions estimates with only a 60-day delay, and it monitors **over 660 million** individual sources worldwide, including power plants, factories, farms and ships. If Ireland's real emissions are higher than the official figures suggest, the country will need to go further and move faster to meet its climate targets.
