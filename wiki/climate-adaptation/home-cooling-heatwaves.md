# Home Cooling During Heatwaves

## What is it

As global temperatures rise, heatwaves that used to be rare are becoming regular events across Europe and the UK. Most British and Irish homes were built to keep heat in, not out. They often lack shutters, cross-ventilation (windows on opposite sides that let air flow through) or insulation that works against summer heat. Because of this, millions of households are thinking about air conditioning for the first time.

There are several ways to keep a home cool: passive methods that use no energy, low-energy fans, and mechanical systems such as air conditioners and heat pumps. Each has a different carbon footprint and works to a different degree.

## Why it matters

Keeping homes cool is a public health issue. Extreme heat kills, especially children, older people and people with health conditions. At the same time, if lots of homes install energy-hungry air conditioners running on fossil-fuel power, that could set back climate goals. Balancing safety against emissions is one of the practical dilemmas of adapting to climate change.

The UK's Climate Change Committee estimates that **22% of British homes** will need active cooling if global warming reaches 2°C, a level that the current path makes likely within decades.

## Key facts

- **4 million UK homes** now have an air conditioner, double the number of three years ago. ([source](../sources/uk-air-conditioning-green-2026.md))
- A portable AC unit running 8 hours/day produces ~4.87kg CO2 over a summer, about the same as driving ~18 miles. ([source](../sources/uk-air-conditioning-green-2026.md))
- Running a fan costs **15–31p/day**, against £5/day for portable AC (UK electricity prices, May 2026). ([source](../sources/uk-air-conditioning-green-2026.md))
- A 2021 Lancet study found electric fans can be as effective as AC at preventing heat stress on hot days. ([source](../sources/uk-air-conditioning-green-2026.md))
- Air-to-air heat pumps deliver about **3 units of cooling per unit of electricity**, far more efficient than standard AC. ([source](../sources/uk-air-conditioning-green-2026.md))
- The UK government offers a £2,500 grant to replace fossil-fuel heating with an air-to-air heat pump. ([source](../sources/uk-air-conditioning-green-2026.md))

## Types of cooling: from greenest to most energy-intensive

1. **Passive cooling**: free and zero-carbon. Window shutters or blinds (which stop the sun's heat before it gets in), opening windows at night, green roofs, extra insulation, wet towels in front of fans.
2. **Electric fans**: very low energy (~15–31p/day). They work best when the air is below body temperature (~35°C) and less well in extreme humid heat.
3. **Air-to-air heat pumps**: efficient and dual-purpose (heating in winter, cooling in summer). The best mechanical option if you are replacing a fossil-fuel heating system. Government grants are available.
4. **Fixed split-system AC**: more efficient than portable units. Getting the right size matters: like a radiator, one that is too big wastes energy.
5. **Portable plug-in AC**: the least efficient option. These units lower the air pressure indoors, which pulls warm air back in through the gap around the exhaust hose. Useful as an emergency backup.

## Can AC be green?

Not fully, until the electricity grid runs entirely on renewables. But you can reduce the impact:

- **Pair it with solar panels**: run AC while your panels are generating, instead of drawing from the grid.
- **Use a home battery**: charge it when there is surplus renewable power, then run AC from the stored energy.
- **Check grid carbon intensity**: sites like [mygridgb.co.uk](https://www.mygridgb.co.uk/) show when the grid is cleanest. Run AC during those times.
- **Pre-cool rooms** before the evening peak in electricity demand, when more of the grid's power tends to come from fossil fuels.
