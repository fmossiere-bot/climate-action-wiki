# Climate Change and Health

## What is it

Climate change affects health in two main ways. The first is direct: more frequent and more intense **heatwaves**, droughts, floods and storms injure and kill people, and put pressure on hospitals. The second is indirect: changing weather affects food supplies, clean water and the spread of some diseases. On top of this, the burning of coal, oil and gas that drives climate change also pollutes the air we breathe.

The main yearly scorecard on this is the Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change, produced by researchers led by University College London in collaboration with the World Health Organization (WHO). Its 2026 edition tracks 54 indicators and finds that many health harms from climate change are now growing faster than the world's response ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).

The good news runs in parallel. Cutting fossil fuel pollution, building stronger health systems and warning people before heatwaves arrive are already saving lives. As WHO's Dr Diarmid Campbell-Lendrum puts it, "climate action is a health intervention."

## Why it matters

Heat is already the deadliest climate hazard for many people, and it hits unevenly. Older people, people with heart or lung conditions, outdoor workers and those living in homes that trap heat are most at risk. Heat deaths are also easy to miss, because heat is rarely written on a death certificate.

Health is also one of the clearest reasons to act now rather than later. The benefits of cleaner air arrive quickly and locally, in the same towns and cities that make the cuts, long before the global climate responds. That makes health a practical argument for climate action that does not depend on what other countries do.

## Key facts

- Yearly heat-related deaths rose by nearly 36% in the years after the Paris Agreement compared with the decade before ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).
- Heat was linked to a record 710,000 deaths worldwide in 2023, and more than 70% of that toll is attributed to human-caused climate change ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).
- Drought, exposure to floods and other water extremes, and the economic losses caused by heat are all increasing ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).
- Countries that have cut fossil fuel air pollution are estimated to be preventing thousands of early deaths each year ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).
- Global clean energy investment reached a record US$2.1 trillion in 2025 ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).
- In summer 2026, a record 52% of Europe was exposed to "very strong" heat stress by the end of the season ([Carbon Brief state of the climate](../sources/state-of-the-climate-2026-carbonbrief.md)).
- "Dynamic and resilient health systems" is one of the ten priorities of the COP31 presidency, and the Lancet Countdown authors call for climate change to be formally recognised as a global health emergency ([COP31 Antalya](../legislation-policy/cop31-antalya-2026.md), [Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)).

## What helps

More countries now have national health adaptation plans and early warning systems that use weather forecasts to alert hospitals and the public before a heatwave or flood ([Lancet Countdown 2026](../sources/lancet-countdown-2026-who.md)). WHO hosts the Alliance for Transformative Action on Climate and Health (ATACH), a network of health ministries working on health systems that can withstand climate shocks while producing less pollution themselves. Since September 2026, WHO has also run a graded emergency response to the expected health effects of the current record El Niño.

<!-- tag: individual-actions -->
For individuals, the most useful steps are local and practical: know who around you is most vulnerable to heat and check on them during heatwaves, keep homes cool with shading and ventilation before reaching for air conditioning, and support cleaner transport and heating in your area, which improves the air you breathe as well as the climate. The wiki's page on [keeping homes cool in heatwaves](home-cooling-heatwaves.md) has more detail.
