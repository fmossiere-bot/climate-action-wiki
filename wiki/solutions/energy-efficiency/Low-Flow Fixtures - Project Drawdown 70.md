# Low-Flow Fixtures: Project Drawdown 70

## What is it

**Low-flow fixtures**, such as low-flow showerheads and taps, cut greenhouse gas emissions in two ways. Using less hot water means less energy is needed to heat it. Using less water overall means less energy goes into treating it and pumping it to homes. These fixtures are cheap and simple to fit, which makes them one of the easier efficiency upgrades for a household.

Showers and baths account for nearly 40% of the water a household uses each day, as covered in [Water: A Finite Resource We Cannot Afford to Ignore](../../biodiversity-land/Water%20-%20A%20Finite%20Resource%20We%20Cannot%20Afford%20to%20Ignore.md). That is why bathroom fixtures are a worthwhile place to save both water and energy.

## Why it matters

Heating water takes a lot of energy, so any cut in hot water use means lower energy bills and lower emissions. Once low-flow fixtures are fitted, nobody has to change their habits, and they usually cost little. That makes them one of the easiest efficiency measures a household can take.

## Key facts

- Low-flow fixtures cut emissions by reducing both the energy needed to heat water and the energy needed to treat and pump water supplies.
- Showers and baths make up nearly 40% of a household's daily water use.
- Low-flow fixtures are cheap, simple to fit, and help manage water resources sustainably as well as saving energy and emissions.
- Project Drawdown rates low-flow fixtures as a cost-effective way to use less water, but with only a modest direct effect on greenhouse gas emissions. It puts the solution in its "**Worthwhile**" category rather than among the top climate levers.
