# Low-Flow Fixtures: Project Drawdown 70

## What is it

**Low-flow fixtures**, such as low-flow showerheads and taps, cut greenhouse gas emissions in two ways. First, by reducing the volume of hot water used, they reduce the emissions from the energy needed to heat that water. Second, by reducing overall water use, they reduce the emissions associated with treating and pumping water for domestic supply. They are low-cost and simple to install, which makes them one of the more accessible household efficiency upgrades available.

Showers and baths account for nearly 40% of daily household water use, as covered in [Water: A Finite Resource We Cannot Afford to Ignore](../../biodiversity-land/Water%20-%20A%20Finite%20Resource%20We%20Cannot%20Afford%20to%20Ignore.md), which is what makes bathroom fixtures such a meaningful target for water and energy savings.

## Why it matters

Heating water is energy-intensive, so any reduction in hot water use translates directly into lower household energy bills and emissions. Because low-flow fixtures require no behaviour change once installed, and typically cost little, they are one of the easiest efficiency measures for a household to adopt.

## Key facts

- Low-flow fixtures reduce emissions by cutting both the energy needed to heat water and the energy needed to treat and pump water supplies.
- Showers and baths make up nearly 40% of a household's daily water use.
- Low-flow fixtures are low-cost, simple to install, and help with sustainable water resource management alongside their emissions and energy benefits.
- Project Drawdown's assessment rates low-flow fixtures as a cost-effective strategy for reducing water consumption, but one with only a modest direct impact on greenhouse gas emissions, categorising the solution as "**Worthwhile**" rather than a top-tier climate lever.
