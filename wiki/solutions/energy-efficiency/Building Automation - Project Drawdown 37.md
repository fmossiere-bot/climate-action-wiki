# Building Automation: Project Drawdown 37

## What is it

Most large commercial buildings run their heating, cooling, electricity and water systems through a centralised control system. **Building automation systems (BAS)** are the automated version of these controls: they regulate a building's heating and cooling, lighting, appliances and more, replacing older conventional pneumatic or electric control systems. Automating these systems in this way can cut a building's energy use by up to 40%.

Because buildings account for such a large share of energy consumption, residential and commercial buildings together are responsible for around a third of global carbon dioxide emissions. Project Drawdown calculates the impact of building automation by looking at the total floor area currently under automated control and the current adoption rate, then estimating adoption scenarios per region and the resulting emissions and cost changes based on electricity and fuel prices.

## Why it matters

Heating, cooling and lighting waste a huge amount of energy in buildings that are not intelligently controlled, whether that is air conditioning running in empty offices or lights left on overnight. Automating these systems is one of the more straightforward efficiency upgrades available, since it does not require replacing the underlying heating or cooling equipment, just controlling it more intelligently. It also tends to pay for itself: the financial return on investment is substantial.

## Key facts

- Building automation systems can reduce a building's energy use by up to 40% by intelligently regulating heating, cooling, lighting and appliances.
- Residential and commercial buildings together account for around a third of global carbon dioxide emissions, largely because of their share of energy consumption.
- Under Project Drawdown's central scenario, wider adoption of building automation could avoid 9.55 gigatons of carbon dioxide equivalent greenhouse gas emissions by 2050.
- That scenario requires an initial additional investment of US$287.70 billion above the reference case, but saves an estimated US$2.27 trillion in operating costs over the lifetime of the technology.
