# Building Automation: Project Drawdown 37

## What is it

Most large commercial buildings run their heating, cooling, electricity and water through a central control system. **Building automation systems (BAS)** are the automated version of these controls. They manage a building's heating and cooling, lighting, appliances and more, replacing older pneumatic or electric controls. Automating these systems can cut a building's energy use by up to 40%.

Buildings use a large share of the world's energy, so homes and commercial buildings together are responsible for around a third of global carbon dioxide emissions. Project Drawdown works out the impact of building automation by looking at how much floor space is already under automated control and how fast it is being adopted. It then estimates adoption in each region and the resulting changes in emissions and costs, based on electricity and fuel prices.

## Why it matters

Buildings without smart controls waste a lot of energy on heating, cooling and lighting, whether that is air conditioning running in empty offices or lights left on overnight. Automation is one of the simpler efficiency upgrades available, because the heating or cooling equipment stays in place and is simply controlled better. It also tends to pay for itself, with a good financial return on the investment.

## Key facts

- Building automation systems can cut a building's energy use by up to 40% by managing heating, cooling, lighting and appliances more intelligently.
- Homes and commercial buildings together account for around a third of global carbon dioxide emissions, mainly because of the energy they use.
- Under Project Drawdown's central scenario, wider use of building automation could avoid 9.55 gigatons of carbon dioxide equivalent greenhouse gas emissions by 2050.
- That scenario needs an extra upfront investment of US$287.70 billion compared with the reference case, but saves an estimated US$2.27 trillion in running costs over the lifetime of the technology.
