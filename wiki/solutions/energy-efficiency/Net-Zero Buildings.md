# Net-Zero Buildings

## What is it

A **net-zero building** is designed to have zero net energy use. It combines efficiency measures with renewable energy generated on site so that, over a year, it produces as much energy as it uses. Its running emissions are therefore low or zero.

Net-zero buildings do not rely on a single technology. They mix several approaches to cut energy demand in the first place, such as passive solar design, natural ventilation and cooling instead of mechanical air conditioning, and collecting rainwater. Renewable generation is then added to cover whatever energy is still needed.

Project Drawdown does not give Net-Zero Buildings their own emissions or cost figure. Those effects are already counted in the related solutions that make net-zero design possible: high-performance glass, green and cool roofs, insulation, and distributed energy storage.

## Why it matters

Buildings use a large share of the world's energy and produce a large share of its emissions, mostly through heating, cooling and lighting. Net-zero design tackles this at the source by keeping the energy a building needs as low as possible, rather than only cleaning up the energy supply afterwards. Because it combines many smaller measures rather than one big fix, it can also be adapted to different climates and types of building.

## Key facts

- A net-zero building produces as much energy as it uses over a year, through a mix of efficiency measures and renewable generation on site.
- Design approaches include passive solar design, natural cooling and ventilation instead of mechanical air conditioning, and rainwater harvesting.
- Project Drawdown counts the emissions and cost impact of net-zero buildings within its related solutions, including high-performance glass, green and cool roofs, insulation, and distributed energy storage, rather than as a separate total.
- [Cool Roofs](Cool%20Roofs%20-%20A%20Simple%20Idea%20with%20a%20Surprisingly%20Large%20Impact.md), one of the building blocks of net-zero design, can lower roof surface temperatures and cut the energy needed for cooling considerably.
