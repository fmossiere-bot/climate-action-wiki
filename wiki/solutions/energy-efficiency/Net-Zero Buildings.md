# Net-Zero Buildings

## What is it

A **net-zero building** is one designed to have zero net energy consumption: it combines efficiency measures with onsite renewable energy generation so that, over a year, it produces as much energy as it uses, resulting in low or no operational emissions. Rather than relying on a single technology, net-zero buildings creatively combine several approaches to reduce energy demand in the first place, such as passive solar design, natural ventilation and cooling instead of mechanical air conditioning, and harvesting rainwater, before adding renewable generation to cover whatever energy demand remains.

Project Drawdown does not calculate a separate emissions or financial impact figure for Net-Zero Buildings as a standalone solution, because those effects are already captured within the related solutions that make net-zero design possible: high-performance glass, green and cool roofs, insulation, and distributed energy storage.

## Why it matters

Buildings are responsible for a large share of global energy use and emissions, largely through heating, cooling and lighting. Net-zero design tackles this at the source by minimising how much energy a building needs in the first place, rather than only trying to decarbonise the energy supply after the fact. Because the approach combines many smaller measures rather than one big fix, it also tends to be adaptable to different climates and building types.

## Key facts

- A net-zero building produces as much energy as it consumes over a year, through a combination of efficiency measures and onsite renewable generation.
- Design strategies include passive solar design, natural cooling and ventilation instead of mechanical air conditioning, and rainwater harvesting.
- Project Drawdown accounts for the emissions and financial impact of net-zero buildings within its related solutions, including high-performance glass, green and cool roofs, insulation, and distributed energy storage, rather than as a separate total.
- [Cool Roofs](Cool%20Roofs%20-%20A%20Simple%20Idea%20with%20a%20Surprisingly%20Large%20Impact.md), one of the building blocks of net-zero design, can cut roof surface temperatures and significantly reduce cooling energy demand.
