# Alternative Cement: Project Drawdown 32

## What is it

Cement is the second most used substance on Earth after water, and making it produces a lot of greenhouse gas. **Alternative cement** replaces some of the usual ingredients, above all clinker, with substitutes such as volcanic ash, certain clays, finely ground limestone, ground bottle glass or industrial waste. These substitutes can keep the finished product just as strong while cutting the emissions from making it by around 15% on average.

It helps to know how cement is normally made. For Portland cement, the most common type, crushed limestone and aluminosilicate clay are roasted together in a kiln. This produces calcium oxide, the ingredient that makes cement bind, but it also gives off carbon dioxide as a waste product. That CO2 makes up roughly 60% of cement's total emissions. The other 40% comes from the energy used to run the kiln. Project Drawdown's Alternative Cement solution works on two levers: making **clinker**, a halfway product in cement-making, more efficiently, and using less clinker in the final cement mix.

## Why it matters

Concrete is used on an enormous scale in roads, buildings, bridges and foundations. That is why the emissions from making cement, its key ingredient, add up to such a large share of the world's industrial emissions. Because cement is used everywhere, even modest cuts in its carbon footprint add up to a meaningful amount of avoided emissions worldwide, without slowing construction down.

## Key facts

- Cement is the second most used substance in the world after water.
- Carbon dioxide released in the kiln, when limestone and clay are roasted to make calcium oxide, accounts for roughly 60% of cement's emissions. The other 40% comes from the energy used to power production.
- Swapping in materials such as volcanic ash, certain clays, finely ground limestone, ground bottle glass or industrial waste for the usual cement ingredients can lower cement's carbon footprint by around 15% on average.
- Project Drawdown estimates this substitution could avoid 7.70 to 15.56 gigatons of CO2-equivalent emissions between 2020 and 2050.
- Alternative cement already holds an estimated 31% share of the global cement market, according to Project Drawdown.
