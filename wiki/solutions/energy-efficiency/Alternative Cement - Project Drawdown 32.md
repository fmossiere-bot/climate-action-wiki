# Alternative Cement: Project Drawdown 32

## What is it

Cement is the second most used substance on Earth after water, and producing it is a major source of greenhouse gas emissions. **Alternative cement** replaces some of the conventional ingredients in cement, most notably clinker, with substitutes such as volcanic ash, certain clays, finely ground limestone, ground bottle glass, or industrial waste products. These substitutes can maintain the strength of the finished product while cutting the emissions associated with making it, by around 15% on average.

To see why this matters, it helps to understand how cement is normally made. To produce Portland cement, the most common type, crushed limestone and aluminosilicate clay are roasted together in a kiln. This produces calcium oxide, the ingredient that gives cement its binding properties, but it also releases carbon dioxide as a waste product. That CO2 makes up roughly 60% of cement's total emissions, with the remaining 40% coming from the energy used to run the kiln. Project Drawdown's Alternative Cement solution targets two levers: making **clinker**, an intermediate product in the cement-making process, more efficiently, and reducing the overall ratio of clinker used in the final cement mix.

## Why it matters

Concrete is used at an enormous scale in roads, buildings, bridges and foundations, which is exactly why the emissions from producing cement, its key ingredient, add up to such a large share of global industrial emissions. Because cement is used everywhere, even modest reductions in its carbon footprint translate into a meaningful volume of avoided emissions worldwide, without requiring construction to slow down.

## Key facts

- Cement is the second most used substance in the world after water.
- Carbon dioxide released during the kiln process, when limestone and clay are roasted to produce calcium oxide, accounts for roughly 60% of cement's emissions; the remaining 40% comes from the energy used to power production.
- Substituting materials such as volcanic ash, certain clays, finely ground limestone, ground bottle glass, or industrial waste for conventional cement inputs can lower the carbon footprint of cement by around 15% on average.
- Project Drawdown estimates this substitution could avoid 7.70 to 15.56 gigatons of CO2 equivalent emissions between 2020 and 2050, with a full-potential estimate of around 7.98 gigatons avoided.
- Alternative cement already holds an estimated 31% share of the global cement market, according to Project Drawdown.
