# LED Lighting: Project Drawdown 18

## What is it

**LEDs (light-emitting diodes)** are the most energy-efficient bulbs available today. Unlike older lighting technologies, they convert most of their energy into light rather than wasting it as heat, using around 90% less energy than traditional incandescent bulbs while lasting far longer. Homes and businesses that switch to LEDs cut their energy bills while reducing emissions, and in low-income countries, LEDs paired with off-grid solar can replace polluting kerosene lamps entirely, delivering climate and health benefits together.

Project Drawdown assumes LEDs will become ubiquitous by 2050, making up 90-95% of household lighting and 80-90% of commercial lighting. As LEDs replace less efficient lighting at that scale, an estimated 14.45 to 15.69 gigatons of carbon dioxide equivalent emissions could be avoided across residential and commercial buildings. This does not even count the additional climate and health gains from replacing off-grid kerosene lighting with solar LED technology.

## Why it matters

Lighting is one of the easiest efficiency upgrades available: swapping a bulb requires no structural change to a building, yet it can cut a significant share of a household or business's electricity use. Because lighting demand is so large and universal, the cumulative effect of shifting the world's bulbs to LEDs adds up to a meaningful chunk of avoided global emissions.

## The Scale of the Opportunity

Project Drawdown estimated total lighting demand separately for residential and commercial buildings, using its Integrated Buildings total addressable market model alongside sources including the International Energy Agency. In 2018, that demand stood at around 37 petalumen-hours a year for household lighting and 62 petalumen-hours a year for commercial lighting. At that point, LEDs supplied only a small share of this demand: an estimated 3% of the commercial lighting market and 2% of the residential lighting market, using 2014 as the base year.

## The EU's Shift Away From Halogen and Incandescent Bulbs

The European Union banned incandescent light bulbs in 2009, which initially made halogen bulbs increasingly popular as the more affordable alternative. The EU then extended the phase-out to halogen bulbs themselves in 2018, after first announcing plans to consult on the move back in 2009. The decision followed studies repeatedly concluding that LED alternatives use around one-fifth of the energy needed to power an equivalent halogen bulb.

The EU estimated the halogen phase-out alone would prevent more than 15 million tonnes of carbon emissions a year, roughly equivalent to Portugal's entire annual electricity usage. Buildings account for around 40% of Europe's energy consumption, and lighting makes up about 15% of that share, so the potential savings from switching lighting technology are significant. Commercial and industrial LED luminaires typically deliver an efficiency of 100-140 lumens per watt, making them around 75% more energy-efficient than halogen equivalents. LEDs carry a higher upfront cost than halogen bulbs, but the EU expects their long lifespan, low maintenance and energy savings to pay back that cost within around 12 months.

Compact fluorescent lamps (CFLs) sit between the two as a middle option: they use around 60-80% less energy than incandescent bulbs and last around 10 years, five times longer than halogen bulbs. CFLs are typically about three times cheaper than LEDs, with prices starting around £2 a bulb, making them a reasonable compromise on cost. Their drawbacks are that they take longer to reach full brightness after switching on, and some models are not compatible with dimmer switches or outdoor use.

## Understanding the New EU Energy Label

Since 1 September 2021, EU energy labels indicating a bulb's efficiency have been transitioning to a new system, with the changeover fully complete by 1 March 2023. The shift was prompted by the EU's Single Lighting Regulation (2019/2020) and Energy Label Regulation (2019/2015), which aim to further protect the environment, improve product sustainability and strengthen consumer protection. These rules were carried into UK law through the Ecodesign for Energy-Related Products and Energy Information (Lighting Products) Regulations 2021 (No. 1095).

The change does not reflect any drop in bulb quality. Because recent LED releases had become so efficient that most were rated in the top category (A, A+, A++), rather than keep adding pluses to the A class, regulators reconfigured the scale into a straightforward A to G rating.

## Practical Tips for Lighting Your Home

Lighting can account for around 12% of a household's energy bill. LEDs can bring 60-90% energy savings compared with older bulbs, because they convert around 20% of the energy they use into light, compared with just 5% for incandescent bulbs, and they typically last 4 to 8 years. According to France's environment and energy agency ADEME, improvements to LED technology over the past decade have delivered a 75% energy saving compared with earlier lighting.

A few practical points to keep in mind when choosing bulbs:

- **Colour temperature** affects how much blue light a bulb emits: warm white sits around 2700-3500K, neutral white around 4000-5500K, and cold white around 6000-8000K, which is best suited to offices rather than living spaces.
- **Wattage equivalence** helps compare brightness: a 5W LED is roughly equivalent to a 50W halogen bulb, and a 20W LED to a 120W halogen bulb.
- **IP ratings**, which indicate a fitting's resistance to dust and moisture, also matter for safety: IP20 is typically suitable for a normal room, while bathrooms require at least IP44.

## Key facts

- LEDs use around 90% less energy than incandescent bulbs and last significantly longer.
- Project Drawdown projects LEDs could avoid 14.45 to 15.69 gigatons of CO2 equivalent emissions by 2050 as they reach 90-95% of household lighting and 80-90% of commercial lighting.
- As of 2018, LEDs supplied only around 2% of residential and 3% of commercial lighting demand, leaving most of the opportunity still ahead.
- The EU's 2018 halogen ban is estimated to prevent over 15 million tonnes of CO2 a year, roughly equal to Portugal's total annual electricity emissions.
- Commercial LED luminaires reach 100-140 lumens per watt, about 75% more efficient than halogen equivalents.
- Lighting represents around 12% of a typical household energy bill, and switching to LEDs can cut that by 60-90%.
