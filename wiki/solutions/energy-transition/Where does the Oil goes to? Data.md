# Where Does Oil Actually Go? A Breakdown of Global Demand

## What is it

Global oil demand is often discussed as a single number, but it is really the sum of quite different end uses. The largest share by far goes to **road transport**, cars, vans, lorries and motorbikes running on petrol and diesel, which makes up roughly half of global oil demand. A significant further share goes to **petrochemicals**, the feedstock used to manufacture plastics, fertilisers and countless other everyday materials, an end use that is not burned for energy at all and therefore is not eliminated simply by switching transport to electricity. The remainder is spread across aviation, shipping, heating, and other industrial uses.

## Why it matters

Understanding where oil actually goes matters for the energy transition because different end uses require completely different solutions. Electrifying cars addresses the largest single slice of demand, road transport, but does little for the petrochemical feedstock used in plastics, or for aviation and shipping, which are far harder to electrify with current technology. This is one reason serious forecasts of declining oil demand still expect a meaningful "residual" demand for oil even in an aggressive transition scenario, concentrated in aviation, shipping and petrochemicals rather than cars.

## Key facts

- Road transport is the single largest end use of oil globally, accounting for roughly half of total demand.
- Petrochemical feedstock, used to make plastics, fertilisers and other materials, is a major and growing share of oil demand that is not addressed by electrifying transport.
- Aviation and shipping rely heavily on oil-derived fuels and are among the hardest sectors to decarbonise with current technology.
- Because oil serves such different end uses, the energy transition needs sector-specific solutions rather than a single fix.
