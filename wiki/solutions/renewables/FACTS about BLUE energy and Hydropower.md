# Facts about Blue Energy and Hydropower

**Blue energy** is renewable power made with new technologies in the ocean, using waves, currents, or the temperature difference between warmer and colder layers of water. These technologies show real promise but are still at an early stage, and they are unlikely to add much to the world's energy supply within the next five years. In theory, though, the potential is huge: estimates suggest the power available is roughly four times current global energy needs (see [CNBC's coverage of wave power](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/09/07/why-wave-power-could-complement-solar-and-wind.html)).

## Hydropower: bigger is not always better

For nature, bigger hydropower projects are not necessarily better. Large dams have brought very cheap electricity and other valuable services, such as irrigation water, to millions of people worldwide. But they also cause serious deforestation and wildlife loss, and they force people from their homes. Research suggests [the costs of mega dams can outweigh their benefits](http://news.mongabay.com/2018/11/mega-dam-costs-outweigh-benefits-global-building-spree-should-end-experts/).

Large dams are not automatically good for the climate either. In tropical regions, they can [contribute more emissions than fossil fuel power plants](https://www.nature.com/articles/nclimate1540) for decades after they are built, because forests flooded by the reservoir rot underwater and release methane, a potent greenhouse gas. Despite these downsides, [more than 500 new dams](https://www.worldwildlife.org/magazine/issues/spring-2021/articles/wwf-study-finds-509-new-dams-planned-or-under-construction-in-protected-areas) are planned or under construction in protected areas around the world, with a large share of those projects located in China.

**Mini- and micro-hydro** projects are a different story. These small systems can power local mini-grids and provide other services, such as water stores for the dry season, without the same damage to nature. They make most sense in hilly regions with streams and waterfalls that flow all year round. For more detail, see the [World Small Hydropower Development Report](https://www.unido.org/our-focus-safeguarding-environment-clean-energy-access-productive-use-renewable-energy-focus-areas-small-hydro-power/world-small-hydropower-development-report) from UNIDO. The director of the [Hydro Empowerment Network](https://www.hpnet.org/) is a mentor with Terra.do for anyone wanting to learn more about small-scale hydro.
