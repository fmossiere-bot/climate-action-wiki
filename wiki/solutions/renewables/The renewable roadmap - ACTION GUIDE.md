# The Renewable Roadmap: A Home Action Guide

## What is it

This guide is a practical roadmap for households looking to cut their energy bills and move towards renewable energy at home. It is aimed mainly at UK and Ireland households, and walks through auditing your current energy use, weighing up renewable options like solar panels and small wind turbines, and building a realistic month-long plan to get started.

In 2023, the electricity produced by renewable generation in the UK was theoretically enough to meet the demand of every home in the country, according to the Energy and Climate Intelligence Unit (ECIU).

On average, households consume very different amounts of electricity depending on where they live:

- US: 11,267 kWh per year
- Spain: 4,928 kWh per year
- UK: 4,266 kWh per year

If your household uses more energy than average, there are usually straightforward ways to cut consumption and cost, and doing so also reduces the carbon emissions tied to your home's energy use.

## Why it matters

With energy costs and climate concerns both rising, moving towards renewable, efficient energy is no longer just a nice-to-have. This guide is meant to make that transition manageable: understand your usage first, then decide whether efficiency upgrades, a renewable installation, or both make sense for your home.

## Section 1: Energy Savings Audit

Start with understanding your energy bill and assessing your home's energy use to identify savings opportunities.

### Understanding your energy bill

Before diving into energy-saving actions, it helps to understand your energy bill properly. A [guide from Home Energy Scotland](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCOr50j-wjE&t=77s) breaks this down and sets the stage for more informed energy use at home.

### Understanding your electricity needs

Before investing in renewable energy for your home, start by calculating your electricity needs. This helps you figure out the size and cost of the system required, how your energy use varies, and where you can cut consumption.

Look at the daily use and wattage of your electrical devices. To calculate total consumption, multiply each appliance's wattage by its daily usage hours, adjusting for seasonal changes. Improving your home's energy efficiency first can mean you end up needing a smaller, cheaper renewable system.

### How much could you save?

Simple steps at home can add up to significant savings on your bills, and reduce your carbon footprint at the same time. Actions like switching appliances off standby, draught-proofing your home, and being mindful of water and electricity use all help. Estimated annual savings for a typical UK household:

| Action | Savings in England, Scotland, Wales | Savings in Northern Ireland |
|---|---|---|
| Switch off standby | £55 | £70 |
| Draught-proof gaps | £95 | £100 |
| Turn off the lights | £25 | £30 |
| Wash at 30 degrees | £29 | £36 |
| Avoid tumble dryer | £60 | £75 |
| Shorten showers | £70 | £70 |
| Swap bath for shower | £11 | £15 |
| Kettle usage | £11 | £14 |
| Dishwasher use | £14 | £18 |
| Insulate cylinder | £50 | £45 |

### Energy efficiency calculator

The [HACKS calculator](https://calculator.topten.eu/heating/) evaluates your home's energy setup and suggests ways to improve its efficiency. It asks questions about your current system and identifies solutions using real-world data.

### Energy efficiency tips, upgrades and incentives

The Energy Saving Trust offers [ways to cut energy costs and make your home greener](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/five-ways-to-futureproof-your-home-against-rising-energy-bills/), [simple upgrades by budget](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/energy-saving-budget/), and an overview of [financial incentives](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/energy-at-home/financial-support/financial-support-for-home-energy/) to help improve your home's energy efficiency.

## Section 2: Exploring Renewable Energy Options

Planning a renewable energy system for your home involves understanding your current electricity usage, local regulations, and whether you'll connect to the grid or go off-grid. Options include solar, wind, microhydropower, and hybrid systems. Setting up can be costly and requires maintenance, but it offers energy independence and a real contribution to reducing emissions.

### Renewable energy options

When selecting a small renewable electric system for your home, it helps to understand the basics: the renewable resources available to you, the economics and costs involved, where to place the system, how big it needs to be, relevant codes and regulations, and what installation and maintenance will be needed. These technologies can work alone, together, or alongside fossil fuels as hybrid systems.

- **Small solar electric systems**: reliable, pollution-free electricity, especially useful in remote areas where traditional power lines are impractical.
- **Small wind electric systems**: cost-effective for home use, with applications beyond electricity generation, such as water pumping.
- **Microhydropower systems**: capable of generating significant electricity, enough to power large homes or small communities, depending on system size.
- **Small hybrid systems**: combining solar and wind can provide a more consistent energy supply by drawing on the strengths of both.

### The right solution for you

[Go Renewable](https://www.gorenewable.org.uk/), a tool from the Energy Saving Trust and MCS, helps you discover suitable renewable technologies for your home. Start with a free online assessment to explore your options, then book a detailed home survey for a full report and an updated Energy Performance Certificate. Finally, compare quotes from MCS-certified installers before installation.

For real examples from people who have already made the switch, see the [Green Homes Network](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/tool/green-homes-network/) case studies from the Energy Saving Trust.

### Solar panels

Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity using cells, typically made of silicon, which work even on cloudy days. A standard home setup has about 10 panels, producing electricity for household use or for export. Before installation, consider your available space and any permissions you might need. Solar panels can reduce electricity bills and carbon emissions, saving approximately one tonne of carbon annually in the UK. The initial cost for a 3.5kWp system is around £7,000, but savings can be made through the [Smart Export Guarantee](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/solar-panels/) for exported electricity.

### Wind turbines

Wind turbines for home use are available as pole-mounted (6kW) or building-mounted (2kW), converting wind into electricity. The initial setup cost for a pole-mounted system is around £35,000, potentially saving £510 a year on electricity and reducing CO2 emissions by 2,000kg annually. Surplus electricity can earn approximately £400 a year through Smart Export Guarantee payments.

Maintenance costs range from £100 to £200 a year, with turbines lasting over 20 years. Inverter replacements may cost £1,000 to £2,000, and off-grid systems might need battery replacements every six to ten years. Source: [Energy Saving Trust](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/wind-turbines/).

### Find certified installers

Once you've chosen a renewable solution, the [Renewable Installer Finder](https://installerfinder.energysavingtrust.org.uk/) helps you find MCS-certified installers in your area, connecting you with trusted professionals.

### Renewable energy incentives

In England, Scotland and Wales, [financial help](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/energy-at-home/financial-support/grants-and-loans/) is available for home renewable technology installations, including:

- **Boiler Upgrade Scheme**: encourages heat pump installations in England and Wales.
- **Smart Export Guarantee**: ensures payment for surplus renewable electricity sent back to the grid in England, Scotland and Wales.

Scottish residents also have access to grants and interest-free loans for home renewables through the Home Energy Scotland Grant and Loan. There is currently no equivalent funding for these technologies in Northern Ireland.

### Finding the right contractors

Choosing accredited professionals matters. The [Microgeneration Certification Scheme (MCS)](https://mcscertified.com/) certifies products and installers of small-scale renewable energy technologies. For insulation and general energy efficiency improvements, [TrustMark](https://www.trustmark.org.uk/) offers a directory of government-endorsed quality tradespeople.

## Section 3: A Four-Week Action Plan

### Week 1: Energy audit and efficiency

**Objective**: understand your energy usage and implement no-cost energy-saving measures.

- **Days 1-2**: Analyse your energy bill. Use resources like the Home Energy Scotland video to understand your consumption patterns and identify areas for improvement.
- **Days 3-4**: Calculate your electricity needs. List all electrical appliances, their wattage and usage frequency, focusing on savings that need no upfront cost, such as adjusting appliance usage times.
- **Days 5-7**: Implement no-cost energy savings. Turning off standby appliances, optimising water use, and being mindful of heating controls can lead to immediate savings.

### Week 2: Research renewable energy options

**Objective**: decide on the most suitable renewable energy solutions for your home.

- **Days 1-3**: Explore renewable energy options. Investigate solar panels, wind turbines and other renewable sources. Use the Go Renewable tool to assess what is feasible for your home.
- **Days 4-5**: Understand the basics of your chosen renewable systems, including size, placement, local regulations, and whether you'll go off-grid or stay connected to the grid.
- **Days 6-7**: Review case studies and real-life examples. The Green Homes Network offers valuable insight into how others have made the transition.

### Week 3: Financial planning for the transition

**Objective**: develop a detailed financial plan for your transition to renewable energy.

- **Days 1-2**: Assess costs and savings for your chosen renewable solution, estimating setup costs and potential bill savings.
- **Days 3-4**: Explore incentives and funding, such as the Boiler Upgrade Scheme and Smart Export Guarantee, and check for regional grants and loans.
- **Days 5-7**: Create your savings plan. Work out how much you need to save monthly, and consider reallocating savings from your no-cost efficiency measures to fund the project.

### Week 4: Finalising your renewable energy plan

**Objective**: finalise your plan, including selecting providers and a project timeline.

- **Days 1-3**: Select your renewable energy providers. Use the Renewable Installer Finder to connect with trusted MCS-certified installers, get quotes and discuss timelines.
- **Days 4-5**: Review and adjust your financial plan against the quotes you've received.
- **Days 6-7**: Plan next steps. Schedule initial consultations or site assessments, and prepare a checklist of actions needed before installation begins, such as applying for incentives or adjusting your household budget.

By the end of these four weeks, you should have a clear understanding of your current energy use, identified cost-free efficiency improvements, chosen the most suitable renewable energy solution, and built a financial plan to make the transition feasible.
