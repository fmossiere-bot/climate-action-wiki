# Sustainable Flights with Hydrogen

[Sustainable Aviation Fuel](../electrification/Sustainable%20Aviation%20Fuel%20-%20%20A%20Necessary%20but%20Complicated%20Solution.md) is not the only route to cleaner flying. Hydrogen has long been considered a serious option for powering transport, particularly cargo aircraft and planes more broadly.

Could genuinely sustainable flights arrive within two years? For smaller aircraft, at least, some companies are confident it can be done, ZeroAvia among them. The company [uses green hydrogen](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-bristol-66205965), with the only byproduct being water, and installs its system on otherwise conventional aircraft engines.

How far can it currently go?

- Up to 15 passengers
- A range of around 300 miles
- The main obstacles are sourcing genuinely "green hydrogen" and building an entirely new logistics chain to transport and store it

Coverage of ZeroAvia's progress has carried headlines promising carbon-free flights "within two years".

## Germany's zero-emission hydrogen aircraft

A separate development comes from Germany, where the **APUS** aircraft has been presented as the first plane with genuinely zero emissions, no CO2 and no NOx (source: *Ecologie360*, Winter 2024-2025). It can cruise at 300km/h and fly as far as 900km, performance that compares well to similar existing four-seater aircraft.

APUS is able to carry **25% more hydrogen** than earlier designs, thanks to careful integration of the storage system into the aircraft's design and a higher compression rate. Hydrogen takes up considerably more space than conventional jet fuel for the same amount of energy, so this kind of packaging improvement is central to making hydrogen aviation practical.
