# Sustainable Flights with Hydrogen

[Sustainable Aviation Fuel](../electrification/Sustainable%20Aviation%20Fuel%20-%20%20A%20Necessary%20but%20Complicated%20Solution.md) is not the only route to cleaner flying. Hydrogen has long been seen as a serious option for powering transport, including cargo aircraft and planes in general.

Some companies, ZeroAvia among them, are confident that truly sustainable flights could arrive within two years, at least for smaller aircraft. The company [uses green hydrogen](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-bristol-66205965), whose only by-product is water, and fits its system to otherwise conventional aircraft.

What it can do today:

- Up to 15 passengers
- A range of around 300 miles
- The main obstacles are finding enough truly "green hydrogen" and building a whole new supply chain to transport and store it

Coverage of ZeroAvia's progress has carried headlines promising carbon-free flights "within two years".

## Germany's zero-emission hydrogen aircraft

In Germany, the **APUS** aircraft has been presented as the first plane with truly zero emissions: no CO2 and no NOx (source: *Ecologie360*, Winter 2024-2025). It can cruise at 300km/h and fly as far as 900km, which compares well with similar four-seater aircraft already flying.

APUS can carry **25% more hydrogen** than earlier designs, because the storage system is built carefully into the aircraft and the hydrogen is compressed more tightly. Hydrogen takes up much more space than conventional jet fuel for the same amount of energy, so better packaging like this is central to making hydrogen aviation practical.
