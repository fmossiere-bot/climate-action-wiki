> Landfills release 12% of the world's methane emissions. This methane can be captured and burnt for energy. Burning it produces CO2, so it is not a complete win, but it is up to 34x less harmful for the climate. 

Landfills and wastewater together account for about 20% of human-caused methane emissions (UNEP Global Methane Assessment, 2021).




This solution sits at the bottom of the waste hierarchy (the ranking of ways to deal with waste, from best to worst). The amount of waste going to landfill will fall as diets change, less is thrown away, and recycling and composting grow. Waste that cannot, or should not, be burnt in waste-to-energy plants will still end up in landfill as a last resort, where it will give off methane and other gases. Even so, capturing that methane is a simple solution for the short to medium term and should not be ignored. 

> Most of what goes into landfill is organic material: food scraps, garden cuttings, scrap wood, wastepaper. As it rots, it produces biogas, which includes methane, a potent greenhouse gas.

Landfill methane can be tapped, captured and used as a fairly clean energy source to make electricity or heat, instead of leaking into the air. The climate benefit is twofold: it stops the landfill emissions, and it replaces coal, oil or natural gas that might otherwise have been used.

> The technology to manage biogas is fairly simple. Perforated pipes are spread out and sunk deep into a landfill to collect the gas, which is piped to a central point where it can be vented or flared (burnt off). Better still, it can be compressed and cleaned for use as fuel in generators or bin lorries, or mixed into the natural gas supply.


Landfill methane capture can reach 85 percent efficiency or more in closed, engineered landfills. It works least well in open dumps, where only about 10 percent of the gas is collected and capture is usually not seen as worth the cost. As a way of treating waste, landfill methane capture is a last resort, better only than landfill without methane capture. But where landfills already exist, it is an important way to cut greenhouse gases.
