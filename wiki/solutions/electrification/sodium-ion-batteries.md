# Sodium-ion batteries: a second chemistry comes of age

## What is it

A **sodium-ion battery** works on the same "rocking chair" principle as a lithium-ion battery: ions shuttle between a positive and a negative electrode as the cell charges and discharges. The only change is the ion: **sodium (Na) instead of lithium (Li)**. Sodium sits right under lithium in the periodic table and behaves similarly, but its larger size means the electrodes have to be different. Sodium batteries use a **hard carbon** anode instead of graphite, an **iron-based cathode** (often a Prussian blue analogue, or a layered oxide) instead of the lithium-cobalt-nickel chemistries, and can use **cheap aluminium foil** on both current collectors instead of expensive copper.

The result is a battery that stores less energy per kilogram than a top-end lithium cell, but that is cheaper, far more abundant, far cleaner to make, and much happier in cold weather.

## Why it matters

For a decade, almost every piece of the clean-energy transition — from grid batteries to electric cars to home storage — has leaned on a single chemistry family. That single-chemistry dependence has three problems: lithium prices spike, cobalt supply is tied to human rights abuses in the Democratic Republic of Congo, and lithium batteries struggle in genuine cold. Sodium-ion solves all three — not for every use case, but for enough of them that it changes the strategic picture.

**The important shift in 2024-2026 is that sodium-ion moved from pilot to commercial deployment at scale.** The world's largest battery maker, CATL, has launched a field-validated sodium-ion grid storage system. BYD is building a dedicated sodium-ion plant. GM is prototyping sodium-ion cells for US data centres. That is a different conversation from "promising lab research".

## Where it fits best

### Grid-scale stationary storage (strongest case)

For a utility, battery weight does not matter. What matters is **cost per kilowatt-hour over the lifetime, cycle life, safety and operating cost**. Sodium-ion wins on most of these:

- Cheap raw materials (sodium costs roughly 1/40 of lithium per tonne)
- Can tolerate wide temperature swings without active cooling, cutting a battery farm's auxiliary power use
- Cannot catch fire while being shipped (see below)
- No cobalt or nickel in the cleanest formulations

Short-duration battery storage (3-8 hours) is the right tool for most grid balancing in a renewables-dominated system. Sodium-ion is a strong fit for that role, complementing lithium-ion rather than replacing it. For longer-duration storage, pumped hydro, compressed air, hydrogen and other technologies take over.

### Entry-level and urban electric vehicles

An average SUV built entirely on sodium-ion batteries gets a **range of around 250 to 350 km**, compared with 400 to 600 km on a top-end lithium pack. That is a serious limit for long-haul driving, but a reasonable fit for:

- Urban cars (daily commute, errand runs)
- Light delivery vans
- E-bikes, mopeds, scooters
- Industrial forklifts
- City buses

Chinese manufacturers (BYD, Hina, Yiwei) are already rolling small sodium-powered EVs off assembly lines.

### Dual-chemistry hybrid packs

A smart compromise: put sodium-ion and lithium-ion cells **inside the same battery enclosure**. The sodium cells handle sub-zero cold starts and winter range, where lithium struggles; the lithium cells deliver the high-density energy that long-distance driving needs. The battery management system switches between them seamlessly. CATL has already shown this architecture publicly.

### Cold-climate home storage

Sodium-ion retains around 90% of its capacity down to -40°C. For home solar storage mounted in a garage, shed or outhouse (common in Ireland, the UK, Scandinavia), that is a real advantage over lithium systems that lose capacity when it gets cold or spend energy keeping themselves warm.

## Where it does not fit

- **Premium long-range EVs** (Tesla, luxury SUVs, long-haul freight) — the density gap is too wide
- **Portable electronics** (phones, laptops) — weight and volume matter too much
- **Grid storage lasting more than ~8 hours** — pumped hydro, compressed air, hydrogen and others take over for weeks-to-months storage (see [intermittency-storage-problem](../../myths/intermittency-storage-problem.md))

## Three honest advantages beyond cost

### 1. Shipping safety

Lithium batteries must keep some charge in transit or they are permanently damaged. That means they always arrive with stored energy, which is a thermal-runaway fire risk during ocean freight. **Sodium-ion cells can be shipped at zero volts** — completely discharged, chemically inert, non-flammable. For island nations like Ireland that import batteries by sea, this is a real logistics and insurance saving.

### 2. Ethics

Roughly 75% of the world's cobalt comes from the Democratic Republic of Congo, where artisanal mining has been widely documented for unsafe conditions, forced labour and child labour. Standard high-density lithium batteries (NMC chemistry) use cobalt and nickel. Sodium-ion batteries, in their cleanest formulations, use **neither cobalt nor nickel** — iron and sodium are the main inputs. The ethical case for sodium is strongest where it displaces NMC chemistry (see [clean-energy-child-labour](../../myths/clean-energy-child-labour.md)).

### 3. Water and ecosystems

Lithium extraction, especially via brine evaporation in South America's "Lithium Triangle", uses millions of litres of water per tonne of lithium produced, straining communities and desert ecosystems. Sodium is extracted from soda ash or salt beds using much simpler, less destructive processes.

## The main obstacle: China does ~95% of planned capacity

The weakest link in the sodium-ion story is **geographic concentration**. Over 95% of the manufacturing capacity planned through 2030 is in China. Earlier non-Chinese entrants have struggled: a US sodium-ion company, Natron Energy, has closed; LG Energy Solution set up its own sodium pilot in Nanjing rather than at home. Europe and North America are therefore buying sodium-ion batteries from China for the time being, which has the same supply-chain-risk profile as the previous generation of solar panels did.

## Technology readiness and cost

Sodium-ion was at **Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 3-4 in 2020** (lab-scale pilots) and reached **TRL 8 by 2024** (first-of-a-kind commercial systems). Industry projections envisage **global sodium-ion stationary-storage capacity growing roughly 15× between 2025 and 2030**, from around 10 GWh to around 150 GWh. Target pack cost is **~$40 per kWh** as the manufacturing base matures — the level at which it undercuts current lithium costs in grid-scale applications.

## Ireland angle

<!-- tag: ireland -->
Ireland's existing grid-scale storage (Lumcloon, Shannonbridge, Cushaling) is lithium-based. Early sodium-ion engagement in Ireland has been through:

- The **TRIDENT project**, supported by the Disruptive Technologies Innovation Fund and the International Energy Research Centre, which has worked on integrating sodium-ion storage with inverters for domestic microgrids.
- The **Galvani home battery** from Eleven Energy, a sodium-ion domestic storage system reportedly distributed in Ireland via Midsummer Ireland, pitched for cold-weather garage and shed installations.

These are reportable from the current source material but not independently verified. If you are using sodium-ion batteries at home in Ireland and are considering outdoor placement, the cold-weather case is genuine. For large-scale deployment, the question is less about chemistry and more about which Chinese supplier Ireland buys from, and when.

## What this does to the storage picture

The one-line summary: **sodium-ion means the storage half of the energy transition does not depend on a single battery chemistry any more**. For critics who worried that scaling lithium batteries would collide with lithium supply, cobalt ethics or cobalt-free alternatives that still needed nickel, the answer that is now shipping at scale is "use sodium batteries for the stationary jobs, use lithium batteries for the mobility jobs, and use dual-chemistry packs where cold is a problem".

That strengthens the answer to the [intermittency-storage-problem](../../myths/intermittency-storage-problem.md) myth. It weakens the "renewables need too much mining" line in [renewables-more-mining](../../myths/renewables-more-mining.md). It takes some of the heat out of [clean-energy-child-labour](../../myths/clean-energy-child-labour.md), because the cobalt dependency is no longer baked into every battery. And it adds a second tool to the kit alongside sand batteries (see [sand-battery-finland-pornainen-2026](../../sources/sand-battery-finland-pornainen-2026.md)) and the existing lithium-ion stack described on [The rise of the batteries](The%20rise%20of%20the%20batteries.md).
