# EV Progress

## What is it

A snapshot of recent electric car sales around the world. Growth is uneven from one region to the next, but the global trend is still clearly upwards, and China is leading it.

## Key facts

- EV sales fell 3% in Europe, while they grew 9% in the United States and 25% worldwide. China led global growth at 40%
- 17 million EVs were sold worldwide in 2024. Europe alone had nearly 9 million EVs on the road
- Europe's EV fleet avoided an estimated 20 million tonnes of CO2-equivalent emissions, roughly what 7 coal-fired power plants emit

## 2026 update: Europe vs China, and the France snapshot

Cédric Philibert, an associate researcher at IFRI, gave this picture of the market in 2026:

<!-- tag: france, eu -->

- In France, EVs now make up **more than 30% of new car sales**. At the current pace, 80% of new sales will be electric within ten years, and by 2050 the vast majority of cars on the road will have switched
- Sales of petrol and diesel cars have passed their peak worldwide. The shift sped up after the Middle East crisis and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz pushed petrol prices up
- European carmakers lost the lead on batteries to China through their own choices. They kept the high-value work at home and sent manufacturing abroad, which handed China the battery lead, and with it the EV lead. Stellantis and Renault also blew hot and cold on electric cars
- Europe is now raising import barriers, pushing Chinese carmakers to build their cars in Europe, and bringing industry back home
- There is no need for alarm: of the ten best-selling EVs in France, nine are European and the tenth is American (Tesla). None are Chinese
- European carmakers are showing they can sell small EVs for **under €20,000** once grants are counted

The bigger picture is simple. Lithium-ion battery prices have fallen ten-fold in a decade, and that is why EVs have won the new-car market. The open question for industrial policy is whether Europe makes those batteries itself or buys them from China.
