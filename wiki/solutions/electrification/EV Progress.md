# EV Progress

## What is it

A snapshot of recent global electric vehicle sales trends: growth is uneven across regions, but the overall global trajectory is still strongly upward, led by China.

## Key facts

- EV sales growth slowed in Europe (down 3%), while growing 9% in the United States and 25% worldwide, with China leading global growth at 40%
- 17 million EVs were sold globally in 2024; Europe alone had nearly 9 million EVs on the road
- Europe's EV fleet avoided an estimated 20 million tonnes of CO2-equivalent emissions, roughly the equivalent of the output of 7 coal-fired power plants

## 2026 update: Europe vs China, and the France snapshot

Cédric Philibert (IFRI, 2026) summarises the current market picture:

<!-- tag: france, eu -->

- **France today: EVs are >30% of new car sales.** On current pace, **80% of new sales will be electric within ten years**; by 2050 the huge majority of cars on the road will have switched
- Sales of petrol and diesel cars have **passed their peak worldwide**; the shift has accelerated since the Middle East crisis and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz sent petrol prices up
- **European carmakers lost battery leadership to China through strategic choice**, not accident — they kept the high-value work at home and offshored manufacturing, which handed China the battery (and so EV) lead. Stellantis and Renault added an up-and-down strategy on electric
- Europe is now **raising import barriers, forcing Chinese carmakers to build in Europe, and bringing industry back**
- Not alarmist: **of the ten best-selling EVs in France, nine are European and the tenth is American (Tesla); none are Chinese**
- European carmakers are showing they can offer small EVs for **under €20,000** once grants are counted

The structural message: lithium-ion battery prices have fallen ten-fold in a decade, which is why EVs have won the new-car market. The industrial-policy question is whether Europe makes those batteries at home or buys them from China.
