### Summary
Irish speciality coffee group Coffeeangel is joining forces with the Dunsany Estate in Co Meath and cleantech company Bionua in an initiative that will see it commit 5 per cent of profits over the next five years to buying biodiversity credits. This is the first time such a deal has been completed in Ireland – and only the second time across Europe."
##### Coffeeangel invests 5% of its profits in biodiversity credits from a rewilded nature reserve

> Restoring biodiversity can bring a double dividend, helping to bring ecosystems back to a healthy state and countering many of the consequences of global warming. It has a critical role in making a sustainable world.


### About Biodiversity Credits
>  Biodiversity credits are legal and financial instruments, serving as tradable assets. Their value is primarily derived from their ability to facilitate absorbing carbon from the atmosphere and significantly bolster biodiversity. They are equivalent to an eco-friendly certificate that shows you have made a piece of land healthier for nature, allowing it to support more plants and animals as well as absorb carbon dioxide. 
### How is it verified?

This is where [Bionua](https://bionua.com/) comes in. It uses technology to monitor nature in real time. Its AI-driven system combines satellite multispectral imaging with sensors on the ground to measure how much carbon is being stored, and how much biodiversity there is, above and below ground.

# What else is Coffeeangel doing for the environment? The full picture

### 1. The Dunsany Nature Reserve partnership

This is still the centrepiece. Coffeeangel has committed 5% of its profits over five years to buying Nature Capital Credits from Dunsany Nature Reserve. The aim is to remove and store at least **80,000 kg of carbon** in the first year alone. The company presents this as contributing to restoration rather than as offsetting. The credits are verified using three ISO standards:

- **ISO 14064-1**: organisational carbon management (how a company measures and reports its own emissions)
- **ISO 14064-2**: carbon and biodiversity measurement at project level (how the carbon stored at Dunsany is calculated)
- **ISO 14064-3**: checking and confirming the carbon data by an independent third party

Monitoring relies on satellite multispectral imaging, ground-based sensors and AI analysis by their partner Bionua.

### 2. Disposable cups: ReCup with EarthCoating® technology

Their disposable cups use EarthCoating® technology, which makes them fully recyclable through Ireland's existing paper recycling system, so they become new products rather than rubbish. Coffeeangel deliberately turned down the "compostable cup" route. They found that compostable cups only break down in commercial composting, and that most end up in general landfill anyway. Those cups were also almost **300%** more expensive than conventional single-use cups. Their choice was to pick something that works in practice rather than something that sounds good on paper.

### 3. Reusable cups (YourCupOurFuture)

They run a long-standing reusable cup campaign. They considered and rejected a simple discount for bringing your own cup. Instead they created *YourCupOurFuture*: every time a customer buys a coffee in a reusable cup, Coffeeangel gives **20¢** to Friends of the Earth Ireland on that customer's behalf. The idea was to have a bigger effect on the community than an individual discount would.


### 4. Industry advocacy and awareness

Through the programme, they have also worked to raise public awareness and to fund research and education on environmental issues, and they have taken part in policy development and industry best practice. They say they were doing this before sustainability became a mainstream topic in the Irish coffee industry.

### 5. Ireland's biodiversity crisis: the context

The Coffeeangel article explains why this kind of initiative matters in Ireland:

- 85% of protected habitats in Ireland are in unfavourable condition.
- Farmland bird populations have declined by 50% since the 1970s.
- Over 30% of Ireland's semi-natural grasslands have been lost in the last decade.
- 1 in 5 assessed species is threatened with extinction in Ireland.
- 1 in 3 bee species is threatened with extinction.

As of 2025, Dunsany Nature Reserve has captured and stored **7,153,104.64 kg of CO₂** since the programme began in 2024. That figure covers the whole reserve and all its partners, not only Coffeeangel's share, which is aimed at 80,000 kg in the first year. Each Nature Capital Unit stands for one tonne of carbon removed plus a proportionate share of the biodiversity gain, which lets corporate partners make claims that can be checked.

### 6. What is missing

Their website makes **no mention of Fairtrade, Rainforest Alliance or any supply chain certification**. Their sustainability story is entirely about the local, Irish environmental side: cups, biodiversity and carbon. How they treat farmers and where the beans come from is not addressed publicly at all. That is a gap compared with other Irish roasters.


Overall, Coffeeangel has a clear sustainability identity rooted in Ireland: Dunsany, the cups and the reusable campaign. They make a fair point about choosing things that work in practice rather than ticking boxes. But it is all about the local environmental side. Supply chain ethics and conditions on coffee farms are a blind spot in what they communicate publicly.
