# Climate Action Wiki style guide

Every page written or rewritten for this wiki follows this guide, whichever model or person writes it. CLAUDE.md sets the structure of pages; this file sets how they read.

## The voice

Centred, matter-of-fact, empathetic.

- **Centred**: no alarm and no cheerleading. Say what is known, what is not, and how big it is.
- **Matter-of-fact**: lead with the fact, then what it means. Short sentences. Plain words.
- **Empathetic**: remember there are people behind the numbers: the coastal family, the farmer, the commuter, the reader who feels guilty. Acknowledge difficulty without lecturing.

Write like a well-informed friend explaining something over coffee, not like an analyst writing a memo.

## Numbers are never softened

Every figure stays. The plain-language explanation goes **next to** the number, never **instead of** it.

- Right: "about 5,672 tonnes of CO2 a year, which would take an average person 860 years to produce"
- Wrong: "what an ordinary person emits over many generations"

This covers every kind of number: tonnes, percentages, money, dates, counts, ratios, ranges, rankings. When a sentence is rewritten, the numbers it carried must survive.

## Do

- One idea per sentence where possible. Vary sentence length.
- Put a number in context ("roughly the yearly footprint of 1,700 people") rather than stacking adjectives.
- When quoting someone, say who they are and why their view matters, in a sentence. No bulleted quote dumps.
- Bold at most one key term or figure per paragraph. Never bold whole sentences.
- Use lists only for things that really are lists (several figures, steps, names). Explanations go in prose.
- British spelling and phrasing ("rubbish", "autumn", "lorry").

## Do not

- No em-dashes (—) anywhere, including titles, headings and Sources lines. Use a comma, full stop, colon or brackets.
- No meta sections: "Tone and angle", "How it connects to the wiki", or a bare "Notable quotes" list. Related pages go in the `related:` frontmatter field.
- No file paths in the body (`wiki/...md`, `raw/...md`).
- No signposting formulas: "Three things:", "Two things:", "The headline:", "The core argument:", "The key takeaway", "Here's why".
- No inflated or filler words: genuinely, crucially, importantly, landmark, sharpest, shareable, powerful, game-changer, striking, honest/honestly.
- No "not just X, but Y" or "It's not X, it's Y" constructions.
- No numbered "1. **Bold claim.** Explanation" lists for arguments.
- No rhetorical flourish to close a section.

## Sources line format

`- Publication, Author, Date. URL`

## Never change

- Numbers, names, dates, links, and the words inside quotation marks (punctuation such as an em-dash inside a quote may become a comma or full stop).
- Frontmatter, except: em-dashes in `title`, `updated:` date, and the `related:` list.
- The six Myth headings read by the app (Say this, Why it sounds right, What is actually true, If they push back, Also heard as, Sources).
- Fabien's own writing (own research and his translations): em-dash fixes only.
- Formatting Fabien chose by hand, such as blockquoted lists, and hand-typed tags.

## Self-check before saving

1. Zero `—` in the file.
2. None of the banned words or formulas.
3. Every number in the original body is still present (checked by script, not by eye).
4. Every name and link is still present, or deliberately moved to `related:`.
5. Frontmatter unchanged apart from the allowed fields.
