On the day before COP31 officially ends, a new public opinion poll finds that 57% of adults in Türkiye and 68% in Australia have not heard of COP31 or are unsure whether they have. Türkiye is hosting COP31 in Antalya, and Australia is leading the negotiations, and majorities in both countries reported that climate change is putting pressure on the economy and their households.



While 77% of people in Türkiye want more government action on climate change and 68% of people in Australia talk about climate change with friends and family at least every few months, 82% of Australians saw little or no coverage of COP31, and 57% of people in Türkiye weren’t sure or hadn’t heard about COP31 at all.

“The silence is not coming from society,” said Ali Karakas and Ebru Sener, the authors of the report. “Even among people unaware of COP31, 62% in Türkiye and 44% in Australia want more news on how climate change affects their country. They say climate change is squeezing their household budgets and want their governments to do more. What is lacking is news that reaches people.”



The survey found that Solar is viewed favourably by 93% in Türkiye and 83% in Australia, while coal is the least-liked energy source in both countries. Had the public been informed about the COP31 negotiations, perhaps they could have organised to demand more solar power and an end to coal exports. 



Instead, among those with an opinion, 51% in Türkiye and 64% in Australia see climate disinformation as organised and deliberate. The result is a “pluralistic ignorance” in that people think fewer others support climate action than really do. Among those who indicated a willingness to pay to fight climate change, 37% in Türkiye and 39% in Australia think people around them do not share their view.



“This study exposes one of the central deceptions of climate disinformation: the public is not turning its back on climate science. Disinformation creates a false impression of widespread public scepticism, making it easier for policymakers to question public support and shy away from stronger climate action,” said Dana Schran, General Coordinator of CAAD. “Governments have recognised that information integrity is essential to effective climate action. Now they must act on that recognition by tackling the disinformation undermining it.”


About the survey

The survey was conducted online and included 4,009 respondents: 2,004 from Australia and 2,005 from Türkiye. Fieldwork was conducted from 3 to 12 November. Climate Action Against Disinformation (CAAD) commissioned the survey. Oslo-based market research company Norstat collected the data, and Istanbul-based Synesis Agora analysed it and authored the report.
